# Death Attitude Profile-Revised (DAP-R)

The Death Attitude Profile-Revised (DAP-R) is a 32-item multidimensional self-report questionnaire designed to assess individuals' attitudes toward death and dying. Developed by Paul T. P. Wong, Gary T. Reker, and Gina Gesser and published in 1994, the instrument revised the earlier Death Attitude Profile (DAP; Gesser, Wong, & Reker, 1987–88). Its primary conceptual contribution is demonstrating that an individual's orientation toward death cannot be reduced to a unidimensional continuum between fear and absence of fear; rather, diverse cognitive and emotional stances can coexist within the same individual.

## Dimensional Structure and Scoring

The DAP-R measures five distinct dimensions of death attitudes across 32 items:
1. **Fear of Death (7 items)**: Measures dread, anxiety, and apprehension regarding the prospect of death or the dying process.
2. **Death Avoidance (5 items)**: Assesses behavioral and cognitive efforts to avoid thinking, talking, or engaging with reminders of death.
3. **Neutral Acceptance (5 items)**: Captures the view of death as an inevitable, natural law of existence, neither greeted with dread nor embraced with yearning.
4. **Approach Acceptance (10 items)**: Measures the belief in an afterlife or positive transcendental existence following physical death, viewing death as a passage to a better state.
5. **Escape Acceptance (5 items)**: Evaluates the perception of death as an escape or relief from painful psychological, physical, or social existence.

Respondents rate items on a 7-point Likert scale ranging from 1 ("strongly disagree") to 7 ("strongly agree"). Subscale scores are calculated as item means, generating a multidimensional profile rather than a single composite score.

## Psychometric Properties

Across normative and specialized populations, the DAP-R subscales display moderate to high internal consistency. Cronbach's alpha coefficients typically range from approximately 0.65 for Neutral Acceptance to above 0.90 for Approach Acceptance. Construct validity is supported by convergent and discriminant correlations: Fear of Death correlates positively with Templer's Death Anxiety Scale (DAS), Approach Acceptance correlates robustly with religious and philosophical beliefs in immortality, and Escape Acceptance correlates with measures of hopelessness and somatic burden.

## Cross-Cultural Standardization and Arabic Versions

The DAP-R has been translated into many languages, including Arabic, and used in studies in several countries. Reported factor structure and reliability in translated versions vary across samples, so local psychometric evidence should be checked before use.

## Clinical and Research Applications

The DAP-R is extensively employed in thanatology, palliative medicine, psycho-oncology, and the psychology of religion. It allows clinicians and researchers to distinguish between normative existential contemplation and severe death anxiety requiring psychological intervention.

## Limitations

The DAP-R is a psychometric research questionnaire and not a formal diagnostic instrument; no clinical diagnostic cut-offs exist. Because death attitudes are sensitive to religious, philosophical, and cultural norms, scores must be interpreted within the respondent's specific cultural context.
