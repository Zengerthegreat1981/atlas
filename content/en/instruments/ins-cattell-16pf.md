# Cattell's Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire (16PF)

The Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire (16PF) is a standardized self-report personality assessment containing 185 items. Designed by Raymond B. Cattell, the instrument measures sixteen primary personality traits and five global dimensions in individuals aged 16 and older, representing a foundational milestone in factor-analytic psychometrics.

## Theoretical Foundations and Design

Cattell formulated the 16PF within the framework of the lexical hypothesis, the premise that all important aspects of human personality are encoded in natural language. Beginning with the 4,500 trait terms identified by Gordon Allport and Henry Odbert (1936), Cattell reduced the list through semantic grouping and factor analysis of peer ratings (life-record or L-data) and self-report ratings (questionnaire or Q-data). 

Cattell distinguished between observable "surface traits" and underlying latent "source traits." The 16PF was constructed to isolate these deeper source traits. The original questionnaire appeared in 1949, with subsequent revisions culminating in the Fifth Edition published in 1993 by Raymond B. Cattell, A. Karen Cattell, and Heather E. P. Cattell.

## Structure and Psychometric Properties

The 16PF Fifth Edition consists of 185 forced-choice items, each presenting three options (e.g., true, in between/uncertain, false). Raw scores are converted into Standard Ten (Sten) scores ranging from 1 to 10, with a mean of 5.5 and a standard deviation of 2.0.

The test measures sixteen primary personality factors:
- **Factor A**: Warmth (Reserved vs. Warm)
- **Factor B**: Reasoning (Concrete vs. Abstract)
- **Factor C**: Emotional Stability (Reactive vs. Emotionally Stable)
- **Factor E**: Dominance (Deferential vs. Dominant)
- **Factor F**: Liveliness (Serious vs. Lively)
- **Factor G**: Rule-Consciousness (Expedient vs. Rule-Conscious)
- **Factor H**: Social Boldness (Shy vs. Socially Bold)
- **Factor I**: Sensitivity (Utilitarian vs. Sensitive)
- **Factor L**: Vigilance (Trusting vs. Vigilant)
- **Factor M**: Abstractedness (Practical vs. Idea-Oriented)
- **Factor N**: Privateness (Forthright vs. Discreet)
- **Factor O**: Apprehension (Self-Assured vs. Apprehensive)
- **Factor Q1**: Openness to Change (Traditional vs. Experimenting)
- **Factor Q2**: Self-Reliance (Group-Oriented vs. Self-Reliant)
- **Factor Q3**: Perfectionism (Tolerates Disorder vs. Perfectionistic)
- **Factor Q4**: Tension (Relaxed vs. Tense)

Factor analysis of these primary scales yields five broad Global Factors that align closely with the Five-Factor Model: Extraversion, Anxiety, Tough-Mindedness, Independence, and Self-Control. 

Short-term test-retest reliabilities for the Fifth Edition typically range between 0.70 and 0.86, with internal consistency coefficients (Cronbach's alpha) averaging approximately 0.75 across primary scales.

## Applications and Criticisms

The 16PF is widely utilized in industrial and organizational psychology for personnel selection, executive leadership assessment, vocational counseling, and marital guidance.

Despite its historical prominence and commercial longevity, the 16PF has faced persistent psychometric critique. Independent psychometricians (such as Hans Eysenck and later Big Five researchers) frequently failed to replicate Cattell's 16-factor primary structure, demonstrating that the scales share substantial variance and that higher-order three- to five-factor models offer superior replicability and statistical parsimony.
