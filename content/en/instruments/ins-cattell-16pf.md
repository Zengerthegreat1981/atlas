# Cattell's Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire

A standardized personality questionnaire of 185 items that measures 16 independent source personality traits and five broad global dimensions for individuals aged 16 and over, and it is considered one of the first instruments built directly on statistical factor analysis.

## Design and Standardization

It was designed by Raymond Cattell on the basis of factor analysis of personality vocabulary (the Lexical Hypothesis), and the first version was published in 1949, followed by successive editions up to the fifth edition in 1993 (Cattell, Cattell and Cattell). The fifth edition was normed on a large American sample. Arabic translations and adaptations of the test exist, but their norms are not unified.

## Structure and Psychometric Properties

The test measures 16 primary factors (warmth, reasoning, emotional stability, dominance, liveliness, rule-consciousness, social boldness, sensitivity, vigilance, abstractedness, privateness, apprehension, openness to change, self-reliance, perfectionism, and tension), in addition to 5 second-order global factors that approximately correspond to the Big Five model. Its test-retest reliability is generally in the 0.70s to 0.80s over short intervals and lower over longer intervals.

## Clinical and Research Use

It is widely used in industrial and organizational assessment, vocational guidance and counseling, family counseling, and comparative academic research in personality psychology.

## Limitations and Criticism

The debate over the stability of the sixteen factors across later independent factor analyses has not been settled, as several studies failed to extract the same structure precisely, which led some researchers to prefer simpler models such as the Big Five in contemporary academic research, even though the 16PF continues to be used commercially and institutionally.

The test still holds an important historical standing in the personality psychology literature as one of the first systematic attempts to build a complete measurement instrument starting from purely statistical factor analysis instead of clinical intuition, an approach that later paved the way for the Big Five model.

It is worth noting that Cattell himself distinguished in his writings between surface traits, which are observable directly, and source traits, which are latent and revealed only by factor analysis, considering that the test measures the deeper source traits. The fifth edition is still used in some programs for selecting employees for senior leadership positions, despite competition from newer instruments built directly on the Big Five model, which has the strongest scientific consensus today.
