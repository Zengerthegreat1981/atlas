# Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5 (SCID-5)

The Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5 (SCID-5) is a semi-structured clinician-administered diagnostic interview designed to establish reliable psychiatric diagnoses in accordance with the diagnostic criteria of the American Psychiatric Association's Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5). Developed under the leadership of Michael B. First, Janet B. W. Williams, Rhonda S. Karg, and Robert L. Spitzer, the SCID series represents the primary criterion standard for clinical research and pharmaceutical trials.

## Historical Evolution and Versions

The instrument originated in the early 1980s under Robert Spitzer and colleagues as the SCID for DSM-III and DSM-III-R (published in 1990), which revolutionized psychiatric research by standardizing the evaluation of operationalized diagnostic criteria. Following the publication of DSM-IV in 1994, the SCID-IV became the international benchmark for psychiatric diagnosis. 

Following the release of DSM-5 (2013), the series was restructured into specialized packages:
- **SCID-5-CV (Clinician Version)**: A streamlined version covering disorders most frequently treated in clinical practice (mood, psychotic, substance use, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, and trauma-related disorders).
- **SCID-5-RV (Research Version)**: A modular, comprehensive protocol that assesses full operational criteria, subtypes, course specifiers, and subthreshold symptoms, adaptable to specific investigation designs.
- **SCID-5-CT (Clinical Trials Version)**: Tailored specifically to determine inclusion and exclusion eligibility for clinical pharmacology trials.
- **SCID-5-PD (Personality Disorders)**: A specialized interview assessing the ten DSM-5 personality disorders across Clusters A, B, and C (superseding the SCID-II).

## Administration Architecture and Decision Logic

The SCID-5 is structured as a hierarchical decision tree. Administration begins with a brief, unstandardized open clinical overview to elicit history of presenting complaints, baseline functioning, and past treatments. 

Following this orientation, the interview proceeds through diagnostic modules using standardized skip-out logic:
1. **Screening Probes**: Each module begins with mandatory screening questions. If the patient denies the essential primary symptoms of a disorder (e.g., neither depressed mood nor anhedonia for Major Depressive Disorder), the interviewer skips the remaining criteria for that module.
2. **Criterion-by-Criterion Evaluation**: When screening criteria are endorsed, the interviewer probes operational requirements step-by-step.
3. **Standardized Rating System**: Each individual criterion is coded using a three-point rating:
   - `?` = Inadequate information to make a determination
   - `-` or `1` = Absent or subthreshold
   - `+` or `2` = Present or threshold criteria met

A formal diagnosis requires satisfying all mandatory symptom thresholds, duration criteria, and psychosocial impairment standards, while systematically verifying that symptoms are not attributable to substance use, medication effects, or a general medical condition.

## Psychometric Properties and Research Standing

The SCID-5 serves as the criterion "gold standard" against which brief screening inventories (such as the PHQ-9, GAD-7, and BDI-II) are validated. Inter-rater reliability across trained clinicians is robust, with Cohen's kappa coefficients typically exceeding 0.80 for Major Depressive Disorder, Bipolar I Disorder, Schizophrenia, and Substance Use Disorders. Lower inter-rater agreement (kappa between 0.50 and 0.65) is observed for disorders with high symptom overlap, such as Generalized Anxiety Disorder and Somatic Symptom Disorder.

## Practical Constraints

The interview requires specialized clinical judgment and formal interviewer training; it cannot be administered by lay assessors. Full administration typically requires between 45 and 90 minutes based on diagnostic complexity, constraining its feasibility in acute triage environments.
