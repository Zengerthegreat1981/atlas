# The Baddeley and Hitch Working Memory Study (1974)

A revolutionary experimental and theoretical study that took apart the simple concept of 'short-term memory' and replaced it with the 'multicomponent working memory' model, through dual-task experiments suggesting that different kinds of information can be maintained and processed in partly separate systems.

## Context and experimental design

Baddeley and Hitch used the "dual-task paradigm": the participant is asked to perform a traditional short-term memory task (such as repeating a series of digits, the Digit Span) while performing a second cognitive task (such as logical reasoning or reading comprehension). The prevailing hypothesis before them (the single-store model of Atkinson and Shiffrin) predicted that any additional load on "short-term memory" would completely disrupt performance on the second task if both consumed the same single limited capacity.

## The three original components of the model

Contrary to the single-store prediction, the researchers found that participants could hold a moderate digit load while reasoning with little impairment, and that performance declined only gradually as the load increased toward the limit of span, which suggested a multicomponent model in place of a single store: the **Central Executive**, a limited-capacity supervisory system that allocates attention among tasks; the **Phonological Loop** (called the "articulatory loop" in the 1974 chapter), a subsystem that stores verbal information and refreshes its trace through silent internal repetition (Subvocal Rehearsal); and the **Visuospatial Sketchpad**, a subsystem for storing visual and spatial information. Later dual-task studies supported the view that verbal and visuospatial tasks interfere with each other less than two tasks within the same domain.

## Experimental Series (1974)

The 1974 chapter reported a series of dual-task experiments in which participants held digit sequences of varying length while performing reasoning, comprehension, or free-recall tasks. Samples were small, within-subjects designs typical of experimental cognitive psychology at the time. Across the series, concurrent loads within the span limit produced only modest impairment on the second task, which was taken as evidence against a single, undifferentiated short-term store.

## Impact on neuroscience and cognitive science and later development

The model established the prevailing cognitive framework for understanding executive dysfunction, learning difficulties, attention and simultaneous interpreting, and it has been related to brain imaging findings, with prefrontal regions implicated in executive functions and left-hemisphere frontal and parietal regions in phonological storage and rehearsal. In 2000 Baddeley, on his own, added a fourth component, the "Episodic Buffer," a limited-capacity temporary storage system that integrates information from the two subsystems with long-term memory into a unified multimodal representation, in order to fill the theoretical gap the original three-component model faced in explaining how information is integrated across different systems.
