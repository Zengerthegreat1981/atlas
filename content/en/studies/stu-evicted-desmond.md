# Evicted

An intensive ethnographic study carried out by Matthew Desmond (published 2016, winner of the Pulitzer Prize), during which he lived among poor tenants and landlords in the American city of Milwaukee, to document the role of "forced eviction" as a forgotten central mechanism in the reproduction of contemporary American urban poverty.

## Method and Content

Desmond lived directly in poor housing complexes (white and Black) in Milwaukee, closely following the lives of families facing repeated forced eviction from their homes because of a slight delay in paying rent, and paralleling this by following the landlords themselves, who run the business of renting to the poor as a profitable commercial model based partly on exploiting tenants' vulnerability. Desmond showed that eviction is not merely a result of poverty but also a direct "cause" of it: losing housing means losing the fixed address needed to apply for a job, losing furniture and possessions that are confiscated or left behind, and difficulty renting a new home with a prior eviction record (which is officially recorded and consulted by later landlords), producing a vicious circle of continuing housing instability that undermines every other aspect of the family's life (children's schoolwork, parents' mental health, job opportunities). He also documented how renting to the poor specifically represents a relatively high-profit model for landlords, given the low value of property in poor neighborhoods against relatively high rents imposed owing to the absence of sufficient housing alternatives for poor tenants.

## Impact

This study shed light on "forced eviction" as a central structural social crisis previously neglected in American urban poverty studies, and informing debates over tenants' "right to counsel" in eviction cases, and it became one of the most influential and widely read contemporary sociological studies well beyond academic circles, stimulating a national debate on the crisis of affordable housing for poor Americans.

## The Ethnographic Method

Desmond's work, as an academic sociologist at Princeton University (formerly Harvard), is distinguished by combining prolonged ethnographic immersion (he lived in a trailer park and then in a rooming house in the neighborhoods he studied) with quantitative analysis, including the Milwaukee Area Renters Study survey and court eviction records, which gave his qualitative conclusions a supporting statistical basis that is rare in classical urban ethnography.

## Impact and Policy Application

Desmond later founded the "Eviction Lab" at Princeton University, a huge national data project documenting eviction rates across the United States, which builds on the study and has supplied data to policy debates on housing and tenant protection, and the book became a widely used course text in American sociology and social policy programmes.
