# Mexico's PROGRESA Conditional Cash Transfer Program

A Mexican government programme launched in 1997 under the name "PROGRESA" (Programa de Educación, Salud y Alimentación, the Education, Health and Nutrition Programme). It is regarded as one of the first large-scale conditional cash transfer programmes to be evaluated with a randomized experimental design from its launch, which made it a global reference model that dozens of countries later followed (Bolsa Família in Brazil, and similar programmes in Colombia, Indonesia, the Philippines and elsewhere).

## The programme's idea: payment in return for investment in human capital

PROGRESA provides monthly direct cash transfers to poor households in rural areas, on condition that they adhere to specific behaviours that invest in children's human capital: regular school attendance (with a payment that rises as children advance through grades, plus an additional transfer for girls at secondary level specifically, to counter their early drop-out from school, which is common), and regular attendance at preventive health visits and nutrition education sessions for mothers. From its launch the programme designed an experimental evaluation in cooperation with academic researchers, exploiting the natural administrative constraints on the speed of expansion (it was not possible to enrol all eligible villages at once) to randomly determine the order of entry to the programme across 506 villages. Roughly three fifths of the villages (320) received transfers immediately, while the rest (186) entered the programme after a delay of about a year and a half. This allowed a clean experimental comparison of the causal effect of the programme during this delay period.

## Results: a tangible improvement in education and health

The evaluation showed a clear rise in school enrolment rates, especially in the transition from primary to secondary school, where the drop-out rate had traditionally been high. The effect was relatively greater among girls, owing to the additional incentive allocated to them. Beneficiary villages also recorded improvements in indicators of preventive health visits and a decline in rates of malnutrition among young children compared with control villages during the evaluation period. This provided early and relatively convincing experimental evidence that conditional cash transfers can achieve a dual effect: immediate poverty alleviation through direct cash income, and long-term investment in human capital through attached education and health conditions.

The programme was renamed "Oportunidades" in 2002 and "Prospera" in 2014, with design modifications; this entry concerns the original PROGRESA evaluation of the late 1990s.

## Global impact: an exported model for dozens of countries

PROGRESA became a global reference model in developing-country social policy. Brazil's well-known "Bolsa Família" programme (launched in 2003, reaching millions of households) belongs to the same family of designs, and several countries in Latin America, Asia and Africa adopted similar forms of conditional cash transfers, partly drawing on the relatively strong experimental evidence provided by the original PROGRESA evaluation as a successful precedent that could be emulated in different national contexts.

## The debate on conditionality: is the condition necessary?

PROGRESA's success prompted a parallel debate on the necessity of the "conditionality" element itself. Would the improvements in education and health have occurred to the same extent, or to a similar degree, if families had been given the same cash income without conditions of regular school and health attendance (that is, as an unconditional cash transfer)? Later comparative studies in other contexts suggested that part of the effect may be attributable to the additional income itself regardless of the condition, while another part is attributable specifically to the element of conditionality and the attached education, a question that remains an ongoing subject of comparative research between conditional and unconditional transfer programmes to this day.
