# South Asian Kinship Systems: Dumont on Hierarchy and Ideology

Louis Dumont (1911-1998), a French anthropologist and Indologist, contributed to South Asian kinship through his fieldwork among the Pramalai Kallar of Tamil Nadu (1949-50) and his analysis of Dravidian kinship terminology and marriage alliance, and then to the wider study of caste through *Homo Hierarchicus* (French 1966; English 1970). The entry is chiefly about how Dumont linked kinship and alliance in South India to a general theory of hierarchy as an ideology.

## Kinship and Alliance in South India

In work from the early 1950s Dumont argued that Dravidian kinship terminology, in which relatives are divided into "kin" (parallel relatives) and "affines" (cross relatives), expresses a rule of marriage with the cross-cousin, so that the terminology encodes alliance between groups rather than only descent. His monograph on the Pramalai Kallar (published in French in 1957; English translation 1986) and the essay "Hierarchy and Marriage Alliance in South Indian Kinship" (1957) describe how affinity, a relation that is transmitted from generation to generation between the same kinds of groups, is a basic organizing principle. This work drew on and extended Lévi-Strauss's alliance theory, though Dumont also owed much to Marcel Mauss and Durkheimian sociology, and he was critical of some strands of structuralism and of British descent-theory approaches.

## Homo Hierarchicus

*Homo Hierarchicus* is not a village ethnography but a general essay on the caste system. Dumont drew on Indian textual, ethnographic and historical sources to argue that caste is best understood as an ideology of hierarchy, organized by the opposition between pure and impure. Key propositions include:

- Hierarchy is the ordering of elements in relation to the whole; the superior encompasses the inferior ("hierarchical opposition").
- The ranking of the Brahmin above the king reflects the primacy of the religious (status) over the political and economic (power) in the ideology, so that power is subordinated to status.
- Caste is a holistic system in which groups are interdependent, in contrast to modern Western ideology, which is individualist and egalitarian. Dumont later developed this contrast in *Essays on Individualism* (1983).

## Reception and Critique

The book was extremely influential but contested. Critics, including McKim Marriott and others, argued that Dumont's emphasis on a single Brahmanical ideology understated regional variation, material interests, dominance by landed castes, and the role of kingship and power. Historians such as Nicholas Dirks argued that caste as described by Dumont was partly shaped by colonial rule, and Dalit scholars have criticized the reliance on Brahmanical texts. Feminist scholars have noted that the work pays little attention to gender. Defenders note that Dumont framed his account as an analysis of ideology, not of everyday behaviour. His comparative contrast between holism and individualism continues to be debated.
