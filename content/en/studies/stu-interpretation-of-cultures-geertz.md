# The Interpretation of Cultures: Geertz on Thick Description and Cultural Analysis

Clifford Geertz's *The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays* (Basic Books, 1973) collects fifteen essays written between the mid-1950s and early 1970s, drawing chiefly on his research in Java and Bali (Indonesia). The volume presents the case for an interpretive approach to anthropology, in which culture is treated as a system of public meanings to be read rather than as a set of laws or mechanisms to be explained. Its opening essay, "Thick Description: Toward an Interpretive Theory of Culture," is among the most cited texts in the discipline.

## Context

Geertz (1926-2006) took his PhD at Harvard (Department of Social Relations) in 1956, where he worked with Clyde Kluckhohn and Talcott Parsons. He conducted fieldwork in Java in 1952-54 (the basis of *The Religion of Java*, 1960), in Bali in 1957-58 with Hildred Geertz, and later in Morocco (from the 1960s), though the Moroccan work is largely represented in later books. He taught at the University of Chicago in the 1960s and from 1970 at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. *The Interpretation of Cultures* emerged in a period of dissatisfaction with structural-functionalism and with behaviourist and evolutionary approaches, and was shaped by Weber, Wittgenstein, Ryle, Langer, Ricoeur and others.

## Central Ideas

- **Culture as meaning.** Geertz, following Max Weber, held that "man is an animal suspended in webs of significance he himself has spun," and that culture is those webs. In "Religion as a Cultural System" he defined religion in terms of a system of symbols that establishes moods and motivations by formulating conceptions of a general order of existence. Culture is described there as an historically transmitted pattern of meanings embodied in symbols.
- **Thick description.** Borrowing the term from the philosopher Gilbert Ryle, Geertz used the example of a twitch and a wink (and a parody of a wink) to show that the same physical movement can have different meanings depending on the code in which it is performed. Ethnography, he argued, is "thick" when it recovers these layers of meaning, not merely when it records behaviour.
- **Interpretation, not law.** Anthropological analysis is, he claimed, "not an experimental science in search of law but an interpretive one in search of meaning." Analysis is itself an interpretation of interpretations, and is always incomplete.
- **Culture as public.** Meanings are carried by public symbols, which can be observed in ritual, art, and everyday events, rather than hidden in minds.
- **Critique of the "stratigraphic" conception of man.** In "The Impact of the Concept of Culture on the Concept of Man," he argued against the view that human nature is a universal biological layer with culture added on top.

## Essays and Cases

The volume contains essays on religion ("Religion as a Cultural System"; "Ethos, World View, and the Analysis of Sacred Symbols"; "Ritual and Social Change: A Javanese Example", which analyzes a disrupted funeral in a Javanese town; "Internal Conversion in Contemporary Bali"), on ideology and politics ("Ideology as a Cultural System"; "The Integrative Revolution"; "After the Revolution"), and on Bali ("Person, Time, and Conduct in Bali"; "Deep Play: Notes on the Balinese Cockfight"). The Moroccan bazaar essay "Suq" is not part of this collection; it appeared in 1979 in *Meaning and Order in Moroccan Society*.

## Reception and Critique

The book had a major influence on cultural anthropology and on the humanities, and helped to prepare the "writing culture" debates of the 1980s. Critics have argued that it underplays power, material conditions and history; that its notion of culture as a coherent text is too integrated; that its interpretive claims are hard to test (see Shankman 1984 on the cockfight and the "thin" side of thick description); and that it neglects women and conflict. Marxist and feminist anthropologists and postcolonial critics have made versions of these objections. The collection remains a standard text on method and on the concept of culture.
