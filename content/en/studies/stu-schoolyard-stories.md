# Schoolyard Stories

"Schoolyard Stories" is the title under which this entry gathers sociological work on children's peer cultures in schools and playgrounds. No single published study under that title has been confirmed for this entry, so it describes the well-established body of research it refers to.

## The Research Tradition

Sociologists and folklorists have studied how children build their own social worlds with their own games, rituals, stories and rules, rather than treating childhood only as preparation for adult life. Iona and Peter Opie's *The Lore and Language of Schoolchildren* (1959) documented the rhymes, games and customs that British schoolchildren passed to one another with little adult involvement. William Corsaro's *Friendship and Peer Culture in the Early Years* (1985) used ethnographic observation in nursery schools to show how young children collectively create "peer cultures" and interpret adult rules. Barrie Thorne's *Gender Play: Girls and Boys in School* (1993) observed children in American elementary schools and argued that gender separation in play is actively produced through "borderwork" between groups of boys and girls rather than simply given. Gary Alan Fine's *With the Boys: Little League Baseball and Preadolescent Culture* (1987) examined how preadolescent boys' talk, humour and rule-making shaped their peer culture.

## Why It Matters

This work treats children as competent social actors and shows that classrooms and playgrounds are sites where norms of gender, status and belonging are negotiated. Its methods are mainly ethnographic and depend on long observation and on the adult researcher's careful negotiation of access and ethical limits when working with children.
