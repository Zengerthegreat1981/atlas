# Pavlov's Original Classical Conditioning and Conditioned Reflex Experiments

The founding physiological experiments conducted by Ivan Pavlov on salivary secretion in dogs, discovering how a neutral stimulus acquires the ability to elicit a physiological reflex as a result of its repeated pairing with food.

## Context and Experimental Design

While studying the physiology of the digestive system, Pavlov noticed that dogs began to secrete saliva merely at the sight of the laboratory attendant or at hearing his footsteps before food was presented. He designed soundproofed rooms and measured salivary secretion quantitatively through a surgically created fistula of the salivary duct, presenting a neutral stimulus (a metronome, a bell, or a light) followed by an unconditioned stimulus (meat powder).

## Results and Conclusions

After several pairings, the neutral stimulus alone (the conditioned stimulus) became able to elicit salivation (the conditioned response). Pavlov described the central phenomena of conditioning: generalization, discrimination, extinction in the absence of reinforcement, and spontaneous recovery after a rest period, laying the experimental foundation for the study of associative learning.

## The Basic Laws of Conditioning
Pavlov identified several laws governing the conditioned reflex: stimulus generalization (Stimulus Generalization), in which dogs respond to sounds similar to the original bell; discrimination (Discrimination), that is, the ability to distinguish the conditioned stimulus from others after selective training; extinction (Extinction) when the conditioned stimulus is repeatedly presented without food; and spontaneous recovery (Spontaneous Recovery), the partial return of the conditioned response after a rest period despite the earlier extinction. He also studied "second-order conditioning," in which a new neutral stimulus is paired with the original conditioned stimulus rather than directly with the unconditioned stimulus.

## Criticism and Methodological Controversy

The simple pairing account later faced challenges from work by Rescorla and others, which showed that conditioning depends on the informational value of the signal (the contingency between stimuli) and not merely on their temporal pairing.

## Impact
Pavlov's experiments, for which he received the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1904 (for his research on the physiology of digestion, not for conditioning itself), were an important influence on John Watson's American behaviorism, which saw in them an objective and measurable model for explaining behavior independent of subjective introspection. Conditioning principles (extinction and counterconditioning) also informed behavior therapy techniques such as the systematic desensitization developed by Joseph Wolpe for phobias.
