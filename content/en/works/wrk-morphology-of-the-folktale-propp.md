# Morphology of the Folktale

The foundational work of Vladimir Propp (1928). In it he analysed one hundred Russian wonder tales (fairy tales) and concluded that their surface diversity (different characters and events) conceals a deep narrative structure common to all of them.

## Fixed Narrative Functions

The central thesis of the book is that, despite differences in characters and details from one tale to another, all the Russian fairy tales Propp studied are composed of a limited set of "narrative functions" (thirty-one in his scheme, plus the initial situation). These are the actions of characters defined by their importance for the development of the plot, such as "absentation," "interdiction," "violation," and "reconnaissance." They appear in a relatively fixed order across all the tales, although not every function appears in every tale.

## Method: Structural Analysis Before Structuralism

The book arose within the Russian Formalist milieu and is an early precursor of structuralist analysis of narrative (it was written independently of, and before the spread of, French structuralism). Propp applied the logic of searching for a fixed formal structure hidden behind a surface diversity. The same logic was later developed by Greimas's semiotic school, with more abstract and formal tools (the actantial model, which draws on Propp's "spheres of action" of the characters, and the semiotic square).

## Corpus and Methodology

Propp based his analysis on a precisely defined corpus of one hundred Russian wonder tales taken from the Afanasyev collection, the principal reference collection of Russian folklore recorded in the nineteenth century. He deliberately limited his analysis to the genre of the "fairy tale" (or "wonder tale") and excluded other genres of Russian folklore, such as realistic or animal tales. This conscious methodological choice limited how far his conclusions could be generalised, but it gave his analysis high precision within that particular genre.

## The Opening Sequence of Functions

The book details the sequence of functions, beginning with an initial situation (the introduction of the members of a family), passing through "absentation" (the departure of one of the older family members), "interdiction" imposed on the hero, "violation" (the hero's breaking of this interdiction), "reconnaissance" and "delivery" (the villain's attempt to gather information about the hero or the victim), and arriving at the "harm or lack" that actually drives the plot and pushes the hero to leave in search of a solution. This opening sequence defines the initial condition for the rest of the tale's course.

## Distinguishing the Function from the Surface Act

The book's most precise methodological innovation is Propp's strict distinction between the "function" (defined by its structural effect on the development of the plot, such as the function of "provision with a magical agent") and the concrete form it takes in each individual tale. The magical agent may be a ring in one tale and a talking horse in another. This separation between the abstract structural level (the fixed function) and the variable surface level (the literal content) is exactly the method that French structuralism later borrowed and applied to fields of analysis far beyond the folktale.

## Impact

Although the work remained relatively unknown outside Russia for decades, its English translation (1958) became very influential in Western structural studies of narrative. It formed the direct basis on which Greimas built his actantial model of narrative roles, and it profoundly influenced narratology as a whole.

## Late Reception and Translation

The work remained almost entirely unknown outside the Soviet Union for three decades after its original publication in 1928. This was partly due to the barrier of the Russian language and partly to the relative academic isolation between the West and the Soviet Union in that period. The situation changed only with its translation into English in 1958 by Laurence Scott, which came with an important critical introduction. The translation made the work available to Western structuralists (Lévi-Strauss, who published a critique of Propp in 1960, and Greimas and Bremond among others), and it influenced them deeply during the 1960s, at a time when French structuralism itself was rising to dominance in the human sciences.

## Applications Beyond Russian Folklore

Although Propp's corpus was limited to Russian folklore, his model was later used as an analytical tool for studying modern narrative patterns far removed from its original context. Examples include analyses of popular films and genre fiction, where some researchers have found correspondences with Propp's sequence of functions. Some writers on screenwriting have adopted his model, or simplified versions of it, as a practical tool for plotting, although such uses go well beyond Propp's own corpus and aims. (Joseph Campbell's "hero's journey" is a separate, mythological model, not a version of Propp's.)

## Editions and Later Reviews

A second, revised English edition of the translation appeared in 1968 (University of Texas Press, revised and edited by Louis A. Wagner, with a new introduction by Alan Dundes, an American folklorist). It is the most widely used edition in academic work and placed the work in its historical and methodological context for Western readers.
