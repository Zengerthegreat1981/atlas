# The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays

*The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays* (1973) brings together Clifford Geertz's essays on culture, religion, ideology, politics, and Indonesian society. Mostly written over the preceding decade and a half, with a new opening statement, they present anthropology as an interpretive study of meaning. The volume became a central text of interpretive and symbolic anthropology and was read widely outside the discipline.

## Plan of the book

The fifteen chapters move from general theory to particular cases. They open with "Thick Description: Toward an Interpretive Theory of Culture". Essays on the concept of culture and the evolution of mind argue that human biology developed together with culture, so that there is no human nature complete before cultural forms. Further groups address religion, ideology, and politics in the new states of Asia and Africa. The closing chapters include "The Cerebral Savage", on Claude Lévi-Strauss; "Person, Time, and Conduct in Bali"; and "Deep Play: Notes on the Balinese Cockfight".

## Culture and thick description

Geertz proposes a semiotic concept of culture. Following Max Weber, he pictures people as suspended in webs of significance that they have spun, and identifies culture with those webs. Elsewhere he calls it a historically transmitted pattern of meanings embodied in symbols. Meaning is public: it can be studied through what people say and do.

Borrowing from Gilbert Ryle, Geertz distinguishes a physical report of a closing eyelid from an account able to separate a twitch from a wink, an imitation of a wink, or a rehearsal. Ethnography is thick description of such layered significance. Its accounts are interpretations, often of the interpretations of participants. Theory does not stand apart as general law; it guides the analysis of cases and is judged partly by how much it makes intelligible.

## Religion and ideology

"Religion as a Cultural System" defines religion as a system of symbols that establishes powerful and lasting moods and motivations by formulating conceptions of a general order of existence and giving them an air of factuality. Symbols are models of reality and models for it. "Ritual and Social Change" examines a disputed Javanese funeral to show that cultural meaning and social structure can fall out of step, a point directed against functionalist assumptions of integration. "Ideology as a Cultural System" criticises accounts that treat ideology only as the expression of interest or strain and asks how its figurative language makes political situations meaningful.

## Politics and the Balinese essays

The political essays examine nationalism and the tension between civic states and attachments of kinship, language, religion, region, or custom, which Geertz called primordial. Later writers objected that this vocabulary can make historically produced identities seem given. The essay on person and time analyses Balinese personal names, birth-order names, kin terms, teknonyms, titles, and calendars to ask how individuals are perceived. The cockfight essay presents a popular contest as a dramatisation of status concerns and develops the image of culture as an ensemble of texts.

## Reception and criticism

The book offered an alternative to behaviourism, to some forms of functionalism, and to structural analysis detached from action. Its prose and concepts influenced history, literary criticism, sociology, and religious studies. Talal Asad argued that its account of religion relies on a particular modern conception of belief and neglects how power authorises religious truth. William Roseberry argued that reading culture as text leaves the social production of meaning insufficiently explained. Others questioned how rival interpretations are tested, how much agreement "a culture" implies, and whose voices are heard, including those of women. Contributors to the later *Writing Culture* debates extended the question of interpretation to the ethnographer's own authority.
