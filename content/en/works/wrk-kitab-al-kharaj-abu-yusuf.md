# Kitab al-Kharaj

A jurisprudential and administrative treatise by Abu Yusuf Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim al-Ansari (d. 182 AH / 798 CE), chief judge of the Abbasid state in the reign of Harun al-Rashid and the leading student of Abu Hanifa al-Nu'man. He is credited with a major share in codifying and spreading the Hanafi school in practice through his tenure as chief judge. *Kitab al-Kharaj* is one of the oldest and most important surviving texts of **Islamic financial and administrative jurisprudence**. It is exceptional in that it was not written as a purely theoretical jurisprudential treatise, but as a practical, directed letter addressed to the caliph himself at his request.

## The Context of Composition: A Consultation from the Caliph

According to the opening of the book itself and the widely transmitted accounts, Harun al-Rashid asked Abu Yusuf to compose this book to serve as a practical guide to the management of state finances, especially matters concerning *kharaj* (the tax imposed on cultivated land acquired by conquest). This arose from the growing need of the expanding Abbasid state to organize its financial resources on clear sharia foundations, given the breadth of its territory, the variety of its conquered lands (taken by force or by treaty), and the diversity of its population between Muslims and *dhimmis* (protected non-Muslims). This context makes the book a unique document combining the character of a "jurisprudential opinion" with that of a "political-administrative report" addressed directly to the decision-maker, a rare combination in early jurisprudential heritage, which was dominated by abstract theoretical classification.

## Content of the Book

The book addresses several matters, most prominently: the types of land and the rulings on their kharaj (a kharaj levied on the land regardless of whether it is actually cultivated, as opposed to a *kharaj muqasama* calculated as a share of the actual produce); the rulings on *'ushr* (tithe), *jizya* (poll tax), charitable alms, and zakat on livestock and trade; the rulings on lands conquered by force (*'anwatan*, such as the lands of al-Sawad in Iraq) compared with those conquered by treaty (*sulhan*); the rulings on the granting (*iqta'*) and reclamation of dead lands (*mawat*); in addition to direct advice addressed to the caliph on justice in tax collection, warnings against the oppression of governors and agents towards the subjects, and questions concerning the treatment of *dhimmis* and their financial rights.

## The Political-Sharia Character (Jurisprudential Sultanic Literature)

The book combines traditional jurisprudential reasoning (from the Qur'an, the Sunna, the reports of the Companions, and especially 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, whose financial policies Abu Yusuf frequently cites) with advice of a direct political character resembling the literature of "counsel to kings" (*Mirrors for Princes*). This makes it among the early foundational documents of what would later be known as **siyasa shar'iyya** (governance in light of the general aims of the sharia in matters on which there is no specific text), although the term itself did not fully crystallize theoretically until later, in the work of jurists such as Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim.

## Its Importance as a Historical and Economic Source

The book's influence goes beyond jurisprudential studies to historical and economic studies of the early Abbasid state. It provides detailed information on the actual financial system then applied, on the nature of the relationship between the centre and the provinces in tax collection, and on the treatment of newly conquered lands. It is therefore cited frequently in studies of early Islamic economics and the administrative history of the Abbasid caliphate, not in jurisprudence alone.

## His Position on the Justice of the Ruler and Control of Governors' Conduct

Abu Yusuf devotes a broad section of his treatise to warning against the oppression of governors and agents in collecting kharaj. He cites the conduct of the rightly guided caliphs, especially 'Umar ibn al-Khattab in his justice and his close supervision of his agents in the provinces. He offers direct practical advice to Harun al-Rashid on how to select capable governors and hold them to account, and on the need not to burden the subjects with taxes beyond their capacity, lest this lead to the ruin of the land and the flight of the farmers. This reflects an early economic awareness of the inverse relationship between excessive taxation and the decline of agricultural productivity over the long term.

## His Distinction between Types of Land and Its Effect on Real Property Jurisprudence

Abu Yusuf makes a detailed distinction between the rulings on land according to the manner of its conquest (by force, or by agreement through treaty) and according to the nature of its ownership (kharaj land that remains the property of its original owners while paying kharaj on it, or land that is divided among the conquering warriors). This distinction later became the basis for wider Hanafi jurisprudential classifications in the chapters on *fay'* and spoils of war and the rulings on conquered lands, and it directly influenced how Hanafi jurisprudence dealt with the question of ownership of agricultural land in newly conquered regions, a question that had direct repercussions on the actual fiscal policy of the Abbasid state in later periods.

## Its Influence on Later Hanafi and Fiscal Jurisprudence

The book laid the groundwork for many concepts that were later developed in Hanafi fiscal jurisprudence, and it influenced later treatises on kharaj, such as *Kitab al-Kharaj* by Yahya ibn Adam (a slightly younger contemporary of Abu Yusuf, d. 203 AH / 818 CE). It was also relied upon in later works of siyasa shar'iyya and sultanic rulings, such as the works of al-Mawardi and Abu Ya'la al-Farra', although those works were broader in scope, dealing with the general rulings of the imamate and authority rather than with finance alone.
