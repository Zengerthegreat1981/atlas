# The Sociological Condition

This entry does not correspond to a single identifiable book or article. The phrase "the sociological condition" is used here as a general label for the situation of seeing individual lives as shaped by social structure and history, an idea that sociology's standard introductory texts have expressed in several ways.

## The Idea

C. Wright Mills, in *The Sociological Imagination* (1959), described the capacity to connect "personal troubles of milieu" with "public issues of social structure" and to see individual biography in relation to history. Peter Berger's *Invitation to Sociology* (1963) characterised the sociological perspective as a habit of seeing through the official interpretations of social life and of recognising the constraints that shape it. Zygmunt Bauman and Tim May's *Thinking Sociologically* (1990) presented sociology as a way of making the familiar strange, by questioning what is taken for granted.
