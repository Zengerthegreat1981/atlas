# The Forest of Symbols: Aspects of Ndembu Ritual

Victor Turner's *The Forest of Symbols: Aspects of Ndembu Ritual* (Cornell University Press, 1967) is a collection of essays, most of them revised from earlier articles, based on his fieldwork among the Ndembu of northwestern Zambia (then Northern Rhodesia) in the early 1950s, carried out under the auspices of the Rhodes-Livingstone Institute. The book sets out a method for studying ritual symbols and contains the essay "Betwixt and Between: The Liminal Period in Rites de Passage", in which Turner reworked Arnold van Gennep's concept of liminality. A fuller statement of liminality and communitas followed in *The Ritual Process* (1969).

## Publication and Context

Turner trained in the Manchester School around Max Gluckman, whose emphasis on conflict and social process shaped his first monograph, *Schism and Continuity in an African Society* (1957). In the 1960s, at Cornell, he turned from village politics to ritual symbolism. *The Forest of Symbols* gathers this work. Its title echoes Baudelaire's line about a "forest of symbols", and Turner treats ritual symbols as the units of the ritual "forest".

## Method: Analysing the Ritual Symbol

Turner proposed that a symbol must be studied through three kinds of data: its exegetical meaning (what informants say about it), its operational meaning (how it is used and what the participants do with it in performance), and its positional meaning (its relation to other symbols in the ritual). He stressed that dominant symbols are multivocal, condensing many meanings at once, and that they have two poles: a sensory (orectic) pole, tied to natural and bodily referents, and an ideological pole, tied to moral and social values. In this way a ritual symbol connects feeling with social norms.

## Ndembu Examples

The best-known example is the *mudyi* (milk tree), whose white latex is associated with mother's milk, the breast, matriliny and the continuity of Ndembu society; it stands at the centre of the girls' puberty rite *Nkang'a*. Other essays treat colour classification (white, red and black), the *Chihamba* ritual of the "white spirit", ritual specialists such as the diviner and healer Muchona, and Ndembu divination.

## Liminality

In "Betwixt and Between", Turner took van Gennep's three phases of rites of passage (separation, margin or limen, and aggregation) and gave special attention to the middle phase, in which neophytes are "betwixt and between" the positions fixed by law, custom and ceremonial. He described them as structurally invisible, often stripped of status and subject to ordeals and instruction.

## Reception and Critique

The book is a foundational text of symbolic and interpretive anthropology and shaped later work on ritual, performance and religion. Critics have noted that the three levels of meaning are hard to separate in practice, that exegesis may depend heavily on a few key informants, and that Turner's psychological reading of symbols often goes beyond the available ethnographic evidence. Later scholars have also questioned how far his model of liminality and communitas can be generalised across cultures and rituals, and whether it underestimates the way rituals reproduce hierarchy and gender inequality.
