# Outline of a Theory of Practice

*Outline of a Theory of Practice* is the English version, translated by Richard Nice and published by Cambridge University Press in 1977, of Pierre Bourdieu's *Esquisse d'une théorie de la pratique* (Geneva: Droz, 1972). It combines ethnography of Kabylia, a Berber-speaking region of Algeria, with an argument about how social scientists should explain what people do. The English text is not a straight translation: it was substantially revised and rearranged.

## The Problem

Bourdieu (1930-2002) argues against two ways of accounting for conduct. Objectivism, which he associates especially with Saussurean linguistics and the structural anthropology of Claude Lévi-Strauss, constructs models and rules and then tends to treat them as the cause of behaviour. Subjectivism, associated with phenomenology and with theories of rational choice, starts from the experience or conscious decisions of actors. He proposes a third approach that also examines the position of the observer, whose detached relation to practice differs from that of people who must act in it, under pressure of time.

## Central Ideas

The key concept is habitus, a system of durable and transposable dispositions produced by particular conditions of existence, which generates practices and perceptions without requiring conscious obedience to rules. Habitus allows improvisation within limits.

Bourdieu replaces the language of rules with that of strategies. His analysis of marriage shows kin using relationships selectively, so that genealogical diagrams capture only part of what people do. His discussion of gift exchange stresses timing: the interval between gift and counter-gift allows the exchange to be experienced as generosity while it also creates obligation.

He uses the term "doxa" for the taken-for-granted sense of the social world, in which the established order appears self-evident, and contrasts it with the field of opinion, where orthodoxy and heterodoxy confront each other openly. He also develops the notions of symbolic capital, the prestige and honour that can be converted into material advantage, and symbolic violence, a gentle and misrecognised form of domination.

## Place in Bourdieu's Work

The book draws on fieldwork carried out during and around the Algerian war of independence. Bourdieu reworked its argument in *The Logic of Practice* (French 1980) and applied related concepts to French society in *Distinction* (1979).

## Assessment

The book is widely used in anthropology and sociology as a statement of practice theory, often discussed alongside the work of Anthony Giddens and later surveyed by Sherry Ortner. Critics argue that habitus tends toward the reproduction of existing conditions and leaves change difficult to explain, that the prose and concepts are hard to apply, and that generalisations drawn from an idealised Kabyle tradition give limited weight to the upheaval of colonial Algeria and to women's own perspectives.
