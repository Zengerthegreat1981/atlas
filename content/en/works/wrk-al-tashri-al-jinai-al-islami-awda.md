# Islamic Criminal Legislation Compared with Positive Law

An encyclopedic jurisprudential-legal work by the Egyptian judge and lawyer **Abd al-Qadir Awda** (1906–1954), who was a leading figure in the Muslim Brotherhood and was executed in 1954. It is regarded as an influential modern Arabic work that attempted to present Islamic criminal jurisprudence with a contemporary legal methodology, one that allows direct dialogue with the concepts of positive criminal law. It draws on its author's dual formation between modern positive legal culture and in-depth study of classical Islamic jurisprudence.

## Motives for writing and context

Awda wrote this work in the context of the mid-twentieth-century Arab intellectual debate on the suitability of the Islamic Sharia to serve as a legislative source in the modern state. This was especially so in the criminal field, where positive laws imported from European (chiefly French) models had replaced the official application of hudud and qisas in most Muslim-majority countries. Drawing on his deep positive legal background, Awda wished to demonstrate that Islamic criminal jurisprudence forms a complete legal system, no less methodologically precise than modern criminal law. In his view it even includes procedural guarantees that sometimes surpass it.

## Structure and method

Awda divides his work into two main parts. The first is a general theoretical part presenting the general principles of criminalization and punishment in Islamic jurisprudence: the elements of the crime, the conditions of criminal liability, grounds of justification, and bars to punishment. It uses the same modern legal terms and classifications employed in analyzing positive criminal law.

The second is a special part treating in detail each kind of Islamic penalty: the hudud in their various kinds (such as the hadd for theft, the hadd for unlawful sexual intercourse, the hadd for false accusation, the hadd for drinking wine, and the hadd for highway robbery), retaliation and blood money, and discretionary punishment (ta'zir). Throughout, it constantly compares each topic with the provisions of Egyptian and French positive criminal law.

## The central argument: the procedural guarantees of the hudud

One of the most distinctive features of Awda's argument is his focus on the strict procedural guarantees that Islamic jurisprudence requires for applying hudud penalties. The most prominent is the rule that "doubt averts the hudud", which requires dropping the hadd penalty upon any reasonable doubt about the full satisfaction of the conditions for its application. Alongside it is the requirement of high levels of certainty in proof, such as requiring four upright witnesses to prove the crime of unlawful sexual intercourse.

Awda presents these guarantees as evidence that Islamic criminal jurisprudence does not seek to expand the application of hudud penalties. Rather, it places strict procedural restrictions on them, making their actual application relatively rare in comparison with a contrary popular impression.

## The dual comparative method

Awda's analysis follows an organized comparative method that presents each issue from two parallel angles: traditional Islamic jurisprudence in its various schools on the one hand, and contemporary positive criminal law with its doctrinal schools on the other. In each issue he attempts to highlight the essential points of convergence and divergence between the two systems. This dual method made the book a reference of double benefit: for the traditional Islamic scholar wishing to understand the position of his jurisprudence within modern legal concepts, and for the positive jurist wishing to become acquainted, in a familiar language, with the Islamic criminal system.

## Impact

This book is frequently cited in contemporary debate on applying Islamic hudud or comparing Islamic criminal jurisprudence with positive law. It has been cited both by supporters of applying the Islamic criminal Sharia, who invoke its strict procedural guarantees, and by academic researchers interested in studying comparative Islamic criminal jurisprudence from a neutral perspective. Its influence has continued in Arabic and Islamic legal and jurisprudential literature throughout the decades following its publication, as an attempt to present Islamic criminal jurisprudence in a modern legal form suitable for comparative academic teaching.

## Its place in the debate on applying hudud

The book occupies a central place in the continuing intellectual and political debate about applying Islamic hudud in contemporary Arab and Muslim states, and it is cited from multiple sides for various purposes. Some Islamist movements cite Awda's analysis of the strict procedural guarantees to refute accusations describing the hudud as excessively harsh. Academic researchers, by contrast, use it as a neutral source for understanding the precise technical structure of Islamic criminal jurisprudence, independent of their political position on its actual application in a contemporary state. This diversity of uses reflects the precise technical character of the book, which makes it a reference suitable for citation from multiple intellectual angles. Its author himself, however, was clearly on record as supporting the application of the Islamic criminal Sharia.
