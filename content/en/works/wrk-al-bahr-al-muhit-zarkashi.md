# Al-Bahr al-Muhit fi Usul al-Fiqh

A vast encyclopedic work on the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) by Badr al-Din Muhammad ibn Bahadur al-Zarkashi (d. 794 AH / 1392 CE), the Egyptian Shafi'i jurist also known for his works on the sciences of the Qur'an (such as *Al-Burhan fi Ulum al-Quran*), hadith and jurisprudence. *Al-Bahr al-Muhit* is one of the broadest works written on usul al-fiqh, in its absorption of several centuries of earlier usuli debate. It became a reference to which later scholars turned to learn the views of earlier usulis when their original sources were lost or difficult to reach.

## The Encyclopedic Character

The work attempts to absorb the inherited body of usul al-fiqh from the time of al-Shafi'i (d. 204 AH) to Zarkashi's own age, combining two methodological schools that recent studies usually distinguish. The first is the method of the **mutakallimun** (associated with the Shafi'is, Malikis, Mu'tazilis and Ash'aris), which builds usuli rules theoretically, independently of the legal branches, and then applies them to those branches, as in al-Juwayni, al-Ghazali, al-Razi and al-Amidi. The second is the method of the **fuqaha** (associated with the Hanafis), which derives usuli rules from the legal branches of the school's own imams, as in al-Sarakhsi and al-Bazdawi. Zarkashi does not limit himself to one of the two approaches. He seeks to collect and compare the views of both camps under each usuli question, often quoting verbatim from dozens of earlier sources, some of which have reached us only through his quotations.

## Documentary and Bibliographic Value

What most distinguishes the book for contemporary research is the abundance of its quotations from earlier usuli works, many of which are now lost, unpublished or rare. This makes *Al-Bahr al-Muhit* a primary source for reconstructing the history of early usuli debate and recovering the opinions of usulis whose books have not reached us directly. This documentary function makes the book sometimes closer to a "historical dictionary of usuli opinions" than to a unified usuli treatise with a single theoretical position, although Zarkashi does not lack his own preferences and selections, which he states from time to time.

## Structure and Arrangement

The book follows the familiar order of the major usul works. It begins with the evidences for rulings (the Qur'an, the Sunna, consensus, analogy and related matters such as istishab, istihsan, al-maslaha al-mursala and others). It then turns to the words and their indications (the general and the specific, the absolute and the qualified, the mujmal and the mubayyan, command and prohibition, the explicit and the implicit meaning), followed by ijtihad, taqlid, tarjih and conflicts among the evidences. Finally, it addresses matters concerning the qualities of the mujtahid and the conditions for issuing fatwas. Under each chapter, Zarkashi presents layers of opinions, sometimes arranged chronologically, with a clear distinction between the terminology of the mutakallimun and that of the fuqaha where the same concept is named differently.

## Its Place Among the Major Usul Encyclopedias

The work is usually compared with other comprehensive usuli works such as al-Amidi's *Al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam*, al-Ghazali's *Al-Mustasfa*, and Taj al-Din al-Subki's *Jam al-Jawami* (roughly of Zarkashi's own period). What distinguishes Zarkashi, however, is his clearer inclination toward comprehensive encyclopedic compilation rather than the tightly organized theoretical composition, with its strongly coherent logical structure, found in al-Amidi. His book is thus closer to a usuli "encyclopedia" than to a focused, philosophically argued usuli treatise.

## Quoting from Rare Hanafi and Mu'tazili Sources

Among the notable features of Zarkashi's method is that he does not confine himself to his own Shafi'i school or to the school of the mutakallimun in general. He also quotes generously from Hanafi usuli works (such as those of al-Bazdawi and al-Sarakhsi) and from early Mu'tazili usuli views that may not reach us directly from their lost original sources. In this way he preserves a degree of intellectual balance among the competing schools, rather than presenting a one-sided account of his own school alone. This openness in quotation strengthens the book's documentary value for researchers who wish to reconstruct the map of usuli debate across different schools, not within a single isolated school.

## The Late Scholarly Edition of the Book

*Al-Bahr al-Muhit* received a complete scholarly edition and wide publication only in the later decades of the twentieth century (a multi-volume Kuwaiti edition appeared from the late 1980s), unlike other earlier usuli works that were printed much sooner (such as al-Mustasfa and al-Ihkam). This partly explains the limited use Western scholars made of its rich material before that date, compared with other usuli works made available to orientalist scholars earlier. Its internal documentary value is no less important than those works, however, and is notable for the many earlier quotations preserved in it.

## Its Later Influence

Later scholars have drawn on *Al-Bahr al-Muhit* as a reference source. It has also been widely cited in modern orientalist and academic studies of the history of usul al-fiqh because of the richness of its historical and documentary material. It nonetheless remains less known among general students than shorter and more widely circulated usuli works used in traditional teaching, such as *Jam al-Jawami* or al-Juwayni's *Al-Waraqat*.
