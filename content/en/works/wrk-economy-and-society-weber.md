# Economy and Society

*Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft* (published 1921-22, after the author's death) is Max Weber's major systematic treatise. It sets out his interpretive sociology and his sociology of economy, domination, law, religion and the city.

## Historical Context

Max Weber (1864-1920) worked on the project for the *Grundriss der Sozialökonomik*, a handbook of social economics, from about 1909-10 onward. The older part was written mainly before the First World War, and a conceptual revision, including the opening chapters, was written in 1919-20. Weber died in June 1920, of pneumonia in the context of the influenza pandemic, before completing the work. His widow Marianne Weber edited the manuscripts for publication, and the volume appeared with the publisher Mohr (Siebeck) in Tübingen in 1921-22. The incomplete state of the manuscripts, and the uncertain ordering of their parts, explain why later editors reorganised the work, notably Johannes Winckelmann in the 1950s and 1960s.

## Main Ideas and Method

In the opening chapter, "Basic Sociological Terms", Weber defines sociology as a science that seeks to understand social action interpretively and thereby to explain its course and effects causally. The method of Verstehen is combined with the construction of ideal types: analytic models formed by accentuating features of a phenomenon, intended as tools for comparison and not as descriptions of any actual case.

Weber distinguishes three pure types of legitimate domination: charismatic, based on the exceptional personal qualities attributed to a leader; traditional, based on the sanctity of inherited custom; and legal-rational, based on formally enacted rules. His analysis of bureaucracy as the technically most efficient form of administration identifies features such as a clear hierarchy of offices, written rules, separation of the official from ownership of the means of administration, and appointment on the basis of qualification. The metaphor of the "iron cage" belongs not to this work but to *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism* (1904-05), although the two are often linked in discussions of rationalization.

The treatise also contains sociological analyses of economic action and its organisation, of law, of religious groups and types of religious leadership, of the city and of the state. Weber developed related comparative studies in his essays on the economic ethics of the world religions (Confucianism and Taoism, Hinduism and Buddhism, ancient Judaism), which were published separately.

## Impact and Critique

The work became a basic reference for Weberian sociology. The most authoritative English edition was edited by Guenther Roth and Claus Wittich and published by the University of California Press in 1978. An earlier partial translation by A. M. Henderson and Talcott Parsons (*The Theory of Social and Economic Organization*, 1947) did much to introduce this part of Weber's work to American sociology. The treatise influenced the sociology of organizations (including Peter Blau and Alvin Gouldner), the comparative historical work of Reinhard Bendix, Jürgen Habermas's discussion of rationalization and George Ritzer's *The McDonaldization of Society* (1993).

Scholars continue to debate the editorial history of the text: how far the arrangement given by Marianne Weber and later editors reflects Weber's intentions, and whether the older and the later parts form a coherent whole. Critics have also questioned the ideal type as a method and the work's Eurocentric comparative frame.
