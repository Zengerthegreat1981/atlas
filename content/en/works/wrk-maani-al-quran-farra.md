# Ma'ani al-Qur'an (al-Farra')

A reference work by al-Farra', one of the oldest Arabic works combining the interpretation of the Qur'an with systematic grammatical analysis of its verses. It represents the grammatical method of the Kufa school in dealing with the Qur'anic text.

## Interpretive-Grammatical Method

Al-Farra' interprets the Qur'anic verses one by one. He draws on the variety of widely transmitted Qur'anic readings (qirā'āt), cites pre-Islamic and early Islamic poetry, and applies the grammatical rules of the Kufan school. Throughout, he explains the possible modes of inflection and their divergent semantic implications. This method combines linguistic precision with interpretive interest.

## Historical Context

Yahya ibn Ziyad al-Farra' (144–207 AH) was of Kufan origin. He studied under al-Kisa'i, and was one of the leading figures of the Kufa grammatical school. He served as tutor to the sons of the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun, and he died on his way to Mecca. His book *Ma'ani al-Qur'an* is one of the oldest complete works of this kind that have come down to us, combining interpretation and grammar. Alongside it stands a contemporary work of similar content, Abu ʿUbayda Maʿmar ibn al-Muthanna's *Majāz al-Qur'ān*. The two methods differed, however. Abu ʿUbayda leaned toward explaining Qur'anic terms through direct poetic citation, while al-Farra' leaned toward analyzing the probable grammatical modes of inflection in greater depth. His analysis started from the principles of the Kufan school, which was known for its flexibility in accepting analogy based on rare evidence, compared with the more conservative Basra school.

## Characteristics of al-Farra's Method

Al-Farra's method is open to the multiple widely transmitted Qur'anic readings. He uses their diversity as a tool for interpreting meaning and for preferring one inflectional mode over another. This is often linked to the Kufans' reputation for accepting evidence from a wider range of Arab speech than the Basrans did. Al-Farraʾ also uses grammatical terms that differ from Sibawayh's. A well-known case is *ṣarf*, the Kufan name for a construction in which a noun after a conjunction is accusative; Basran accounts of the same pattern often invoke a different explanation (the dispute is also discussed under *khilāf*). The book is a principal source for these terminological and analytical differences.

## Impact on the Science of Qur'anic I'rab

This book paved the way for the later development of an independent branch of Qur'anic studies known as *iʿrāb al-Qurʾān* (grammatical parsing of the Qur'an). That branch analyzes the possible grammatical modes of each verse and their effect on the interpretive meaning. The book influenced later exegetes who used grammatical analysis as a basic interpretive tool, such as al-Zajjaj in *Ma'ani al-Qur'an wa I'rabuhu* and al-Nahhas in *I'rab al-Qur'an*. It also had an indirect influence on al-Tabari, who drew on al-Farra's linguistic material in his comprehensive commentary.

## The Position of al-Farra' Among the Kufan Scholars

Al-Farraʾ stands, with al-Kisāʾī, among the main named authorities of the Kufan grammatical tradition. Earlier Kufan teaching is associated with Abū Jaʿfar al-Ruʾāsī, about whom little survives. The bibliographical tradition, including Ibn al-Nadīm's *al-Fihrist* (fourth century AH), attributes to al-Farraʾ other works, among them *al-Ḥudūd*, *al-Mudhakkar wa-l-muʾannath* and *al-Maqṣūr wa-l-mamdūd*. *Maʿānī al-Qurʾān* itself mixes grammar, lexicography and Qur'anic readings, fields that were not yet separated in his time. Traditional reports say he dictated it over several years near the end of his life (often placed in 202–204 AH / 817–819 CE), at the suggestion of ʿUmar ibn Bukayr. His teaching of the sons of the caliph al-Maʾmūn is a separate biographical report.

## Comparison with the Basran Method

The contrast between al-Farra's Kufan method and that of his Basran contemporaries becomes clear when compared with the works of Sibawayh, al-Mazini, and later al-Mubarrad. The Basrans are traditionally said to have adopted a more restrictive approach to grammatical analogy, preferring to exclude rare or anomalous evidence. Al-Farra' and his school showed greater flexibility in accepting the interpretation of such evidence within multiple explanatory frameworks. The disagreement is often described as more than a difference of terms: one side is said to have preferred a tighter analogy, the other a wider range of attested usage. Later grammarians drew on both schools, sometimes in the same work. Modern historians treat the Basra–Kufa contrast as a later schematization of a more mixed early record, and they use *Maʿānī al-Qurʾān* as evidence for that mixture rather than as a pure Kufan manifesto.
