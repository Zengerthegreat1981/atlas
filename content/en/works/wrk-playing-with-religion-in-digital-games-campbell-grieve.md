# Playing with Religion in Digital Games

An edited volume co-edited by Heidi A. Campbell and Gregory Price Grieve, published by Indiana University Press in 2014 within the *Digital Game Studies* series. It is regarded as an early and influential survey of the field of religion and video game studies.

## Definition

The book brings together contributions by a number of scholars in addition to the two editors and offers an early broad academic survey of how religion intersects with digital video games, a subject that had previously received limited attention within the study of religion.

## Historical Context

The study of religion and new digital media (digital religion) grew rapidly from the early 2000s with the spread of the internet and of video games as mass cultural media. Scholars such as Heidi Campbell had studied how religious communities adopt, negotiate or resist new media. This volume focuses on video games, an interactive medium that differs from traditional narrative media in that it involves the player directly in building the experience.

## Main Ideas and Method

The volume considers how religion appears in and around games: games with explicit religious content, religious themes and myths drawn on by mainstream games, and the ways players and communities engage with games, sometimes in ways that resemble ritual or religious practice. Contributors address a range of traditions beyond Christianity; for example, Vít Šisler writes on Islamic games and Xenia Zeiler on Hindu themes in games. Contributors draw on methods from media studies, anthropology and religious studies.

## Principal Editors

Heidi Campbell and Gregory Price Grieve are leading figures in the study of religion and digital media. Campbell's *When Religion Meets New Media* (2010) examines how religious communities interact with digital technologies, and Grieve's *Cyber Zen* (2016) studies Buddhist identity and practice in the virtual world of Second Life.

## Impact and Critique

The volume helped stimulate growth of a sub-field within both game studies and religious studies. As a multi-contributor volume, its chapters naturally vary in method and quality, as is common with inclusive edited collections; its main value lies in mapping the field and showing the range of questions it raises.
