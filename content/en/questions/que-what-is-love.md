# What Is Love?

An eternal question in philosophy, literature and psychology: is love an emotion? A decision? A neural state? An existential experience? The Arab-Islamic heritage devoted independent books to it, which count among the finest ever written on the subject.

## Love in the Arab-Islamic Heritage

Ibn Hazm of al-Andalus (d. 1064) wrote *The Ring of the Dove* (*Tawq al-Hamama*) on affection and its associations (c. 1022), a precise psychological and social description of the emergence of love, its signs, forms and afflictions. In it he defined love as a connection between parts of souls which were divided from an original noble element, recalling a Platonic idea of souls that were neighbours before birth. Ibn Sina, in a *Treatise on Love*, argued that love is a general inclination in all beings towards the good and the perfect: in animals, humans and intellects it is an image of existence striving towards its completion. In the religious domain, al-Ghazali, in the book of love, longing, intimacy and contentment in *The Revival of the Religious Sciences*, discussed the love of God and its causes, which he related to knowledge of Him; before him Rabi'a al-Adawiyya (d. 801) became famous for her conception of pure love of God, neither out of fear nor out of desire. The Qur'an mentions love in several forms: God's love for His servants and their love for Him (Al-Ma'ida 54), and affection and mercy between spouses (Al-Rum 21). Ibn al-Qayyim, in *The Garden of Lovers* (*Rawdat al-Muhibbin*), described the states of love and its degrees.

## The Types Among the Greeks and in Christianity

The Greeks distinguished several words: *eros*, desire and longing for beauty, as in Plato's *Symposium* (c. 380 BCE), where the lover ascends from the beauty of bodies to the beauty of the Form; *philia*, love between equal friends, as in Books Eight and Nine of Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics*, where it rests on reciprocity and virtue (Aristotle also discusses self-love, *philautia*, in Book Nine); and *agape*, unconditional love, as in 1 Corinthians 13. Then John Alan Lee, in *The Colors of Love* (1973), proposed six styles of love: *eros*, *ludus*, *storge*, *pragma*, *mania* and *agape*. These are Greek names, but the classification is modern. In the *Confessions* (c. 400), Augustine made love the soul's inclination towards God until it "rests in Him."

## Philosophical and Psychological Debate

In *The Metaphysics of Sexual Love* (1844), a chapter in the second volume of *The World as Will and Representation*, Schopenhauer held that the lover believes they are seeking their own happiness while in fact serving the will of the species in producing the best offspring: love is a biological deception. Erich Fromm, in *The Art of Loving* (1956), held in contrast that love is a skill to be learned, resting on care, responsibility, respect and knowledge. Robert Sternberg, in "A Triangular Theory of Love" (*Psychological Review*, 1986), set out three components: intimacy, passion and commitment, from which the types of love are composed. Hazan and Shaver (1987) linked romantic love to adult attachment styles derived from John Bowlby's attachment theory: secure, anxious and avoidant. Attachment, in this sense, names the enduring bond through which a person seeks security and closeness, and it is narrower than love as a whole, which can also include passion, friendship, admiration, or care. Bartels and Zeki (2000), using brain imaging, showed that looking at a picture of a beloved activates regions associated with reward, while activity in regions of the cortex decreases. A role for oxytocin and vasopressin in bonding is attributed in animal studies, but generalising this to humans remains a matter of research.

## Significance and Limits

Love is the foundation of human relationships, and much research points to a link between supportive relationships and better health, although the studies are correlational. The concept of love varies between cultures: romantic love as the basis of marriage is a relatively late historical phenomenon in the West, preceded by courtly love, which some scholars believe was influenced by Arabic poetry in al-Andalus, a view that is disputed. Brain scans do not replace philosophical understanding of what love is, since they describe the correlation, not the essence.
