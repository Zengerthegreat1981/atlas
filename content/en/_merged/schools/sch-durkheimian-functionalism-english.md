# Durkheimian Structural Functionalism

"Durkheimian structural functionalism" is a retrospective label for the approach of Émile Durkheim (1858-1917) and the circle around him, as read through the later functionalist tradition. Durkheim did not use the phrase. The approach treats society as a reality that cannot be reduced to individual psychology and asks what social institutions contribute to the cohesion of the whole.

## Setting and Circle

Durkheim taught at Bordeaux from 1887 and at the Sorbonne from 1902. In 1898 he launched *L'Année sociologique*, which gathered collaborators including Marcel Mauss, Henri Hubert, Célestin Bouglé, François Simiand and Maurice Halbwachs. They reviewed international scholarship and applied a shared method to religion, law, economy and social morphology. The First World War killed several younger members, and Durkheim died in 1917.

## Core Ideas

In *The Rules of Sociological Method* (1895) **social facts** are ways of acting, thinking and feeling that are external to individuals and exercise constraint on them. Durkheim asked for two distinct inquiries: into the efficient cause of a social fact and into the function it fulfils. A function does not by itself explain why the fact arose.

*The Division of Labour in Society* (1893) contrasts **mechanical solidarity**, based on likeness and a strong collective conscience, with **organic solidarity**, based on the interdependence of differentiated roles. **Anomie** denotes a weakness of moral regulation, discussed there and in *Suicide* (1897), which relates differences in suicide rates to degrees of social integration and regulation. *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912) argues that collective ritual sustains shared ideas of the sacred and renews group bonds.

## Later Development

A. R. Radcliffe-Brown carried Durkheim's concern with function into British social anthropology, defining the function of an institution by its contribution to the maintenance of social structure. Talcott Parsons discussed Durkheim at length in *The Structure of Social Action* (1937) and later built a systems theory around problems of integration. Robert K. Merton revised functional analysis by distinguishing manifest from latent functions, allowing for dysfunctions and questioning the assumption that every element is indispensable. Mauss and Halbwachs developed other sides of the inheritance, in the study of gift exchange and collective memory, which are not well described as functionalism.

## Criticism

Critics argue that explanation by function can become circular if no mechanism shows how an effect sustains its cause, and that an emphasis on integration gives too little weight to conflict, power and change. Others reply that Durkheim analysed abnormal and conflictual forms of the division of labour and paid close attention to historical transformation, so that reading him only as a theorist of order is one-sided. His use of official suicide statistics has also been questioned.
