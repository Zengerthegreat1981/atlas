# Feminist Anthropology

Feminist anthropology examines gender as a part of social organisation and questions accounts that treat men's experience as the experience of a society. It combines criticism of anthropology's own record with research on kinship, work, the body, religion, state power, and political movements. It contains several theoretical positions that disagree about equality, difference, and who may speak for whom.

## Women and the anthropology of women

Earlier women anthropologists, including Margaret Mead and Phyllis Kaberry, had given sustained attention to women's lives. In the 1970s a new literature asked why female subordination appeared widespread and how it should be explained. *Woman, Culture, and Society* (1974), edited by Michelle Rosaldo and Louise Lamphere, proposed several accounts, including Rosaldo's contrast of domestic and public spheres. Sherry Ortner asked whether women were symbolically associated with nature and men with culture. Rayna Reiter's *Toward an Anthropology of Women* (1975) included Gayle Rubin's "The Traffic in Women", which analysed a sex/gender system through kinship, Marx, and Freud. Eleanor Leacock argued that claims of universal subordination overlooked egalitarian societies and the effects of colonialism and class.

## From women to gender

Scholars soon questioned the universal categories. Carol MacCormack and Marilyn Strathern's *Nature, Culture and Gender* (1980) showed that the nature–culture opposition could not simply be projected onto other societies. Rosaldo herself reconsidered the domestic–public model. Strathern's *The Gender of the Gift* (1988) argued that Western ideas of the individual and of ownership distort Melanesian relations. Jane Collier and Sylvia Yanagisako urged that gender and kinship be analysed together without assuming biological reproduction as their given base.

## Non-Western and decolonial feminisms

Chandra Talpade Mohanty's "Under Western Eyes" criticised writing that produced a single image of the oppressed "Third World woman". Black, Indigenous, and postcolonial scholars showed how race, class, empire, and gender operate together. Lila Abu-Lughod's work among Awlad Ali Bedouin analysed poetry and honour, and later questioned claims that Muslim women need saving by outsiders. Saba Mahmood's *Politics of Piety* (2005), on a women's mosque movement in Cairo, argued that agency should not be identified only with resistance to norms. Such work raises the question of how feminist analysis can take seriously aims that it does not share.

## Beyond a binary

Research on transgender, intersex, and locally named gender positions shows that two fixed categories do not describe every system. Serena Nanda's study of hijras in India is a well-known example, though the label "third gender" can conceal differences. Writers influenced by Judith Butler examine how repeated conduct produces gendered subjects. Feminist anthropologists have also debated reflexive writing, collaboration, harassment and safety in fieldwork, and the unequal treatment of women's scholarship within the discipline.
