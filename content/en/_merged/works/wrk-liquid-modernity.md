# Liquid Modernity

*Liquid Modernity* (Polity Press, 2000) is a book by Zygmunt Bauman (1925-2017), a Polish-born sociologist who spent the later part of his career at the University of Leeds. It describes a passage from a "heavy" or "solid" phase of modernity to a "light" or "liquid" one, in which social forms change faster than the habits and life plans built around them.

## The Metaphor

Bauman begins from the physical contrast between solids, which hold their shape, and fluids, which do not. Modernity, he argues, was from the start a process of "melting" inherited institutions and obligations. Its earlier, solid phase aimed to replace them with new and more durable orders, such as the large factory, the bureaucratic nation-state and stable class organisations. In the liquid phase the melting continues without a settled order to follow, and the task of finding or building patterns of life falls increasingly on individuals.

## Five Themes

The book has a foreword on lightness and liquidity and five chapters, on emancipation, individuality, time and space, work, and community.

On emancipation, Bauman revisits the concerns of critical theory and contrasts being an individual by right (de jure) with having the practical capacity to determine one's own life (de facto). He argues that the gap between the two has widened and that private troubles are less readily translated into public issues. Drawing on Ulrich Beck, he notes that people are asked to seek individual solutions to problems produced socially.

On individuality, he contrasts a producer society oriented to norms and long-term goals with a consumer society organised around choice and desire that has no fixed end point.

On time and space, he argues that when movement and communication become nearly instantaneous, mobility itself becomes a major source of power. Elites able to move or withdraw hold an advantage over those tied to a place. This is linked to the claim that power no longer depends chiefly on the close mutual engagement of supervisors and supervised represented by the Panopticon.

On work, he describes the weakening of the long-term mutual dependence of capital and labour. Short-term contracts and flexibility encourage uncertainty and discourage the postponement of gratification.

On community, he examines the wish for belonging and safety under these conditions, and describes short-lived "cloakroom" or "carnival" communities that gather around a spectacle or event and then disperse without creating lasting solidarity.

## Place in Bauman's Work

The book followed his writings of the 1980s and 1990s on modernity and postmodernity, including *Modernity and the Holocaust* (1989), *Modernity and Ambivalence* (1991) and *Globalization: The Human Consequences* (1998). The vocabulary of liquidity increasingly replaced that of postmodernity in his work, and he extended it in *Liquid Love* (2003), *Liquid Life* (2005), *Liquid Fear* (2006) and *Liquid Times* (2007).

## Assessment

The book is widely used in discussions of individualisation, consumer society, precarious work and intimate relationships. Critics argue that the solid-liquid contrast is too broad, that it can idealise the stability of earlier industrial societies, that it rests more on essayistic illustration than on systematic evidence, and that it gives limited attention to variation by country, class and gender and to institutions that have remained durable.
