# Anselm L. Strauss

Anselm Leonard Strauss (1916-1996) was an American sociologist in the symbolic interactionist and pragmatist tradition. He worked on identity, occupations, medical organizations and chronic illness, and with Barney Glaser developed grounded theory.

## Life and Context

Strauss was born in New York City in December 1916. He studied biology at the University of Virginia before turning to sociology at the University of Chicago, where he completed his doctorate in 1945 and encountered the ideas of George Herbert Mead through Herbert Blumer. He taught at Lawrence College, Indiana University and the University of Chicago, and worked at the psychiatric research institute of Michael Reese Hospital in Chicago. In 1960 he moved to the University of California, San Francisco, where he later established a department of social and behavioral sciences. He died in San Francisco in 1996.

## Main Ideas

- **Identity as process.** In *Mirrors and Masks* (1959) he treated identity as formed and revised through language, interaction and membership in groups over the life course.
- **Negotiated order.** Research in psychiatric hospitals led Strauss and colleagues to argue that organizational order is not fixed by formal rules alone but is continually worked out through negotiation.
- **Awareness and trajectory.** Studies of dying patients examined what patients, relatives and staff know about approaching death, and how the course of dying is managed.
- **Social worlds and arenas.** He analyzed groups formed around shared activities and the arenas in which they contest issues.

## Case Studies

*Boys in White* (1961), written with Howard Becker, Blanche Geer and Everett Hughes, was a field study of students at the University of Kansas medical school. It showed how students developed shared perspectives for coping with an unmanageable workload and faculty demands. *Awareness of Dying* (1965), based on fieldwork in hospitals, distinguished closed awareness, suspected awareness, mutual pretense and open awareness, and traced how each shapes conduct on the ward.

## Method

*The Discovery of Grounded Theory* (1967) argued that theory can be generated systematically from data through constant comparison and theoretical sampling. Strauss later elaborated coding procedures in *Qualitative Analysis for Social Scientists* (1987) and, with Juliet Corbin, in *Basics of Qualitative Research* (1990). Glaser rejected this later formulation as forcing data into preset categories.

## Influence and Criticism

His work has been influential in medical sociology, nursing research and organization studies. Critics have questioned the idea that analysis can begin without prior theory, and some argue that a focus on negotiation understates durable power and structural constraint.

## Related Concepts

Symbolic interactionism, pragmatism, negotiated order, grounded theory; related figures include Herbert Blumer, Everett Hughes, Howard Becker and Barney Glaser.
