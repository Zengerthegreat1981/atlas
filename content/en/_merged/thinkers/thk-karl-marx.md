# Karl Marx

Karl Marx (1818-1883) was a German philosopher, political economist and socialist revolutionary. His analysis of class, labor and capitalism is one of the main starting points of sociological theory.

## Life and Context

Marx was born in Trier in 1818, studied at Bonn and Berlin and received a doctorate from Jena in 1841. He edited the *Rheinische Zeitung* until censorship ended his position, then moved to Paris, where his lasting partnership with Friedrich Engels began. Expelled from France and later from Belgium, he took part in the revolutionary events of 1848-1849 and settled in London in 1849. He worked there as a journalist and researcher, often in poverty, was active in the International Working Men's Association, and died in 1883.

## Main Ideas

- **Social relations of production:** people produce their lives under relations, especially of property, that they do not simply choose. These relations and the productive forces form the basis for understanding law, politics and ideas.
- **Class:** classes are defined by their position in production; capitalists own the means of production, while wage workers sell their capacity to work.
- **Exploitation:** workers produce more value than they receive in wages, and this surplus value is the source of profit.
- **Alienation:** wage labor separates workers from their product, their activity, other people and their human potential.
- **Commodity fetishism:** relations among producers appear as properties of the things they exchange.
- **Contradiction and change:** competition drives accumulation, technical change, concentration and recurring crises, while class struggle may transform the social order.

## Major Works

- *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts* (written 1844)
- *The German Ideology* (written 1845-1846, with Engels)
- *The Communist Manifesto* (1848, with Engels)
- *The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte* (1852)
- *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy* (1859)
- *Capital*, volume 1 (1867); volumes 2 and 3 edited by Engels (1885, 1894)

## Case Studies

*The Eighteenth Brumaire* analyzes how classes, factions and the state shaped Louis Bonaparte's coup in France. In *Capital* the chapters on the working day and machinery draw heavily on British factory inspectors' reports.

## Method

Marx combined a materialist transformation of Hegel's dialectic with historical study and a critique of classical political economy, treating economic categories as expressions of historically specific social relations.

## Influence and Criticism

His thought shaped labor movements, revolutionary states, conflict theory, critical theory, world-systems analysis and the sociology of work. Weber argued that status, political power and religious ideas cannot be reduced to class and economy. Critics have disputed the labor theory of value, economic determinism and predictions of class polarization and revolution. How far Marx's work bears responsibility for governments that invoked it remains debated, and interpreters differ on the continuity between the early and late writings.

## Cross-References

Compare Weber on class, status and party, Durkheim on the division of labor, and Gramsci on hegemony.
