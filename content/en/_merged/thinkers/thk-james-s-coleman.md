# James S. Coleman

James Samuel Coleman (1926-1995) was an American sociologist whose research on education, social capital, mathematical sociology and rational choice theory was influential in both scholarship and public policy.

## Life and Context

Coleman was born in Bedford, Indiana, in 1926. He took a degree in chemical engineering at Purdue University and worked in industry before studying sociology at Columbia University, where Robert Merton and Paul Lazarsfeld were major influences; he received his doctorate in 1955. He taught at the University of Chicago, founded the department of social relations at Johns Hopkins University, and returned to Chicago in 1973. He was president of the American Sociological Association in 1991-1992 and died in 1995.

## Main Ideas

- **Schools and families:** the Equality of Educational Opportunity study found that family background and the social composition of a student body were more strongly associated with achievement than differences in school resources.
- **Adolescent society:** high school students form status systems that can compete with academic goals.
- **Social capital:** relations among people, including obligations, information channels and norms, are resources for action. Closed networks, in which parents know one another's children, help sustain norms.
- **Micro and macro:** explaining system-level change requires showing how social conditions shape individual action and how actions combine into collective outcomes, often drawn as "Coleman's boat".
- **Rational choice:** purposive actors who control resources and have interests in others' resources form the basis of his general theory.

## Case Studies

The 1966 report, commissioned under the Civil Rights Act of 1964, surveyed students and schools across the United States and fed debates on desegregation. In the 1970s his research on "white flight" associated with school desegregation provoked intense controversy. His later comparison of public, Catholic and other private high schools reopened debate about school choice.

## Method

Coleman used large surveys, network data and formal models, and argued that sociology should contribute to the design of social institutions.

## Influence and Criticism

His work shaped the sociology of education, social capital research and analytical sociology. Critics argued that rational choice theory gives a thin account of norms, emotions, identity and power. Methodologists debated his comparisons of school sectors, particularly the problem that students are not randomly assigned to schools. Others note that his concept of social capital differs from Bourdieu's, which emphasizes inequality and class reproduction.
