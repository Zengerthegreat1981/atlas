# Judith Butler

Judith Butler (born 1956) is an American philosopher and gender theorist whose concept of gender performativity became one of the most influential ideas in contemporary social and cultural theory.

## Life and Context

Butler was born in Cleveland, Ohio, and received a doctorate in philosophy from Yale University in 1984. Their first book, *Subjects of Desire* (1987), dealt with Hegel's reception in twentieth-century French thought. Butler has been a professor at the University of California, Berkeley, in the departments of rhetoric and comparative literature. Their work draws on Hegel, Foucault, Simone de Beauvoir, Derrida, J. L. Austin and psychoanalysis.

## Main Ideas

- **Gender performativity.** In *Gender Trouble* (1990), Butler argued that gender is not an expression of an inner essence but is produced through repeated acts and norms. The apparent stability of "man" and "woman" is an effect of those repetitions.
- **Critique of the heterosexual matrix.** They argued that sex, gender and desire are linked by norms that make some identities intelligible and others marginal.
- **Materiality of bodies.** *Bodies That Matter* (1993) responded to critics who thought the theory denied the body, discussing how norms shape which bodies count as livable.
- **Vulnerability and precarity.** After 2000, Butler turned to grief, violence and ethics in *Precarious Life* (2004) and *Frames of War* (2009), arguing that some lives are socially treated as more grievable than others.
- **Assembly and nonviolence.** Later works include *Notes Toward a Performative Theory of Assembly* (2015) and *The Force of Nonviolence* (2020).

## Method

Butler's method is philosophical and genealogical, working through close critical readings of texts and concepts instead of empirical data collection. Sociologists have taken the idea of performativity into empirical work on how gender is done in interaction.

## Influence and Criticism

Butler's work underpins queer theory and has shaped gender studies, sociology and the humanities. Critics, including Martha Nussbaum in "The Professor of Parody" (1999), have charged that the writing is obscure and that the politics are too focused on symbolic resistance. Others have argued that the theory underplays material inequality. Butler has also been the target of public political controversy.
