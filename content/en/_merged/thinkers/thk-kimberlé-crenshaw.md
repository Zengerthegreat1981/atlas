# Kimberlé Crenshaw

Kimberlé Williams Crenshaw (born 1959) is an American legal scholar who introduced the term "intersectionality" and was a central figure in the formation of critical race theory. Her work is widely used in sociology.

## Life and Context

Crenshaw was born in Canton, Ohio, in 1959. She studied at Cornell University, Harvard Law School and the University of Wisconsin Law School. She has taught law at the University of California, Los Angeles, and at Columbia University. She co-founded the African American Policy Forum, which links research with public advocacy.

## Main Ideas

- **Intersectionality:** race, sex and other relations of inequality can operate together, so that experiences are missed when each is examined on its own.
- **Single-axis frameworks:** antidiscrimination law and political movements that treat race and gender separately tend to take Black men as typical of racial discrimination and white women as typical of sex discrimination.
- **Structural intersectionality:** overlapping disadvantages shape people's actual circumstances, for example access to help after violence.
- **Political intersectionality:** feminist and antiracist agendas may each marginalize women of color.
- **Representational intersectionality:** cultural images of women of color draw on combined racial and gender stereotypes.
- **Critical race theory:** law helps produce and maintain racial hierarchy, including through rules presented as neutral or color-blind.

## Major Works

- "Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex" (1989)
- "Mapping the Margins: Intersectionality, Identity Politics, and Violence against Women of Color" (1991)
- *Words That Wound* (1993, with Mari Matsuda, Charles Lawrence and Richard Delgado)
- *Critical Race Theory: The Key Writings That Formed the Movement* (1995, edited with Neil Gotanda, Gary Peller and Kendall Thomas)

## Case Studies

The 1989 article discusses *DeGraffenreid v. General Motors*, in which a court declined to let Black women bring a claim combining race and sex discrimination. "Mapping the Margins" examines battering and rape, including obstacles faced by immigrant women and women of color in shelters and legal institutions. Later, the #SayHerName campaign drew attention to Black women subjected to police violence.

## Method

Crenshaw combines close analysis of legal cases and doctrine with examination of public policy, social movements and cultural representation.

## Influence and Criticism

Intersectionality has become a major framework in sociology, gender studies, public health, law and human rights work. Related arguments had earlier been developed in Black feminist thought and activism, including the Combahee River Collective statement of 1977, and Patricia Hill Collins extended them in sociology. Scholars debate whether intersectionality is a theory, a method or a heuristic, how to choose which categories to analyze, and whether wide use has detached it from its concern with power; Jennifer Nash is among those who have examined these questions. Critical race theory has also become the target of political controversy in the United States.
