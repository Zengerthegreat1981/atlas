# Michel Foucault

Michel Foucault (1926-1984) was a French philosopher and historian of systems of thought whose analyses of power, knowledge and the formation of subjects are widely used in sociology.

## Life and Context

Foucault was born in Poitiers in 1926 and studied philosophy and psychology in Paris at the École Normale Supérieure. He held posts in Sweden, Poland and West Germany, then taught at Clermont-Ferrand, in Tunisia and at the experimental university of Vincennes. From 1970 he held a chair at the Collège de France. In the early 1970s he helped establish the Prisons Information Group, which sought to make prisoners' accounts public. He died in Paris in 1984.

## Main Ideas

- **Discourse:** historically specific rules shape what can count as a meaningful or true statement.
- **Power and knowledge:** forms of expertise and techniques of governing support one another.
- **Relational power:** power operates through many local relations and practices, not only through law and the state, and resistance arises within these relations.
- **Discipline:** timetables, surveillance, ranking and examination train bodies and establish norms.
- **Biopower:** modern authorities seek to manage the life of populations as well as individual bodies.
- **Governmentality:** government concerns the guidance of conduct, including the ways people govern themselves.
- **Subjects:** categories such as the delinquent or the homosexual became ways of knowing and acting upon persons.

## Major Works

- *History of Madness* (1961)
- *The Birth of the Clinic* (1963)
- *The Order of Things* (1966)
- *The Archaeology of Knowledge* (1969)
- *Discipline and Punish* (1975)
- *The History of Sexuality*, volumes 1-3 (1976 and 1984)
- Collège de France lectures, including *Security, Territory, Population* and *The Birth of Biopolitics* (published posthumously)

## Case Study

*Discipline and Punish* contrasts a public execution of 1757 with a later timetable for young prisoners to pose the question of how penal power changed. It then follows disciplinary techniques through prisons, armies, schools and workshops, using Bentham's Panopticon as a diagram of surveillance.

## Method

Archaeology describes the rules that organize bodies of discourse. Genealogy, indebted to Nietzsche, traces the contingent struggles and practices through which present categories and institutions took shape. Both challenge accounts of history as steady progress.

## Influence and Criticism

His work informs research on medicine, psychiatry, punishment, education, sexuality, organizations and digital surveillance. Historians have disputed parts of his evidence and periodization. Jürgen Habermas and Nancy Fraser questioned the normative basis of his critique, and others argue that his account leaves little room for agency or neglects gender and colonialism. His later work on ethics and practices of the self gives greater attention to how individuals shape their own conduct.

## Cross-References

Compare Weber on discipline and bureaucracy, Goffman on total institutions, and Butler on subject formation.
