# Georg Simmel

Georg Simmel (1858-1918) was a German philosopher and sociologist who analyzed the forms of social interaction, the money economy and the experience of modern city life.

## Life and Context

Simmel was born in Berlin in 1858 and received his doctorate in philosophy from the University of Berlin in 1881. He taught there as a private lecturer from 1885 and as an associate professor from 1901. Although his lectures drew large audiences, a full chair long eluded him, a situation often linked to antisemitism and to the unconventional character of his work. He was among the founders of the German Society for Sociology in 1909. In 1914 he became professor at Strasbourg, where he died in 1918.

## Main Ideas

- **Sociation:** society exists wherever individuals interact and affect one another; it is a continuing process more than a fixed substance.
- **Form and content:** the same forms, such as conflict, competition, subordination or exchange, appear in settings with very different purposes. Sociology can study these forms.
- **Numbers and group life:** the move from a dyad to a triad creates new possibilities, including mediation, the third who benefits, and divide and rule.
- **Social types:** figures such as the stranger, who is both near and distant, are defined by their relation to others.
- **Money:** a money economy extends impersonal relations and calculation while also widening individual freedom.
- **The metropolis:** city dwellers protect themselves against intense stimulation with intellectual detachment and a blasé attitude.
- **Objective and subjective culture:** the accumulated products of culture outgrow the individual's capacity to absorb them, which he described as a tragedy of culture.

## Major Works

- *On Social Differentiation* (1890)
- *The Philosophy of Money* (1900)
- "The Metropolis and Mental Life" (1903)
- *Sociology: Inquiries into the Construction of Social Forms* (1908)
- *Fundamental Questions of Sociology* (1917)

## Illustrations

Simmel's essays took up fashion, secrecy and secret societies, sociability, adventure, the senses and the poor. His analysis of fashion shows how it joins imitation of a group with the wish to be distinct from others.

## Method

He worked through essays and comparative examples drawn from history and everyday life, isolating recurring forms without building a closed system. He did not conduct systematic field research.

## Influence and Criticism

Simmel influenced Robert Park and Chicago urban sociology, Georg Lukács, conflict theory through Lewis Coser, and later network and interaction analysis. Critics, including Émile Durkheim, questioned whether the study of forms gave sociology a coherent method, and others have found the work fragmentary and weak on institutions and power. Later readers have valued the same essayistic style for its insight into modernity.

## Cross-References

Compare Weber on rationalization, Marx on money and commodities, and Goffman on the details of interaction.
