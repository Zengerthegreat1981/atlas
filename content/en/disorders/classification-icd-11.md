# International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)

The *International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision* (ICD-11) is the global standard diagnostic taxonomy for health conditions, mortality, and health services reporting maintained by the World Health Organization (WHO). Adopted by the 72nd World Health Assembly in May 2019 and officially entering into legal effect on 1 January 2022, ICD-11 encompasses all medical specialties. Chapter 06 focuses specifically on **Mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders**, reflecting major advances in clinical neuroscience, psychiatric epidemiology, and public health utility.

## Clinical Ceiling

This entry outlines the taxonomic architecture, structural innovations, and institutional development of ICD-11 and its Chapter 06; it is not a diagnostic instrument. Formal diagnostic assessment requires direct evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional applying the clinical requirements established in the official WHO diagnostic guidelines.

## Historical Evolution and Post-2022 Maintenance

The International Classification of Diseases traces its lineage to the 1893 Bertillon Classification of Causes of Death. The World Health Organization assumed stewardship with ICD-6 in 1948, introducing mental disorders for the first time. ICD-10 was adopted in 1990 and governed global healthcare for three decades.

ICD-11 represents the first fully digital, ontologically grounded revision of the classification:
- **Digital Platform and Foundation Component**: ICD-11 is built upon a multidimensional semantic web architecture (the Foundation Component) from which linearizations (such as mortality and morbidity statistics or specialized clinical guidelines) are derived.
- **Annual Maintenance Releases**: Following its official enactment in 2022, the WHO maintains an active annual update mechanism. Maintenance releases (e.g., January 2023, January 2024, and January 2025) update index terms, refine extension codes, integrate feedback from multilingual implementations, and harmonize diagnostic descriptions without destabilizing core statistical code structures.

## Structure of Chapter 06: Mental, Behavioural or Neurodevelopmental Disorders

Chapter 06 organizes mental disorders into 21 clinically meaningful groupings using an alphanumeric coding architecture (running from 6A00 to 6E8Z):
- **6A00–6A0Z**: Neurodevelopmental disorders (including Autism Spectrum Disorder, ADHD)
- **6A20–6A2Z**: Schizophrenia or other primary psychotic disorders
- **6A40–6A4Z**: Catatonia (classified as an independent clinical block)
- **6A60–6A8Z**: Mood disorders (unifying depressive and bipolar disorders)
- **6B00–6B0Z**: Anxiety or fear-related disorders
- **6B20–6B2Z**: Obsessive-compulsive or related disorders
- **6B40–6B4Z**: Disorders specifically associated with stress
- **6B60–6B6Z**: Dissociative disorders
- **6B80–6B8Z**: Feeding or eating disorders
- **6C00–6C0Z**: Elimination disorders
- **6C20–6C2Z**: Disorders of bodily distress or bodily experience
- **6C40–6C5Z**: Disorders due to substance use or addictive behaviours
- **6C70–6C7Z**: Impulse control disorders
- **6C90–6C9Z**: Disruptive behaviour or dissocial disorders
- **6D10–6D11.5**: Personality disorders and related traits
- **6D30–6D3Z**: Paraphilic disorders
- **6D50–6D5Z**: Factitious disorders
- **6D70–6E0Z**: Neurocognitive disorders
- **6E20–6E2Z**: Mental or behavioural disorders associated with pregnancy, childbirth or the puerperium
- **6E40**: Psychological or behavioural factors affecting disorders or diseases classified elsewhere
- **6E60–6E6Z**: Secondary mental or behavioural syndromes associated with disorders or diseases classified elsewhere
- **6E8Y / 6E8Z**: Other specified / unspecified mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders

## The Clinical Descriptions and Diagnostic Requirements (CDDR)

In 2024, the WHO formally published the *Clinical Descriptions and Diagnostic Requirements for ICD-11 Mental, Behavioural and Neurodevelopmental Disorders* (CDDR), the clinical companion guide for mental health practitioners. Developed through extensive global field trials, the CDDR provides:
- **Essential (Required) Features**: Detailed descriptions of mandatory core characteristics required for a positive diagnosis, minimizing artificial symptom-counting cutoffs.
- **Boundaries with Normality**: Clear clinical thresholds distinguishing psychopathology from normal transient distress or developmental variations.
- **Boundaries with Other Disorders**: Explicit differential diagnostic criteria comparing overlapping syndromes.
- **Culture-Related Features**: Guidance on cross-cultural symptom expression, linguistic variations in distress, and localized idioms of distress.

## Major Taxonomic Distinctions from DSM-5-TR

1. **Complex PTSD (6B41)**: ICD-11 recognizes Complex Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder as a distinct entity characterized by severe PTSD core symptoms accompanied by pervasive disturbances in self-organization (affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, and relational disturbances), whereas DSM-5-TR does not recognize CPTSD as a distinct diagnosis.
2. **Dimensional Model of Personality Disorders**: ICD-11 replaces traditional categorical personality disorder types with a unified diagnosis based on global severity (Mild, Moderate, Severe), accompanied by optional domain trait qualifiers: Negative Affectivity, Detachment, Dissociality, Disinhibition, and Anankastia.
3. **Acute Stress**: ICD-11 classifies Acute Stress Reaction (QE84) outside Chapter 06 in the section on factors influencing health status, treating it as an expectable physiological and psychological stress response rather than a mental disorder.
4. **Behavioral Addictions**: ICD-11 includes Gaming Disorder (6C51) alongside Gambling Disorder (6C50) as official diagnostic entities in Chapter 06, whereas DSM-5-TR retains Internet Gaming Disorder under Section III (conditions for further study).
5. **Prolonged Grief Disorder (6B42)**: ICD-11 established Prolonged Grief Disorder within its stress-related disorders block, subsequently adopted into Section II of DSM-5-TR in 2022.
