# Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR)

The *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) is the official compendium of mental disorders published by the American Psychiatric Association (APA) in March 2022. Serving as the primary diagnostic taxonomy in the United States and heavily influencing international psychiatric research, DSM-5-TR employs a categorical diagnostic framework while incorporating dimensional specifiers. It provides standardized criteria sets for clinical practice, health insurance reimbursement via ICD-10-CM codes, and epidemiological research.

## Clinical Ceiling

This entry documents exclusively the institutional history, structural organization, and taxonomic methodology of DSM-5-TR; it does not serve as a diagnostic instrument. The presentation of criteria does not substitute for comprehensive psychiatric or psychological evaluation by a licensed healthcare professional exercising trained clinical judgment.

## Historical Evolution

The American Psychiatric Association published the first edition of the DSM in 1952, followed by DSM-II (1968). The publication of DSM-III in 1980 marked a paradigm shift toward an explicit, descriptive, neo-Kraepelinian model designed to maximize diagnostic reliability and eliminate unproven psychoanalytic etiology. DSM-IV followed in 1994 (with a text revision, DSM-IV-TR, in 2000), and DSM-5 was published in 2013, removing the multiaxial diagnostic system. 

DSM-5-TR (2022) represents a comprehensive text revision incorporating updated literature reviews, revised terminology (such as culturally sensitive language and updated gender dysphoria criteria), modified diagnostic codes aligned with ongoing ICD-10-CM updates, and the addition of Prolonged Grief Disorder to Section II.

## Section I: Guidance on Manual Usage and the Definition of Mental Disorder

Section I provides the conceptual framework, clinical rationale, and coding conventions necessary for navigating the manual:
- **Definition of a Mental Disorder**: DSM-5-TR defines a mental disorder as a syndrome characterized by clinically significant disturbance in an individual's cognition, emotion regulation, or behavior that reflects a dysfunction in the psychological, biological, or developmental processes underlying mental functioning. It explicitly excludes expectable, culturally approved responses to common stressors (e.g., uncomplicated bereavement) and socially deviant behavior or political conflicts unless the deviance results from an underlying dysfunction.
- **Clinical Judgment and Diagnostic Thresholds**: The manual establishes that diagnostic criteria serve as clinical guides rather than rigid algorithmic checklists. Clinical training is required to differentiate pathology from transient distress. Meeting criteria does not automatically determine legal competence, disability status, or forensic liability.
- **Subtypes, Specifiers, and Severity Ratings**: Clinicians record diagnostic specificity using subtypes (mutually exclusive phenomenological subgroups) and specifiers (non-exclusive features describing course, severity, or clinical features, such as "with anxious distress").
- **Provisional and Other Specified/Unspecified Categories**: When clinical information is incomplete or presentations do not meet the full threshold of an established category, clinicians utilize "other specified" or "unspecified" designations to document the condition without false diagnostic precision.

## General Structure and the 22 Section II Chapters

DSM-5-TR is organized into three major sections:
- **Section I**: Basics of manual application, historical orientation, and clinical governance.
- **Section II**: Diagnostic criteria sets and ICD-10-CM codes, organized across 22 chapters reflecting a developmental and neurobiological lifespan gradient:
  1. Neurodevelopmental Disorders
  2. Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders
  3. Bipolar and Related Disorders
  4. Depressive Disorders
  5. Anxiety Disorders
  6. Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders
  7. Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders
  8. Dissociative Disorders
  9. Somatic Symptom and Related Disorders
  10. Feeding and Eating Disorders
  11. Elimination Disorders
  12. Sleep-Wake Disorders
  13. Sexual Dysfunctions
  14. Gender Dysphoria
  15. Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders
  16. Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders
  17. Neurocognitive Disorders
  18. Personality Disorders
  19. Paraphilic Disorders
  20. Other Mental Disorders and Additional Codes
  21. Medication-Induced Movement Disorders and Other Adverse Effects of Medication
  22. Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention (Z-codes)
- **Section III**: Emerging measures and models, including cross-cutting symptom measures, the Cultural Formulation Interview (CFI), the Alternative Model for Personality Disorders (AMPD), and conditions requiring further research.

## Coding and Relationship to ICD Systems

Unlike the World Health Organization's ICD-11, which utilizes an independent alphanumeric coding architecture, DSM-5-TR maps all diagnoses to official U.S. clinical modification codes (**ICD-10-CM**). Several structural differences distinguish DSM-5-TR from ICD-11:
- DSM-5-TR retains a categorical model for personality disorders in Section II (with the dimensional AMPD in Section III), whereas ICD-11 fully transitioned to a dimensional personality classification.
- DSM-5-TR categorizes Acute Stress Disorder as a mental disorder in Section II, whereas ICD-11 categorizes Acute Stress Reaction as a non-disorder stress response outside Chapter 06.
- Complex PTSD is recognized as an independent diagnostic entity in ICD-11 (6B41), but is not classified as a separate disorder in DSM-5-TR.
