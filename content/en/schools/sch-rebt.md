# Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT)
The historically first cognitive-behavioral school, founded by the American psychotherapist Albert Ellis in New York in 1955, before Aaron Beck developed cognitive therapy in the 1960s and 1970s. REBT is regarded as the founding model that shifted clinical psychology from a focus on the depth analytic structure to a focus on the conscious cognitive structure, and reconnected the clinic with the ancient Stoic philosophical tradition.

## Intellectual and Historical Context

The founding of REBT resulted from Ellis's disappointment with the limits of classical psychoanalysis in bringing about rapid change in therapy sessions. Ellis was directly influenced by Stoic philosophy, especially Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius, who stressed that human disturbance arises not from events themselves but from one's judgments about them. In the early 1950s Ellis moved away from psychoanalytic practice, and in 1955 he presented what he then called "Rational Therapy"; the name changed to "Rational Emotive Therapy" (RET) in 1961 and to "Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy" (REBT) in 1993, to stress the behavioral side of the approach.

Ellis founded the Institute for Rational Living in New York in 1959 (later the Institute for Rational-Emotive Therapy and then the Albert Ellis Institute), which became the main training center for REBT.

## The Central Thesis

REBT rests on the principle that unhealthy psychological suffering does not arise from direct events (A) but from the beliefs and irrational beliefs (B) that the individual adopts about those events, which produce undesirable emotional and behavioral consequences (C). This formulation, known as the ABC model, is regarded as the cornerstone of the model, and Ellis set it out in his book *Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy* (1962).

Ellis holds that the core of psychological illness lies in "demandingness" (Demandingness), that is, turning natural desires into absolute and rigid demands. Among the most prominent irrational beliefs he identified (paraphrased):

- I must have the love and approval of every significant person in my life.
- I must be thoroughly competent in everything I do.
- Others must always treat me fairly and kindly.

## The Clinical Method

REBT is distinguished by an active, directly didactic, and confrontational therapeutic style that relies on refutation and debate (Disputation) to challenge irrational beliefs and replace them with flexible rational beliefs. In the extended model this process is "D" (disputation), followed by "E" (an effective new philosophy) and "F" (new feelings and behaviors). The therapy aims to achieve "Unconditional Self-Acceptance" (USA), high frustration tolerance (High Frustration Tolerance, HFT), and unconditional acceptance of others and of reality. Unconditional self-acceptance means accepting oneself as a fallible human being without rating one's worth globally, in contrast to self-esteem that depends on performance or approval.

## The Evidence and Position in the Field of Psychotherapy

Meta-analyses of REBT and related cognitive-behavioral methods (for example David and colleagues, 2018) report benefits across a range of problems, although the quality of many trials is modest and the research base is smaller than for Beck-style CBT. REBT is regarded as one of the earliest cognitive-behavioral therapies. Beck developed his cognitive therapy largely independently, and the two men disagreed on clinical and philosophical points, so questions of priority and influence between them are debated.

## Impact and Legacy

The influence of REBT extended beyond clinical psychotherapy to include education, management training, couples counseling, and self-therapy through the rational "self-help" literature. The book *A Guide to Rational Living* (1961; revised as *A New Guide to Rational Living*, 1975), co-authored by Ellis and Robert A. Harper, was a widely read popular statement of the approach.
