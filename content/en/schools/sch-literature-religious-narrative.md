# Comparative Religious Literature and Narrative Studies

An interdisciplinary academic field that applies the tools of literary criticism, semiotics and hermeneutics to religious texts and narratives across cultures. It seeks the shared narrative and symbolic structure between "literature" and "sacred scripture," and the ways in which the meaning of a text is renewed across generations. The field took shape in the 1950s and 1960s through the work of Canadian, American and French critics who tried to move beyond a purely theological-doctrinal reading of the holy books toward reading them as great literary works with their own narrative and figurative structure.

## Literary Reading of Scripture in the Arabic Heritage

The Arabic heritage has a literary reading of the Qur'an that predates the Western field by centuries. Al-Jahiz is reported to have written on the *nazm* (composition) of the Qur'an, though his work is lost, and al-Baqillani (d. 1013 CE), in *I'jaz al-Qur'an* (The Inimitability of the Qur'an), made the arrangement and style of the Qur'an a basis for demonstrating its inimitability. 'Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani (d. 1078 CE), in *Dala'il al-i'jaz*, elaborated the theory of *nazm* (textual arrangement), meaning the relations of composition, precedence and deferment through which meaning is produced. In the twentieth century Amin al-Khuli, and after him his students Muhammad Ahmad Khalafallah and 'A'isha 'Abd al-Rahman (Bint al-Shati'), carried this direction into "literary exegesis." Sayyid Qutb wrote *al-Taswir al-fanni fi al-Qur'an* (1945) on image and scene. Bint al-Shati' wrote *al-Tafsir al-bayani li-l-Qur'an al-karim* (1962–1969), using the linguistic induction of each word across all its occurrences. Khalafallah presented *al-Fann al-qasasi fi al-Qur'an* (1947), reading Qur'anic stories as an art whose purpose is guidance rather than historical record, which provoked an academic crisis. Angelika Neuwirth studied the literary structure of the Meccan surahs and their development in a German monograph, *Studien zur Komposition der mekkanischen Suren* (1981). She later read the Qur'an as an oral, dialogic text in *Scripture, Poetry, and the Making of a Community* (2014). Navid Kermani studied the aesthetic experience of the Qur'an in *Gott ist schön* (1999; English translation *God Is Beautiful*, 2015). Issa Boullata compiled *Literary Structures of Religious Meaning in the Qur'an* (2000). In the field of narrative, the literature of the "Tales of the Prophets" (al-Tha'labi in the eleventh century) was studied by Roberto Tottoli in *Biblical Prophets in the Qur'an and Muslim Literature* (2002). All this shows that the question of the relation between literature and revelation is old in Arab culture, and that the dispute between the aesthetic and the doctrinal was never settled in Arab culture either.

## Intellectual Foundations

- **Northrop Frye and biblical symbolic patterns**: In *Anatomy of Criticism* (1957) and *The Great Code: The Bible and Literature* (1982), Frye argued that the Bible forms the "great code" on which Western literature built its symbolic system (paradise, the flood, the exodus, redemption). He read it as a coherent system of recurring literary images rather than as merely a doctrinal document. In *Secular Scripture* (1976) he traced how "high" literature intersects with the narrative "popular" literature since Homer, suggesting that the boundaries between religious and secular narrative are more flexible than they appear.
- **Erich Auerbach and Robert Alter**: In *Mimesis* (1946; English translation 1953), Auerbach compared Homer's description of Odysseus's scar with the story of Abraham's offering of his son, to show that biblical narrative leaves the background obscure and loads the text with layers of meaning. Robert Alter, in *The Art of Biblical Narrative* (1981), re-read the books of the Bible with the tools of close literary criticism, and with Frank Kermode edited *The Literary Guide to the Bible* (1987).
- **Paul Ricoeur and narrative hermeneutics**: In *Time and Narrative* (1983–1985) Ricoeur developed the theory that narrative shapes human time. In *Figuring the Sacred* (a collection of essays published in English in 1995) he argued that the narrative elements within the sacred text open the way to its continual reinterpretation across generations.
- **The debate over the referent of narrative**: There is a well-known academic debate over whether the biblical narrative carries its meaning within its own narrative world (as the theologian Hans Frei argued) or whether meaning is renewed in the act of reading and the reader's appropriation of the text (as Ricoeur argued). This disagreement is a central methodological dispute in the field.

## Main Themes

Analysis of the sacred text's structure examines the literary structure of verses, chapters and books: parallelism, repetition and narrative rhythm. In narrative and myth, the inquiry asks how religious narrative serves doctrinal and moral purposes, building on Alan Dundes's *Sacred Narrative: Readings in the Theory of Myth* (1984). In symbols and metaphors, the literary image (light, water, the journey) and its transmission of spiritual meanings across traditions are studied. In interpretation and reception, the changes in a single text's meaning as its audience changes are tracked, from medieval monastic reading to modern critical reading.

## Limits and Criticism

Much of this literature leans toward the Christian tradition (the Bible in both testaments) as its reference case, and its application to the Qur'an, Hindu and Buddhist texts has been comparatively less cumulative, despite its existence. The disagreement between a "literary-aesthetic" and a "doctrinal-historical" reading of the sacred text itself has not been resolved. Some theologians and jurists object to treating revelation as a literary work, since for them the text is the speech of God and not a human creation to be measured by standards of artistry. Critics have also charged Frye's school with treating the Bible as a literary unity, in tension with the findings of multi-source historical criticism.
