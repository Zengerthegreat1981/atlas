# Social Psychology

Social psychology is the scientific study of **how individuals perceive others, influence them, and are influenced by them**, in real social contexts. It examines how situations shape behavior, how situations are in turn shaped by individuals, and how the interaction between the two produces socio-psychological phenomena: conformity, obedience, prejudice, discrimination, cooperation, aggression, leadership, belonging, and identity. It is "the science that stands between psychology and sociology," though methodologically it is closer to experimental psychology.

## Intellectual Context

- **American pragmatism** (James, Dewey): emphasis on action and experience in context.
- **Gestalt psychology** (Wertheimer, Köhler, Koffka): the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.
- **Applied Gestalt psychology** (Lewin): "There is nothing so practical as a good theory."
- **Experimental psychology** (Wundt, Pavlov, Watson).
- **Sociology** (Durkheim, Weber, Simmel): social phenomena as an independent object of study.

## Historical Stages

- **Pre-foundation** (1890s): Norman Triplett's 1898 study of the effect of competition on cycling and reeling performance, often cited as the first experiment in social psychology and a forerunner of research on social facilitation.
- **Foundation** (1908): the first textbooks of social psychology appeared, William McDougall's *An Introduction to Social Psychology* (Britain) and E. A. Ross's *Social Psychology* (United States). Floyd Allport's experimental *Social Psychology* followed in 1924.
- **First flourishing** (1930s-1940s): Kurt Lewin (Germany, then the United States) develops field theory and group dynamics. T. W. Adorno and colleagues' work on the authoritarian personality (*The Authoritarian Personality*, 1950).
- **Classic experiments** (1950s-1960s): Asch (1951, group pressure), Milgram (1963, obedience), Zimbardo (1971, Stanford prison). Festinger (*When Prophecy Fails*, 1956, cognitive dissonance).
- **The crisis** (1970s): critiques of experimental social psychology, including Kenneth Gergen's "Social Psychology as History" (1973), which argued that social behavior is historically contingent, and concerns about the ethics and artificiality of laboratory studies, prompted debate about method and about the field's relation to sociology.
- **Globalization and multiculturalism** (1980s-present): cross-cultural social psychology (Triandis, Markus, Kitayama), and the social psychology of discrimination (Allport, Tajfel, Turner).
- **Social neuroscience** (from the 1990s-2000s): the neural basis of social cognition and social behavior.

## Foundational Concepts

- **Situations**: the social environment as a direct force on behavior.
- **Social cognition**: how the individual interprets social information (biases, systematic errors, categories, schemas).
- **Social influence**: conformity, obedience, compliance, persuasion.
- **Persuasion**: the Yale attitude-change program (Hovland and colleagues, 1950s): source, message, and audience.
- **Cognitive bias**: self-serving bias, confirmation bias, the halo effect, the selective-recall effect.
- **Cognitive dissonance (Festinger)**: the tension arising from two conflicting beliefs.
- **Conformity (Asch)**: matching the group even in the presence of contrary evidence.
- **Obedience (Milgram)**: submitting to authority even to the point of harming others.
- **Discrimination and intergroup conflict** (Tajfel, Turner, *Social Identity Theory*): social identity, in-group favoritism, discrimination against the out-group.
- **Social facilitation vs. social inhibition** (Zajonc): the presence of others improves performance on simple tasks and impairs performance on complex ones.
- **Prosocial behavior**: conditions for helping, the Latané and Darley model.
- **Aggression**: the frustration-aggression theory (Dollard, Miller), the social-emotional model.
- **Attraction**: similarity, proximity, physical attractiveness.
- **Compliance** (Cialdini): principles of influence such as reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity (a seventh, unity, was added in later work).

## Major Figures

- **Norman Triplett** (1861-1934): experimental pioneer.
- **Kurt Lewin** (1890-1947): a founding figure of experimental social psychology and group dynamics.
- **Solomon Asch** (1907-1996): group pressure.
- **Leon Festinger** (1919-1989): cognitive dissonance.
- **Gordon Allport** (1897-1967): personality, prejudice.
- **Stanley Milgram** (1933-1984): obedience.
- **Philip Zimbardo** (1933-2024): the Stanford prison experiment, time as a psychological factor.
- **Albert Bandura** (1925-2021): observational learning, self-efficacy.
- **Henri Tajfel** (1919-1982): social identity theory.
- **Robert Cialdini** (1945-): persuasion (*Influence*, 1984).
- **Daniel Kahneman** (1934-2024) and **Amos Tversky** (1937-1996): judgment and decision-making, a basis of behavioral economics.

## Major Sub-Currents

- **Experimental social psychology** (Lewin, Festinger).
- **Social identity theory** (Tajfel, Turner).
- **The social psychology of discrimination** (discrimination, unconscious bias, *Implicit Bias*).
- **Cross-cultural social psychology** (Markus & Kitayama, Triandis, Hofstede).
- **Social neuroscience** (Ochsner, Lieberman, from the early 2000s).
- **Evolutionary social psychology** (Buss, Cosmides, Tooby, 1990s-).
- **Critical social psychology** (for example Henriques and colleagues, *Changing the Subject*, 1984).

## Clinical and Community Applications

- **Intervention in discrimination** (unconscious-bias training, though sharply criticized).
- **Crisis management** (message design in epidemics and disasters).
- **Marketing and public health** (vaccination, health campaigns).
- **Intergroup relations** (intervention, contact, the extended contact model).
- **Forensic psychology** (jury decisions, media effects).
- **Clinical applications** (for example research on social anxiety and stigma).

## Relation to Neighboring Schools

- **Evolutionary psychology**: evolutionary social psychology integrates the two.
- **Neuropsychology**: social brain networks.
- **Behavioral economics**: direct intersection (Kahneman, Thaler).
- **Clinical psychology**: cognitive biases are part of disorders.
- **Family therapy**: behavior within the social system.
- **Political psychology**: direct intersection with political understanding.
- **Feminist psychology**: feminist social psychology.

## Criticism and Limits

- **Western, student, laboratory bias**: experiments on WEIRD university students (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic).
- **Excessive repetition of the classic experiments**: Asch, Milgram, Zimbardo.
- **The replication crisis** (2010s): large-scale replication projects (such as the Open Science Collaboration, 2015) found that many published psychological findings, including some in social psychology, did not replicate or replicated with smaller effects.
- **Laboratory bias**: laboratory situations may not match real life.
- **Limited applied impact**: some findings are hard to translate into effective interventions.
