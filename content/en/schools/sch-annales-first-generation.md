# Annales School — First Generation (Bloch and Febvre)

The first generation of the Annales School was an intellectual and methodological movement founded in 1929 by French historians Marc Bloch (1886–1944) and Lucien Febvre (1878–1956) with the establishment of the journal *Annales d'histoire économique et sociale* at the University of Strasbourg. The movement challenged the prevailing positivism of the French Methodical School (typified by Charles-Victor Langlois and Charles Seignobos), which restricted historical research to elite diplomatic correspondence, military campaigns, and political narrative (*histoire événementielle*).

## The Intellectual Program: Total History and Interdisciplinarity

Bloch and Febvre argued that political events represent merely the superficial tremors of human society. To construct a "total history" (*histoire totale*), historians needed to investigate the underlying economic structures, agrarian systems, social networks, and collective mentalities (*mentalités*) that shape human behavior over extended periods:

- **Cross-Disciplinary Integration**: The founders systematically dismantled the isolation of academic history by incorporating concepts from sociology (Émile Durkheim, François Simiand), human geography (Paul Vidal de La Blache), economics, and linguistics.
- **Problem-Oriented History (*Histoire-problème*)**: Instead of passive chronological narration, historical inquiry must begin with an explicit, theoretical question, formulating hypotheses and cross-examining diverse types of evidence.
- **Expansion of Historical Traces**: Challenging the dogma that history is made solely from state documents, Bloch and Febvre expanded legitimate sources to include aerial photography, cadastral surveys, field systems, tools, folklore, ecclesiastical rituals, and vernacular linguistics.

## Major Monographic Contributions

Both founders illustrated their programmatic vision through landmark historical studies:

- **Marc Bloch**:
  - *The Royal Touch* (*Les Rois thaumaturges*, 1924): An innovative comparative inquiry into the supernatural healing power attributed to French and English monarchs (the curing of scrofula), analyzing royal miracle claims not as political events, but as windows into the collective religious psychology and legitimizing symbolism of medieval kingship.
  - *French Rural History* (*Les Caractères originaux de l'histoire rurale française*, 1931): A major agrarian history reconstructing ancient open-field and enclosed systems through retrogressive analysis (reading past landscapes through modern traces).
  - *Feudal Society* (*La Société féodale*, 2 vols., 1939–1940): A comprehensive synthesis examining vassalage, ties of dependence, and the mental climate of the medieval European world.
  - *The Historian's Craft* (*Apologie pour l'histoire ou Métier d'historien*): Bloch's classic methodological testament, composed in manuscript while active in the clandestine French Resistance during the Nazi occupation, and published posthumously after his execution by the Gestapo in June 1944.
- **Lucien Febvre**:
  - *Philippe II et la Franche-Comté* (1911): A study analyzing how regional social structures, economic interests, and administrative factions conditioned religious and political allegiance.
  - *A Geographical Introduction to History* (*La Terre et l'évolution humaine*, 1922): A trenchant critique of geographic determinism, advancing "possibilism"—the principle that physical environments offer possibilities rather than determining human choices.
  - *The Problem of Unbelief in the Sixteenth Century: The Religion of Rabelais* (1942): A landmark study in historical mentalities arguing that modern atheism was anachronistic and intellectually impossible in the sixteenth century due to the lack of conceptual and lexical apparatus (*outillage mental*).

## Historiographical Legacy

The first generation had a major influence on the discipline of history. By transforming the historian's workshop into an open laboratory engaging the social sciences, Bloch and Febvre prepared the ground for the subsequent structural history of Fernand Braudel (second generation) and the anthropological history of mentalities (third generation).
