# Annales and Aggregate Social History

"Annales and Aggregate Social History" designates the quantitative, demographic, and structural phase that dominated the second generation and the transition to the third generation of the French Annales School (*École des Annales*) from approximately 1960 to 1980. Building on the foundational insights of Marc Bloch and Lucien Febvre, this movement replaced conventional narrative history with serial history (*l'histoire sérielle*), utilizing statistical aggregation, parish registers, fiscal ledgers, and demographic modeling to reconstruct long-term socioeconomic structures.

## The Institutional and Theoretical Hegemony of Fernand Braudel

During the post-World War II decades, Fernand Braudel established the institutional hegemony of the Annales through the Sixth Section of the École Pratique des Hautes Études (later reorganized as the École des Hautes Études en Sciences Sociales, EHESS) and the Maison des Sciences de l'Homme in Paris.

In his masterwork *The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II* (1949), Braudel formulated the tripartite hierarchy of historical temporality:
1. **The *Longue Durée***: The quasi-immobile time of geography, ecology, and climate shaping human possibilities over millennia.
2. **Conjunctures (*Conjonctures*)**: Medium-term socioeconomic cycles lasting decades or half-centuries, encompassing demographic growth, trade rhythms, and price fluctuations.
3. **Events (*Événements*)**: Short-term political, diplomatic, and military episodes, which Braudel characterized as ephemeral "surface disturbances."

## The Quantitative and Serial Turn (*L'Histoire Sérielle*)

During the 1960s and 1970s, Annales historians operationalized Braudel's structural model by applying rigorous quantitative methods to mass documentary corpuses:

- **Price History and Economic Cycles**: Developed by Ernest Labrousse (*Esquisse du mouvement des prix et des revenus en France au XVIIIe siècle*, 1933; *La Crise de l'économie française à la fin de l'Ancien Régime*, 1944), historians analyzed long-term price series to argue that subsistence agricultural crises triggered revolutionary political upheaval.
- **Historical Demography and Family Reconstitution**: Drawing on methods pioneered by demographer Louis Henry, Pierre Goubert produced the landmark regional monograph *Beauvais et le Beauvaisis de 1600 à 1730* (1960). By linking baptismal, marriage, and burial records from parish registers, Goubert mapped fertility rates, average age at marriage, infant mortality, and the devastating impact of subsistence crises (*crises de subsistance*) on local populations.
- **Agrarian Long Waves**: Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie's *The Peasants of Languedoc* (*Les Paysans de Languedoc*, 1966) utilized tithe records, land tax surveys (*compoix*), and wage data to trace a multi-century "Malthusian cycle" of demographic expansion, land fragmentation, and economic contraction between the fifteenth and early eighteenth centuries—a trajectory Le Roy Ladurie famously characterized as "history that stands still" (*l'histoire immobile*).
- **Maritime and Trade Series**: Pierre Chaunu conducted monumental serial analyses, such as his multi-volume study *Séville et l'Atlantique (1504–1650)*, which quantified transatlantic shipping tonnage to track imperial trade cycles.

## Methodological Critique and the Cultural Turn

While aggregate social history produced important empirical findings, it encountered significant theoretical critiques in the late 1970s:
- **Structural Determinism**: Critics argued that extreme statistical quantification rendered historical subjects passive cogs within economic and demographic trends, eliminating conscious political agency, individual resistance, and ideological conflict.
- **The Third-Generation Shift**: In response, historians associated with the Annales third generation—including Jacques Le Goff, Georges Duby, and Le Roy Ladurie himself (in *Montaillou*, 1975)—pivoted from aggregate numbers toward historical anthropology and the "history of mentalities" (*histoire des mentalités*), investigating the lived experiences, folk beliefs, and emotional worlds of ordinary individuals.
