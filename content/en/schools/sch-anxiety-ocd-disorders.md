# Anxiety Disorders and the OCD Spectrum

This umbrella groups two diagnostic categories that were formally separated in DSM-5 (2013), after having been together under one heading, "anxiety disorders," in DSM-IV: anxiety disorders in the narrow sense (excessive fear and anticipation of an unreal or disproportionate threat), and obsessive-compulsive spectrum disorders (intrusive thoughts and the compulsive or repetitive behaviors associated with them). The separation was made in recognition of the distinctness of their neural and cognitive mechanisms and their patterns of therapeutic response, despite their shared history and high clinical overlap.

## Origins and Context

Early diagnostic systems (DSM-I and DSM-II) classified anxiety and OCD under "neurosis" within a psychoanalytic framework. DSM-III (1980) marked a decisive shift by separating panic disorder from generalized anxiety through independent descriptive criteria, influenced by Donald Klein's research on the selective response of panic attacks to tricyclic antidepressants, which supported the hypothesis that panic disorder is a biological entity distinct from generalized anxiety. Aaron Beck, and then David Clark and Paul Salkovskis, developed detailed cognitive-behavioral models for each anxiety disorder during the 1980s and 1990s, and these models became the basis of standard CBT protocols. In DSM-5, a new category, "Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders," was created as a separate chapter that includes, alongside OCD itself, body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding disorder (previously considered a subtype of OCD), trichotillomania, and excoriation disorder.

## Diagnostic Anchors

- **Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD):** excessive, hard-to-control anxiety and worry about several areas of life, lasting at least six months, accompanied by at least three physical or cognitive symptoms out of six (such as fatigue, muscle tension, or sleep disturbance).
- **Panic disorder:** recurrent, unexpected panic attacks (a peak of intense fear within minutes, accompanied by four or more physical symptoms), followed by at least one month of persistent worry about another attack, or a maladaptive behavioral change linked to it.
- **Agoraphobia:** in DSM-5 it became an independent diagnosis that does not require a prior panic disorder, and it requires marked fear of at least two of five situations (such as public transport, open spaces, enclosed spaces, standing in a queue or crowds, or leaving the home alone).
- **Social anxiety disorder:** marked fear of one or more social or performance situations because of the possibility of negative evaluation, persisting for at least six months.
- **Specific phobia:** clear fear of a specific object or situation (an animal, height, blood, flying), persisting for at least six months.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD):** the presence of obsessions (unwanted intrusive thoughts, urges, or images) and/or compulsions (repetitive behaviors or mental acts performed in response to an obsession or according to rigid rules), which consume considerable time (more than one hour per day) or cause distress or marked functional impairment.
- **ICD-11 position:** the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision, maintains a similar separation between anxiety and fear disorders on the one hand, and obsessive-compulsive disorder and related disorders on the other, with criteria that are essentially close to DSM-5-TR, although they differ in details of the temporal criteria and exclusions.

## Key Figures and Contributions

David Clark developed an influential cognitive model of panic disorder, explaining it through the catastrophic misinterpretation of normal bodily sensations. His work extended to a cognitive model of social anxiety disorder in collaboration with Adrian Wells. Paul Salkovskis developed a cognitive model of OCD that focuses on misinterpreting inflated responsibility for intrusive thoughts, which provided the theoretical cognitive basis for exposure and response prevention (ERP), whose behavioral origins Victor Meyer had established as early as 1966. David Barlow, in *Anxiety and Its Disorders*, presented an integrative model of the biological and psychological vulnerabilities shared among anxiety disorders, which later contributed to developing the "unified protocol" for treatment across diagnoses.

## Limits

The comorbidity among the disorders of this umbrella is high: many patients with panic disorder also have agoraphobia, and OCD spectrum disorders share the feature of compulsive repetitiveness with some impulse-control disorders without necessarily matching their neural mechanisms. The separation of anxiety disorders from the OCD spectrum in DSM-5 itself is debated, as some researchers argue that the clinical anxiety that accompanies OCD makes the sharp separation between them artificial clinically more than theoretically.
