# School of Principle (Lixue / Cheng-Zhu)

One of the two great branches of Neo-Confucianism, and the more influential of the two, called "Lixue" (理學, the study of principle) or the "Cheng-Zhu school" after the brothers Cheng Hao and Cheng Yi and then Zhu Xi. It makes "principle" (li) the normative and rational structure of reality, and makes "energy" (qi) the matter in which it is embodied. It became the official state philosophy in China from the early fourteenth century to the early twentieth century, and from there passed to Korea, Japan and Vietnam. It is also known as the "learning of the Way" (Daoxue) when this is meant to denote the Neo-Confucian current in general.

## Precursors and Founding

- **Zhou Dunyi** (1017-1073): author of the *Explanation of the Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate* (Taiji tu shuo), which showed how the Supreme Ultimate (taiji) generates yin and yang, then the five elements, then things.
- **Zhang Zai** (1020-1077): author of the *Western Inscription*, which saw the cosmos as one family, and held that "qi" is the origin of the cosmos.
- **Cheng Hao** (1032-1085): linked principle to the harmony of the heart-mind and the cosmos, and was known for his emphasis on "harmony with creation" and on inner uprightness; he is closer to the School of Mind.
- **Cheng Yi** (1033-1107): made "human nature is principle" (xing ji li), and taught that realizing principle consists in "seriousness and reverence" (jing) and in "the extension of knowledge through the investigation of things" (ge wu zhi zhi), and gave special weight to the *Great Learning* and the *Doctrine of the Mean*; it was Zhu Xi who later assembled the "Four Books" as the core curriculum.
- **Zhu Xi** (1130-1200): the synthesizer and culminating figure of the philosophy; in the *Collected Commentaries on the Four Books* (1190) he laid the foundation of the method, produced the *Reflections on Things at Hand* (Jinsi lu, 1175) with Lü Zuqian, while the *Classified Conversations of Master Zhu* (Zhuzi yulei) is a posthumous compilation of his students' records, edited in 1270. He established himself as the teacher through commentaries on the *Analects*, *Mencius*, the *Great Learning* and the *Doctrine of the Mean*, and on the *Book of Changes*.

## Central Theses

- **Principle and energy**: everything is composed of "li" and "qi"; principle is that by which a thing is what it is and what it ought to be, and energy is the matter of realization; they are not separate in reality, but principle is logically prior.
- **One principle, many manifestations** (li yi fen shu): the cosmos has one universal principle (the Supreme Ultimate), and in each thing there is a share of it, just as one moon is reflected in many waters.
- **Nature and feelings**: "nature" (xing) is principle in the human being and is good, while "feelings" (qing) arise from energy and may go astray. The "heart-mind" (xin) unites nature and feelings.
- **The investigation of knowledge**: it is accomplished by investigating the principles of things through gradual study, including the classical texts, until the day of "comprehensive breakthrough."
- **Seriousness and reverence** (jing): the practice of inner attention and focus in daily life, which is the bridge between knowledge and action.
- **Social ethics**: the *Family Rituals* (Jia li), attributed to Zhu Xi, reorganized family rites, and arranged lineage, ancestral traditions and the education of the young.

## Spread

- Zhu Xi's commentaries were recognized as the authorized text in the civil service examinations in 1313 under the Yuan dynasty, and remained so until the abolition of the examinations in 1905, so that the "Four Books" formed the common culture of the Chinese elite.
- An Hyang helped introduce it to Korea in the late thirteenth century, and it became the official doctrine of Joseon and deepened with Yi Hwang (Toegye) and Yi I (Yulgok). In Japan Hayashi Razan adopted it under the Tokugawa, and in Vietnam it became the state ideology.

## The Two Cheng Brothers

Later tradition traces two tendencies to the Cheng brothers. Cheng Hao stressed the unity of the person with all things and the identity of principle with humaneness (ren), a line later claimed by Lu Jiuyuan and Wang Yangming. Cheng Yi distinguished principle from qi more sharply and taught gradual investigation of things combined with reverent attention, the line Zhu Xi inherited. Modern scholars note that this division is partly a retrospective construction.

## Rivalry and Critique

- **The School of Mind**: Lu Jiuyuan (1139-1193), in the Goose Lake debate of 1175, held that "the heart-mind is principle" and that wide study is a distraction, and he was followed by Wang Yangming (1472-1529) with the theory of the "unity of knowledge and action."
- **Wang Fuzhi** (1619-1692): denied that principle is prior to energy, and affirmed the primacy of sensible things.
- **Dai Zhen** (1724-1777): accused the school of using "principle" to kill people, and of the rigid adherence to "principle" serving as a cover for the will of the powerful.
- **Evidential learning**: faulted the school for its lack of interest in the texts as they are.
- **Modern revival**: Feng Youlan (1895-1990) formulated a "New Lixue" (Xin lixue) in a book of that name in 1939, and Mou Zongsan (1909-1995), in *Mind as Substance and Nature as Substance* (1968-1969), classified Zhu Xi as a "separate branch" from the line of original Confucianism because he made principle merely "being without activity," an influential critique.

## Limits of the School

- The price of official adoption was rigidity in examinations oriented toward memorization, and it was criticized by reformers in the late Qing.
- Many critics held that the separation of principle and energy may suggest a metaphysical dualism that is hard to reconcile with cosmic unity.
