# Philosophical Hermeneutics (Philosophische Hermeneutik)

A philosophical current founded by **Hans-Georg Gadamer** (1900-2002) in *Truth and Method* (Wahrheit und Methode, 1960), aiming to transform hermeneutics from an **interpretive method for texts** into a **comprehensive philosophy of human understanding**. This school holds that understanding is not merely a cognitive act but a **way of being** (Seinsweise), and that every relation to the world is an interpretive one. This page focuses on the long history of the art of interpretation and on Gadamer's own philosophy, while the page on Critical Hermeneutics deals with the controversy over ideology and power.

## Ancient Roots and Traditions of Interpretation

The word "hermeneutics" comes from the Greek verb *hermēneuein* ("to interpret, to translate"), and from it comes the title of Aristotle's treatise *On Interpretation* (Peri hermeneias). It flourished in the traditions of sacred texts: the interpretation of the Church Fathers (Origen and Augustine) at its literal and symbolic levels, the Jewish midrash, and, in the Islamic tradition, the sciences of tafsir and ta'wil and the principles of jurisprudence. Al-Shafi'i (d. 820) set out in the *Risala* (early ninth century) the rules for the signification of words and for clarity of expression; al-Ghazali, in his "canon of interpretation" within *Faysal al-Tafriqa*, investigated the limits of interpreting texts when their apparent sense conflicts with reason; and Averroes, in *Fasl al-Maqal*, made demonstration the criterion of interpretation, so that the apparent sense must be interpreted when it conflicts with demonstration, provided that the interpretation is not divulged to the general public. Scholars have compared these traditions with Western hermeneutics without considering them equivalent to it.

Within Protestant theology, Luther's principle of *sola scriptura* and Matthias Flacius Illyricus's *Clavis Scripturae Sacrae* (1567) developed rules for interpreting scripture by its own context; this biblical hermeneutics is the immediate background of Schleiermacher's general theory, and in the twentieth century Rudolf Bultmann's programme of demythologizing the New Testament drew explicitly on Heidegger's analysis of understanding.

## From Schleiermacher to Dilthey

- **Friedrich Schleiermacher** (1768-1834): made hermeneutics a general "art of understanding" not confined to sacred texts, and distinguished between grammatical interpretation and psychological interpretation, which reconstructs the author's thought.
- **Wilhelm Dilthey** (1833-1911): held that the "human sciences" rest on **understanding** (Verstehen) of lived experience and its expression, as opposed to the causal **explanation** (Erklären) of the natural sciences (*The Formation of the Historical World in the Human Sciences*, 1910).
- **Heidegger** (1889-1976): in *Being and Time* (1927) he made understanding a structure of human existence (Dasein) itself, and made "fore-understanding" a condition of meaning.

## Gadamer's Philosophy

Gadamer moved to asking how understanding is possible, and not only how to ensure its correctness. His most important concepts:
- **The hermeneutic circle**: we understand the whole through the parts and the parts through the whole; this is not a methodological defect but our way of being in the world.
- **Prejudices (Vorurteile)**: not obstacles to be removed but conditions of understanding; we come to the text with preconceptions that the encounter modifies.
- **Effective history** (Wirkungsgeschichte and effective-historical consciousness): all understanding is shaped by the history of the text's reception, and one cannot leap over one's historicity.
- **Fusion of horizons (Horizontverschmelzung)**: understanding is an encounter between the horizon of the interpreter and the horizon of the text, not the copying of a ready-made meaning.
- **Truth as disclosure**: it goes beyond the idea of correspondence to the experience of truth in art, history and language, in the manner of Heidegger's concept of disclosure (aletheia).
- **Application (Anwendung)**: understanding applies meaning to the present situation, as a judge or a preacher does, an idea he drew from the Protestant tradition and from legal jurisprudence.
- **Language**: "Being that can be understood is language"; the world opens up in language and dialogue.

## Key Figures

- **Gadamer** (1900-2002): *Wahrheit und Methode* (1960), and the *Kleine Schriften* (1967-1977).
- **Paul Ricoeur** (1913-2005): developed a hermeneutics of symbol, text and narrative, and proposed that textual "distanciation" is a condition of understanding.
- **Emilio Betti** (1890-1968): criticized Gadamer in the name of the objectivity of interpretation in *Hermeneutics as the General Methodology of the Human Sciences* (1962).
- **E. D. Hirsch Jr.** (b. 1928): defended the fixed meaning of the author in *Validity in Interpretation* (1967).
- **Herder and Hamann**: predecessors who saw language and history as the basis of understanding.

## Arab and Islamic Reception

Hermeneutics entered Arab thought from the 1980s. Nasr Hamid Abu Zayd, in *The Concept of the Text: A Study in the Sciences of the Qur'an* (1990) and *Problems of Reading and Mechanisms of Interpretation* (1992), drew on it to read the Qur'anic text as a historical discourse tied to its linguistic and cultural context; his work provoked sharp controversy that led to an Egyptian court ruling in 1995, later upheld, that annulled his marriage on grounds of apostasy. Fazlur Rahman, in *Islam and Modernity* (1982), formulated the method of the "double movement" from the situation to the principle and then back to the situation, which is close to the idea of application. Mohammed Arkoun offered a critical reading of the heritage, and Abdolkarim Soroush linked the development of the understanding of religion to changes in human knowledge. Opponents of this trend argue that tying the sacred text to the historicity of understanding undermines the permanence of the Shari'a.

## Limits and Critique

- **The charge of traditionalism**: Jürgen Habermas (*On the Logic of the Social Sciences*, 1967) criticized Gadamer for holding that acknowledging the authority of tradition may justify it without critique.
- **The charge of relativism**: Betti and Hirsch held that denying a fixed meaning opens the door to arbitrariness.
- **Ricoeur's reply**: understanding is mediated by explanation (the methods of structural analysis and critique of ideology), so that critique becomes part of interpretation rather than its opponent.
- **The problem of the other**: does the interpreter genuinely listen to the foreign text, or project the self onto it?

## Applications

Theology and biblical interpretation after historicism, law (the judge between rule and case), medicine (reading the patient's story), literature and criticism, sociology and anthropology (Geertz), and the history and philosophy of science (Thomas Kuhn acknowledged a hermeneutic dimension in his method).
