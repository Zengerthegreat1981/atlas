# Social Cognitive Learning Theory

The decisive bridging movement led by Albert Bandura, which moved behaviorism from mechanical determinism toward integrating cognitive processes, social modeling and self-regulation.

## The Central Thesis

The theory puts forward the concept of "triadic reciprocal determinism," in which behavior, cognitive and personal factors, and the environment interact in a continuous relationship of mutual influence. It also demonstrated that learning can occur through "observation and modeling" (vicarious learning), without the need for direct practice or immediate reinforcement.

## The Central Clinical Concept

The concept of "self-efficacy" (a person's belief in their ability to carry out the behaviors needed to achieve their goals) is a central cognitive variable in this theory and has been found to predict outcomes in many behavior-change interventions. Bandura set out this concept in detail in his 1997 book, showing that its four sources are direct mastery experiences, vicarious learning through observing successful models, verbal persuasion, and the person's physiological and emotional state.

## Principal Works and Experiments

Albert Bandura, together with Dorothea and Sheila Ross, conducted the famous Bobo doll experiment in 1961 at Stanford University. Children observed adults attacking an inflatable doll physically and verbally, and many of them then imitated this aggression even without direct reinforcement, which undermined the radical behaviorist position that learning requires direct experience and immediate reinforcement. Bandura published his full theory in the book *Social Learning Theory* in 1977, then renamed his framework "social cognitive theory" in *Social Foundations of Thought and Action* (1986) to stress the central role of cognitive processes.

## Clinical Applications

The concept of self-efficacy is used in health behavior change programs such as smoking cessation and weight management, where the intervention is built on strengthening small, graduated experiences of success rather than merely providing information. Video modeling has also been used in programs training children with autism in social skills, and in training athletes by having them watch exemplary performance before actual execution.

## Impact and Criticism

This movement moved from being a laboratory learning theory to a theoretical foundation of modern cognitive-behavioral therapy, and of interventions in smoking cessation, chronic pain management and athletic performance. It has been criticized for underweighting biological and unconscious factors in explaining behavior, and for the difficulty of measuring "self-efficacy" independently of the actual performance it is supposed to predict.
