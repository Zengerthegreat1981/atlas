# Radical Behaviorism and Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA)

The philosophy of the science of behavior formulated by B. F. Skinner beginning in the 1930s, which established the experimental analysis of operant conditioning and provided the theoretical framework for Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA).

## Historical Context

Skinner published his foundational experimental monograph, *The Behavior of Organisms*, in 1938, differentiating operant conditioning (behavior emitted and shaped by its consequences) from Pavlovian respondent conditioning (behavior elicited by antecedent stimuli). He later expanded the philosophical and social implications of radical behaviorism in *Science and Human Behavior* (1953) and *Beyond Freedom and Dignity* (1971), rejecting internal mentalist agency in favor of selective environmental determination (selection by consequences across phylogenic, ontogenic, and cultural levels). The applied wing of the discipline was formally established in 1968 with the founding of the *Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis* and the landmark paper by Donald Baer, Montrose Wolf, and Todd Risley defining its core dimensions.

## The Central Thesis

Radical behaviorism differs from methodological behaviorism by refusing to dismiss internal mental phenomena. Instead, Skinner treated thoughts, bodily sensations, and emotional feelings as "private events"—covert physical behaviors occurring within the organism's skin that are subject to the same functional laws of reinforcement, punishment, and stimulus control as overt behaviors. 

Clinical and educational interventions center on the **functional analysis of behavior**, analyzing interactions through the **three-term contingency**:
1. **Antecedent (A)**: The discriminative stimulus setting the occasion for behavior.
2. **Behavior (B)**: The observable or measurable operant response.
3. **Consequence (C)**: The reinforcing or punishing stimulus that alters future probability of the response.

## Applied Extension

Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) operationalized these principles to assess and treat behavioral difficulties, develop communicative repertoires, and design instructional environments. It became a widely researched and established intervention for autism spectrum disorder, developmental disabilities, and classroom management, utilizing systematic techniques such as discrete trial training (DTT), task analysis, differential reinforcement, and prompt fading.

## Criticism and Contemporary Debates

Radical behaviorism and ABA have faced sustained scrutiny on two major fronts:
- **Cognitive and Linguistic Critique**: Initiated famously by Noam Chomsky's 1959 critique of Skinner's *Verbal Behavior*, arguing that associative conditioning cannot adequately account for the generative syntax, rapid acquisition, and creative flexibility of human language.
- **Neurodiversity and Disability Studies**: Autistic self-advocates and contemporary disability scholars have criticized early and intensive ABA practices for their historical reliance on aversive contingencies, excessive compliance training, and the suppression of self-regulatory behaviors (such as stimming) to enforce neurotypical conformity. In response, modern applied behavior analysis emphasizes compassionate care, functional communication training (FCT), trauma-informed practice, and client autonomy.
