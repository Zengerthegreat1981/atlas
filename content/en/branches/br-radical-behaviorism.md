# Radical Behaviorism and Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA)

The comprehensive behaviorist philosophy formulated by B. F. Skinner beginning in the 1930s, which established the approach of operant conditioning and its broad clinical and educational applications in Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA).

## Historical Context

Skinner published his first laboratory reference, *The Behavior of Organisms*, in 1938, establishing an experimental approach based on studying the relationship between behavior and its consequences, independent of any assumptions about internal mental processes. Skinner later expanded his philosophical thesis in *Beyond Freedom and Dignity* (1971), which provoked wide controversy by arguing against the notion of individual free will in favor of a comprehensive environmental behavioral explanation. His followers later developed practical applications; Applied Behavior Analysis was formalized in the late 1960s (Baer, Wolf and Risley, 1968), and its applications extended to special education and the treatment of autism spectrum disorder. Albert Bandura, who was not a Skinner student, took a different, social-cognitive path, including *Principles of Behavior Modification* (1969).

## The Central Thesis

Radical behaviorism is distinguished by regarding behavior as governed by its consequences in the environment, through the mechanisms of positive and negative reinforcement and punishment, and by not denying the existence of internal processes such as thoughts and feelings, but treating them as "private events" that are subject to the same environmental laws that govern behavior observable from outside. Clinical application centers on the functional analysis of behavior in terms of the three-term contingency (antecedent, behavior, consequence; often called the ABC model in practice), and on modifying the environment to gradually shape the desired adaptive behavior.

## Applied Extension

This philosophy developed into a broad applied field known as Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA), which became a widely used and well-researched intervention for autism spectrum disorder and learning difficulties, through techniques such as contingency management and structured behavioral reinforcement.

## Criticism

This current faces growing criticism from the field of disability studies, which considers some historical ABA practices coercive and excessively normalizing of the behavior of autistic people according to external standards that do not take their own needs into account, in addition to a broader philosophical critique from the cognitive revolution, which regarded radical behaviorism's disregard of internal mental processes as an oversimplification of the complexity of human cognition.

## Private Events

The concept of "private events" is among Skinner's most controversial and misunderstood contributions. Skinner did not deny the existence of thoughts and feelings, as many suppose, but insisted that they are real events occurring "within the skin" of the organism and subject to the same principles of operant conditioning that govern overt behavior, although they cannot be directly observed by a third party as overt behavior can.
