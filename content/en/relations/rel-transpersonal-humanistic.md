# Transpersonal and Humanistic Psychology: The Birth of the 'Fourth Force'

Transpersonal psychology emerged in the late 1960s as an extension of humanistic psychology, which was itself called the "third force" after psychoanalysis and behaviorism. Abraham Maslow, one of humanistic psychology's founders, helped to launch it, in an attempt to include experiences and values that go beyond individual self-actualization, such as mystical, peak and spiritual experiences. The later development of the field, including Ken Wilber's integral theory, has complicated the relation and drawn criticism from within transpersonal psychology.

## Starting Point: Maslow's "Fourth Psychology"

- In the preface to the second edition of *Toward a Psychology of Being* (1968), Maslow describes humanistic or "third force" psychology as a transitional step toward a still "higher" **fourth psychology**, which is "transpersonal" and "transhuman," centered in the cosmos rather than in human needs and interests.
- The conventional sequence of "forces": the first is psychoanalysis, the second behaviorism, the third humanistic psychology, the fourth transpersonal psychology. Counting varies in the literature, and the schema is a convenient historical shorthand.

**Philosophical stance:**
- Humanistic: growth and self-actualization of the person.
- Transpersonal: the study of experiences and development that extend beyond the individual ego, including mystical and unitive states.

## Founding (1967-1970)

- **Abraham Maslow** (1908-1970): developed the hierarchy of needs and the concept of peak experiences (*Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences*, 1964). He died in 1970.
- **Anthony Sutich** (1907-1976): a founder of the Association for Humanistic Psychology (1961) and founding editor of the *Journal of Humanistic Psychology* (1961) and the *Journal of Transpersonal Psychology* (1969).
- **Stanislav Grof** (born 1931): Czech-born psychiatrist who did research on LSD psychotherapy in Prague and later at the Maryland Psychiatric Research Center; he is credited with suggesting the term "transpersonal" in discussions with Maslow and Sutich around 1967-68.
- **Charles Tart** and others contributed early work on altered states of consciousness (*Altered States of Consciousness*, 1969) and the anthology *Transpersonal Psychologies* (1975).
- The Association for Transpersonal Psychology was founded in 1972.
- **Esalen Institute** (founded 1962 in California by Michael Murphy and Richard Price) was a major center of the human potential movement in which both humanistic and transpersonal ideas circulated.

## Points of Divergence

| Dimension | Humanistic (Maslow, Rogers) | Transpersonal |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Self-actualization | Self-transcendence; development beyond the personal self |
| The ego | A center of experience deserving support | Often treated as a stage to be transcended, depending on the author |
| Consciousness | Individual conscious experience | States and levels, including non-ordinary states |
| Sources of knowledge | Experience and science | Experience, science and contemplative traditions |
| The sacred | Not central | Central for many authors |
| Death | Existential theme | Often treated within spiritual frameworks |
| Religion | Critical of institutions, open to spirituality | Draws on Buddhism, Vedanta, Sufism, shamanic traditions and others |

## Points of Convergence

1. **Criticism of a reductive, pathology-centered view of persons.**
2. **Focus on growth** and not only on illness.
3. **Value of immediate experience.**
4. **Peak experiences** as a shared topic: Maslow's peak experiences are a conceptual bridge to later work on mystical states.
5. **Resistance to biological and behavioral reductionism.**
6. **Receptivity to Eastern thought** (Zen, Taoism, Vedanta); Maslow himself read widely in these traditions.

## Chain of Influence

### Maslow to Grof and others
- Maslow described peak experiences in the early 1960s.
- Grof's *Realms of the Human Unconscious* (1975) reported on LSD psychotherapy and proposed an expanded cartography of the psyche. After LSD research was curtailed in the 1970s, Grof and his wife Christina developed **holotropic breathwork** at Esalen, a method using accelerated breathing, music and bodywork.

### Maslow to Wilber
- Ken Wilber's *The Spectrum of Consciousness* (1977) was his first book, followed by *The Atman Project* (1980) and *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995), in which he set out the four quadrants of an "integral" theory. His later works (for example *A Theory of Everything*, 2000, and *Integral Psychology*, 2000) formalized the AQAL ("all quadrants, all levels") model.
- Wilber sought a comprehensive framework in which psychoanalysis, behaviorism, humanistic and transpersonal approaches are treated as true but partial, each addressing a different level or quadrant.

## Bridges and Contributions

### Contemplative psychotherapy
- **Chögyam Trungpa** (1939-1987), a Tibetan Buddhist teacher, founded the Naropa Institute in Boulder, Colorado (1974; now Naropa University), where a contemplative psychotherapy program combines Buddhist meditation practice with Western clinical training.

### Holotropic breathwork
- Developed by Stanislav and Christina Grof as an alternative to psychedelic work. Evidence for its effectiveness is limited, and it is practiced outside mainstream clinical settings.

### Buddhism and psychology
- Contemplative teachers such as Thich Nhat Hanh (1926-2022), Jack Kornfield and Tara Brach have popularized meditation in psychological contexts. This is a related current and not a formal school of transpersonal psychology.

## Philosophical Debates

- **Is consciousness the subject matter of psychology?** Humanistic psychology would answer yes with emphasis on the person's experience; transpersonal psychology includes states beyond the usual ego.
- **Should psychology include the spiritual?** Humanistic psychologists differ; many transpersonal authors say yes, while critics argue that spiritual claims are outside the reach of empirical psychology.
- **Is research on experience scientific?** Maslow argued in *The Psychology of Science* (1966) for a broader conception of science that includes experiential knowledge. Critics find that such views undermine standards of evidence.
- **Is Wilber's integral model a solution?** Admirers see a comprehensive map; critics, notably Jorge Ferrer (*Revisioning Transpersonal Theory*, 2002), argue that Wilber's hierarchical and perennialist assumptions are unwarranted and that the field should adopt a participatory approach.

## Clinical Differences Today

- **Humanistic:** person-centered therapy, focusing, emotion-focused therapy, existential-humanistic therapy.
- **Transpersonal:** holotropic breathwork, spiritually informed psychotherapy, work with spiritual emergencies, and contemplative approaches.
- **Mindfulness-based interventions:** mindfulness-based stress reduction (Kabat-Zinn, from 1979) and mindfulness-based cognitive therapy come from Buddhist practice by way of medicine and cognitive therapy. They share a cultural current with transpersonal psychology, but they should not be described as derived from it.
- **Evidence:** mindfulness-based interventions have a large evidence base for several conditions; holotropic breathwork and integral therapy have little controlled research.

## Why the Relation Matters

- For the history of psychology: the sequence of "forces" (psychoanalysis, behaviorism, humanistic, transpersonal) shows how psychology has repeatedly widened its subject matter.
- For the present: spirituality, meaning and meditation are now topics in mainstream clinical research, and the transpersonal tradition is one of several roots.
- The status of transpersonal psychology is still contested: it is debated whether it is a science, a philosophy or a spiritual movement.

## Psychedelic Research: A Resurgence

- Grof and others used LSD in psychotherapy in the 1960s and early 1970s, including with patients who had life-threatening illness. The early studies were limited by their methods, and LSD research effectively ended after drug control legislation in the early 1970s.
- Since the 2000s, controlled trials of psilocybin for distress in patients with life-threatening cancer (for example Griffiths and colleagues, 2016; Ross and colleagues, 2016) reported reductions in anxiety and depression. Research continues, and findings are not conclusive. The Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (founded 1986) has been a major advocate of this research.

## Summary

- Transpersonal psychology extends humanistic psychology and was begun by Maslow and his colleagues, so the relation is mainly internal.
- Wilber reinterpreted the tradition within an integral framework, which has itself been criticized from inside the field.
- The mindfulness movement overlaps with transpersonal themes but has a separate lineage.
- The debate over whether spiritual experience can be studied scientifically continues.
