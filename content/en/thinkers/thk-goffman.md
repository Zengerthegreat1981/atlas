# Erving Goffman

Erving Goffman (1922-1982) was a Canadian-American sociologist who developed a detailed analysis of face-to-face interaction. His dramaturgical approach, studies of stigma, and analysis of total institutions made him one of the most influential microsociologists.

## Life and Context

Goffman was born in Mannville, Alberta, in 1922. He studied at the University of Toronto and took his master's degree and his PhD (1953) at the University of Chicago, with fieldwork on social life in a Shetland Island community. There he was influenced by Everett Hughes and W. Lloyd Warner, and by the reading of Durkheim and Simmel. In the mid-1950s he conducted fieldwork at St. Elizabeths Hospital in Washington, D.C. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, from 1958 and from 1968 at the University of Pennsylvania, and was president of the American Sociological Association in 1981-1982. He died in 1982.

## Main Ideas

- **Dramaturgy and impression management:** social life resembles theatrical performance. People manage the impressions they give, and distinguish front stage from back stage regions. Teams cooperate to sustain a definition of the situation.
- **Face and face-work:** interaction involves mutual protection of each person's claimed social value. Deference and demeanor give the self a ritual standing.
- **Civil inattention:** strangers acknowledge one another briefly and then withdraw attention.
- **Stigma:** a gap between virtual and actual social identity produces discredited and discreditable people who manage information about themselves.
- **Total institutions:** places such as asylums, prisons and monasteries, where inmates live under a single authority, undergo mortification of the self, and develop secondary adjustments.
- **Interaction order:** face-to-face interaction is a domain of social life with its own rules, the theme of the presidential address he prepared in 1982, published posthumously in 1983.
- **Frame analysis:** people organize experience through interpretive frameworks, and activity can be transformed into play, rehearsal or deception.

## Method

Goffman used participant observation, close description, literary and anecdotal sources, and conceptual analogies (theater, games, ritual) rather than formal hypothesis testing. His style was essayistic and often drew on etiquette manuals and memoirs.

## Influence and Criticism

His work shaped symbolic interactionism, ethnomethodology, conversation analysis, and the sociology of deviance, emotion, mental health, disability, gender and the media. Critics have charged that his accounts are unsystematic, that his cynical view of the self emphasizes strategy, and that he neglects history and large-scale structure. He is often located at the margin of interactionism, and he did not found a school.

## Applications and Cross-References

His concepts are applied to workplaces, online self-presentation, and health institutions. Compare Blumer on interactionism, Mead on the self, Durkheim on ritual, Garfinkel on ethnomethodology, labeling theory, and Foucault on institutions of discipline.
