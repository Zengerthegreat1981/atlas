# Giambattista Vico

## Definition

An Italian philosopher and jurist, professor of rhetoric at the University of Naples, regarded as the first to propose a modern systematic speculative account of the course of human history, more than a century before Hegel and Marx.

## Historical Context

Vico led a relatively modest academic life in Naples, far from the great centres of European thought in Paris and London, and under the dominance of Cartesian rationalism and natural law philosophy (Grotius, Pufendorf, Hobbes), which explained human society by abstract rational standards transcending history. His intellectual project was an implicit response to this current. Instead of seeking a fixed natural law outside history, Vico sought to explain the emergence of human institutions (language, religion, law, the family) from within the course of human history itself.

## Main Ideas and Method

- ***The New Science*** *(Scienza Nuova, 1725; revised editions 1730 and 1744)*: In it he set out a new science for studying the "civil world", the world made by humans themselves, as distinct from the natural world, which God alone knows completely. The central methodological principle (*verum-factum*, "the true is what is made") justifies the possibility of certain knowledge of human history, unlike nature.
- **The three cycles of civilisation (*corsi e ricorsi*)**: Every nation passes through three successive stages: the age of the gods (theocratic rule, myth), the age of heroes (military aristocracy), and the age of men (democracy and rationality), before it dissolves and a new cycle begins (*ricorso*), in a cyclical rather than linear model.
- **The method of "philosophical philology"**: Combining historical linguistic analysis (tracing the origins of words and myths) with philosophical reflection to reconstruct the mentality of primitive peoples from their linguistic and mythical traces. This method is regarded as an early precedent for historical anthropology.
- **Critique of rationalist natural law**: Vico opposed Grotius and Pufendorf, who assumed a single rational natural law valid for all times and places. He argued that customs and laws arise historically from changing needs and circumstances, not from an abstract, fixed reason. This makes him an early forerunner of historical cultural relativism.

## Major Works and Representatives

Vico remained relatively neglected during his lifetime and for nearly a century after it. He was rediscovered by historians of ideas in the nineteenth century (Michelet translated his works into French), and then by twentieth-century philosophers (Collingwood, Isaiah Berlin), who regarded him as the founder of a complete tradition in speculative philosophy of history that preceded Hegel and Marx, and as a source of inspiration for the theory of *Verstehen* (understanding) later developed by Dilthey.

## Influence and Criticism

Vico influenced the development of the concept of hermeneutic understanding in the human sciences, and the theory of cyclical history that Spengler and Toynbee later revived in different forms. However, his comprehensive speculative system has been criticised by empirical historians for relying on a predetermined scheme (the three ages) that may impose a forced structure on the diversity of actual history, and for the obscurity of some of his philological arguments by the standards of modern linguistics.

## Major Works

- *The New Science* (*Scienza Nuova*, 1725; final edition 1744).
- *On the Most Ancient Wisdom of the Italians* (*De antiquissima Italorum sapientia*, 1710).
- *Autobiography* (*Vita di Giambattista Vico*, written 1725–1728).

## Supplementary Notes

One of the most intriguing aspects of Vico's thought is his conception of myth and poetry as a legitimate form of knowledge characteristic of the "age of the gods", not merely superstitions to be overcome by reason, as the French Enlightenment philosophers of his time held. Vico argued that primitive humans thought by a "poetic logic" based on sensory imagination rather than conceptual abstraction, and that the study of ancient myths and poetry (Homer in particular) reveals a real collective mentality rather than the individual inventions of a single genius poet. This thesis anticipated modern theories of collective folklore by a century and a half. This positive appreciation of mythical knowledge as a necessary and legitimate stage in the development of human consciousness, rather than a deviation from reason, distinguished Vico radically from the rationalism of his age. It later made him a reference point for German Romantic thinkers (Herder) interested in the distinctive spirit of each nation's people.

It should also be noted that Vico wrote in the local Neapolitan Italian as well as in Latin. This linguistic choice, unusual for his time, made his ideas less widely known among the French-speaking or Latin-reading European elite of his day, which may partly explain his relative neglect until he was rediscovered by readers outside Italy.

Vico remains a constant subject in the history of the philosophy of language today, since he anticipated modern theories of the origin of language from gesture and natural sound before conceptual abstraction. This has led some contemporary historical linguists to return to *The New Science* in search of early seeds of their own ideas on the origin of human language and its gradual development through the successive stages of civilisation.
