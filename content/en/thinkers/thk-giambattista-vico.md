# Giambattista Vico

Giambattista Vico (1668–1744) was an Italian philosopher, rhetorician, and jurist who held the chair of rhetoric at the University of Naples from 1699 to 1741. In his masterpiece, *The New Science* (*Scienza Nuova*), he formulated the earliest comprehensive philosophy of history, anticipating the historicist, anthropological, and hermeneutic revolutions of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

## Historical and Intellectual Context

Vico worked on the intellectual periphery of early modern Europe, in Spanish-ruled and later Austrian Naples. While the dominant intellectual currents of his era were Cartesian mathematical rationalism and natural law jurisprudence, Vico challenged both. He argued that René Descartes's deductive rationalism was blind to the historical nature of human consciousness, and that abstract mathematical certainty could never serve as a valid foundation for the moral and human sciences (*studia humanitatis*).

## The *Verum-Factum* Principle and the New Science

In *De antiquissima Italorum sapientia* (1710) and *The New Science* (first published in 1725, with major revisions in 1730 and 1744), Vico set forth his foundational epistemological axiom: ***verum et factum convertuntur*** ("the true and the made are convertible"). According to Vico, humans cannot attain complete, certain knowledge of the natural physical world because God alone created nature. However, humans can achieve genuine scientific knowledge of the "civil world" (*mondo civile*)—human culture, language, laws, customs, and institutions—precisely because human beings made it themselves.

## The Three Ages and Historical Recurrence (*Corsi e Ricorsi*)

Vico rejected linear models of static progress, proposing instead an organic, cyclical theory in which all nations traverse three developmental stages:

1. **The Age of the Gods (*l'età degli dei*)**: Governed by sensory imagination, terror of natural forces, and religion. Primitive humans interpreted the world through thunder and signs, giving rise to divine government (theocracy), sacred institutions, and patriarchs.
2. **The Age of the Heroes (*l'età degli eroi*)**: Dominated by martial aristocracies (exemplified by Homeric Greece), in which heroic nobles asserted dominance over plebeians, and social virtues were centered on physical valor, honor, and customary law.
3. **The Age of Men (*l'età degli uomini*)**: Characterized by fully developed reason, egalitarian jurisprudence, civil equality, and democratic or monarchical governance.

When civil societies in the Age of Men succumb to rationalistic corruption, hyper-individualism, and moral skepticism (what Vico termed the "barbarism of reflection"), they dissolve from within and collapse into a renewed cycle (*ricorso*), returning to primitive origins to begin the civilizing process anew.

## Philosophical Philology and Critique of Natural Law

Vico revolutionized historical method by uniting **philosophy** (the search for universal truths) with **philology** (the critical study of languages, myths, and historical documents). In doing so, he mounted a systematic critique of contemporary natural law jurists—principally Hugo Grotius, Samuel von Pufendorf, and Thomas Hobbes. Vico charged these thinkers with the "conceit of scholars" (*boria dei dotti*), the fallacy of anachronistically projecting the modern, fully rational, and self-interested individual back into primordial human origins. Vico argued that law, property, and the state did not arise from an abstract rational contract, but emerged gradually out of archaic poetic metaphors, religious taboos, and burial rituals.

In his analysis of Homer, Vico argued that "Homer" was not an individual historic poet creating conscious allegories, but an embodiment of the collective memory and poetic wisdom of the ancient Greek people.

## Historiographical Legacy

Largely ignored by contemporary Enlightenment philosophes who favored Cartesian or Newtonian paradigms, Vico was rediscovered in the nineteenth century. Jules Michelet translated and popularized his works in France, while German Romantics (Johann Gottfried von Herder) and historicists drew on his concepts of cultural individuality. In the twentieth century, philosophers such as R. G. Collingwood and Isaiah Berlin helped revive interest in Vico, and he is often seen as an early forerunner of historical understanding (*Verstehen*).

## Major Works

- *On the Most Ancient Wisdom of the Italians* (*De antiquissima Italorum sapientia*, 1710).
- *The New Science* (*Principj di scienza nuova*, 1725; second edition 1730; definitive third edition 1744).
- *Autobiography* (*Vita di Giambattista Vico scritta da se medesimo*, 1725–1728; published 1731).
