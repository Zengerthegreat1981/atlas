# Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Mawardi

A Shafi'i jurist, judge and diplomat from the scholars of Baghdad in the fifth century AH, regarded as a leading early author in the field of **al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyya** (Sultanic ordinances) as an independent branch of jurisprudence concerned with the theory of governance, the imamate, and the public institutions of the Islamic state, and his best-known book is often compared to a theory of **public law** in the modern sense.

## Biography and Context

Al-Mawardi was born in Basra, where he studied Shafi'i jurisprudence under its senior scholars before moving to Baghdad, where he came into contact with the Abbasid caliphate. He held the judgeship in several cities, then attained a high standing at the court of the Abbasid caliph al-Qa'im bi-Amr Allah, and was entrusted with delicate diplomatic missions, including mediating between the caliph and the Buyid and Seljuk amirs during a period when the Abbasid caliphate suffered real political and military weakness against the growing influence of army commanders and governors. He was given the title "the most judicious of judges" (*aqda al-qudat*), and lived these political tensions at close quarters, which partly explains his deep practical interest in the theory of legitimizing authority and distributing its powers. He died in Baghdad in 450 AH / 1058 CE.

## Central Ideas

### The Sultanic Ordinances: Founding the Theory of the Imamate and Public Institutions

In his pivotal book *al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyya wa al-Wilayat al-Diniyya* (The Sultanic Ordinances and Religious Authorities), al-Mawardi offered an early and influential systematic jurisprudential treatment of the theory of the **contract of the imamate**: the conditions of the imam (such as justice, knowledge and capacity), the manner in which the imamate is established (by selection by the people of binding and loosing, or by appointment by a preceding imam), and the causes of its termination. He also elaborated the theory of **delegation of authority**, distinguishing between the "ministry of delegation" (in which the vizier is empowered with broad, almost executive powers) and the "ministry of execution" (in which the vizier's role is limited to carrying out the imam's directives without independence in decision-making). He discussed the system of regional governorships, the judiciary, *hisba* and land tax (*kharaj*), in an exposition approaching a comprehensive theory of the structure and public functions of the state.

### Political Realism Within the Legal Framework

The book shows a realist tendency on al-Mawardi's part to adapt the ideal theoretical conditions of the imamate to the reality of the Abbasid caliphate's actual weakness in his time. For example, he permitted legitimizing the authority of amirs who seize power by force, provided they nominally acknowledged the caliph's allegiance and abided by the rulings of the Sharia. This reveals an attempt to offer a flexible legal framework that preserves the symbolic unity of the community even when the actual authority of the caliph has dissipated, rather than a description of an ideal realized reality.

### Literature of the Judge and Literature of This World and Religion

Alongside the *Sultanic Ordinances*, al-Mawardi wrote on the etiquette of judging (*Adab al-Qadi*), detailing the duties and conduct of the judge and the rules for managing litigation. In his ethical work *Adab al-Dunya wa al-Din* (The Etiquette of This World and Religion), he examined the relationship between individual ethics and the general social order. These writings complement his project in the *Sultanic Ordinances* as a comprehensive theory of good governance, not a merely formal distribution of powers.

### His General Standing in Jurisprudence: The Great Hawi

Alongside his contribution to political jurisprudence, al-Mawardi was a major Shafi'i jurist in detailed jurisprudence, and authored the vast jurisprudential encyclopedia *al-Hawi al-Kabir* (The Great Compendium), a commentary on the *Mukhtasar* of al-Muzani, which is among the broadest comparative encyclopedias of Shafi'i jurisprudence, treating the internal disagreements of the school and its disagreements with other schools in precise detail. This reveals that his interest in the theory of governance and the state was not separate from a deep and detailed jurisprudential command of all the traditional areas of jurisprudence.

## Impact and Criticism

The *Sultanic Ordinances* is a foundational text in the history of Islamic political thought, and it remained a fundamental reference that everyone who wrote on the theory of the caliphate subsequently invoked, including Abd al-Razzaq al-Sanhuri in his Parisian dissertation on the caliphate in the twentieth century, when he analyzed the outcomes of this theory after the fall of the Ottoman caliphate. Contemporary scholars (among them Patricia Crone in her studies of Islamic political thought) have observed that al-Mawardi's political realism, specifically his justification of legitimizing seizure of power by force, shows that his book is closer to an attempt at a legal adaptation to a declining political reality than to a purely normative theory, although this reading is not shared by all researchers to the same degree.

## A Work of the Same Title: Abu Ya'la al-Farra'

A book with the same title was written by the Hanbali jurist Abu Ya'la al-Farra' (d. 458 AH / 1066 CE). The two works are strikingly similar in structure and content, and scholars have discussed their relationship.

## His Language and Method of Exposition

Al-Mawardi's style in the *Sultanic Ordinances* is an organized juristic language close to the writing style of the administrative chanceries of his time. He presents each chapter on the institutions of the state (such as the vizierate, the emirate, the judiciary and *hisba*) by stating its definition, its divisions, and the conditions of those who hold it, in a coherent methodological structure that made the book a practical rather than purely theoretical reference, used in practice by chancery officials and judges in his time and after.
