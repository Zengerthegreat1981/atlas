# Moojan Momen

A British historian and scholar, a historian of the Bábí and Bahá'í religions, who has written on their origins and development in a scholarly rather than purely devotional manner. His intellectual position combines a notable duality: he is a scholar who personally belongs to the Bahá'í community, but in his scholarly writing he adheres to the standards of critical academic history, based on the cross-checking of independent testimonies, rather than a doctrinal defence of the community's account of itself. This position resembles in many respects that of believing scholars who write to rigorous academic standards about their own religious traditions in other fields of religious studies.

## Combining Linguistic Competence with Archival Access

Momen drew on his command of Persian and Arabic and on his access to a range of documents and sources: official Bahá'í sources, Shi'i sources contemporary with the events, and early Western sources such as the writings of the British orientalist Edward Granville Browne, who visited Iran in 1887-1888 and wrote about the Bábí movement. On this basis he constructed a historical periodisation of the founding events: the Báb's declaration of his mission in 1844, his execution in Tabriz in 1850, the divergence between Bahá'u'lláh and his half-brother Subh-i-Azal over leadership of the movement (the two were exiled to Baghdad, then Istanbul and Edirne, after which Bahá'u'lláh was sent to Akka), and the later establishment of the Bahá'í administrative order. Momen compares these conflicting accounts with one another rather than adopting a single official or hostile version exclusively.

## The Bábí and Bahá'í Religions, 1844–1944

His best-known historical work is a documentary survey published in 1981, which gathers contemporary Western accounts (diplomatic, missionary and orientalist) of the origin and development of the movement over its first century, with editorial commentary. It is a widely used starting point for study of the movement.

## Later and Encyclopaedic Works

Alongside his foundational historical work, Momen produced broader reference works such as *A Short Introduction to the Bahá'í Faith* (1997) and *The Phenomenon of Religion: A Thematic Approach* (1999). His *An Introduction to Shi'i Islam* (1985) is a standard English-language survey of Twelver Shi'ism, the background from which the Bábí movement arose.

## Edited Volumes on Bábí and Bahá'í History

Momen also contributed as an editor to the series published by Kalimát Press in Los Angeles, begun under the title *Studies in Bábí and Bahá'í History*. He edited its first volume (1982), a collection of research by several authors that helped give the newer academic study of these movements a regular outlet. He later edited *Studies in Honor of the Late Hasan M. Balyuzi* (1988), the fifth volume, issued under the series title *Studies in the Bábí and Bahá'í Religions*, in tribute to a Bahá'í historian. Such collective volumes complement his own documentary work by bringing specialized studies of particular events, communities and texts into print.

## Distinctive Method

Momen consistently distinguishes between what the official Bahá'í sources say about themselves and what the independent documents contemporary with the events reveal. This allows a critical historical examination that avoids both the trap of the internal promotional narrative and the trap of the hostile polemical rejection that dominated some earlier Shi'i and orientalist writings on the Bábí movement. This methodological distinction is what gives his work an academic credibility that goes beyond his personal denominational position.

## Professional and academic career

Momen's own curriculum vitae, hosted by the Wilmette Institute, sets the scholarly work inside a medical career. Born in Iran in 1950, he moved to England in 1955 after his mother was widowed, attended schools in Bournemouth and Taunton, studied natural and medical sciences at Cambridge, graduated in 1971, finished clinical training at Guy's Hospital, and qualified in 1974. After five years of hospital medicine he returned to Cambridge for the Arabic and Islamic course. From then on he combined medical practice with research on the Middle East, Islam, Iranian history, religion and the Bahá'í Faith, and he also published on genocide and on social and economic development.

The same CV records postgraduate lectures at Cambridge, Oxford, the School of Oriental and African Studies and Hebrew University, a 1996 lecture tour in China, and regular papers for British associations in the study of religion and of the Middle East. He is a Fellow of the Royal Asiatic Society. Listed encyclopedia work includes *Encyclopaedia Iranica* and *The Oxford Encyclopedia of the Modern Islamic World*. Articles include "The Social Basis of the Babi Upheavals in Iran" (*International Journal of Middle East Studies*, 1983) and, with Juan R. Cole, "Mafia, Mob and Shi'ism" (*Past and Present*, 1986). The CV also notes voluntary research at the Bahá'í World Centre in Haifa.

## Other Scholars in the Field

Momen's position as a believing scholar raises the question of how belonging and academic objectivity can be combined. His approach differs from that of scholars such as Denis MacEoin, who studied the Bábí movement from a more externally critical position.

## The Bahá'í Administrative System

In his general works Momen describes the development of the Bahá'í administrative system after the death of Bahá'u'lláh in 1892, from 'Abdu'l-Bahá to Shoghi Effendi and then the Universal House of Justice (first elected in 1963), noting that authority passed through written testamentary documents. Scholars often contrast this with movements that fragmented over succession.
