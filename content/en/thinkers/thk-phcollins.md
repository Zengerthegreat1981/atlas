# Patricia Hill Collins

Patricia Hill Collins (born 1948) is an American sociologist and social theorist whose work on Black feminist thought and intersectionality has had a major influence on sociology, gender studies and race studies.

## Life and Context

Collins earned her doctorate in sociology at Brandeis University and taught at the University of Cincinnati, where she was a professor of African American studies, before moving to the University of Maryland, College Park. In 2009 she became the first Black woman to serve as president of the American Sociological Association. Her work grows out of the traditions of Black women intellectuals and activists and engages with American sociology and feminist theory.

## Main Ideas

In *Black Feminist Thought*, Collins argued that Black women have developed a distinctive standpoint shaped by their location at intersections of race, class and gender, and that this knowledge has often been excluded from mainstream scholarship. She described the outsider-within position, in which Black women see social life from vantage points that reveal contradictions in dominant ideologies. She analyzed controlling images, such as stereotypes of the mammy or the welfare queen, that justify oppression. She proposed the matrix of domination to describe how systems of power are organized across structural, disciplinary, hegemonic and interpersonal domains. Her later work on intersectionality treats it as both an analytic tool and a form of critical social theory, linked to praxis and social justice.

## Major Works

- *Black Feminist Thought: Knowledge, Consciousness, and the Politics of Empowerment* (1990; revised edition 2000)
- *Fighting Words: Black Women and the Search for Justice* (1998)
- *Black Sexual Politics* (2004)
- *From Black Power to Hip Hop* (2006)
- *Intersectionality* (2016, with Sirma Bilge)
- *Intersectionality as Critical Social Theory* (2019)

## Method

Collins draws on interpretive and historical sources, including literature, music and the writings of Black women, together with sociological analysis, and she argues for taking lived experience seriously as a source of knowledge.

## Influence and Criticism

Her work, alongside that of Kimberle Crenshaw, helped establish intersectionality as a widely used framework across the social sciences. Critics have argued that standpoint theory risks essentializing group experience, and that intersectionality can be conceptually ambiguous or difficult to operationalize in empirical research.
