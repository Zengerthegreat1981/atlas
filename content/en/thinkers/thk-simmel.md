# Georg Simmel

Georg Simmel (1858-1918) was a German sociologist and philosopher who helped establish sociology as a discipline by studying the forms of social interaction, from the stranger and the metropolis to money and fashion.

## Life and Context

Simmel was born in Berlin and studied history, philosophy and psychology at the University of Berlin. He taught there for many years as a Privatdozent and then an extraordinary professor, a position without full academic standing, and was only given a regular chair at Strasbourg in 1914. He lectured to wide audiences and wrote in newspapers and journals. He was a founding member of the German Society for Sociology in 1909 along with Weber and Toennies.

## Main Ideas

- **Forms of sociation.** Simmel held that sociology should study the recurring forms of interaction, such as conflict, exchange, domination, cooperation and secrecy, apart from their varied content. He called this "formal sociology".
- **Quantity and group size.** In his analysis of the dyad and the triad, he showed how adding a third person changes social relations.
- **The stranger.** In his essay on the stranger, he described a person who is both near and far, within the group yet not of it.
- **The metropolis.** In "The Metropolis and Mental Life" (1903), he argued that urban life, with its stimuli, anonymity and money economy, fosters intellectualism, reserve and individual freedom.
- **Money and modernity.** *The Philosophy of Money* (1900) discussed how a money economy shapes abstraction, calculation and personal freedom, along with new forms of alienation.
- **Conflict.** He treated conflict as a form of sociation that can unite as well as divide.

## Major Works

- *On Social Differentiation* (1890)
- *The Philosophy of Money* (1900)
- "The Metropolis and Mental Life" (1903)
- *Sociology* (*Soziologie*, 1908)
- *Fundamental Questions of Sociology* (1917)

## Method

Simmel wrote in an essayistic style, drawing on small observations to reveal general patterns, and sought to find the general in the particular.

## Influence and Criticism

Simmel strongly influenced the Chicago School, notably Robert Park, who had attended his lectures, as well as symbolic interactionism, network analysis and the sociology of culture. Critics have found his work unsystematic and fragmentary, and some have faulted the lack of a clear research method. His reputation has grown in the study of modernity and urban life.
