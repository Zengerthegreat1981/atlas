# Steven C. Hayes

American psychologist (born 1948), founder of **Acceptance and Commitment Therapy** (ACT) in the 1980s, and developer of **Relational Frame Theory** (RFT), the theoretical foundation of ACT. A university professor and prolific author (dozens of books and hundreds of articles).

## What he contributed

### 1. Relational Frame Theory (RFT)

Hayes developed RFT as a **theory of human language and cognition**:
- **"Relational framing"**: the human ability to relate anything to anything (for example: "the cloud is like a blanket").
- This is what distinguishes language from animal behavior.
- **Relational learning** explains how we learn words.
- RFT forms the foundation of ACT.

### 2. ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, 1980s–)

Hayes developed ACT as a "third-generation" treatment within cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT):
- **Philosophy**: human suffering comes not from "negative thoughts" but from the **attempt to get rid of** them.
- **Goal**: **psychological flexibility** — the ability to act in line with one's values even in the presence of difficult feelings.
- **Six core processes (Hexaflex)**:
  1. **Acceptance**: accepting feelings as they are.
  2. **Cognitive defusion**: seeing thoughts as thoughts, not as "truth."
  3. **Present moment awareness**: living in the present.
  4. **Self-as-context**: the "I" is larger than my thoughts.
  5. **Values**: what truly matters to the individual.
  6. **Committed action**: acting in line with one's values.

### 3. His relation to traditional CBT

- **Traditional CBT**: "Change your negative thoughts."
- **ACT**: "Accept your negative thoughts, and act in line with your values."
- ACT is not "opposed" to CBT but **complementary** to it, within the "third wave" of behavioral therapies (Hayes, Linehan, and others).

### 4. Its wide applications

- **ACT** for **anxiety, depression, PTSD, OCD, eating disorders, addiction**.
- **ACT in medicine**: for chronic illness and chronic pain.
- **ACT in organizations**: leadership and performance.
- **ACT in education**: child rearing.
- **The ACT Matrix**: a simplified practitioner tool developed by Kevin Polk and colleagues.

## Personal background

Hayes has said that a first panic attack in 1978 and his subsequent struggle with panic shaped the thinking that led to ACT; the details of that experience are known mainly from his own accounts.

## His place in the movement

- **Versus CBT**: a "third wave" approach that accepts thoughts rather than "changing" them.
- **Versus MBSR/MBCT**: ACT differs in its focus on "values" and "action," not only "awareness."
- **Versus Freud**: not the "unconscious," but the "influence of language."
- **Versus Buddhist psychology**: ACT was influenced by it, but is more "scientific" (evidence-based).

## His major works

- **"Acceptance and Commitment Therapy" (1999)** — with Strosahl & Wilson.
- **"Relational Frame Theory: A Post-Skinnerian Account of Human Language and Cognition" (2001)** — with Barnes-Holmes & Roche.
- **"Get Out of Your Mind and Into Your Life" (2005)** — with Spencer Smith.
- **"ACT Made Simple" (2009, 2019)** — with Russ Harris (an author independent of Hayes, not a co-authorship with him).
- **"A Liberated Mind" (2019)** — a popular book.

## Research

- **Hayes et al. (1999)**: "Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: An Experiential Approach to Behavior Change."
- **Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda & Lillis (2006)**: "Acceptance and commitment therapy: model, processes and outcomes," *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 44(1), 1–25.
- **A-Tjak et al. (2015)**: a meta-analysis of ACT, with moderate effectiveness.
- **Powers, Zum Vörde Sive Vörding & Emmelkamp (2009)**: a meta-analytic review of ACT across conditions.

## Criticisms

- **RFT**: a complex theory that may not be necessary for understanding ACT.
- **"Acceptance"** as a strategy: it may seem "resigned" in cases of abuse.
- **The popular movement**: ACT has "become commercial," with a large number of "certified" therapists.
- **The research–clinical gap**: many studies are small.

## Significance for the atlas

- One of the most influential therapies of the twenty-first century.
- It combines behavioral psychology, Buddhist philosophy, and a theory of language.
- A framework for integrating therapy and spiritual practice.
