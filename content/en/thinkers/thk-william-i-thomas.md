# William I. Thomas

William Isaac Thomas (1863-1947) was an American sociologist of the early Chicago school. He is remembered for the concept of the definition of the situation and for pioneering empirical research on immigration using personal documents.

## Life and Context

Thomas was born in Virginia and trained in languages and literature before moving into sociology. He joined the University of Chicago in the 1890s and became a leading figure in its sociology department. His career at Chicago ended in 1918 following a scandal and his dismissal. He later worked at the New School for Social Research in New York and was president of the American Sociological Association in 1927.

## Main Ideas

Thomas argued that human action cannot be explained by objective conditions alone but depends on how people subjectively interpret their circumstances, which he called the definition of the situation. This idea is condensed in the Thomas theorem, formulated with Dorothy Swaine Thomas in *The Child in America* (1928): if people define situations as real, they are real in their consequences. With Florian Znaniecki, he distinguished between social attitudes and values, and analyzed how migration disrupts traditional community controls and leads to social disorganization and reorganization. In *The Unadjusted Girl*, he examined wishes, such as security, recognition, response and new experience, as motives behind behavior viewed as deviant.

## Major Works

- *Sex and Society* (1907)
- *Source Book for Social Origins* (1909)
- *The Polish Peasant in Europe and America* (1918-1920, with Florian Znaniecki)
- *The Unadjusted Girl* (1923)
- *The Child in America* (1928, with Dorothy Swaine Thomas)

## Method

*The Polish Peasant* is a landmark in the use of life histories and documents, including letters exchanged between Polish immigrants and their families, and a long life-history manuscript written by a young Polish immigrant, Wladek Wisniewski, as primary sociological data. Thomas advocated combining such materials with attention to both objective social conditions and subjective attitudes.

## Influence and Criticism

His work shaped symbolic interactionism, the sociology of migration and later labeling theory, and the Thomas theorem became a standard formulation of the self-fulfilling nature of social perception. Herbert Blumer's 1939 critique of *The Polish Peasant* questioned the representativeness of the documents and the reliability of their interpretation and the testing of theory.

## Applications and Cross-References

Robert K. Merton generalised the theorem into the self-fulfilling prophecy (1948), which is widely applied to bank runs, labelling in schools and reputation in policing. The framework is closely tied to Znaniecki's work, to Herbert Blumer's symbolic interactionism and to the study of social disorganization at Chicago.
