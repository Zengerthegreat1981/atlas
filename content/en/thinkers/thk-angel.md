# Ernest Angel

Ernest Angel (born Ernst Angel; 1894–1986) was an Austrian-American writer, psychoanalyst, and psychotherapist who played a pivotal role in the introduction of European existential psychiatry and phenomenological psychology to the English-speaking world. He is best known as co-editor, alongside Rollo May and Henri F. Ellenberger, of the landmark volume *Existence: A New Dimension in Psychiatry and Psychology* (1958), widely regarded as the foundational text of American existential psychotherapy.

## Life and Historical Context

Angel was born in Vienna on August 11, 1894. He studied at the University of Vienna, served in the Austro-Hungarian Army during World War I, and became an active figure in Viennese and Weimar cultural circles during the interwar years as an Expressionist poet, playwright, publisher, and film director. Following the Nazi annexation of Austria (the Anschluss) in 1938, Angel fled persecution, emigrating via France and Great Britain to the United States in 1939.

Settling in New York, Angel transitioned into psychology and psychoanalysis. He underwent analytic training and established a private clinical practice, joining Theodor Reik's National Psychological Association for Psychoanalysis (NPAP), founded in 1948 to provide non-medical psychoanalytic training. Angel became a senior training analyst and served for many years as vice president of the NPAP, contributing actively to the clinical training of American non-medical psychotherapists during an era when the psychiatric establishment sought to restrict psychoanalytic practice to physicians.

## Contributions to Existential Psychotherapy

In the 1950s, American clinical psychology and psychiatry were divided between radical behaviorism and orthodox Freudian drive theory. Simultaneously, European psychiatrists—such as Ludwig Binswanger, Eugène Minkowski, Erwin Straus, and Roland Kuhn—had developed phenomenological and existential frameworks (notably *Daseinsanalyse*), yet their seminal writings remained virtually inaccessible in English.

Angel collaborated with Rollo May and medical historian Henri F. Ellenberger to bridge this divide. In *Existence* (Basic Books, 1958), Angel provided editorial leadership and essential translations of complex German psychiatric texts. Specifically, Angel translated Ludwig Binswanger's clinical monograph "Insanity as Life-Historical Phenomenon and as Mental Disease: The Case of Ilse" and co-translated (with May) Roland Kuhn's "The Attempted Murder of a Prostitute." These translations introduced American clinicians to detailed case analyses demonstrating how severe psychopathology could be understood as a structured distortion of being-in-the-world (*Dasein*) rather than merely an instinctual or biological defect.

## Influence and Legacy

*Existence* catalyzed the development of humanistic and existential psychology in the United States, directly shaping the clinical thinking of figures such as James Bugental and Irvin Yalom. While Rollo May became the public voice of American existential therapy through his theoretical introductions, Angel's European clinical background, bilingual scholarship, and institutional standing within the NPAP were critical in making existential analysis clinically credible to American practitioners. Angel died in New Jersey on January 10, 1986.
