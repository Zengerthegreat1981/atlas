# Ernest Angel

Ernest Angel (also written Ernst Angel) is known in the history of psychotherapy for one specific role: co-editor, with Rollo May and Henri Ellenberger, of *Existence: A New Dimension in Psychiatry and Psychology* (1958), the book widely regarded as the starting point of the American existential-humanistic movement. Little else about him is firmly documented, so this entry limits itself to that role.

## Life and Context

Biographical information is scarce. He is described as an Austrian-born figure who worked in the United States, and his dates are not established. It is also unresolved whether he is the same person as the Austrian writer of the same name active in Vienna and Berlin before emigration, or a different person with a similar name. No claim is made here about his training, profession or clinical affiliation. In particular, there is no secure basis for calling him a psychoanalyst or a psychotherapist.

The context in which he appears is clear. In the 1950s American psychiatry and psychology were dominated by psychoanalysis and behaviorism, and the European existential and phenomenological work of figures such as Ludwig Binswanger, Eugène Minkowski and Erwin Straus was little known in English. A small group of editors set out to change that.

## Main Ideas

Angel is not associated with an independent theoretical position that can be documented. His significance lies in editorial and translational work. *Existence* (Basic Books, 1958) brought together two introductory essays by Rollo May with translated papers from European authors, including Binswanger's case study "The Case of Ellen West," along with contributions by Minkowski, Straus, Victor von Gebsattel and Roland Kuhn. The book introduced English-speaking clinicians to the idea that mental suffering can be understood through a person's way of being in the world, rather than only through drives or behavior. Angel is credited as one of the three editors; his specific contributions to the volume are not established here.

## Major Works

- *Existence: A New Dimension in Psychiatry and Psychology* (1958), edited with Rollo May and Henri F. Ellenberger. No independent book or article by Angel is documented in this entry.

## Influence and Criticism

*Existence* is often cited as a founding document of American existential psychotherapy and helped prepare the ground for later work by May, Yalom, Bugental and others. Angel's share in that influence is that of one of three editors; the framing and introductory essays are associated mainly with May, and the historical and phenomenological scholarship with Ellenberger. Historians of the field consequently credit May and Ellenberger far more than Angel.

The main limitation is evidentiary. There is no verifiable study, translation or article under his name alone, and standard histories of existential psychology mention him only as one of the editors. Anyone wanting more should consult the front matter of the 1958 Basic Books edition and archival sources, rather than rely on secondary claims.
