# Ernest Angel

Ernest Angel (born Ernst Angel; 1894–1986) was an Austrian-American writer, psychoanalyst, and psychotherapist who contributed to the introduction of European existential psychiatry and phenomenological psychology to the English-speaking world. He is best known as co-editor, alongside Rollo May and Henri F. Ellenberger, of the landmark volume *Existence: A New Dimension in Psychiatry and Psychology* (1958), widely regarded as the foundational text of American existential psychotherapy.

## Life and Historical Context

Angel was born in Vienna in 1894. He was active in German-language literary and publishing circles in the interwar years. After the Nazi annexation of Austria (the Anschluss) in 1938, he moved to Berlin that September, was arrested during the November pogrom and held at Sachsenhausen until December, emigrated to England in 1939, and reached the United States in early 1940.

Settling in New York, Angel transitioned into psychology and psychoanalysis. He underwent analytic training and established a private clinical practice, joining Theodor Reik's National Psychological Association for Psychoanalysis (NPAP), founded in 1948 to provide non-medical psychoanalytic training. Angel held a senior role at the NPAP, contributing to the clinical training of American non-medical psychotherapists during an era when the psychiatric establishment sought to restrict psychoanalytic practice to physicians.

## Contributions to Existential Psychotherapy

In the 1950s, American clinical psychology and psychiatry were divided between radical behaviorism and orthodox Freudian drive theory. Simultaneously, European psychiatrists—such as Ludwig Binswanger, Eugène Minkowski, Erwin Straus, and Roland Kuhn—had developed phenomenological and existential frameworks (notably *Daseinsanalyse*), yet their seminal writings remained virtually inaccessible in English.

Angel collaborated with Rollo May and medical historian Henri F. Ellenberger to bridge this divide. In *Existence* (Basic Books, 1958), Angel took part in the editing of the volume, which made translations of German-language psychiatric texts by Binswanger, Kuhn, Minkowski, Straus and others available in English, including detailed case analyses demonstrating how severe psychopathology could be understood as a structured distortion of being-in-the-world (*Dasein*) rather than merely an instinctual or biological defect.

## Influence and Legacy

*Existence* was an important early source for humanistic and existential psychology in the United States. While Rollo May became the public voice of American existential therapy through his theoretical introductions, Angel's bilingual scholarship helped make European existential analysis accessible to American practitioners. Angel died in 1986.
