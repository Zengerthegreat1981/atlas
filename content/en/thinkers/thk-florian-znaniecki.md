# Florian Znaniecki

Florian Witold Znaniecki (1882-1958) was a Polish philosopher and sociologist who worked in Poland and the United States. He is best known for *The Polish Peasant in Europe and America*, written with W. I. Thomas, and for a humanistic approach to sociological method.

## Life and Context

Znaniecki was born in 1882 in the Prussian-ruled part of Poland. He studied philosophy in Warsaw, Switzerland and Paris and received a doctorate in Kraków in 1910. Work with an organization assisting emigrants brought him into contact with Thomas, who was studying Polish migration, and in 1914 he moved to Chicago to collaborate with him. He returned to the newly independent Poland in 1920, took a chair at the University of Poznań, founded a sociological institute there and launched the journal *Przegląd Socjologiczny*. He spent 1931-1933 at Columbia University. The outbreak of the Second World War found him again outside Poland, and he settled at the University of Illinois. He served as president of the American Sociological Society in 1954 and died in 1958.

## Main Ideas

- **Attitudes and values:** with Thomas, he argued that social change must be explained through the interplay of objective social values and the subjective attitudes of individuals.
- **Social disorganization and reorganization:** established rules lose their hold on members of a group under rapid change, and new forms of cooperation may then be built.
- **Humanistic coefficient:** cultural data are always data of someone's experience and activity, so the researcher must consider what they mean to the participants.
- **Culture as the subject of sociology:** he distinguished the cultural sciences from the natural sciences and analyzed social actions, relations, roles and groups.
- **Social roles of knowledge:** he examined the social positions occupied by people who specialize in knowledge, such as technologists, sages and scholars.

## Case Study

*The Polish Peasant* used personal letters, newspaper material, records of institutions and a long autobiography written by a Polish migrant, Władek Wiszniewski, to follow the effects of migration and modernization on family and community in Poland and in the United States.

## Method

Znaniecki favored the analysis of personal documents. In *The Method of Sociology* he formulated analytic induction, which develops general statements by intensive study of cases and revises them when a case does not fit.

## Influence and Criticism

The collaboration with Thomas was a landmark for Chicago sociology and for life-history research, and Znaniecki shaped the development of sociology in Poland. Herbert Blumer's 1939 appraisal of *The Polish Peasant* argued that its theoretical claims could not be adequately tested by the documents presented. Analytic induction has also been criticized for identifying conditions that accompany a phenomenon without showing when it will occur.

## Cross-References

Compare W. I. Thomas on the definition of the situation, Weber on interpretive understanding, and Blumer on interactionist method.
