# Richard A. Posner

An American judge and jurist (born 1939), who served on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit from 1981 until 2017, and the most prominent theorist of the **economic analysis of law** after **Ronald Coase**.

**Note**: He is not related to Michael I. Posner, the cognitive neuroscientist whose entry is in the psychology section of the atlas; the two share only a surname.

## "Economic Analysis of Law" (1973): Efficiency as a Comprehensive Standard

In his landmark book *Economic Analysis of Law* (first published in 1973; the ninth edition appeared in 2014), Posner extends Coase's analytical method to cover almost all branches of American law, including contracts, torts, property, procedure, and even criminal and family law. He proposes a unified analytical framework: **most rules of common law can be explained (and evaluated) as attempts, conscious or unconscious, to achieve economic efficiency** (the maximization of the total value of resources in society).

## The Thesis of "Evolutionary Efficiency" in the Common Law

Posner advances a descriptive thesis that has provoked controversy: that the system of judicial precedent in the common law tends historically, without conscious central planning by the judges themselves, toward **economically more efficient rules** over time. On one influential explanation (developed by Posner and by Paul Rubin and George Priest), this is because parties harmed by inefficient rules are more inclined to litigate and challenge them than parties who benefit from rules that are already efficient, so inefficient rules tend to erode gradually through accumulated precedents.

## Criticism

Posner's theses have been criticized from several directions: by **critical legal studies** for reducing the value of justice to the single criterion of efficiency while ignoring distribution, and later by **behavioural law and economics** (associated with Christine Jolls, Cass Sunstein and Richard Thaler) for assuming a full economic rationality of actors that empirical psychological evidence does not support.

## Practical Adjudication and the Application of Economic Analysis

Posner is distinguished from many purely academic theorists of law and economics by having actually practised his theory from the bench for more than three decades on the Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals. His opinions became well known for applying economic analysis directly to justify judicial decisions, particularly in antitrust, contract and intellectual property cases, in an explicit manner rarely adopted by other judges with such methodological clarity. This combination of academic theorizing with actual judicial practice made him a unique figure in the history of modern American law: he not only proposed a descriptive theory of precedent's tendency towards efficiency, but also contributed personally to advancing that tendency through his opinions.

## Intellectual Development: From Pure Efficiency to Pragmatism

Posner's thought underwent a notable evolution over his career. Whereas his early work (in the 1970s and 1980s) focused on wealth maximization as a quasi-exclusive criterion for evaluating legal rules, his later writings (especially *The Problems of Jurisprudence*, 1990, and *Law, Pragmatism, and Democracy*, 2003) shifted towards a more flexible pragmatic position. It recognizes that economic efficiency is an important criterion but not the only one, and that good adjudication also requires sensitivity to practical consequences and changing social contexts beyond the pure calculus of efficiency. Some scholars attribute this shift to the accumulation of his actual judicial experience, which revealed the limits of the abstract economic model when faced with the complexity of real disputes.

## Legacy and Influence

Despite criticism, Posner remains one of the most influential and prolific American jurists of the twentieth century. Together with Coase and Calabresi he established a complete academic field that changed the teaching method of law in major American universities, and his book *Economic Analysis of Law* became the standard textbook for generations of law students. He also founded the *Journal of Legal Studies* (1972), a leading academic platform for the field, and was a senior lecturer and professor at the University of Chicago Law School before his appointment to the bench.

## Antitrust Law as a Model Field of Application

One of the first areas of law to which Posner applied his method in depth was antitrust law. His book *Antitrust Law: An Economic Perspective* (1976) helped redirect the enforcement of American competition law away from the traditional focus on the mere size of a company or its market share, towards a more precise economic standard that measures the actual effect on **consumer welfare** and market efficiency. This standard, known as the "consumer welfare" standard, became the dominant framework in decisions of the U.S. Supreme Court and antitrust enforcement agencies for decades, before facing renewed criticism from the "New Brandeis Movement", which sees it as an excessive neglect of the political and economic power dimensions that the original antitrust laws were designed to address.
