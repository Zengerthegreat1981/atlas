# Ibrahim Anis

An Egyptian linguist and one of the pioneers who introduced modern phonetics into academic Arabic studies, and the author of *al-Aswat al-Lughawiyya* (Speech Sounds), an early and influential modern Arabic study in this field.

## The phonetics of Arabic speech sounds

In *al-Aswat al-Lughawiyya*, Ibrahim Anis applied the tools of modern phonetics (the classification of sounds by place and manner of articulation) to the Arabic sound system. He went beyond the traditional phonetic description of the early scholars of recitation (*tajwid*) and grammar, such as Sibawayh and al-Khalil, without neglecting their contributions. Instead, he compared their descriptions with modern phonetic knowledge.

## The study of Arabic dialects

Anis also treated contemporary Arabic dialects descriptively in *Fi al-Lahajat al-Arabiyya*. He did not treat dialects only as a deviation from Standard Arabic. He described them as linguistic systems, including phonetic and morphological differences such as the varying pronunciations of *qāf* and *jīm* across regions, and treated many of those differences as historical sound change rather than as simple "corruption."

## His method: comparing heritage and modernity

Anis read the early grammarians critically rather than setting them aside. He compared phonetic observations in Sībawayh's *al-Kitāb* and Ibn Jinnī's *Sirr Ṣināʿat al-Iʿrāb*, including al-Khalīl's account of places of articulation, with the categories of modern articulatory phonetics, and often found the older descriptions precise even without instrumental methods.

## Disputed questions of Arabic phonetics

In *al-Aswat al-Lughawiyya*, Anis treated disputed Arabic phonetic questions with a scientific method. Among them were the nature of the ancient Arabic *dad* sound (whose ancient pronunciation differs from its modern one), and the evolution of the articulation points of some sounds across the ages. He drew on the descriptions of the early grammarians and the tradition of Qur'anic recitation.

## Legacy

Anis established an Arabic academic tradition in phonetics and dialect studies that remained a reference point in Arab universities. He was later a member of the Arabic Language Academy in Cairo.

## His studies of semantics and poetic meter

Alongside his phonetic and dialectal work, Anis contributed to studies in Arabic semantics through his book *Dalalat al-Alfaz* (The Semantics of Words), in which he treated phenomena of the development of the meaning of Arabic words across the ages: the widening and narrowing of meaning, metaphorical transfer, and the phonetic development accompanying semantic change. In another book, *Musiqa al-Shi'r* (The Music of Poetry), he studied the prosodic rhythm of Arabic poetry from a modern phonetic perspective, reconsidering the Khalilian meters in light of modern phonetic ideas of rhythm rather than relying solely on the traditional scansion description.

## Institutional standing

Anis was elected to the Arabic Language Academy in Cairo in 1961. He had studied at Dār al-ʿUlūm, then at the University of London (doctorate 1941), and later taught Arabic linguistics in Cairo, where descriptive phonetics entered the university curriculum.

## His place among the pioneers of Arabic linguistics of his generation

Anis is usually mentioned alongside Tammam Hassan and Ali Abd al-Wahid Wafi as pioneers of introducing modern Western linguistics into academic Arabic studies in the twentieth century. Anis stood out through his particular focus on phonetics and his systematic comparison between the descriptions of the early grammarians and modern findings.

## His position in the debate on renewing Arabic grammar

Anis took part in twentieth-century Arab debates on renewing the study of Arabic. He favored applying modern descriptive linguistics to the inherited material while treating Standard Arabic as the language of the heritage, a moderate position between rejecting the grammatical tradition and repeating it uncritically.
