# Pierre Bourdieu

Pierre Bourdieu (1930-2002) was a French sociologist whose theory of practice, built around habitus, capital and field, reshaped research on culture, education, class and power.

## Life and Context

Bourdieu was born in 1930 in Denguin, in southwestern France, into a family of modest rural origins. He studied philosophy at the École normale supérieure in Paris. Military service took him to Algeria during the war of independence, and he then taught at the University of Algiers. His fieldwork there, including studies of Kabyle society and of uprooted peasants, turned him from philosophy to ethnology and sociology. Back in France he became a director of studies at the École pratique des hautes études, led a research centre, founded the journal *Actes de la recherche en sciences sociales* in 1975, and was elected to the chair of sociology at the Collège de France in 1981. In the 1990s he became a prominent public critic of neoliberal policy. He died in Paris in 2002.

## Main Ideas

- **Habitus:** durable dispositions acquired through socialization that guide perception and action without requiring conscious rule-following.
- **Capital:** resources that take economic, cultural and social forms, which can be converted into one another and become symbolic capital when recognized as legitimate.
- **Field:** a relatively autonomous arena, such as art, science or politics, in which agents compete over stakes specific to it.
- **Symbolic violence:** the imposition of meanings and hierarchies that are accepted as natural, including by those they disadvantage.
- **Reflexivity:** sociologists should analyse their own position and the assumptions of the academic world.

## Research

With Jean-Claude Passeron, in *The Inheritors* (1964) and *Reproduction* (1970), he argued that schools reward the cultural competences of privileged families and so legitimate inequality as differences of merit. *Distinction* (1979) used survey and interview material to show how taste in food, art and leisure is linked to class position and marks social boundaries. *Homo Academicus* (1984) turned the analysis on French universities, *The Rules of Art* (1992) examined the formation of the literary field, and *The Weight of the World* (1993), a collective work based on interviews, documented forms of social suffering. *Masculine Domination* (1998) applied the idea of symbolic violence to gender.

## Method

Bourdieu combined ethnography, surveys, statistical techniques such as correspondence analysis, and historical study. He sought to move beyond the opposition between objectivism, which explains action by external structures, and subjectivism, which explains it by individual experience and choice.

## Influence and Criticism

His concepts are widely used in the sociology of education, culture, consumption, media and science. Critics have argued that the theory of reproduction leaves little room for change and reflective agency, that habitus is difficult to specify empirically, and that the model of competition over capital extends an economic logic too far. Others, working on societies outside France, have questioned how far the French pattern of cultural distinction travels.

## Major Works

- *Outline of a Theory of Practice* (French 1972; English 1977)
- *Distinction* (French 1979; English 1984)
- *The Logic of Practice* (French 1980; English 1990)
