# Luc Boltanski

A French sociologist, one of the founders of "pragmatic sociology" (or the sociology of justification), which studied how people justify their actions and judgments in everyday disputes by appealing to multiple, competing systems of worth (Orders of Worth).

## Central Idea

With Laurent Thévenot, in *On Justification: Economies of Worth* (De la justification: Les économies de la grandeur, 1991), Boltanski developed a theoretical framework alternative to Bourdieu's critical sociology. It assumes that social actors are not merely passive bearers of a class habitus but are capable of critical justification and of entering disputes about "justice" by appealing to multiple "worlds" or "cities" (Cités) for evaluating worth (civic, industrial, market, domestic, fame, inspiration). Later, with Ève Chiapello in *The New Spirit of Capitalism* (1999), he analyzed how capitalism absorbed the artistic and social critique directed at it after 1968 by adopting a new "projective city", suited to a "connexionist" world, that justifies the flexibility and instability of work in the name of the very values that had criticized it.

## Historical Context

Luc Boltanski began his intellectual career as a close student of Pierre Bourdieu at the Centre de sociologie européenne in Paris from the 1960s, taking part in Bourdieu's major research projects, before gradually breaking away from his critical theoretical framework. Boltanski held that Bourdieu's sociology, despite its explanatory power, implicitly carries a "top-down" stance in which the sociologist alone is assumed to possess the tools for uncovering the objective truth behind social actors' false consciousness (such as their belief that they possess a free personal "taste" when it is in reality an automatic reflection of their class position). Social actors themselves, in Boltanski's view, remain rational and fully capable of criticism, justification and independent moral judgment, and are not merely passive bearers of a predetermined class habitus. In the mid-1980s he and Laurent Thévenot, among others, founded the Groupe de sociologie politique et morale at the École des hautes études en sciences sociales in Paris to develop this alternative.

## Key Ideas (in Detail)

Boltanski and Thévenot identified six "cities" (Cités), or orders of worth, to which actors appeal to justify their positions in everyday disputes: the civic city (collective will and democratic representation), the industrial city (technical competence and efficiency), the market city (competition and market value), the domestic city (personal trust and tradition), the city of opinion or fame (public recognition), and the inspired city (creativity and uniqueness). When a dispute arises between two parties (such as a disagreement over an employee's merit for promotion), each party appeals to a different order of worth, and the dispute ends either with one party prevailing or with a "compromise" (compromis) that combines elements of several orders together.

## Impact

This current founded contemporary French "pragmatic sociology" and influenced studies of social conflict and moral justification in institutions and markets. His joint analysis with Chiapello of "the new spirit of capitalism" offered an influential account of how neoliberal capitalism absorbed the artistic critique opposed to institutional authoritarianism (demanding creativity and personal autonomy at work), by dismantling the traditional bureaucratic hierarchy in favor of a "connexionist world" that at the same time justifies the absence of long-term job security in the name of the very values the critics had originally demanded.

## Critique and Assessment

Critics from the Bourdieusian tradition and other critical theorists treated this shift as a surrender of critical sociology's capacity to uncover objective structures of domination that exceed the actors' own awareness, and as a turning of sociology into a mere neutral description of how people justify their positions, without an independent critical judgment on the justice of those positions themselves. Boltanski later defended himself by attempting to reconcile the two approaches in his book *On Critique* (De la critique, 2009), proposing a formula that combines the pragmatic sensitivity to actors' capacity for self-criticism with recognition at the same time of objective structures of domination that exceed their individual awareness, thereby trying to bridge the gap between his early Bourdieusian heritage and his later pragmatic innovation.

## Major Works

Besides *On Justification* (1991) and *The New Spirit of Capitalism* (1999), Boltanski wrote other prominent works over his long career, including *Love and Justice as Competences* (L'Amour et la justice comme compétences, 1990), which analyzes the logic of love as a regime of action (agape) that lies outside the logic of public justification, and *On Critique: A Sociology of Emancipation*, which represents his most mature attempt to synthesize his entire intellectual project. His work is widely cited in French and international economic and political sociology as an attempt to move beyond deterministic structural critique without abandoning sociology's critical ambition.
