# Florus

Florus compiled a concise history of Rome emphasizing its military achievements and moral development.

## Definition and Historical Significance

Florus (fl. 2nd century CE) wrote an epitome of Roman history emphasizing Rome's military development and moral character. His concise account stressed Rome's achievements in war and the virtue that had guided Roman expansion.

## Historical Context

Florus lived during the height of the Roman Empire under the Antonines, a period of stability and prosperity. His work reflected imperial ideology emphasizing Rome's greatness.

## Key Contributions and Methodology

Florus contributed epitomized account of Roman military history; emphasis on moral dimensions of Roman development; accessible popular history.

## Theoretical Framework

Florus organized history chronologically while emphasizing military achievements and moral character.

## Examples and Case Studies

His accounts of major wars stressed Roman military superiority; his treatment of republican conflicts emphasized moral issues.

## Contemporary Relevance

Florus provides accessible popular history perspective and emphasizes how Romans understood their military achievements.

## Limitations and Gaps

His epitomized form means less detail than comprehensive histories. His moral emphasis can substitute for analytical depth. His popularity as a school text led to variations in manuscript tradition.
