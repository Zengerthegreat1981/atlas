# Hans W. Frei

A German-born American theologian, born in Breslau (then in Germany) in 1922. He emigrated to the United States in the late 1930s and taught at Yale University (Divinity School and Department of Religious Studies) until his death in 1988. He is considered one of the founders of what came to be known as "Narrative Theology" and the "Yale School", a current that reads sacred texts as narratives that produce religious identity rather than as mere doctrinal statements.

## Life and Formation

Frei grew up in a German family of Jewish origin, some of whose members converted to Christianity. He left Germany for the United States in 1938, later joined the American Episcopal Church, and studied at Yale, where H. Richard Niebuhr was an important teacher. His doctoral dissertation (1956) was on Karl Barth and his break with liberal theology. Barth remained a constant reference point for his work; from him Frei learned to let the text determine its own meaning before an external theory is imposed on it.

## The Core Work: *The Eclipse of Biblical Narrative*

Frei published his foundational book, *The Eclipse of Biblical Narrative: A Study in Eighteenth and Nineteenth Century Hermeneutics* (Yale University Press, 1974), a historical study tracing how European hermeneutics in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries lost the Bible's, in both Testaments, "realistic narrative" reading, that is, reading it as a coherent story whose meaning is understood through what it says about its events and characters. Before that, interpreters from the church fathers to Calvin read the biblical stories in a "literal-figural" manner that linked the two Testaments into a single line. Then rational debate and historical criticism pushed interpreters toward two external questions: did the events actually happen historically? And what general religious idea does the text express? As a result, the meaning produced by the narrative itself receded. Frei traced this recession across different interpreters, from defensive theology to historical criticism, and found that they all agreed that meaning lies outside the story.

## The Critical Position and the Later Project

Frei did not call for a return to literalist reading of the Bible. Instead, he asked theologians to acknowledge that the Gospels are a "history-like" text that operates through narrative mechanisms: character, plot and climax, and that the identity of the person of Jesus is determined through the story told about him. In *The Identity of Jesus Christ* (Fortress Press, 1975), he applied this to the Gospels and argued that the identity of Jesus is known through the story told of his deeds, sufferings and resurrection. Works published after his death included *Types of Christian Theology* (Yale University Press, 1992), edited by George Hunsinger and William Placher, in which he classifies the types of Christian theology according to the relation between philosophy and the description of practice.

## Influence and Connection to Other Traditions

Frei had a decisive influence on George Lindbeck and his colleagues at Yale. Lindbeck's book *The Nature of Doctrine* (Westminster Press, 1984) developed the theory of "cultural-linguistic" doctrine. Frei's work opened a comparative question: if the narrative structure is the basis of Christian religious identity, what about other traditions? Islam offers a direct point of comparison: the Qur'an calls its stories "the best of stories" (Yusuf 12:3), and regards story as a means of guidance. A related question was raised by Muhammad Ahmad Khalafallah in his thesis "The Artistic Narrative in the Holy Qur'an" (Cairo University, 1947, later published), namely whether the Qur'anic story is bound by literal historical truth or is a narrative art serving a religious purpose. His thesis met with sharp rejection at the university. In modern times, Mustansir Mir studied Surat Yusuf as a coherent narrative unit combining plot, characters and purposes ("The Qur'anic Story of Joseph", *The Muslim World*, 1986). These cases confirm that the question of the relationship between history and narrative in sacred texts is not specific to Christianity, although they do not show any influence from Frei.

## Methodological Position and Limits

Frei stands at the intersection of the critique of theological discourse, the literary analysis of sacred texts, and the epistemology of religion. His work has been criticized from several directions. Some exegetes held that his contrast between the "realistic narrative" reading and "historical criticism" is sharper than necessary, and that some exegetes of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries did not abandon narrative. Liberal theologians held that neglecting the historical reference of events exposes faith to the danger of closing in on the text. His work remained largely confined to a Protestant Christian context, so applying it to other traditions requires substantial adjustments.
