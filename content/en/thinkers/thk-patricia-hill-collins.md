# Patricia Hill Collins

Patricia Hill Collins (born 1948) is an American sociologist and social theorist known for her work on Black feminist thought, intersecting systems of power and intersectionality.

## Life and Context

Collins was born in Philadelphia in 1948. She studied at Brandeis University, took a master's degree in teaching at Harvard, and worked in schools and community education before returning to Brandeis for a doctorate in sociology (1984). She taught for many years in African American studies at the University of Cincinnati and later became a professor of sociology at the University of Maryland, College Park. In 2009 she served as president of the American Sociological Association, the first African American woman to hold the office.

## Main Ideas

- **Standpoint.** Black women's experiences at the intersection of race, class and gender have generated distinctive knowledge, developed in families, communities, literature, music and activism as well as in universities.
- **Outsider within.** Her 1986 article described the vantage point of people who are present in an institution without fully belonging to it, such as Black women in domestic service or academia.
- **Controlling images.** Stereotypes such as the mammy, the matriarch and the welfare mother help to make inequality appear natural.
- **Matrix of domination.** Systems of oppression interlock and operate through structural, disciplinary, hegemonic and interpersonal domains of power, each of which can also be a site of resistance.
- **Intersectionality.** Drawing on a term introduced by Kimberlé Crenshaw and on a longer tradition of Black feminist activism, Collins treats intersectionality as a form of inquiry and praxis and examines what it would require to become a critical social theory.

## Epistemology and Method

Collins challenges the assumption that valid knowledge requires detachment from lived experience. In *Black Feminist Thought* she outlines an alternative epistemology that values experience as a criterion of meaning, the use of dialogue in assessing knowledge claims, an ethic of care and an ethic of personal accountability. She draws on historical, literary and cultural materials together with sociological analysis.

## Later Work

*Fighting Words* (1998) asks what makes a social theory critical. *Black Sexual Politics* (2004) analyses gender and sexuality in contemporary racism, including popular media. *From Black Power to Hip Hop* (2006) addresses nationalism, feminism and youth culture, and *Intersectionality* (2016, with Sirma Bilge) surveys the field's uses in scholarship and activism.

## Influence and Criticism

Her work is widely read in sociology, gender studies, education and race studies. Critics of standpoint theory have asked whether it assumes a uniform group experience or leaves the assessment of competing knowledge claims unclear; Collins has stressed that a standpoint is collective and heterogeneous. Others note the difficulty of turning intersectional analysis into research designs.
