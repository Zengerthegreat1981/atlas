# Eduardo Viveiros de Castro

A Brazilian anthropologist who conducted his main fieldwork among the Araweté and other peoples of the Brazilian Amazon. He is a leading figure in the "ontological turn" in contemporary anthropology, and his Araweté ethnography was published as *From the Enemy's Point of View: Humanity and Divinity in an Amazonian Society* (1992).

## Amazonian Perspectivism

In his seminal essay "Cosmological Deixis and Amerindian Perspectivism" (1998), Viveiros de Castro put forward a radical idea drawn from the mythology of Amazonian peoples. Contrary to the standard Western assumption of a single objective "nature" that humans interpret through multiple "cultures" (multiculturalism: many cultures over one nature), many Amazonian cosmologies conceive the relation as reversed. There is a single "culture" shared by all beings: humans, animals, and spirits all share being "persons" who see themselves as human and who observe rituals and social customs. But each kind of being sees the world from a different bodily perspective, which makes it perceive the same things in radically different forms. What humans see as blood, jaguars see as manioc beer. This is "multinaturalism over a single culture" (Multinaturalism), not the reverse.

## Borrowing from Lévi-Strauss and Going Beyond Him

Viveiros de Castro borrowed tools from the structuralism of Claude Lévi-Strauss, especially his analysis of indigenous American myths in *Mythologiques*. However, he directed them toward an ontological rather than a purely structural conclusion. Differences between cultures are not differences in the symbolic classification of one shared natural reality. They may instead reflect radically different existential (ontological) conceptions of what exists in the first place and how it is interrelated.

## Impact

This proposal provoked a wide debate in contemporary anthropology over whether non-Western cosmological systems should be taken seriously as alternative "ontologies" deserving serious philosophical consideration, rather than being treated merely as cultural "beliefs" that can be reduced to symbolic explanation within a single pre-given natural framework, as classical anthropology generally did. This body of work is central to what is known as the "ontological turn," and has been taken up by Martin Holbraad and others; it developed alongside, but independently of, Philippe Descola's comparative work on ontologies.

## Cannibal Metaphysics and Political Critique

In *Cannibal Metaphysics* (published in French in 2009; English translation 2014), Viveiros de Castro extended his argument into an explicit political critique of Western anthropology itself. He called for "decolonizing thought" by taking indigenous concepts seriously as philosophical tools for rethinking basic Western notions such as the subject, the object, and nature. One example is the ritual cannibalism of the historical Tupinambá, in which he finds a way of constituting the self through the perspective of the enemy Other, rather than merely a brutal practice as portrayed in colonial narratives.

## The Debate over the Ontological Turn

The ontological turn led by Viveiros de Castro provoked wide debate within the field. Some critics, including those writing from Marxist and feminist perspectives, have accused it of an excessive focus on "multiple ontologies," which they argued could divert attention from analyzing the actual power relations and economic exploitation that Indigenous peoples face today, such as the plundering of Amazonian land. These critics preferred a direct political-economic framework over philosophical abstraction about ontology.

## His Intellectual Style

Viveiros de Castro writes in a style that deeply blends fine-grained ethnography with Continental philosophy, Deleuze and Guattari in particular. This has made his work widely read beyond anthropology itself, in philosophy, environmental studies, and contemporary art circles concerned with critiquing Western anthropocentrism and the relationship between humans and non-humans.

## His Work at the National Museum of Brazil

Viveiros de Castro is a professor at the National Museum of the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro. This long-established institution was severely damaged by a major fire in 2018 that consumed a large part of its rare archaeological and ethnographic collections. The museum has been a historical center for the study of Brazil's Indigenous peoples, and its loss was widely mourned in the discipline.

## His Relationship with Contemporary Indigenous Movements

Viveiros de Castro has publicly spoken in support of Indigenous peoples in Brazil and their struggles over land. He has presented his work as a way of taking Indigenous thought seriously as thought, and has linked it to the defence of these peoples' ways of life.

## Conclusion

Viveiros de Castro remains one of the most influential and controversial contemporary anthropologists. His work has brought Amazonian myths and cosmologies prominently into philosophical debate on the nature of reality and the relationship between humans and non-humans, reaching beyond the narrow boundaries of anthropological specialization.
