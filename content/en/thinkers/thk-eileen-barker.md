# Eileen Barker

A British sociologist (born 1938), Professor Emerita of Sociology at the London School of Economics (LSE), and the leading academic researcher in the study of new religious movements. She founded the INFORM organization (Information Network Focus on Religious Movements) in 1988 to supply the British public and government with balanced information about these movements, away from media polarization.

## Historical Context

Barker's research came at the height of the moral panic over "cults" in Britain and America during the 1970s, when media narratives of "brainwashing" were widespread. These narratives portrayed people who joined new religious movements (such as the Unification Church of Sun Myung Moon, the International Society for Krishna Consciousness and Scientology) as victims who had been deceived and coerced, not as chosen agents. Barker undertook a long-term independent study of the Unification Church in Britain, which gave her extensive field access to the movement itself.

## Foundational Study: The Making of a "Moonie"

In her best-known field study, *The Making of a Moonie: Choice or Brainwashing?* (1984), Barker followed for years a sample of people who joined the Unification Church and of those who left it, using systematic questionnaires and interviews. She reported that only a small proportion of those who attended the movement's introductory workshops went on to join, and that many who joined later left, which she argued did not support the hypothesis of irresistible "brainwashing." She treated joining as closer to a choice made within a socially pressured context than to a total nullification of the will, as some media outlets and anti-cult organizations of the period claimed, while acknowledging that the movement used strong persuasive methods.

## Major Works

Alongside *The Making of a Moonie* (1984), Barker's major works include *New Religious Movements: A Practical Introduction* (1989). It was published by HMSO (the British government publisher) as a neutral guide for the public, teachers and social workers about dozens of new religious movements active in Britain. Her work also includes dozens of peer-reviewed articles on questions of socialization, joining and leaving these movements.

## Methodological Impact

Her long-term empirical methodology (following individuals over time rather than relying on individual testimonies after leaving) helped transform the field of new religious movements from an area dominated by media controversy into an academic field that relies on systematic empirical evidence. Her work has been widely cited in public and policy discussion of these movements.

## The INFORM Organization

Barker founded INFORM in 1988 as an independent charity, with support from the British Home Office and the mainstream Christian churches. Its purpose was to provide families, teachers, journalists and researchers with academically documented information about new religious movements, as an alternative to the biased information provided either by the movements' own proselytizing groups or by anti-cult organizations. It became a widely used reference source in this field.

## Academic Standing

Barker has served as president of the Society for the Scientific Study of Religion and has been consulted by governments and international bodies on policy toward new religious movements.

## Conclusion

Barker combined empirical sociological research with public information work. Her long-term design, following individuals over time rather than relying on retrospective testimony, became a methodological model for studying controversial movements, and her approach is generally regarded as having helped move the field from media controversy toward systematic evidence.
