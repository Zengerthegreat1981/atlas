# Alessandro Duranti

## Definition

Alessandro Duranti (born 1950 in Italy) is a linguistic anthropologist and professor at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). He is a leading figure in contemporary linguistic anthropology, working in the line of the ethnography of communication, and is known for his field research in Samoa and for his work on language, agency and intentionality.

## Historical Context

Duranti has held senior academic positions at UCLA, including service as dean of the Division of Social Sciences, and he has been active in the Society for Linguistic Anthropology. 

He trained academically between Italy and the United States, influenced by the ethnography of communication developed by Dell Hymes and John Gumperz and by conversation analysis, and he worked closely with Elinor Ochs. His main field research was carried out in Western Samoa from the late 1970s onward, in a village where he studied political and ritual discourse in the traditional councils (*fono*), as well as other speech events.

## Main Ideas and Method

In his analysis, Duranti drew on lengthy audio and video recordings of actual *fono* sessions, applying the detailed transcription and analytic tools of conversation analysis (such as attention to pauses, overlap and turn-taking) to ethnographic material. This combined methodological precision from linguistic rigor with deep cultural anthropological interpretation of the meaning of discourse within the traditional Samoan value system (*fa'a Samoa*).

In his book *From Grammar to Politics* (1994), Duranti argued that language in Samoan councils is not merely a tool of communication but a political practice in itself, in which authority and status are shaped through patterns of speaking, silence and turn-taking. In earlier work on Samoan political discourse (1990) he showed how grammatical choices, such as the use of the ergative case, can express different degrees of agency and responsibility, and he later developed this into a broader analysis of agency in language. He also contributed to formulating a comprehensive theoretical framework for linguistic anthropology that combines linguistics, social anthropology and phenomenological philosophy (particularly his engagement with Husserl and Merleau-Ponty in his later work on intentions and intersubjectivity).

## Key Works and Figures

He wrote a widely used introductory textbook titled *Linguistic Anthropology* (Cambridge University Press, 1997), which became a standard teaching reference, offering a comprehensive survey of the field from Boas and Sapir to Hymes and Gumperz and later developments.

Among his most important works are *From Grammar to Politics: Linguistic Anthropology in a Western Samoan Village* (1994), the edited volumes *Rethinking Context: Language as an Interactive Phenomenon* (1992, with Charles Goodwin), *Linguistic Anthropology: A Reader* (2001, with a later edition in 2009) and *A Companion to Linguistic Anthropology* (2004), and the monograph *The Anthropology of Intentions* (2015).

## Impact and Critique

Duranti is among the leading figures of linguistic anthropology after the generation of Hymes and Gumperz, and his textbooks are widely used in teaching.

Duranti also worked with Elinor Ochs on Samoan language and on the study of context, and his work is related to the language socialization tradition developed by Ochs and Bambi Schieffelin.
