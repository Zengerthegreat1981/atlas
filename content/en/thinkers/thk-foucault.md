# Michel Foucault

Michel Foucault (1926-1984) was a French philosopher and historian of ideas whose studies of madness, medicine, prisons, and sexuality reshaped sociology, especially the analysis of power, knowledge, and discipline.

## Life and Context

Foucault was born in Poitiers in 1926 and studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris. He taught in Sweden, Poland, and Germany, and later at the University of Clermont-Ferrand and Vincennes. In 1970 he was elected to the Collège de France, holding a chair in the history of systems of thought until his death in Paris in 1984. He was politically active, notably in prison reform through the Groupe d'information sur les prisons.

## Main Ideas

- **Power/knowledge:** power is not only repressive but productive, and it is bound up with the production of knowledge. Knowledge systems and forms of power shape each other.
- **Discipline and surveillance:** in modern institutions, power operates through observation, normalization, and examination, which produce docile bodies. The Panopticon, drawn from Jeremy Bentham, serves as a model.
- **Discourse and episteme:** discourses define what can be said and known in a period, and he analyzed shifting historical frameworks of knowledge.
- **Genealogy and archaeology:** methods for tracing the contingent historical formation of concepts and institutions.
- **Biopower and governmentality:** from the eighteenth century, states manage populations through health, birth, and life; later lectures developed governmentality.
- **Subjectivation:** individuals become subjects through practices of power and of self-formation.

## Major Works

- *Madness and Civilization* (1961)
- *The Birth of the Clinic* (1963)
- *The Order of Things* (1966)
- *The Archaeology of Knowledge* (1969)
- *Discipline and Punish* (1975)
- *The History of Sexuality*, volume 1 (1976); volumes 2 and 3 (1984)

## Method

Foucault combined historical research in archives with philosophical analysis. His archaeological work described the rules of discourse in a period, while genealogy analyzed the emergence of practices and the power relations behind them.

## Influence and Criticism

His work is central to critical theory, gender and queer studies, postcolonial studies, criminology, and the sociology of medicine. Critics have questioned the selectivity and accuracy of some of his historical claims, his ambiguity about normative grounds for critique (a charge made by Jürgen Habermas and Nancy Fraser), and his treatment of resistance. He rejected labels such as structuralist.

## Applications and Cross-References

Researchers use his concepts to study prisons, schools, clinics, and surveillance technologies. Compare Weber on rationalization and bureaucracy, Goffman on total institutions, and Marx on power and ideology.
