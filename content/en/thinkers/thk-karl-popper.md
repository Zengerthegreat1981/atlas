# Sir Karl Popper

One of the most influential philosophers of science of the twentieth century and the originator of critical rationalism; he proposed a solution to the problem of distinguishing science from non-science by announcing the "criterion of falsifiability and refutation" (Falsifiability) in place of the positivist principle of verification; author of "The Open Society and Its Enemies", in which he directed a scathing critique at the totalitarianism and historical determinism of Plato, Hegel and Marx.

## Biography

Karl Popper was born in Vienna on 28 July 1902 to a family of Jewish origin that had converted to Christianity; his father was a cultured lawyer, and he obtained his doctorate in Vienna in 1928 in the psychology of thinking. He knew the members of the Vienna Circle without being one of them, and said later that it was he who had finished off logical positivism, because the criterion of verification in his view obscures the question of falsification. "The Logic of Scientific Discovery" (Logik der Forschung) appeared in 1934 in Vienna, and he left Austria in 1937 for Canterbury University College in New Zealand after the rise of Nazism, and wrote there "The Open Society and Its Enemies" during the war. In 1946 he moved to the London School of Economics and was appointed professor of logic and scientific method in 1949, and was knighted in 1965. He died in London on 17 September 1994.

## What he contributed

- **The criterion of falsifiability**: his decisive shift in the philosophy of science. The question is not "how do we verify the theory?" but **"what could refute it?"** A theory is scientific if it forbids something, if there is a possible outcome which, were it to occur, would invalidate it. Hence his reversal against **logical positivism**: the criterion of verification makes a theory stronger the more its confirmations multiply, whereas there is no limit to the number of confirming observations for any vague claim. His slogan is that **a million white swans do not prove that all swans are white, while one black swan is enough to refute it**.
- **The problem of demarcation**: his application of it to what he considered "pseudo-sciences". He compared **general relativity**, which predicted the bending of light with a specific prediction that could have been refuted in the 1919 observation, with **Freudian psychoanalysis** and **Marxism**, which he found explain everything: whoever accepted the interpretation is an example, and whoever rejected it is resistant, and no fact could contradict them. His verdict was that this **apparent strength is in reality a weakness**, since a theory that forbids nothing tells us nothing. This very critique is the most famous epistemological objection directed at psychoanalysis, and has occupied the field for decades in replying to it.
- **Critique of induction and rejection of justification**: we never infer the law from observations; rather **we conjecture a hypothesis** and then expose it to the hardest test we can. A theory that has survived **is not confirmed** but "provisionally corroborated", that is, not yet falsified. Scientific knowledge for him is **always conjectural**, with no certainty in it at all.
- **"Objective knowledge" and the three worlds**: he divided reality into **World 1** (material things), **World 2** (subjective mental states), and **World 3** (the objective contents of thought: theories, problems and arguments once they have been formulated). World 3 is produced by us and then **becomes autonomous** with problems that we did not intend, as number theory produces problems that its framers did not set.
- **"The Open Society and Its Enemies" (1945)**: an application of the same method to politics. A critique of **Platonism, Hegelianism and Marxism** as "historicism" claiming to know deterministic laws of the course of history. The right political question for him is not "who should rule?", a question that opens onto tyranny, but **"how do we design institutions that enable us to remove a bad ruler without violence?"** That is, democracy is a mechanism of falsification applied to power.
- **"Piecemeal social engineering"**: his alternative to totalitarian projects: limited reforms that can be evaluated and reversed, because we know more about evils than about goods, and because the effect of broad intervention cannot be predicted.

## His position in the tradition

Popper is an Austrian who emigrated to New Zealand and then to the London School of Economics. His position in the philosophy of science is that of the **hinge**: the schools that followed arose out of him and against him: **Kuhn**, who held that scientists do not abandon a paradigm at the first falsification, **Lakatos**, who tried to reconcile through research programmes, and **Feyerabend**, who was at first influenced by him and then turned against him radically. His importance is twofold: the **criterion of demarcation**, which provides a tool for assessing claims in psychology itself, and his **critique of psychoanalysis**, which is the most famous structural objection to the legitimacy of the field.

## Critique of Popper and the limits of his criterion

The most prominent objection to the criterion of falsification is that a theory does not face the test alone but is tested together with auxiliary hypotheses about instruments and conditions, so if the result conflicts with observation the blame can be put on another hypothesis, an idea tied to the names of Duhem and Quine. Kuhn argued that the actual history of science does not proceed by direct falsification, and Lakatos built "research programmes" to keep the spirit of Popper and go beyond the naivety of his falsificationism. Moreover, statistical statements, such as saying that the probability of an event is high, cannot be refuted by a single case. As for his reading of Plato, Hegel and Marx in "The Open Society", many historians have considered it selective, and Walter Kaufmann in his study "The Hegel Myth and Its Method" (1951) criticized his way of citing Hegel. Critics of Popper on Marxism hold that he made it at one time a refuted theory and at another a theory immunized against falsification, and that this judgment needs a distinction between Marx and his followers.

## Major works

- *Logik der Forschung* (The Logic of Scientific Discovery, 1934).
