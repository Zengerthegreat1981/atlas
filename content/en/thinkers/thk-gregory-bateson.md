# Gregory Bateson

Gregory Bateson (1904–1980) was a British-born anthropologist whose work moved across ethnography, visual research, psychiatry, communication theory, cybernetics, and ecology. A common concern links these subjects: how patterns of relationship develop, correct themselves, or run out of control. Born in England, the son of the geneticist William Bateson, he trained in natural science and then anthropology at Cambridge, and later worked mainly in the United States.

## New Guinea and *Naven*

Bateson's *Naven* (1936) analysed a ceremony of the Iatmul of the Sepik River in which relatives, notably a mother's brother, mark a young person's achievements through acts that include reversal of gendered dress and conduct. Bateson approached the rite from several angles: social structure, emotional style, which he called ethos, and cultural logic, which he called eidos. He proposed the term schismogenesis for cumulative differentiation through interaction. In symmetrical forms, each side answers with more of the same, as in rivalry; in complementary forms, different behaviours such as assertion and submission intensify each other. The book openly questioned the status of its own analytic categories.

## Bali and the visual record

With Margaret Mead, whom he married in 1936, Bateson did fieldwork in Bali in the later 1930s. They made large numbers of photographs and film sequences of posture, play, teaching, trance, and parent–child interaction. *Balinese Character: A Photographic Analysis* (1942) arranged photographs with commentary so that images carried part of the argument. The work is an early landmark of visual anthropology. Its psychological generalisations about Balinese character have been questioned.

## Communication, cybernetics, and the double bind

Bateson took part in the Macy conferences on cybernetics, where feedback offered a language for self-regulating systems. With the psychiatrist Jurgen Ruesch he wrote *Communication: The Social Matrix of Psychiatry* (1951). His analysis of play noted that animals and people exchange signals about how other signals should be taken, an idea later important to frame analysis. In 1956 Bateson, Don Jackson, Jay Haley, and John Weakland proposed the double bind: repeated, contradictory demands at different levels of communication, within a relationship the recipient cannot easily leave. They offered it as a factor in schizophrenia. That causal account has not been supported as an explanation of the disorder, and it could lead to blaming families, although the concept influenced family therapy.

## Ecology of mind

*Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972) collected essays from several decades. *Mind and Nature: A Necessary Unity* (1979) argued that learning and biological evolution show related patterns, and described information as difference that makes a difference. Mind, in this account, is a property of organised circuits that may include an organism and its surroundings. Bateson criticised action that maximises one variable while ignoring the system that sustains it. Readers value the reach of these arguments; others find them suggestive but difficult to test. *Angels Fear*, completed by Mary Catherine Bateson, appeared in 1987.
