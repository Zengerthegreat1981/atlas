# Robert Fogel

An American economic historian and professor at the University of Chicago. He was one of the founders of the field of quantitative history (Cliometrics) and shared the 1993 Nobel Prize in Economics with Douglass North.

## What He Gave

- **Railroads and American Economic Growth (1964)**: he used a counterfactual model to estimate what would have happened to American economic growth had the railroads not been built. He concluded that their impact was less decisive than the traditional historical account assumed. This was a pioneering application of econometrics to historical questions (see `sch-counterfactual-historical-causation`).
- **Time on the Cross (with Stanley Engerman, 1974)**: a controversial quantitative analysis of the American slave economy, which argued that the slave system was "efficient" by productivity standards. This conclusion faced sharp methodological and moral criticism from historians who saw it as an abstraction that overlooked the human suffering of enslaved people behind the numbers.
- **Counterfactual history as a disciplined tool**: he argued that every causal historical explanation already contains an implicit counterfactual assumption, and that openly stating the statistical model used (rather than leaving it implicit) is what makes the assumption testable and open to scientific criticism.

## Position in the Current

Fogel, together with Douglass North, pioneered the application of formal economic theory and standard statistical methods to economic history. He transformed the field from a descriptive narrative into a quantitative testing of hypotheses. This methodological shift has provoked continuing debate about the tension between statistical precision and the interpretive sensitivity needed to understand human experience.

## Major Works

- *Railroads and American Economic Growth: Essays in Econometric History* (1964).
- *Time on the Cross: The Economics of American Negro Slavery* (with Stanley L. Engerman, 1974).
- *Without Consent or Contract: The Rise and Fall of American Slavery* (1989).
