# Peter M. Blau

An American sociologist of Austrian origin (1918–2002) who emigrated to America fleeing Nazism, and who extended social exchange theory from the direct individual interaction of George Homans to an explanation of large-scale social structures and relations of power and inequality.

## What He Contributed

- **Exchange and power**: In *Exchange and Power in Social Life* (1964) he showed how "power" arises from imbalance in an exchange relationship. When one party needs a resource held by another party (help, expertise, protection) without having anything to offer in return, that party has four alternatives: supply something the other values in return, obtain the resource elsewhere, take it by force, or learn to do without it. If none of these is available, the remaining course is to subordinate oneself to the provider, that is, to comply, and such compliance, repeated over time, produces a stable relationship of power.
- **From individual interaction to structure**: He extended this logic of exchange to explain the emergence of formal organizations and of class stratification itself as the cumulative product of repeated unequal exchange relations on a wide societal scale, not merely isolated individual interactions as in Homans. Blau, who earned his doctorate at Columbia under Robert K. Merton rather than studying with Homans, was a critic as well as a continuer of Homans's psychological approach. He moved methodologically from microscopic behavioral explanation to a broader structural explanation that restores the importance of the influence of existing social structures (the distribution of resources and power) on the conditions of the exchange process itself.
- **Sociology of organizations**: In *The Dynamics of Bureaucracy* (1955) he reported field studies of a state employment agency and a federal law-enforcement agency, showing how informal relations and exchanges among officials shaped formal procedures; with W. Richard Scott he wrote *Formal Organizations* (1962).
- **Social mobility and structure**: With Otis Dudley Duncan he wrote *The American Occupational Structure* (1967), a landmark quantitative study of occupational mobility and status attainment, and in *Inequality and Heterogeneity* (1977) he derived propositions about how the size and distribution of groups affect intergroup relations.

## His Place in the Current

Blau's work represented a methodological and theoretical bridge between the microscopic exchange theory of his teacher Homans and the theory of large-scale social structure, and it deeply influenced the development of the sociology of organizations and, through its focus on relations among positions, later structural and network approaches to social structure.

- *Inequality and Heterogeneity: A Primitive Theory of Social Structure*. Free Press, 1977.
