# Judith Butler

Judith Butler (born 1956) is an American philosopher whose theory of gender performativity has had a wide influence on the sociology of gender, queer theory and social theory.

## Life and Context

Butler was born in Cleveland, Ohio, in 1956 and received a doctorate in philosophy from Yale University in 1984. After posts at Wesleyan, George Washington and Johns Hopkins universities, Butler moved to the University of California, Berkeley, in the 1990s, in rhetoric and comparative literature. The first book, *Subjects of Desire* (1987), treated Hegel's reception in twentieth-century French thought. Butler has also been active in debates on war, public mourning, Israel and Palestine, and academic freedom, and received the Theodor W. Adorno Prize in 2012. Butler uses they/them pronouns.

## Main Ideas

- **Performativity:** gender is constituted through the repeated, norm-governed acts that appear to express it; it is not simply a voluntary performance by a pre-existing self.
- **Sex and gender:** the distinction between a natural sex and a cultural gender is itself questioned, since bodies become intelligible through social norms. *Bodies That Matter* (1993) answered critics who read performativity as a denial of the body, by asking how norms decide which bodies count as livable.
- **Heterosexual matrix:** a framework that expects coherence among sex, gender and desire renders some lives unintelligible.
- **Subversion and agency:** because norms depend on repetition, they can be repeated differently, as Butler discussed with reference to drag.
- **Subjection:** power both subordinates people and forms them as subjects.
- **Precarity and grievability:** all lives are vulnerable and dependent, but political frames distribute protection unequally and mark which losses count as grievable.
- **Assembly:** bodies gathering in public can make claims about the conditions needed for a livable life.

## Illustrations

*Excitable Speech* examines hate speech and its regulation in the United States, arguing that injurious words draw force from repetition and can sometimes be reappropriated. *Frames of War* analyzes how media images and wartime discourse shape which populations are mourned.

## Method

Butler works through philosophical and textual argument, drawing on Hegel, Michel Foucault, J. L. Austin, Jacques Derrida, psychoanalysis and feminist theory. Simone de Beauvoir is among the feminist sources of that argument. The work does not rest on empirical social research. Sociologists have since carried performativity into empirical studies of how gender is done in interaction.

## Influence and Criticism

The concepts are widely used in studies of gender, sexuality, the body and identity. Seyla Benhabib questioned whether this approach leaves adequate room for agency. Martha Nussbaum's essay "The Professor of Parody" (1999) criticized the prose and what she saw as a retreat from practical politics. Some sociologists argue that the theory underplays material institutions and interaction, and some transgender studies scholars have questioned early treatments of embodiment and identity.
