# Louis Hjelmslev

A Danish linguist, co-founder of the Copenhagen Linguistic Circle and author of the doctrine of **glossematics**, one of the most abstract attempts to develop Saussurean structuralism as a formal theory.

## The Project

Hjelmslev sought to build a **purely formal** linguistic theory, completely independent of any concrete material, psychological or social content, that describes language as a system of pure logical relations. He developed a distinction between **form** and **substance** at each of two levels: the level of **expression** (sound or writing) and the level of **content** (meaning). This is a formal extension of Saussure's distinction between signifier and signified.

## The Empirical Principle and the Cenemes/Pleremes Duality

Hjelmslev established the "Empirical Principle," a strict methodological requirement for any linguistic description. A description must be free of contradiction, comprehensive (covering all the data), and as simple as possible, in exactly this order of priority. Within the level of expression he developed a distinction between "cenemes" (Cenemes, the smallest abstract sound units with no meaning in themselves, closer to the phoneme). Within the level of content he identified parallel units he called "pleremes" (Pleremes, the smallest units of semantic content). He attempted to build a symmetrical formal correspondence between the levels of expression and content in the abstract.

## Catalysis and Purport

Among his other formal tools is the concept of "catalysis" (Catalysis): the inference of implicit elements that do not appear explicitly in the text under study but that the linguistic system implicitly presupposes in order to fill systematic structural gaps. There is also the concept of "purport" (Purport), or "amorphous continuum," which describes the raw material that has not yet taken shape, whether phonetically or semantically. Each language cuts and divides this raw material into distinct units in its own structural system. This idea is a deep formal radicalization of Saussure's principle of the arbitrariness of the sign.

## Impact

Glossematics remained a current of limited diffusion outside Denmark because of its extreme abstraction and the difficulty of its technical vocabulary. Nevertheless, it influenced the development of structural semiotics, especially in the work of Greimas, who directly borrowed the distinction between form and substance and the concept of connotative semiotics. It is taught today as one of the most ambitious attempts to formalize structural linguistics.

## Connotative Semiotics and Metasemiotics

Hjelmslev also distinguished between "denotative semiotics" (the basic linguistic system that directly expresses a content through an expression) and "connotative semiotics" (Connotative Semiotics), in which the complete basic system (expression and content together) becomes the "expression" of a higher, additional connotative level of meaning, such as a particular dialect's association with additional social meanings that go beyond its direct semantic content. He also distinguished "metasemiotics" (Metasemiotics), in which the basic semantic system as a whole becomes the object of study or description by another system that speaks about it. Linguistics itself, as a "metasemiotic" system describing natural language, is an example. This precise formal distinction had a direct influence on Greimas's later development of similar semiotic concepts.

## Key Works

His own general statement is *Omkring sprogteoriens grundlæggelse* (1943), translated by Francis J. Whitfield as *Prolegomena to a Theory of Language* (revised English edition, 1961). With Hans Jørgen Uldall he planned a more technical *Outline of Glossematics*. Only Part I, *General Theory*, appeared, under Uldall's name, in 1957 in the *Travaux du Cercle Linguistique de Copenhague*; the planned second part did not appear.

## The Copenhagen Circle

Hjelmslev and Viggo Brøndal founded the Copenhagen Linguistic Circle (*Cercle linguistique de Copenhague*) in 1931, on the model of the Prague Linguistic Circle. Hjelmslev chaired it for many years. The circle published the series *Travaux du Cercle Linguistique de Copenhague*, a main outlet for glossematics. Its international audience stayed narrower than that of the Prague Circle.

## Early Training

Hjelmslev studied comparative linguistics in Copenhagen, including under Holger Pedersen, and in Paris attended the lectures of Antoine Meillet. He later held the chair of comparative linguistics at the University of Copenhagen, teaching historical linguistics while developing glossematics.

## Reception of His Thought outside Denmark

Despite the difficulty of his highly abstract linguistic theses, which limited their wide spread in Anglophone linguistic circles compared with American structuralism or the Prague circle, Hjelmslev's thought received a more enthusiastic reception in France. This was especially so among the pioneers of French structural semiotics (Greimas and Roland Barthes), who directly used his binary terms (such as expression and content) in building their own analytical tools for analyzing texts and popular culture. This made Hjelmslev strongly present in one specific French tradition, more than in a universal global presence.
