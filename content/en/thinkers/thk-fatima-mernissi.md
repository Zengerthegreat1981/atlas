# Fatima Mernissi

Fatima Mernissi (1940-2015) was a Moroccan sociologist and writer who examined gender, Islam, politics and modernity in the Arab world. She combined academic sociology with memoir and public writing.

## Life and Context

Mernissi was born in Fez, Morocco, and grew up in a household shaped by the urban, segregated domestic life she later described in her memoir. She studied in Rabat and at the Sorbonne, and received her doctorate at Brandeis University in the early 1970s. She taught sociology at Mohammed V University in Rabat and worked as a researcher and public intellectual. She received the Prince of Asturias Prize for Literature in 2003, shared with Susan Sontag.

## Main Ideas

- **Gender relations in Muslim societies.** In *Beyond the Veil* (1975), she argued that sexual segregation and the fear of women's sexuality reflect social and economic organisation and the need to control women's roles, rather than being a fixed feature of Islam.
- **Critique of religious misogyny.** In *The Veil and the Male Elite* (French original *Le harem politique*, 1987; English 1991), she examined early Islamic sources and argued that certain misogynistic traditions were later constructions linked to male political interests, and that early Islam contained egalitarian potential.
- **Women in Muslim political history.** *The Forgotten Queens of Islam* (French original 1990; English 1993) studied women who held political power.
- **Democracy and modernity.** *Islam and Democracy: Fear of the Modern World* (1992) discussed Arab anxieties about modernity, the state and political participation.

## Case Studies

*Beyond the Veil* drew on her doctoral research in Morocco, including interviews with women and men about family life, work and sexuality. *The Veil and the Male Elite* examined specific traditions attributed to the Prophet and transmitted by early figures such as Abu Hurayra, and the controversies around Aisha's role in the early community, to show how some misogynistic claims were contested and constructed.

## Method

She worked with interviews, field studies and close textual analysis of historical religious sources, and often wrote in an accessible style intended for a broad readership.

## Related Concepts

Mernissi's work connects with several wider concepts. Her analysis of sexual segregation is closely tied to patriarchy and to the distinction between public and private space, since *Beyond the Veil* treats the control of boundaries as a way of ordering relations between women and men. Her historical reading of religious traditions belongs to Islamic feminism, which argues for women's rights through renewed engagement with sacred texts and early history, although her work also draws on secular social science. The critique of Western images of the harem in *Scheherazade Goes West* links to Orientalism, the concept Edward Said used for Western representations of the East that serve relations of power. Her studies of women rulers raise the concepts of political legitimacy and authority, while *Islam and Democracy* engages with modernity, secularism, citizenship and the state. Her attention to education, paid work and urban change in Morocco also relates to social change and the gendered division of labour. These links are points of comparison and do not imply that she adopted every term.

## Influence and Criticism

Mernissi is a key figure in Islamic and Arab feminism and in debates about the veil and women's rights. Critics have questioned her reading of the sources and her historical claims, and some Western uses of her work have been called simplifying. Others see her critique of Orientalist stereotypes, notably in *Scheherazade Goes West*, as a corrective to Western images of Muslim women.
