# Herbert Blumer

Herbert Blumer (1900-1987) was an American sociologist who named symbolic interactionism and developed it into an influential theoretical and methodological position.

## Life and Context

Blumer was born in St. Louis in 1900 and studied at the University of Missouri. He moved to the University of Chicago, where he was shaped by George Herbert Mead and received his doctorate in 1928. He also played professional football during these years. He taught at Chicago until 1952 and then became the first chair of the sociology department at the University of California, Berkeley. He was president of the American Sociological Association in 1956 and died in 1987.

## Main Ideas

Blumer coined the term "symbolic interactionism" in 1937. His later statement rests on three premises:

- people act toward things on the basis of the meanings those things have for them;
- these meanings arise out of social interaction;
- meanings are handled and modified through an interpretive process.

From this he argued that society consists of people in action, who fit their lines of conduct together by interpreting one another. Structures and roles set conditions for conduct but do not determine it. He also wrote on collective behavior, fashion, industrialization and race relations. In "Race Prejudice as a Sense of Group Position" (1958), he argued that prejudice concerns a group's sense of its proper place relative to other groups more than individual feelings.

## Case Study

*Movies and Conduct* formed part of the Payne Fund studies of film and young people. It relied largely on written accounts in which young people described how films had affected their behavior, feelings and ambitions.

## Method

Blumer rejected the idea that social life could be understood mainly by correlating variables, since this passes over the interpretation linking them. He proposed naturalistic inquiry: exploration, which builds close familiarity with a field, followed by inspection, which examines analytic elements carefully against it. He also distinguished "sensitizing concepts", which suggest directions in which to look, from definitive concepts with fixed indicators.

## Influence and Criticism

His position shaped qualitative research, the study of deviance, social problems and social movements, and a generation of interactionist sociologists. Critics argued that it neglects large-scale structure and power and offers limited guidance for testing claims. Some scholars have also disputed how faithfully Blumer represented Mead. Sociologists such as Sheldon Stryker developed a more structural version of interactionism that uses survey methods.

## Cross-References

Compare Mead on the self, Goffman on the interaction order, and Howard S. Becker on labelling and collective activity.
