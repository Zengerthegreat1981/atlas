# John Rogers Searle

A leading American philosopher of mind and language, born in Denver on 31 July 1932. He devised the famous "Chinese Room" thought experiment, arguing that computer programs and the processing of formal symbols (syntax) cannot produce real understanding or semantic consciousness (semantics); and he defended the theory of "biological naturalism" of consciousness and intentionality. He died on 17 September 2025.

## What He Contributed

- **The "Chinese Room" argument (1980)**: one of the most famous thought experiments in contemporary philosophy of mind. A man who does not know Chinese is locked in a room; Chinese symbols reach him, and with him is a rule book in English telling him which symbols to send out in response to which symbols come in, so that it appears to those outside that he **understands Chinese** when he understands not a word of it. Its conclusion: **syntax is not sufficient for semantics**; understanding requires **intentional content** and not merely procedural correctness.
- **The distinction between strong and weak artificial intelligence**: his formulation, which became standard. **Weak**: the computer is a tool for simulating and studying the mind, and he raises no objection to it. **Strong**: the computer programmed with the right program **is actually a mind** and has mental states, and this is the view he takes the Chinese Room to refute. In the same 1980 text he formulated the objections and the replies: the **Systems Reply** (the system as a whole understands even if the man does not), to which his answer is that if the man memorized the whole rule book and became the system he would still understand not a word; and the **Robot Reply** (put the program in a body that moves and senses), to which his answer is that adding receptors and motors adds no semantics, since the robot processes symbols as it did before. Prominent critics of the argument include **Daniel Dennett** (whose reply appeared with the 1980 text) and **Paul and Patricia Churchland** (notably in *Scientific American*, 1990).
- **Speech acts (*Speech Acts*, 1969)**: his founding work in the philosophy of language, built on **J. L. Austin's** *How to Do Things with Words* (1962); the distinction among locution, illocution and perlocution is Austinian rather than Searlean, and Searle's contribution is the systematization and the conditions rather than the original idea. Speaking is an **act** and not merely a description; in "A Taxonomy of Illocutionary Acts" (1975) he classified speech acts into five: **assertives** (describe a state), **directives** (request an action), **commissives** (commit the speaker), **expressives** (show a psychological state), and **declarations** (create a reality by being uttered: "I now pronounce you husband and wife"). He specified for each kind **felicity conditions** for the act to succeed.
- **Intentionality** (*Intentionality*, 1983): the mental state is **about something**: belief is belief that, and desire is desire for. Intentionality for him is a **biological property** that brains produce as the stomach produces digestion; he therefore denies that software suffices to produce it.
- **Biological naturalism** (*The Rediscovery of the Mind*, 1992): consciousness is **real, subjective and irreducible**, and denying it as the eliminativists do is a mistake; it is at the same time a **biological phenomenon** arising from neural processes as the liquidity of water arises from the behavior of its molecules. So there is no second substance and no denial of experience.
- ***The Construction of Social Reality* (1995)**: his analysis of how **institutional facts** exist: money, marriage, borders and property. Its formula for him is **"X counts as Y in context C"** by virtue of **collective intentionality** and mutual recognition. The banknote is money because we all treat it as such, and nothing in its physics makes it so.

## His Place in the Current

Searle was a professor at Berkeley and was shaped by **Austin** at Oxford. He stands as a direct opponent of **Dennett** and **functionalism** on the question of artificial intelligence and consciousness, an opponent of **Churchland** on eliminativism, and he engaged in a sharp exchange with **Derrida** over speech acts. He is best known as the author of a famous argument that **symbol processing is not understanding**.

## Limits and Criticism

- **The premise "syntax is not sufficient for semantics" is contested as assuming the conclusion**: **Dennett** (in his reply published with the 1980 text in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*) and the **Churchlands** hold that the Chinese Room is an "intuition pump" that elicits the verdict from the smallness of the imagined scene (a man and a book) and not from a demonstration, and that the intuition changes if the system is imagined at its true size and speed.
- **Biological naturalism is a position of double denial more than a described mechanism**: it denies dualism and reduction together, but *The Rediscovery of the Mind* (1992) offers no statement of which particular neural properties produce consciousness; this is the problem that **Chalmers** calls the "hard problem" and that Searle considers manufactured from inherited dualist vocabulary.
- **The argument concerns symbolic systems**: the 1980 formulation is aimed at strong artificial intelligence based on the processing of formal symbols, and the extent of its application to later neural networks and probabilistic models is a matter of dispute and not a settled verdict.
- **A documented ethical matter in his career**: in 2019 the University of California, Berkeley withdrew his emeritus status following a university investigation of allegations of sexual harassment and retaliation.
