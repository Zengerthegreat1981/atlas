# Søren Kierkegaard

Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855) was a Danish philosopher and theologian. He is the furthest root of this field: he did not write psychology, but without him it does not exist in its present form.

## Life and Context

Kierkegaard was born in Copenhagen on 5 May 1813. His father, Michael Pedersen, a wealthy and intensely devout wool merchant, left him a lasting sense of guilt. He studied theology at the University of Copenhagen and received his master's degree in 1841 for *The Concept of Irony with Continual Reference to Socrates*. He became engaged to Regine Olsen in 1840 and broke the engagement the following year, never explaining it satisfactorily and writing around it for the rest of his life. In Berlin he attended Schelling's lectures and left disappointed. In 1846 a quarrel with the satirical paper *The Corsair* made him a figure of ridicule in the street. From 1854 he attacked the established Danish church, in the pamphlets of *The Moment* (1855), collapsed in the street and died in Frederik's Hospital on 11 November 1855.

## Main Ideas

**From "what is the human being?" to "how do I exist?"** Against Hegel's all-encompassing system, which explains history but tells no one what to do tomorrow morning, Kierkegaard insisted on the existing individual.

**Pseudonyms.** He published his major works under pseudonyms: *Either/Or* under Victor Eremita, *Fear and Trembling* under Johannes de Silentio, *The Concept of Anxiety* under Vigilius Haufniensis and *The Sickness unto Death* under Anti-Climacus. His identity was known in Copenhagen, so the device was not concealment but method: each name occupies an existential position and speaks from within it, and Kierkegaard asked not to be quoted as the author of their words. Any citation therefore needs the question, who is speaking?

**Three spheres.** The aesthetic (living for the moment and for possibility, avoiding commitment, ending in boredom and despair), the ethical (commitment to universal duty, as in marriage) and the religious (the individual before the absolute). Moving between them is a leap, not a deduction.

**Anxiety and despair.** Anxiety is the dizziness of freedom, the awareness of possibility, not a symptom. In *The Sickness unto Death* the self is "a relation that relates itself to itself" and despair is a disorder in that relation. He distinguishes forms of despair, among them not being aware of being in despair (the most common and least complained of), not willing to be oneself, and willing to be oneself apart from what grounds the self.

**Subjectivity is truth.** This does not mean that whatever you believe is true. In matters on which how one lives depends, such as faith, love and commitment, it is not enough that a claim be correct in itself; how one holds it matters.

**Suspension of the ethical.** In *Fear and Trembling* de Silentio reads the story of Abraham and Isaac and asks what faith is if it is not obedience to a universal rule. He contrasts the "tragic hero", who sacrifices for a general duty everyone understands, with the "knight of faith", who stands alone and cannot explain himself.

## What Remains of Him in the Clinic

The idea that anxiety is not always an illness, that choice is painful by nature, and that the individual cannot be reduced to a classification. May drew on him heavily in *The Meaning of Anxiety* (1950), and his language of anxiety and despair shaped later existential psychotherapy.

## Criticism and Limits

He is not presented as though he spoke in contemporary psychological terms. Any connection of this kind is labeled a retrospective reading. Because of the pseudonyms, attributing a view to him is delicate, and much of what psychological literature quotes comes from books he did not sign. The religious sphere, the aim of his project, is usually dropped from clinical presentations, making him look like a secular existentialist when everything he wrote about anxiety, despair and choice served the question of how to become a Christian. Critics also object that his emphasis on the isolated individual undervalues community and ethics.

## Related Concepts

Anxiety, despair, authenticity, freedom, the leap, existentialism and its religious wing.
