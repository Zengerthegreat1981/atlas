# Abu al-Barakat Ibn al-Anbari

An Iraqi grammarian and linguist from the late period of the flourishing of Arabic grammar in Baghdad, known for his book *al-Insaf fi Masa'il al-Khilaf bayn al-Nahwiyyin al-Basriyyin wa al-Kufiyyin* (Justice in the Points of Disagreement between the Basran and Kufan Grammarians). After centuries of debate between the two schools, this book became the classical reference to which the disagreement between the Basran and Kufan schools of Arabic grammar is reduced.

## Biography

He was Kamal al-Din Abu al-Barakat Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn Ubayd Allah al-Anbari. He was born in Baghdad in 513 AH (1119 CE) and grew up there, studying in Baghdad, where his teachers included Ibn al-Shajari and, at the Nizamiyya madrasa, al-Jawaliqi, which was still a center where the Basran and Kufan heritages met, after they had been practically merged into a single school sometimes called the "Baghdadi school." He became known for his remarkable memory and broad knowledge of the sayings of earlier grammarians from both camps, and he led teaching in Baghdad until his death in 577 AH (1181 CE). This Ibn al-Anbari (Kamal al-Din, sixth century AH) is distinguished from an older grammarian bearing nearly the same name, Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn al-Qasim ibn al-Anbari (d. 328 AH), a different figure who preceded him by two centuries and wrote his own books on language and grammar.

## What he contributed

### *Al-Insaf fi Masa'il al-Khilaf bayn al-Nahwiyyin al-Basriyyin wa al-Kufiyyin*

This is his central work. Its subject is a summary of about one hundred and twenty disputed questions between the grammarians of Basra and the grammarians of Kufa, on matters of inflection, morphology and derivation. For each question it presents the arguments of both sides, then weighs them by evidence and gives a preference. The book is not merely a descriptive history; it is a systematic dialectical work that presents each question as a disagreement with argued positions rather than a mere variance of transmissions. As a result, it became the standard entry point used by later and contemporary scholars to understand the nature of the disagreement between the two schools. Much of what we know today about the arguments of the Kufans in particular, whose original works were largely lost, has come down to us through this book.

### Preserving the heritage of both schools

By gathering the scattered sayings of both camps from earlier works (such as the Kufan works of al-Kisa'i and al-Farra', and later writers like Tha'lab), Ibn al-Anbari preserved much of the Kufan arguments that were threatened with loss. Most of what was written in the history of Arabic grammar after Sibawayh was composed by Basrans or by those affiliated with the dominant Basran current.

## Legacy

*Al-Insaf* has long been read in the study of Arabic grammar and is studied as a methodological introduction to understanding the logic of grammatical argument among the Arabs, not merely for memorizing rules. It has been relied upon extensively in Western and modern Arab studies of the history of Arabic grammar and the origins of the Basran and Kufan schools, and today it is the primary source to which researchers refer when discussing any disputed question between the two schools.

### The method of grammatical argument

*Al-Insaf* presents each question with a fixed method: Ibn al-Anbari first states the opinion and arguments of the Basrans, then the opinion and arguments of the Kufans, then the objections each side raises against the other, and finally he prefers one of the two opinions or reconciles them. This fixed four-part structure (opinion, argument, objection, preference) made the book a model imitated in the literature of "grammatical dispute" (a distinct literary genre in the Arabic heritage, with parallels in jurisprudence and the principles of religion). Ibn al-Anbari was not the first to write about the disagreement between the two schools, but his book is the most fully developed dialectical treatment of it, presenting both opinions while stating his own preference.

## Limitations

The book presents the disagreement in a sharp binary form (Basrans versus Kufans), which may exaggerate the rigidity of the boundaries between the two schools. Modern studies of the history of Arabic grammar (for example by Versteegh and by Bernards) suggest that the dividing line between the two currents was in many questions less sharp than Ibn al-Anbari's presentation suggests, and that many grammarians moved between positions or combined them. Moreover, Ibn al-Anbari's own preferences tend often toward the Basran opinion, which limits his impartiality as an arbiter between the two camps despite the title of his book.

## Major works

- **Al-Insaf fi Masa'il al-Khilaf bayn al-Nahwiyyin al-Basriyyin wa al-Kufiyyin**: his most famous and widely circulated work.
- **Asrar al-'Arabiyya** (The Secrets of Arabic): on the foundations of grammar and its causes (*'illa*).
- **Nuzhat al-Alibba' fi Tabaqat al-Udaba'** (The Pleasure Garden of the Learned in the Generations of Men of Letters): on the biographies of linguists and grammarians.
- **Lum' al-Adilla fi Usul al-Nahw** (The Gleam of the Proofs in the Foundations of Grammar): on the foundations of grammar and its methods of argument.
