# W.E.B. Du Bois

William Edward Burghardt Du Bois (1868-1963) was an American sociologist, historian and civil-rights activist. He pioneered empirical sociology in the United States and was a leading analyst of race and racism.

## Life and Context

Du Bois was born in Great Barrington, Massachusetts. He studied at Fisk University and Harvard, spent time at the University of Berlin, and in 1895 became the first African American to earn a Harvard doctorate. He taught at Wilberforce University, spent a year at the University of Pennsylvania conducting the study that became *The Philadelphia Negro*, and from 1897 taught at Atlanta University, where he directed influential research studies. In 1909 he helped found the NAACP and edited its magazine *The Crisis*. In his later years he became more radical and moved to Ghana, where he died.

## Main Ideas

- **The color line.** He argued that race and its social consequences would be a central problem of the twentieth century.
- **Double consciousness.** In *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903), he described the sense of seeing oneself through the eyes of a society that despises you, a divided awareness shared by Black Americans.
- **The veil.** This metaphor referred to the barrier of racial separation that shapes experience.
- **Racism as structural.** *The Philadelphia Negro* (1899) attributed the social conditions of Black residents to discrimination and history, not to supposed racial traits.
- **Reconstruction.** *Black Reconstruction in America* (1935) challenged the dominant historical account by stressing the role of Black Americans and the part played by labour and class.
- **The Talented Tenth.** He argued that an educated Black elite should lead the race.

## Applications and Cross-References

At Atlanta University Du Bois led a series of annual studies (1898-1914) on Black life, covering topics such as mortality, business, education and crime. For the 1900 Paris Exposition he prepared charts and photographs documenting Black American life, an early example of visual presentation of social data. His ideas on race and empire led him to take part in the Pan-African Congresses. He wrote on the color line in a global frame, and his notion of double consciousness is often discussed alongside Georg Simmel's stranger and Robert Park's marginal man. He also corresponded with Max Weber, and his work on Reconstruction was a direct response to the then dominant Dunning-school interpretation.

## Method

*The Philadelphia Negro* combined house-to-house surveys, statistics, historical analysis and interviews, and is regarded as an early model of empirical urban sociology. The Atlanta University studies extended this work to many topics.

## Influence and Criticism

Du Bois was long marginalised within mainstream sociology, a point developed by Aldon Morris in *The Scholar Denied* (2015). Today he is recognised as a founder of the discipline and of Black studies. His debate with Booker T. Washington over education and strategy is well known. Critics have also noted the elitism of the Talented Tenth idea, which he himself later qualified.
