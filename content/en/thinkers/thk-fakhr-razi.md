# Fakhr al-Din Muhammad ibn Umar al-Razi

A leading mutakallim of Islam in the sixth and seventh centuries AH (the twelfth and thirteenth centuries CE), combining kalam, philosophy and Qur'anic exegesis. He was born in Rayy (Iran) and moved between Rayy, Khwarazm, Transoxiana and Herat. He died in Herat in 606 AH (1210 CE), and is styled "the Imam", "the shaykh of the mutakallimun" and "the son of the preacher of Rayy".

## Biography and formation

He was born in Rayy in Ramadan 544 AH (about 1150 CE) to a father, Diya' al-Din Umar, preacher of Rayy, who was his first teacher; he studied kalam with him, then took jurisprudence, its principles and philosophy from Majd al-Din al-Jili in Maragha, who had studied philosophy and medicine. He then moved to Khwarazm and Transoxiana, where he disputed with the Mu'tazila, the Karramiyya, the philosophers and the Isma'ilis, and according to his biographies he went to Ghazna on some of his journeys. He settled in Herat after the Ghurid sultans and the Khwarazmshah honored him, and he had a gathering of preaching, teaching and disputation attended by princes and scholars. His pupils reported his disputations and anecdotes, and Ibn Abi Usaybi'a wrote a biography of him in *Uyun al-Anba' fi Tabaqat al-Atibba'* because he also wrote on medicine.

## What he gave

- **"The Great Exegesis" (Mafatih al-Ghayb / Keys to the Unseen)**: about 32 parts in the printed editions, the most famous kalam-oriented exegesis of the Qur'an, combining the Mu'tazila, the Ash'aris, the philosophers and the Sufis. The early authorities mention that he did not complete it and that his two pupils Shihab al-Din al-Khuwayyi and Najm al-Din al-Qamuli completed it, and the famous saying that it contains "everything except exegesis", in criticism of its kalam and natural-philosophical digressions, may be attributed to Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi.
- **"Al-Mahsul fi Ilm Usul al-Fiqh" (Al-Mahsul)**: among the most important works written on the principles of jurisprudence after al-Shafi'i's *al-Risala* and al-Ghazali's *al-Mustasfa*; it was abridged by the two al-Urmawis (in *al-Hasil* and *al-Tahsil*) and commented on by al-Qarafi in *Nafa'is al-Usul*, so it became a basis for later texts.
- **"Sharh al-Isharat"**: a commentary on Ibn Sina's *al-Isharat wa-l-Tanbihat*, with philosophical discussions.
- **"Al-Mabahith al-Mashriqiyya"**: on kalam and philosophy, in which he criticized Ibn Sina on some questions.
- **"Al-Matalib al-Aliya min al-Ilm al-Ilahi"**: a late major work on metaphysics and theology, regarded as containing his mature positions.

## Kalam, philosophy and the soul

Al-Razi contributed to transforming Ash'ari kalam from a pattern relying on traditional dialectical proofs into one in which Avicennan philosophical categories are interwoven, combining the sections of logic, natural philosophy and metaphysics in the structure of the kalam book; this trend was called "the method of the later authorities". In *al-Mabahith al-Mashriqiyya* and elsewhere he discussed questions of the soul: its quiddity, immortality, relation to the body and faculties, and he rebutted some of Ibn Sina's proofs for the soul's immateriality, presenting arguments and doubts, and wrote on physiognomy. In the exegesis of "Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds" he discussed the possibility of multiple worlds other than our world, one of his best-known cosmological questions, the study of which has been revived today. In method he tended to hold that reason alone is incapable of deciding some metaphysical questions, and so ended in suspension of judgment or submission.

## His place in the movement

- **The bridge between Ash'arism and philosophy**: he used the philosophers (Ibn Sina especially) in Ash'ari kalam.
- **His doctrinal affiliation**: he was an Ash'ari and a Shafi'i according to his biographers. The claim that he moved toward Imami Shi'ism is a minority, later view that mainstream scholarship does not accept.
- **Influence**: on Mulla Sadra, who benefited from *Mafatih al-Ghayb* in his exegesis; and on Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, not as a pupil but as a productive adversary, since al-Tusi wrote his commentary on Ibn Sina's *Isharat*, called *Hall Mushkilat al-Isharat*, in reply to al-Razi's objections, so that it was said that al-Razi's commentary is a wound and al-Tusi's is a commentary.
- **Influence on later kalam**: his books, especially *al-Muhassal*, *al-Mahsul* and *al-Arba'in*, became foundational texts that were commented on, taught and abridged in the later Ash'ari schools.

## Main works

- *Mafatih al-Ghayb* (The Great Exegesis / Keys to the Unseen)
- *Al-Mahsul fi Ilm Usul al-Fiqh* (foundational work on legal methodology)
- *Sharh al-Isharat wa-l-Tanbihat* (philosophical commentary on Ibn Sina)
- *Al-Mabahith al-Mashriqiyya fi Ilm al-Ilahiyyat wa-l-Tabi'iyyat* (major philosophical compendium)
- *Al-Matalib al-Aliya min al-Ilm al-Ilahi* (late metaphysical masterwork)
- *Muhassal Afkar al-Mutaqaddimin wa-l-Muta'akhkhirin* (survey of philosophical and theological doctrines)
- *Al-Ma'alim fi Usul al-Din* (concise handbook of theology)
- *Nihayat al-Uqul fi Dirayat al-Usul* (detailed theological treatise)
- *Lawami' al-Bayyinat fi Sharh Asma' Allah Ta'ala wa-l-Sifat* (treatise on the divine names)

## His method

Al-Razi was known for a dialectical, exhaustive method: presenting the opponent's view, then raising objections to it, then often stopping at the limits of doubt or suspension (*tawaqquf*) and not at a categorical decision, a prominent feature of his later works in general, most notably *Muhassal Afkar al-Mutaqaddimin wa-l-Muta'akhkhirin*, which al-Tusi made the object of his direct criticism in *Talkhis al-Muhassal*.

## Limits and criticism

His doctrinal affiliation is Ash'ari by the consensus of his biographers, against a later minority claim of Shi'ism. Later hadith scholars and Hanbalis criticized him, among them Ibn Taymiyya in *Dar' Ta'arud al-Aql wa-l-Naql* and *Bayan Talbis al-Jahmiyya*, who attacked his rationalist kalam method and his rule of giving priority to reason in case of conflict ("the universal rule"). His opponents the Karramiyya also accused him in connection with the disputations and political quarrels that took place between him and them in Herat. It is noticeable that his perplexity and suspension of judgment at the end of his life led scholars to differ in determining his "final position", and that some of what is attributed to him, such as the books on magic and talismans like *al-Sirr al-Maktum*, is doubtful as to the authenticity of the attribution. Critics also faulted his works for digression and excessive subdivision.
