# Aletta Biersack: Historical Anthropology and Highland Papua New Guinea

Aletta Biersack is an American anthropologist, long based at the University of Oregon, who works on the Papua New Guinea Highlands and on the theory of history, culture, and ecology in anthropology. She is best known for her ethnographic research among the Ipili and neighbouring peoples (such as the Paiela) of Enga Province, for editing *Clio in Oceania* (1991), a volume that helped define the "historical anthropology" of the Pacific, and for later work on political ecology.

## Fieldwork and Ethnographic Focus

Biersack's fieldwork has been in the western Highlands of Papua New Guinea, in the Porgera and Paiela region of Enga Province. The region later became the site of large-scale gold mining, and her writing attends to how local people interpret the land, its spirits, and its mineral wealth in the context of colonial history and the modern state. Her article "The Mount Kare Python and His Gold: Totemism and Ecology in the Papua New Guinea Highlands" (*American Anthropologist*, 1999) examines how a mythic python and the discovery of gold at Mount Kare were joined in local accounts of land, ancestors, and wealth. She also edited *Papuan Borderlands: Huli, Duna, and Ipili Perspectives on the Papua New Guinea Highlands* (1995), a comparative volume on neighbouring Highland peoples.

## Theoretical Contributions

### History and anthropology

In "Local Knowledge, Local History: Geertz and Beyond" (1989), a chapter in Lynn Hunt's *The New Cultural History*, Biersack discussed how interpretive anthropology in the tradition of Clifford Geertz could be extended to take history seriously. Her introduction to *Clio in Oceania* (1991) and the volume as a whole argued for a historical anthropology in which cultural meanings are not treated as timeless but as shaped and transformed in historical encounters, including colonialism, mission activity, and state formation. The book gathered case studies from across the Pacific, with several contributors reflecting on the work of Marshall Sahlins and others on the relation between structure and event.

### Political ecology

With James B. Greenberg she edited *Reimagining Political Ecology* (2006), which called for a political ecology that combines political-economic analysis with attention to culture, discourse, and meaning, rather than treating nature as a purely material backdrop. She also contributed to debates on ecological anthropology in the legacy of Roy Rappaport.

## Major Works

- *Clio in Oceania: Toward a Historical Anthropology* (editor, Smithsonian Institution Press, 1991).
- *Papuan Borderlands: Huli, Duna, and Ipili Perspectives on the Papua New Guinea Highlands* (editor, University of Michigan Press, 1995).
- "The Mount Kare Python and His Gold: Totemism and Ecology in the Papua New Guinea Highlands." *American Anthropologist* 101(1), 1999.
- *Reimagining Political Ecology* (co-editor with James B. Greenberg, Duke University Press, 2006).

## Influence and Critique

Biersack's work belongs to the broader movement that brought history and anthropology together in the 1980s and 1990s, alongside the work of Sahlins, Sherry Ortner, and others. Critics of that movement have asked whether culturally grounded accounts of history sufficiently address colonial power and political economy, and whether Western historical categories fit local understandings of time. Her work on political ecology is part of a wider effort to bring together cultural and material analyses of environmental change.
