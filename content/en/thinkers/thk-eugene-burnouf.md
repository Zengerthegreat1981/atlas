# Eugène Burnouf

A French orientalist (1801–1852), professor of Sanskrit at the Collège de France (from 1832). He is regarded as a founding figure of the Western academic study of Buddhism (Buddhology) as an independent philological-historical field, and as the teacher of a generation of European orientalists, among them Max Müller himself, who studied under him in Paris before moving to Oxford.

## Historical Context

Before Burnouf, Europe's knowledge of Buddhism was scattered and distorted. It relied mainly on the reports of missionaries and travelers in Asia, who often described it in haughty or polemical terms without a direct textual basis. Burnouf came with a rigorous philological background in Indo-European languages (he had also published foundational works on the Avestan *Yasna* and on Old Persian inscriptions, in the 1830s, and, with Christian Lassen, an *Essai sur le Pali* in 1826). He applied to Buddhism the same method that had been applied to the classical Greek and Latin texts: establishing the original text, dating its layers, and translating it directly without an intermediary.

## *Introduction to the History of Indian Buddhism* (1844)

In this foundational work, Burnouf drew on rare Sanskrit Buddhist manuscripts brought from Nepal (sent to him by Brian Hodgson). He offered an early and influential systematic philological-historical analysis of Buddhism as a religious and philosophical phenomenon with a textual history that can be investigated, rather than a vague "Eastern paganism" as it was popularly portrayed in Europe at the time. He treated Buddhist literature historically, distinguishing among kinds of texts and stages of the tradition, an approach that became influential in later Buddhist studies.

## The Translation of the *Lotus Sutra*

Burnouf translated the *Lotus Sutra* (Saddharmapuṇḍarīka Sūtra) from Sanskrit into French (published after his death in 1852). It is one of the central texts of Mahayana Buddhism. Through this translation, European readers gained access to a major Mahayana scripture in a European language, translated from its Sanskrit original rather than from a Chinese or Tibetan version.

## Major Works

Burnouf's major works include *Introduction to the History of Indian Buddhism* (1844); his translation of the *Lotus Sutra* (1852, published after his death); and his earlier work on the Avesta (*Commentaire sur le Yaçna*, 1833-1835) and on Old Persian cuneiform inscriptions (1836), which established his philological method before he turned to Buddhist studies.

## Influence

Burnouf trained a generation of orientalists, including Max Müller, and French Indology and Buddhist studies continued through his students. He influenced the establishment of the Western academic field of "Buddhist studies," which later expanded through Thomas Rhys Davids (founder of the Pali Text Society) and the German school of orientalism. He is often paired with Rhys Davids in accounts of the philological foundations of modern Western Buddhist studies, Burnouf with Sanskrit sources and Rhys Davids with Pali ones.

## Later Critique

Donald Lopez and other scholars, in critical studies of the history of Buddhist studies under colonialism, have observed that Burnouf's founding generation, despite its great methodological merit, sometimes tended to treat written Sanskrit texts as "true Buddhism," the purest form, in contrast to the living contemporary practice of Asian Buddhists, which was at times implicitly regarded as a popular "deviation" from this imagined textual origin. This critique is a later reassessment of the entire founding generation's project, not of Burnouf alone. It is mentioned here to place his enormous philological achievement within its full critical context.

## His Importance in the History of Comparative Philology

Burnouf is also a significant figure in the history of European comparative philology in general, not only Buddhist studies. His early works on the Avesta and Old Persian contributed to the understanding of the ancient Indo-Iranian languages, a central concern of nineteenth-century comparative Indo-European philology.

## Conclusion

Burnouf was an encyclopedic philologist who moved from Avestan and Old Persian to Sanskrit and Pali, combining these fields in a historical-philological project that helped lay the foundations of modern Buddhist studies and Indology. He is commonly cited as a starting point of the academic study of Buddhism in Europe.
