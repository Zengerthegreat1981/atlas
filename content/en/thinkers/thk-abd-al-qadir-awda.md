# Abd al-Qadir Awda

An Egyptian judge and lawyer who received a modern positive legal training before turning to the study of Islamic criminal jurisprudence with a rigorous comparative method. His book *Criminal Legislation in Islam Compared with Positive Law* (*al-Tashri' al-Jina'i al-Islami Muqarana bi-l-Qanun al-Wad'i*) is among the influential modern Arabic works that attempt to present Islamic criminal jurisprudence in contemporary legal language capable of direct dialogue with the concepts of positive criminal law.

## Professional and Intellectual Career

Abd al-Qadir Awda trained in law in Egypt and worked as a judge and lawyer within Egypt's modern judicial system, which was influenced by European legal traditions, especially French ones. This background gave him a precise technical familiarity with the concepts of positive criminal law, such as the material and mental elements of a crime, the theory of punishment, and grounds of justification. It later enabled him to draw precise, systematic comparisons between these concepts and their counterparts in classical Islamic criminal jurisprudence.

## What He Offered: Islamic Criminal Legislation Compared with Positive Law

This encyclopedic work presents Islamic criminal jurisprudence (hudud, qisas, diya and ta'zir) using a modern analytical method. It employs contemporary legal terms and classifications, such as the elements of a crime, the conditions of criminal responsibility, grounds of justification and grounds of exemption from punishment, with continuous comparison to the provisions of Egyptian and French positive criminal law on corresponding matters. Awda did not limit himself to presenting traditional juristic rulings. He sought to show that Islamic criminal jurisprudence contains procedural guarantees (such as requiring certainty in proving the hudud and averting the hudud on the basis of doubt) that, in his view, compare favorably with those of modern positive criminal law.

## His Comparative Method

Awda's style is marked by a commitment to an organized comparative method that presents each issue from two angles: classical Islamic jurisprudence in its various schools, and contemporary positive law with its doctrinal schools. In each issue he sought to highlight points of convergence and divergence between the two systems. This method made his book a doubly useful reference: for the researcher in Islamic jurisprudence wishing to understand its position relative to modern legal concepts, and for the positive lawyer wishing to become acquainted with the Islamic criminal system in a language he can understand.

## His Position on the State and Governance

Alongside his interest in criminal legislation, Awda also wrote on the question of the system of government in Islam, adopting a position opposed to that of Ali Abd al-Raziq. He held that Islam has a system of government with clearly defined features, resting on the principles of consultation (*shura*), justice and the sovereignty of Sharia. This contrasted with Abd al-Raziq's thesis, which denied that Islam establishes a system of government binding by a definitive text. The two thinkers' positions thus represent opposing models of two main currents in the modern Arab intellectual debate on the relationship of Islam to the state.

## Political Context and His End

Abd al-Qadir Awda was affiliated with the Muslim Brotherhood in Egypt and was executed in 1954 in the aftermath of an assassination attempt on Gamal Abdel Nasser, which the group was accused of planning. The case remains a subject of historical and political debate about the fairness of the trial and the validity of the accusations against him. This debate does not usually affect the academic assessment of his specialized legal work on comparative criminal jurisprudence.

## Impact

Awda's book is among the widely cited modern Arabic works in discussions of applying the hudud or comparing Islamic criminal jurisprudence with positive law. Its influence has continued in the later writings of Islamic movements and of academic researchers alike, as a pioneering attempt to present Islamic criminal jurisprudence in a modern legal form that can be taught and directly compared with positive law.

## His Place in the History of Comparative Criminal Jurisprudence

Abd al-Qadir Awda occupies a special place in the history of modern Arabic juristic writing. He preceded in time many later efforts to present Islamic criminal jurisprudence in a modern legal language capable of addressing lawyers trained in positive law, not only traditional jurists. His book has remained a reference cited in contemporary debates on applying the hudud, whether by those who call for this application on the basis of the strict procedural guarantees Awda presented, or by those who question whether these rulings can be applied in a modern state. The book is thus used as a reference in several intellectual and political directions, though its author himself held a clear position supporting the application of Sharia.

## Major Works

- **Criminal Legislation in Islam Compared with Positive Law**: an encyclopedia of comparative Islamic criminal jurisprudence.
- **Islam and Our Political Conditions** (*al-Islam wa Awda'una al-Siyasiyya*): a study of the system of government in Islam.
- **Islam and Our Legal Conditions** (*al-Islam wa Awda'una al-Qanuniyya*): a study of the place of Sharia in the contemporary legal system.
