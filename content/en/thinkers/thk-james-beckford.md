# James A. Beckford

A British sociologist (1942–2022), emeritus professor at the University of Warwick. He was a leading researcher in the sociology of new religious movements, alongside Eileen Barker, but with a different focus. Rather than the long-term ethnographic study of a particular membership, Beckford concentrated on analyzing the **institutional and comparative response** of states, official churches and the media to these movements across different European countries.

## His Founding Study: Societal Response to the Unification Church

In *Cult Controversies: The Societal Response to the New Religious Movements* (1985), Beckford compared how societies, including Britain, the United States, France and West Germany, responded to new religious movements such as the Unification Church (the "Moonies"). He argued that the character of the response is shaped substantially by each society's institutional arrangements for religion and by the interests of the groups involved, and not by the characteristics of the movements alone.

## The Methodological Contribution: From the Individual to the Structure

Beckford's work complements Barker's. Where Barker focused on the precise empirical question ("Is the individual who joins being made through brainwashing?"), Beckford opened the broader structural question ("Why do the institutions of one society respond with greater panic than those of another society to the same movement?"). He thus offered a sociological-institutional framework that complements the brainwashing debate rather than replacing it.

## Religion in Prison

At a later stage of his career, Beckford extended his institutional interest to a very different context: how religious practice is organized inside prisons. In *Religion in Prison: Equal Rites in a Multi-Faith Society* (with Sophie Gilliat-Ray, 1998), he examined how the British prison administration negotiates the provision of religious services to prisoners of many faiths (Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Sikhism and others). The study examines tensions between formal equality of provision and the historically privileged position of the Church of England, and it is an influential study of religion in prisons.

## The General Theoretical Framework

In *Social Theory and Religion* (2003), Beckford surveyed how the major sociological theorists (Durkheim, Marx, Weber, and later Bourdieu and Foucault) dealt with the religious phenomenon. He argued that religion remains a central subject for understanding social modernity, rather than receding to the margins as classical secularization theories assumed.

## Muslims in Prison

With Danièle Joly and Farhad Khosrokhavar, Beckford co-authored *Muslims in Prison: Challenge and Change in Britain and France* (2005), a comparative study of how prison systems in the two countries accommodate Muslim inmates and how religion figures in prisoners' lives.

## Academic Standing

Beckford is considered a prominent British theorist of the sociology of religion. Through his long career at the University of Warwick he has influenced research on new religious movements and on religion and state in comparative European contexts.

## Additional Works

Beckford has also written on how European states manage religious diversity, and his book *Religion and Advanced Industrial Society* (1989) is a notable contribution to the sociology of religion.

## Concluding Academic Standing

Beckford is a significant figure in the development of the comparative sociology of religion in Europe. He has also been active in international associations for the sociology of religion.

## Conclusion

Beckford complements the individual ethnographic perspective of Barker with a structural perspective on how institutions respond to new religious movements and manage religious plurality.
