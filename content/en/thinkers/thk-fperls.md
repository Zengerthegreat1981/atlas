# Fritz Perls

German-American psychiatrist (1893–1970), one of the founders of **Gestalt Therapy**.

## His Place in the Field

Friedrich Samuel Perls was born in 1893 in Berlin to a Jewish family and specialized in psychiatry. He was analyzed by **Karen Horney** and later by **Wilhelm Reich**, and from Reich came a deep influence on his work: attention to the body and muscular tension. He worked as an assistant to **Kurt Goldstein** at the Frankfurt institute for neurology, and there he met Lore Posner (Laura); from Goldstein he took the idea of the organism as a whole that cannot be understood by dividing it. He fled Nazi Germany in 1933, first to the Netherlands and then to South Africa, where he and Laura founded an institute for psychoanalysis in Johannesburg, and then they emigrated to the United States in 1946.

In New York he crossed paths with Maslow, Rollo May and Erich Fromm, and with **Paul Goodman** and **Ralph Hefferline** he put together the book *Gestalt Therapy* (1951); in 1952 the **New York Institute for Gestalt Therapy** was founded and was carried on by Laura Perls after Fritz turned to travel. In 1964 he moved to live at the **Esalen Institute** in Big Sur and became the star of the confrontation workshops, then left it in 1969 for Lake Cowichan on Vancouver Island to found a resident therapeutic community, and he died in Chicago in March 1970.

## The Question of Attribution

The foundations of the method are attributed to **Perls, Hefferline and Goodman** in *Gestalt Therapy* (1951): **Goodman** wrote the greater part of the theoretical volume, and **Hefferline** contributed the exercises of the practical volume and tried them out on his students. **Laura Perls** contributed to *Ego, Hunger and Aggression* (1942) without her name on the cover and took part in founding the New York Institute in 1952, so attributing the school to Fritz alone is a recurrent error.

## Principal Contributions

- **Here-and-Now**: he rejected the analytic emphasis on the unconscious past in favor of the present moment.
- **Figure-Ground**: he transferred the principles of Gestalt perception (the Berlin school: Wertheimer, Köhler and Koffka) to psychological experience — the therapist's task is to help the patient become aware of the figure he avoids.
- **Unfinished Business**: past experiences left hanging that consume the patient's energy in the present; the treatment is to complete them dramatically.
- **The Empty Chair technique**: the patient addresses an empty chair on which he imagines another person or an aspect of himself; from it the "two-chair technique" passed into emotion-focused therapy.
- **Contact Boundary**: personality takes shape at the point of interaction between the individual and his environment. Its theoretical formulation is in the 1951 book, not in Perls's solo writings.
- **Creative Indifference**: he borrowed it from the philosopher **Salomo Friedlaender** — standing at the zero point between two opposite poles without prior bias.

## Major Works

- *Ego, Hunger and Aggression* (1942) — written in South Africa.
- *Gestalt Therapy: Excitement and Growth in the Human Personality* (1951) — with Hefferline and Goodman.
- *Gestalt Therapy Verbatim* (1969) — from recordings of his workshops.
- *In and Out the Garbage Pail* (1969) — his autobiography.
- *The Gestalt Approach & Eye Witness to Therapy* — published posthumously in 1973.

## The Controversy Around Him

The best-known controversy concerns the **Esalen years** (1964–1969) and his style in them: sharp confrontation before an audience, and therapy displayed in a single session before a workshop. This became the image of Gestalt therapy in people's minds, while the theoretical foundation laid by Goodman and Hefferline in the 1951 book remained the least-read book in the school. In his last years Perls left Esalen for Cowichan (1969) to found a resident community, a move away from the workshop format that had made him famous. Then the next generation — Laura Perls, Isadore From, and Erving and Miriam Polster — restored the relational dimension and the 1951 text to the method.

## Limitations

His techniques were tested after their transfer to other schools (the empty chair within emotion-focused therapy is the clearest example), whereas the confrontational Perlsian form as practiced at Esalen was never translated into a verifiable protocol. And *In and Out the Garbage Pail* (1969) is a literary text he wrote about himself, so it cannot properly be taken as an independent biographical source.
