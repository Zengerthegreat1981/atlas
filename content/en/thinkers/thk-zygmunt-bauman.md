# Zygmunt Bauman

Zygmunt Bauman (1925-2017) was a Polish-British sociologist and philosopher best known for the concept of "liquid modernity" and for his analyses of modernity and the Holocaust, consumerism, globalization, and ethics.

## Life and Context

Bauman was born in Poznań, Poland, in 1925 into a Jewish family. His family fled to the Soviet Union after the German invasion in 1939, and he served in the Polish army formed there. After the war he became a sociologist at the University of Warsaw. In 1968, during an antisemitic campaign led by the Polish government, he was dismissed and left Poland. After a period in Israel he settled in 1971 at the University of Leeds, where he became professor of sociology and later emeritus. He died in Leeds in 2017.

## Main Ideas

- **Modernity and the Holocaust:** Bauman argued that the Holocaust was not a lapse from modernity but a product of modern bureaucratic rationality, technology, and the administrative organization of society.
- **Liquid modernity:** contemporary society is marked by fluidity, uncertainty, and the dissolving of fixed structures, in contrast with a "solid" earlier modernity.
- **Individualization and consumerism:** identity is increasingly a task for the individual, and consumption shapes social relations.
- **Globalization:** it divides people into mobile elites ("tourists") and those who are immobile ("vagabonds").
- **Postmodern ethics:** moral responsibility toward the Other, influenced by Emmanuel Levinas, cannot be reduced to rules.
- **Liquid love and fear:** relationships become more fragile and fears are diffuse.

## Method

Bauman wrote in an essayistic, interpretive, and literary style, drawing on philosophy, history, and everyday observation, and he saw sociology as a dialogue that interprets experience rather than a search for laws.

## Influence and Criticism

He was widely read beyond sociology and received the European Amalfi Prize for Sociology and Social Sciences and the Theodor W. Adorno Prize (1998). Critics have argued that his work is impressionistic, that "liquid" metaphors lack precise definition and empirical testing, and that his account of the Holocaust understates its specific German and ideological sources. Revelations about his early service in Polish security and military structures also prompted public controversy.

## Applications and Cross-References

Liquid modernity is used in studies of precarious work, identity, and consumption. Compare Weber on rationalization, Durkheim on solidarity, and Foucault on discipline.
