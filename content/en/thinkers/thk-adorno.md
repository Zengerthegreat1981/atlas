# Theodor Adorno

Theodor W. Adorno (born Theodor Ludwig Wiesengrund, 1903–1969) was a German philosopher, sociologist, musicologist and composer, one of the leading figures of the Frankfurt School of critical theory. His work combines Hegelian-Marxist social theory, a philosophy of music and aesthetics, and a sustained critique of Enlightenment rationality, the culture industry and the philosophy of Heidegger.

## Life

Adorno was born in Frankfurt am Main, studied philosophy, musicology, sociology and psychology there, and took his doctorate in 1924 with a dissertation on Husserl. He studied composition in Vienna with Alban Berg in 1925–1926. His habilitation on Kierkegaard (*Kierkegaard: Construction of the Aesthetic*, 1933), supervised by Paul Tillich, appeared as the Nazis came to power; he lost his university licence in 1933 and emigrated, first to Oxford (1934) and then, in 1938, to the United States, where he worked with Max Horkheimer's Institute for Social Research in New York and Los Angeles. He returned to Frankfurt in 1949, became a professor and, in 1958, director of the Institute. He died in 1969 at Visp in Switzerland.

## Principal ideas

- **Critique of instrumental reason**: in *Dialectic of Enlightenment* (written with Horkheimer, circulated in 1944 and published in 1947) he argued that Enlightenment, in seeking to master nature through calculation, tends to turn into new forms of domination and myth, and that the "culture industry" turns art into standardized commodities.
- **Negative dialectics**: in *Negative Dialectics* (1966) he rejected any identity of thought and object and any final synthesis. Concepts never fully capture what they name, and philosophy should attend to the "non-identical", the particular that systems suppress. He treated the suffering of individuals as a form of truth that resists totalizing systems.
- **Critique of Heidegger and "authenticity"**: in *The Jargon of Authenticity* (1964) he argued that the vocabulary of existential philosophy and of Heidegger (authenticity, anxiety, resoluteness) functions as an ideology that mystifies social conditions and disguises itself as profundity. *Negative Dialectics* also contains a critique of Heidegger's "ontological need".
- **Aesthetics and music**: *Philosophy of New Music* (1949) contrasts Schoenberg and Stravinsky, and *Aesthetic Theory* (published posthumously in 1970) holds that autonomous art expresses social contradictions in its form and so resists the culture industry.
- **Ethics after catastrophe**: in *Minima Moralia* (1951), a collection of aphorisms written in exile, he wrote that "there is no right life in the wrong one" (§18), and in an essay of 1949 he remarked that writing poetry after Auschwitz is barbaric, a statement he later qualified.
- **Empirical social research**: he took part in *The Authoritarian Personality* (1950) and in the German "positivism dispute" with Karl Popper's followers in the 1960s.

## Place in the tradition

Adorno belongs to the Frankfurt School, and not to existentialism; his relation to existentialism and to Heidegger is one of critique from the standpoint of critical theory and a heterodox Marxism. He influenced later critical theory (Jürgen Habermas was his assistant and later a critic), aesthetics, cultural studies and the philosophy of music.

## Limits and criticisms

- **Elitism and pessimism**: critics, including Habermas in some respects, find his view of mass culture dismissive and his philosophy of history pessimistic, leaving little room for political action.
- **Reading of Heidegger**: scholars dispute whether the *Jargon* does justice to Heidegger's philosophy, since it criticizes a style of language more than it analyses Heidegger's arguments in detail.
- **Style**: his dense, paratactic writing and refusal of systematic exposition make his work difficult to interpret.

## Principal works

*Dialectic of Enlightenment* (*Dialektik der Aufklärung*, 1947, with Max Horkheimer) · *Philosophy of New Music* (1949) · *The Authoritarian Personality* (1950, with others) · *Minima Moralia* (1951) · *The Jargon of Authenticity* (*Jargon der Eigentlichkeit*, 1964) · *Negative Dialectics* (*Negative Dialektik*, 1966) · *Aesthetic Theory* (*Ästhetische Theorie*, 1970).
