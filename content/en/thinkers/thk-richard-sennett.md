# Richard Sennett

An American-British sociologist, professor at New York University and the London School of Economics, and one of the best-known contemporary writers on work, the city and public life. He is known for his influential diagnosis of the effect of the new "flexible capitalism" on individual character and stable human relationships.

## Life and Context

Sennett was born in Chicago in 1943 and trained as a cellist until a hand injury ended his musical career. He then turned to the social sciences and history and took his doctorate at Harvard, where David Riesman was a formative influence. Hannah Arendt was an early mentor whose work on public life shaped his interests. He has taught at New York University and at the London School of Economics for many years.

## Early Work

*The Uses of Disorder* (1970) argued that cities should tolerate conflict and diversity rather than enforce order. *The Hidden Injuries of Class* (1972, with Jonathan Cobb) was an interview study of white working-class men in Boston about dignity, respect and the psychological costs of class inequality. *The Fall of Public Man* (1977) traced the decline of public life and the rise of intimacy as the dominant social ideal from the eighteenth century onward.

## The Central Idea

In *The Corrosion of Character: The Personal Consequences of Work in the New Capitalism* (1998), Sennett drew on interviews with people affected by corporate restructuring, including former IBM employees and workers in a computerized Boston bakery. He argued that the "flexible capitalism" of continual restructuring, short-term projects and temporary work undermines what stable careers once supplied: long-term mutual commitment, a coherent occupational identity, and the ability to narrate one's life as a connected story. He warned that this erosion does not remain in the workplace but affects character, intimate relationships and the capacity for long-term trust. His diagnosis was an early and influential sociological statement of themes later developed by researchers such as Jennifer Silva and Allison Pugh with more recent ethnographic methods, by Arne Kalleberg with quantitative data on job polarization and insecurity (*Good Jobs, Bad Jobs*, 2011), by Guy Standing, who argued that insecurity defines a new class, the "precariat", and by Steven Vallas, who has studied platform work.

## Later Work

*The Craftsman* (2008) examined skilled practice and the satisfaction of doing something well for its own sake, in implicit contrast with the flexible economy. *Together: The Rituals, Pleasures and Politics of Cooperation* (2012) studied the skills of cooperation amid social fragmentation. Both belong to a trilogy on material culture and social life, completed by *Building and Dwelling* (2018), which returns to the city.

## Recognition

Sennett has received several honours, among them the Hegel Prize (2006) and the Prince of Asturias Award for the Social Sciences (2010).

## Critique and Assessment

Some critics argue that Sennett generalizes from a relatively small set of qualitative interviews, so that broader quantitative evidence is needed to show how widespread the pattern is. Others suggest that his account carries nostalgia for the stable industrial career, overlooking its monotony and rigid hierarchy. His argument about the link between work flexibility and the fragility of identity nevertheless remains a standard reference in discussions of the human consequences of changing labor markets, including the platform economy.
