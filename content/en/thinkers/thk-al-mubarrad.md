# Abu al-Abbas al-Mubarrad

A grammarian, philologist and man of letters of the Basran school, one of the last great imams of the Basran grammatical tradition, author of *al-Muqtadab*, a major work of Arabic grammar after Sibawayh's *al-Kitab*, and the famous rival of Abu al-Abbas Thaʿlab in the assemblies of Baghdad.

## Biography

His name was Abu al-Abbas Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Mubarrad. He was born in Basra in 210 AH (c. 825 CE), where he studied the sciences of grammar and philology under its leading scholars, most notably al-Mazini and al-Jarmi, from whom he inherited the tradition of Sibawayh and al-Akhfash, which connected directly back to the circle of al-Khalil ibn Ahmad. He was summoned to Samarra to serve at the court of the Abbasid caliph al-Mutawakkil. After al-Mutawakkil was killed in 247 AH (861 CE), al-Mubarrad moved to Baghdad, where he spent most of his subsequent life as a teacher and author. There he presided over the assemblies of grammar and literature as a rival to Thaʿlab, the leader of the Kufans in the capital, so that the two leading schools came together in Baghdad. He died in Baghdad in 286 AH (or 285 AH), about 898 CE.

## His Contributions

### *al-Muqtadab*

His major grammatical work, and a principal Arabic grammar after Sibawayh's *al-Kitab*. It builds on Sibawayh's material but is an independent, systematically organized treatment with al-Mubarrad's own judgments; he also criticized some of Sibawayh's positions in a separate refutation (the subject of Bernards's study). The book arranges the chapters of Arabic grammar in a more methodical order than *al-Kitab* does, which made it a widely used educational reference among later students. *al-Muqtadab* was widely influential on later Basran grammarians such as Ibn al-Sarraj.

### *al-Kamil fi al-Lugha wa-l-Adab* (*The Complete Work on Language and Literature*)

An encyclopedic work that combines grammar, philology, literature and history. In it al-Mubarrad presents selections of poetry, proverbs, narratives and sayings of the Prophet, subjecting them to grammatical and literary analysis. It blends the function of a linguistic reference with that of a literary anthology. It is a well-known example of this kind of writing that unites language and literature in a single work, comparable in genre to the later *adab* compilations.

### Defending the Basran Method against the Kufans

In Baghdad, al-Mubarrad represented the strict Basran position against Thaʿlab and his Kufan school. Their assemblies and debates were a living part of the continuing dispute between the two schools after the generation of al-Kisa'i and al-Farra'. This dispute was documented by later sources (most notably Ibn al-Anbari in *al-Insaf*), which treat it as a direct continuation of the early debates between Sibawayh and al-Kisa'i.

### His Students and the Continuation of His School

A number of major philologists and grammarians were trained in al-Mubarrad's circle in Baghdad and transmitted the Basran doctrine to later generations. The most prominent among them was Abu Bakr ibn al-Sarraj, who later wrote *al-Usul fi al-Nahw* under the influence of his teacher's method of organization and reasoning (*taʿlil*). Another was al-Zajjaj, who combined grammar with linguistic interpretation of the Quran in a manner close to what the Kufan al-Farra' had done earlier. In this way al-Mubarrad's grammatical school continued for a generation after him, maintaining the Basran line within Baghdad, which had by then become the meeting point of the two schools.

## Impact

*al-Muqtadab* became an important reference text in the Arabic grammatical tradition after *al-Kitab*, and later grammarians drew on it in organizing their material. His debates with Thaʿlab also provide a living model of the scholarly assembly culture of Abbasid Baghdad, which preserved many details of the grammatical dispute between the Basrans and Kufans. Modern scholars (such as Monique Bernards and Ramzi Baalbaki) have drawn on his work to study how the Sibawayh tradition was reread and transformed across successive generations of commentators.

## Limitation

Al-Mubarrad is sometimes criticized for his strong partisanship toward the Basran school. This sometimes made his presentation of Kufan views in his works closer to refutation than to neutral exposition. His personal biography also contains accounts of a sharp rivalry with Thaʿlab that sometimes descends to the level of personal attack. This makes it difficult, when reading later sources, to separate the serious methodological disagreement from personal competition for the leading position in Baghdad.

## Principal Works

- **al-Muqtadab**: his major grammatical work, in several volumes.
- **al-Kamil fi al-Lugha wa-l-Adab**: a philological and literary encyclopedia.
- Other works, including a refutation of Sibawayh and smaller works on language, prosody and rhyme, listed in sources such as *al-Fihrist*.
