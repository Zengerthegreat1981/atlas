# Ibn Khaldun

Ibn Khaldun (1332-1406) was a North African Arab historian and scholar whose *Muqaddimah* is often regarded as a pioneering work of sociology and the philosophy of history. He sought to identify the underlying laws governing the rise and decline of dynasties and states.

## Life and Context

Abd al-Rahman ibn Khaldun was born in Tunis in 1332 into a family of Andalusian origin. He was educated in the Islamic sciences, law, and philosophy. He held posts in the courts of Tunis, Fez, Granada, and Bejaia, and experienced political intrigue and imprisonment. Around 1375-1378 he withdrew to a fortress at Qal'at ibn Salama in present-day Algeria and began writing. He later moved to Cairo, where he taught and served as a Maliki judge. He met Timur (Tamerlane) outside Damascus in 1401. He died in Cairo in 1406.

## Main Ideas

- **Asabiyyah:** social cohesion or group solidarity, strongest among nomadic and tribal groups, which enables them to conquer and found states.
- **Dynastic cycle:** dynasties rise through solidarity, enjoy prosperity, and decline as luxury and weakened solidarity set in. He associated this with a span of roughly three or four generations.
- **Umran:** the science of civilization, which he proposed as a new science studying society and its conditions.
- **Bedouin and sedentary life:** contrasts between rural nomadic and urban settled societies drive historical change.
- **Economics:** he discussed labor as a source of value, the division of labor, and the effects of taxation on production.
- **Critical historiography:** he criticized accounts of the past that accepted reports without testing their plausibility against social conditions.

## Major Works

- *Kitab al-Ibar* (Book of Lessons), a universal history, completed in its main form in the 1370s-1380s
- *Al-Muqaddimah* (the Introduction, 1377), the theoretical prolegomenon to the *Kitab al-Ibar*
- *Al-Ta'rif* (autobiography)

## Method

Ibn Khaldun used historical comparison and reasoning about causes in social conditions, treating human society as having regularities that can be investigated. He insisted that historical reports be judged by the nature of social life.

## Influence and Criticism

Rediscovered by European scholars in the nineteenth century, including through Silvestre de Sacy and the translation by William McGuckin de Slane, and later by Franz Rosenthal's English translation (1958), he has been compared with Machiavelli, Vico, Montesquieu, and Comte. Scholars debate whether he was truly a sociologist in the modern sense, whether his cycle generalizes beyond the Maghreb, and the degree to which modern readings project later ideas onto him.

## Applications and Cross-References

Asabiyyah has been used in studies of state formation, ethnicity, and political cohesion. Compare Comte's stages, Weber's studies of legitimate domination, and Durkheim's work on solidarity.
