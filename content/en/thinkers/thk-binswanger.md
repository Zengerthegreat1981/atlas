# Ludwig Binswanger

A Swiss psychiatrist (1881–1966), long-time director of the Bellevue Sanatorium in Kreuzlingen, and the originator of "existential analysis" (Daseinsanalyse). He was one of the first to apply Husserl's phenomenology and Heidegger's analysis of Dasein to psychiatric practice, and one of the first to be accused of misunderstanding Heidegger.

## Life and Context

Born in Kreuzlingen in 1881 into a family of psychiatrists, he studied medicine at Lausanne, Heidelberg and Zurich, trained under Eugen Bleuler at the Burghölzli clinic and worked with C. G. Jung. In 1907 he met Sigmund Freud, with whom he kept a friendship until Freud's death in 1939. From 1910 until 1956 he directed the Bellevue Sanatorium founded by his grandfather. He also drew on Husserl, whom he visited in Freiburg, and on Martin Buber, and found in Heidegger's *Being and Time* (1927) a way of describing human existence that avoided reducing the person to a natural object. He died in Kreuzlingen in 1966.

## What He Did

He built "existential analysis": describing the patient's world as a whole (his time, space, body, and relationships) instead of breaking it down into symptoms or explaining them from hypothetical mechanisms. His detailed case studies, among them Ellen West, Suzanne Urban and Lola Voss, became classics of phenomenological psychiatry.

## The Key Concept

Every human being has a private world-design (*Weltentwurf*), a way in which everything takes shape for him. And disorder is a narrowing or distortion of this design, not a fault in a machine. In his later work he described forms of missed existence such as *Verstiegenheit* (extravagance), *Verschrobenheit* (eccentricity) and *Manieriertheit* (mannerism), and he added to Heidegger's care (*Sorge*) the dimension of love as a basic mode of being with others.

## His Relationship with Freud

A friendship that continued despite deep theoretical disagreement, which is one of the rare cases in the history of this field. Binswanger respected Freud's clinical genius while rejecting what he saw as a naturalistic reduction of the person, and he recorded the friendship in his *Erinnerungen an Sigmund Freud* (1956).

## The Objection to Him

That his reading turned an ontological analysis into a theory of the self, that is, it returned it to what Heidegger was transcending. Heidegger himself, in the Zollikon seminars with Medard Boss, regarded this as a misreading, and Boss developed a rival Daseinsanalysis that stayed closer to Heidegger's text.

## His Most Important Works

*Grundformen und Erkenntnis menschlichen Daseins* (1942) · *Über Ideenflucht* (1933) · *Drei Formen missglückten Daseins* (1956) · *Schizophrenie* (1957) · *Melancholie und Manie* (1960) · *Being-in-the-World*, selected writings translated by Jacob Needleman (1963)
