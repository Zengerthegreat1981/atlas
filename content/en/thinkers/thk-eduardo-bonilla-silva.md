# Eduardo Bonilla-Silva

An American sociologist of Puerto Rican origin, professor of sociology at Duke University, and one of the most important theorists of contemporary race and racism in American sociology. He is known for his central concept of "color-blind racism," which describes how American racism after the civil rights era turned into a more hidden and less explicit form that is no less effective in reproducing racial inequality.

## The Central Idea

In his central book *Racism without Racists: Color-Blind Racism and the Persistence of Racial Inequality in the United States* (2003), Bonilla-Silva put forward the thesis that the explicit racial prejudice of the segregation ("Jim Crow") era has been replaced in recent decades by a new, more hidden form of racism that he called "Color-Blind Racism": a system of "taken-for-granted assumptions" that appear racially neutral and allow individuals who are apparently well-intentioned to support an actually existing system of racial inequality without feeling that they are racially bigoted: "racism without racists." He identified four main discursive frames that make up this racism: "abstract liberalism" (justifying opposition to racial affirmative-action policies in the language of abstract individual freedom of choice without acknowledging existing structural inequality); "naturalization" (explaining residential or social racial segregation as a "natural" result of people's spontaneous preferences for similarity rather than structural discrimination); "cultural racism" (attributing racial inequality to the supposedly deficient "culture" of a given ethnic group rather than to social structure); and "minimization of racism" (claiming that racism has become a historical problem of the past that does not deserve serious contemporary attention).

## Racialized Social Systems

Earlier, in "Rethinking Racism: Toward a Structural Interpretation" (*American Sociological Review*, 1997), Bonilla-Silva argued that racism should be understood structurally, as the foundation of "racialized social systems" in which economic, political, social and ideological positions are partly allocated by racial classification, rather than as a matter of individual prejudice. In later work he argued that the US racial order is becoming more like the stratification found in much of Latin America and the Caribbean, with a "tri-racial" hierarchy (a proposition that has drawn debate). He served as president of the American Sociological Association in 2018.

## Impact

Bonilla-Silva made a central founding contribution to contemporary sociological theory of race and racism, offering a deeply influential analytical framework for understanding the persistence of racial inequality in a post-civil-rights era that officially pretends to complete racial neutrality. The concept of "color-blind racism" became one of the most cited and taught frameworks in the contemporary sociology of race, influencing later research on racial discourse and public debate about racial justice. Critics have questioned how far interview evidence from a limited set of surveys and interviews can establish a general system of discourse, and some argue that the frames overlap or that the concept understates change in racial attitudes.
