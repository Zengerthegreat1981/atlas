# Alfried Längle

Alfried Längle is an Austrian physician, clinical psychologist, and psychotherapist, born in Götzis on 27 March 1951. A former student and close collaborator of Viktor Frankl, Längle became a central figure in the theoretical and institutional development of modern Existential Analysis (Existenzanalyse) as an independent, comprehensive psychotherapeutic modality.

## Core Theories: The Four Fundamental Existential Motivations

Längle's major theoretical contribution is the model of the **four fundamental existential motivations** (Vier Grundmotivationen). While classical logotherapy placed the "will to meaning" at the center of clinical intervention, Längle argued that meaning cannot be addressed effectively in therapy if preceding personal and emotional conditions remain unfulfilled. He formulated four sequential existential questions:

1. **First Fundamental Motivation (The Physical and Ontological Dimension)**: *Can I be in this world?* This concerns space, protection, and somatic grounding. When fulfilled, it yields existential trust and a basic ability to endure conditions; its deficit produces anxiety and insecurity.
2. **Second Fundamental Motivation (The Emotional and Relational Dimension)**: *Do I like my life?* This addresses proximity, relationships, emotional resonance, and the experience of value. Fulfillment fosters love of life and emotional depth; its deficit leads to depression and loss of vitality.
3. **Third Fundamental Motivation (The Personal and Social Dimension)**: *Am I permitted to be myself?* This concerns personal authenticity, justice, self-respect, and boundary recognition. Fulfillment produces self-worth and genuine identity; its deficit leads to shame, guilt, and histrionic or narcissistic adaptations.
4. **Fourth Fundamental Motivation (The Practical and Meaning Dimension)**: *What should I live for?* Only upon the foundation of the prior three motivations can the individual meaningfully discern existential callings, context, and future action. Fulfillment results in existential meaning; its deficit results in emptiness and existential despair.

## Clinical Method: Personal Existential Analysis (PEA)

To apply this framework clinically, Längle developed **Personal Existential Analysis** (Personale Existenzanalyse, PEA). PEA is a structured, phenomenological therapeutic method comprising three functional phases:
- **PEA 1 (Perception and Description)**: Clarifying reality, identifying the client's spontaneous emotional impression, and articulating the primary phenomenological facts.
- **PEA 2 (Personal Resonance and Appraisal)**: Deepening self-understanding, exploring inner dialogue, and assisting the client in taking a personal stance toward their feelings and values.
- **PEA 3 (Response and Integrated Action)**: Deciding upon authentic action, formulating an answer to reality, and committing to meaningful practical implementation.

## The Separation from Frankl and Institutional Development

In 1983, Längle and Frankl co-founded the Society for Logotherapy and Existential Analysis (Gesellschaft für Logotherapie und Existenzanalyse, GLE-Wien) in Vienna, with Frankl serving as honorary president. However, beginning in the late 1980s, significant theoretical differences emerged. Längle incorporated psychodynamic depth, emotional processing, and self-worth into the clinical work, contending that Frankl's focus on objective values and self-transcendence neglected the vulnerable, subjective human ego.

Frankl and orthodox logotherapists (such as Elisabeth Lukas) rejected this shift, arguing that it psychologized existential analysis and compromised logotherapy's distinctive focus on the spiritual/noetic dimension. In 1991, Frankl resigned as honorary president of GLE, formally severing institutional ties. Under Längle's leadership, GLE established Existential Analysis as an officially accredited psychotherapy approach under Austrian and Swiss law, expanding through international affiliates across Europe and the Americas, while the Viktor Frankl Institute preserved classical logotherapy.
