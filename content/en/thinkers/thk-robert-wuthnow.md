# Robert Wuthnow

An American sociologist, professor emeritus at Princeton University and former director of its Center for the Study of Religion, one of the most prominent American sociologists of religion and the author of more than thirty books on religion, culture and civil society.

## Life and Context

Robert Wuthnow (born 1946) received his doctorate in sociology from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1975, where Robert Bellah's work on religion and culture was an important influence. He later moved to Princeton University, where he spent most of his career. His best-known book, *The Restructuring of American Religion*, appeared after the rise of the conservative Christian political movements of the late 1970s and 1980s (the Moral Majority and similar groups), which seemed to cut across the old denominational divisions.

## Approach

Wuthnow shares with Bellah an interest in culture, moral order and the public role of religion, but relies more on large-scale surveys and historical-institutional analysis than on Bellah's cultural-symbolic interpretation (such as the idea of an American "civil religion"). Earlier works such as *Meaning and Moral Order* (1987) developed a cultural sociology that asks how symbolic systems are produced and sustained by social conditions.

## The Restructuring of American Religion

In *The Restructuring of American Religion: Society and Faith Since World War II* (1988), Wuthnow argued that the basic structure of American religious divisions changed after the Second World War. The historically most important division had been among denominations (the Protestant bodies, Catholicism, Judaism). He argued that the most consequential division now cuts across denominations: a liberal current and a conservative current, each present inside every major tradition (liberal and conservative Protestants, liberal and conservative Catholics). He attributed the shift to several interacting factors: the expansion of higher education and of the professional middle class; the growing role of the federal government in moral and social questions such as civil rights, abortion and religion in public schools; and the rise of special-purpose organizations and movements that organize around ideological positions rather than denominational identity.

## Other Work

*Acts of Compassion: Caring for Others and Helping Ourselves* (1991) examined volunteering and caring behavior in American society, drawing on national survey data and interviews, and discussed how religious involvement and civic ties encourage helping and how caring is understood in a culture that prizes individual success. Wuthnow also wrote on spirituality outside traditional institutions (*After Heaven: Spirituality in America Since the 1950s*, 1998) and on small groups, civil society and religion in public life.

## Influence and Critique

Wuthnow's thesis became a standard framework for discussing religious-political polarization in the United States, for example over abortion and homosexuality, as an extension of a liberal-conservative divide that crosses denominational lines. Critics have argued that the thesis may overgeneralize the cross-denominational pattern and understate the continuing importance of denominational, ethnic and local identities, especially in immigrant religious communities, where they may remain stronger organizing principles than national ideological camps.
