# James S. Coleman

James Samuel Coleman (1926-1995) was an American sociologist who combined large-scale empirical research on education with a programme for a rational-choice foundation for social theory. He is widely associated with the concept of social capital.

## Life and Context

Coleman was born in Bedford, Indiana. He trained as a chemical engineer before turning to sociology, and received his doctorate at Columbia University in 1955, where he was influenced by Paul Lazarsfeld and Robert K. Merton. He taught at the University of Chicago, then Johns Hopkins University, and from 1973 the University of Chicago again. He served as president of the American Sociological Association in the early 1990s.

## Main Ideas

- **Rational-choice sociology.** In *Foundations of Social Theory* (1990), he argued that social phenomena should be explained as the outcome of purposive actors in structured situations.
- **Micro-macro link.** His schematic of macro conditions shaping individual action, and individual actions aggregating to macro outcomes, is often called "Coleman's boat" or "bathtub".
- **Social capital.** In "Social Capital in the Creation of Human Capital" (1988), he described social capital as features of social relations, such as trust, norms and information channels, that make cooperation possible.
- **Adolescent society.** *The Adolescent Society* (1961) examined how peer culture in American high schools shapes values and effort.
- **Educational equality.** The report *Equality of Educational Opportunity* (1966), known as the Coleman Report, concluded that family background and peer composition were strongly related to achievement, while school resources mattered less than expected.

## Major Works

- *Union Democracy* (1956, with Seymour Martin Lipset and Martin Trow)
- *The Adolescent Society* (1961)
- *Introduction to Mathematical Sociology* (1964)
- *Equality of Educational Opportunity* (1966)
- *High School Achievement: Public, Catholic, and Private Schools Compared* (1982, with Thomas Hoffer and Sally Kilgore)
- *Foundations of Social Theory* (1990)

## Method

Coleman used survey research, mathematical models and formal theory, and he favoured policy-relevant social research.

## Influence and Criticism

His social-capital concept was taken up by Robert Putnam and many others, and his report reshaped debate on school policy. Critics have argued that the term social capital is vague and risks circular reasoning, that rational-choice assumptions overlook culture and emotion, and that his later work on school desegregation and on private schools was politically contested.
