# Arlie Russell Hochschild

Arlie Russell Hochschild (born 1940) is an American sociologist, professor emerita at the University of California, Berkeley, known for her work on the sociology of emotion, work and family life, and, more recently, the emotional foundations of American politics.

## Life and Context

Hochschild studied at Swarthmore College and earned her doctorate at the University of California, Berkeley, where she later became a professor. Her early studies examined social life among older people and the sociology of emotions, and she became a leading figure in feminist sociology and the study of work and family. In later decades she turned to ethnographic research on political polarization in the United States.

## Main Ideas

In *The Managed Heart*, based on research with flight attendants and bill collectors, Hochschild introduced the concept of emotional labor: the management of feeling to create a publicly observable facial and bodily display as part of paid work. She distinguished surface acting from deep acting and described feeling rules, the social norms that guide what people feel is appropriate. In *The Second Shift*, written with Anne Machung, she documented how employed women did the bulk of domestic and childcare work after returning home from paid jobs. *The Time Bind* examined how workplaces that promised family-friendly policies could still draw parents toward work, as home became a place of stress. With Barbara Ehrenreich she edited *Global Woman*, which explored global care chains in which migrant women provide care labor. In *Strangers in Their Own Land*, she introduced the idea of a deep story, a feels-as-if narrative that underlies political views, and the empathy wall that separates people across political divides.

## Case Studies

*The Managed Heart* drew on research with Delta Air Lines flight attendants and with bill collectors. *The Time Bind* was based on fieldwork at a large manufacturing company that the book calls Amerco. *The Unexpected Community* studied a San Francisco-area apartment complex for older residents. *Strangers in Their Own Land* grew out of several years of interviews with Tea Party-aligned residents of the Lake Charles area of Louisiana.

## Method

Hochschild uses in-depth interviews and long-term ethnography, often immersed in a field site, to interpret how structural conditions are felt and narrated by participants.

## Influence and Criticism

Her concept of emotional labor became widely used in sociology, management studies and public discussion, though some scholars argue that its popular usage has stretched beyond her original meaning. Critics of *Strangers in Their Own Land* have asked how far findings from one region of Louisiana can be generalized, and whether the deep story gives enough weight to racial attitudes and structural factors.
