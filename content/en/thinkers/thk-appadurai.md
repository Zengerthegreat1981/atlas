# Arjun Appadurai: Ethnography of Globalization and the Circulation of Culture

Arjun Appadurai (born 1949) is an Indian-American anthropologist and a leading figure in the anthropology of globalization. He is best known for the argument, developed in the essay "Disjuncture and Difference in the Global Cultural Economy" (1990) and in *Modernity at Large* (1996), that global cultural flows can be analysed along five dimensions, which he called ethnoscapes, mediascapes, technoscapes, financescapes, and ideoscapes. His work redirected anthropological attention from bounded communities to circulation, imagination, and the production of locality under global conditions.

## Biographical Context

Appadurai was born in Bombay (now Mumbai) in 1949. He studied in India and the United States and received his PhD in anthropology from the University of Chicago in 1976. He taught at the University of Pennsylvania, then at Yale and the University of Chicago, and later at New York University, and served as senior adviser for global initiatives at The New School in New York. He co-founded the journal *Public Culture* and has been involved in research and activism with housing movements of the urban poor in Mumbai. His position as a scholar who moved between India and the American academy shaped his interest in how people elsewhere engage with global processes rather than merely receive them.

## The Five Scapes and the Role of Imagination

In *Modernity at Large*, Appadurai argued that the global cultural economy is marked by disjunctures between flows that do not follow a single centre-periphery model. The "scapes" are fluid, perspectival constructs, shaped by the standpoint of the actors who inhabit them:

- **Ethnoscapes**: the movement of tourists, migrants, refugees, exiles, and guest workers.
- **Technoscapes**: the global configuration of technology.
- **Financescapes**: the flows of capital and currency.
- **Mediascapes**: the distribution of images and information, and the narratives they supply.
- **Ideoscapes**: the circulation of political ideas and ideologies.

He treated imagination as a social practice rather than mere fantasy, and drew partly on Benedict Anderson's work on imagined communities. Electronic media and migration, he argued, give people a wider repertoire of possible lives, so that the imagination becomes a daily resource for identity-making. Globalization, on this view, does not simply homogenize culture; people appropriate global forms for local purposes and produce new, uneven mixtures.

## Major Works

- **Worship and Conflict under Colonial Rule: A South Indian Case** (1981): an historical ethnography of the Parthasarathy temple in Triplicane, Madras, examining the management of ritual honours and disputes over authority under colonial rule.
- **The Social Life of Things: Commodities in Cultural Perspective** (1986, editor): essays on how commodities acquire value and meaning as they circulate; Appadurai's introduction, on the "politics of value," is widely cited.
- **Modernity at Large: Cultural Dimensions of Globalization** (1996): his most influential book, setting out the five scapes and the role of imagination.
- **Fear of Small Numbers: An Essay on the Geography of Anger** (2006): an argument about how globalization generates anxieties about minorities and, in some settings, violence against them.
- **The Future as Cultural Fact: Essays on the Global Condition** (2013) and **Banking on Words: The Failure of Language in the Age of Derivative Finance** (2015): later work on aspiration, the future, and finance.

## Critiques and Responses

The scapes framework has been criticized as too abstract or systemic, and for saying little about power and inequality: some people and capital move easily while others face severe restrictions on movement. Postcolonial critics have argued that it does not sufficiently centre the colonial histories that shaped contemporary globalization, and others suggest that its emphasis on imagination and creativity can understate material inequality and domination. Appadurai's later work, including *Fear of Small Numbers*, engages more directly with violence and inequality.

## Postcolonial Positioning and Non-Western Modernity

Appadurai's concept of "modernity at large" holds that modernity is not simply a Western export but a plural and uneven set of possibilities that different peoples engage in diverse ways. His writing on India, including cricket and Bombay's urban life, treats formerly colonized societies as active producers of global culture rather than passive recipients.

## Legacy

The scapes vocabulary is now standard in the study of globalization, migration, diaspora, and transnational media, and has been influential beyond anthropology in geography, cultural studies, and development studies. Questions about how digital and social media extend or alter the circulation of images and imagination continue to draw on his framework.
