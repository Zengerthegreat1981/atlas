# Max Weber

Max Weber (1864-1920) was a German scholar of law, economics, history and sociology whose studies of social action, authority, religion and rationalization are central to modern social theory.

## Life and Context

Weber was born in Erfurt in 1864 and grew up in Berlin. He studied law and related subjects and received his doctorate in Berlin in 1889. He held chairs in economics at Freiburg (1894) and Heidelberg (1896). A severe breakdown from the late 1890s led him to leave regular teaching, although he remained highly productive after recovering. He co-edited the *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft und Sozialpolitik* and helped found the German Society for Sociology. He returned to teaching in Vienna and Munich and died in Munich in 1920. Marianne Weber, his wife and a scholar in her own right, edited much of the posthumous work.

## Main Ideas

- **Interpretive sociology:** sociology seeks to understand the meaning of social action and thereby explain its course and effects.
- **Types of action:** instrumentally rational, value-rational, affectual and traditional.
- **Ideal types:** deliberately sharpened concepts used for comparison, not descriptions of reality or moral ideals.
- **Legitimate domination:** traditional, charismatic and legal-rational authority.
- **Bureaucracy:** offices, rules, hierarchy, files and trained officials make administration efficient and calculable but can constrain freedom.
- **Class, status and party:** inequality has economic, honorific and political dimensions.
- **Rationalization and disenchantment:** calculation and systematic mastery spread through modern institutions.
- **Values and science:** scholarship cannot decide ultimate values, although values guide the choice of questions.

## Major Works

- *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism* (1904-1905; revised 1920)
- *The Religion of China*, *The Religion of India* and *Ancient Judaism* (English collections of his comparative studies of religion)
- "Science as a Vocation" and "Politics as a Vocation" (lectures, published 1919)
- *Economy and Society* (published posthumously, 1921-1922)

## Case Study

*The Protestant Ethic* argues that ascetic Protestantism, especially currents shaped by the doctrine of predestination, encouraged a methodical conduct of life that had an affinity with the spirit of modern capitalism. Weber did not claim that religion alone caused capitalism.

## Method

Weber combined interpretation of meaning with causal analysis and wide historical comparison, asking why particular institutions developed in some civilizations and not others.

## Influence and Criticism

His work shaped the sociology of organizations, politics, religion, stratification and method; Talcott Parsons did much to introduce it to American sociology. Historians have contested the evidence linking Protestantism and capitalism. Scholars have also criticized his portrayal of non-Western societies, including Islam, the abstraction of his typologies and his German nationalism.

## Cross-References

Compare Marx on class and capitalism, Durkheim on religion, and Simmel on money.
