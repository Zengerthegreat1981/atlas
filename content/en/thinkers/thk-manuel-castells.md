# Manuel Castells

Manuel Castells (born 1942) is a Spanish sociologist known for his analyses of urban social movements and, above all, for his theory of the network society in the information age.

## Life and Context

Castells was born in Spain and became politically active against the Franco regime while a student, which led him into exile in France. He studied in Paris, where he was influenced by Alain Touraine and Marxist urban theory. He taught at the University of Paris and then at the University of California, Berkeley, from the late 1970s, later moving to the University of Southern California and the Open University of Catalonia.

## Main Ideas

Castells's early work, especially *The Urban Question*, applied a structural Marxist approach to cities, treating urban space as an arena of collective consumption and conflict. In *The City and the Grassroots* he studied urban social movements across several countries. His major synthesis is the trilogy *The Information Age*, in which he argued that new information and communication technologies, together with the restructuring of capitalism, produced a network society. Key concepts include the space of flows, in which power and wealth are organized through networks rather than places, timeless time, and the tension between the global Net and the local Self, where identity movements form in response to global networks. Later works, notably *Communication Power* and *Networks of Outrage and Hope*, analyzed mass self-communication and the role of digital networks in social movements.

## Case Studies

*The City and the Grassroots* examined urban movements in cities such as Madrid, Paris and San Francisco, including the gay community in the Castro district. *Networks of Outrage and Hope* analyzed the Tunisian and Egyptian uprisings, the Spanish Indignados and the Occupy movement as networked movements built on the internet and urban public space.

## Method

Castells combines comparative empirical studies, historical interpretation and large-scale synthesis of data on economies, technology and culture, with a strong orientation toward theory-building.

## Influence and Criticism

His work shaped urban sociology, communication studies, globalization research and the study of digital social movements. Critics have charged him with technological determinism, with building broad claims on loosely documented evidence, and with overstating the novelty of networked forms of organization.
