# Florian Znaniecki

Florian Znaniecki (1882-1958) was a Polish-American sociologist and philosopher. He co-authored a landmark study of Polish migration and developed a distinctive methodology and theory of social action.

## Life and Context

Znaniecki was born in Poland, studied philosophy in Warsaw, Geneva, Zurich and Krakow, and received his doctorate at Krakow. He worked with William I. Thomas in the United States on a study of Polish peasants in Europe and America, and later held a chair in sociology at the University of Poznan in newly independent Poland, founding a sociological institute there. After the outbreak of World War II he settled in the United States, and taught at the University of Illinois until retirement. He was president of the American Sociological Society (renamed the American Sociological Association in 1959) in 1954.

## Main Ideas

- **Humanistic coefficient.** Znaniecki held that social facts are always facts as experienced by participants, so the sociologist must understand the meanings people attach to their situations.
- **Definition of the situation.** With Thomas, he showed how people interpret situations and act on them, linking individual attitudes to social values.
- **Social action and roles.** In *Social Actions* (1936) and later works, he developed a theory of social action and social roles.
- **Analytic induction.** He proposed a method of reasoning from a small number of cases to universal statements by revising hypotheses until no exceptions remain.
- **Cultural sciences.** In *Cultural Sciences* (1952), he distinguished the study of culture from the natural sciences.

## Major Works

- *The Polish Peasant in Europe and America* (1918-20, with W. I. Thomas)
- *The Method of Sociology* (1934)
- *Social Actions* (1936)
- *The Social Role of the Man of Knowledge* (1940)
- *Cultural Sciences: Their Origin and Development* (1952)

## Method

*The Polish Peasant* made innovative use of personal documents, including letters and a life-history written by a migrant, along with newspapers and organisational records. It helped establish documentary and life-history research in sociology.

## Influence and Criticism

The work shaped Chicago-school sociology, qualitative research and the study of migration. Critics, including Herbert Blumer in his 1939 critique of the study, questioned whether personal documents could verify theory, and whether the framework of attitudes and values was adequately tested. Znaniecki's analytic induction is still discussed in qualitative methodology, though also criticised for focusing on confirming cases and for not providing a statistical basis for generalisation.

## Applications and Cross-References

Analytic induction was applied in classic studies such as Alfred Lindesmith's work on opiate addiction (1947) and Donald Cressey's study of embezzlers (1953). Znaniecki's concerns link to the Chicago school, to symbolic interactionism and to W. I. Thomas's definition of the situation.

## Further Biographical and Theoretical Detail

Znaniecki was born in the Prussian-ruled part of Poland and also studied in Paris; he received his Kraków doctorate in 1910. His work for an organisation assisting emigrants brought him into contact with Thomas, and in 1914 he moved to Chicago to collaborate with him. He returned to Poland in 1920, later launched the journal *Przegląd Socjologiczny*, and spent 1931-1933 at Columbia University. *Cultural Reality* (1919) is among his philosophical works. With Thomas he argued that social change must be explained through the interplay of objective social values and subjective attitudes, and described social disorganisation, in which established rules lose their hold on a group's members, and reorganisation, in which new forms of cooperation are built. The long autobiography in *The Polish Peasant* was written by a migrant, Władek Wiszniewski. *The Social Role of the Man of Knowledge* examines the positions of people who specialise in knowledge, such as technologists, sages and scholars.
