# John Forbes Nash Jr.

**John Forbes Nash Jr.** was an American mathematician whose work on non-cooperative games became part of modern economics. He shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1994 for that contribution. The Nash equilibrium is a standard tool in microeconomics. His life, including a long illness diagnosed as schizophrenia, became widely known through Sylvia Nasar's biography and the film *A Beautiful Mind* (2001).

## Early Academic Path

Nash studied at the Carnegie Institute of Technology (now Carnegie Mellon University), then obtained his doctorate from Princeton University in 1950. His dissertation, "Non-Cooperative Games", was very short and set out the Nash equilibrium. He then taught at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and consulted for the RAND Corporation, before the onset of paranoid schizophrenia in 1959. This disrupted his active research career for decades afterwards.

## Nash Equilibrium: 1950

In his doctoral dissertation Nash presented a general concept of equilibrium in non-cooperative games, covering any finite number of players, including mixed strategies. This extended the game theory founded by John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern in the 1940s, which had been limited mainly to two-player zero-sum games. A "Nash equilibrium" describes a strategic situation in which each player chooses the best possible response to the strategies of the other players. No individual player benefits from changing their strategy alone, as long as the others keep theirs. Nash proved that such an equilibrium (in mixed strategies) always exists in any finite game, using a fixed-point theorem (Kakutani's in the 1950 version; Brouwer's in the 1951 paper). He also proposed the Nash bargaining solution (1950). This general mathematical result has found wide applications beyond economics, in political science, evolutionary biology and computing.

## A Central Impact on Modern Microeconomics

The concept of Nash equilibrium became a cornerstone in analysing strategic interaction in modern economics. It is used to model oligopolistic competition among a small number of firms, to analyse auctions, and to study bargaining. More precise concepts were later built upon it, such as the "subgame perfect equilibrium," developed by Reinhard Selten to handle games played sequentially over time. Game theory thereafter became a standard part of advanced microeconomics and of mechanism design.

## Years of Illness and Partial Recovery

Nash suffered severe schizophrenic episodes beginning in 1959 that lasted for decades. These were interspersed with repeated periods of admission and hospitalisation in psychiatric facilities, during which his capacity for regular academic work was almost entirely diminished. In the 1980s and 1990s his condition gradually improved, and he returned to limited research and teaching activity at Princeton University, where he continued to visit the campus even after his official activity had declined. This path was documented in the biography by Sylvia Nasar, *A Beautiful Mind* (1998), which was adapted into the 2001 film; the film departs from the biography in a number of respects.

## The Nobel Prize and the Abel Prize

Nash was awarded the Nobel Prize in Economics in 1994, shared with John Harsanyi and Reinhard Selten, for "their pioneering analysis of equilibria in the theory of non-cooperative games." Later in life, in 2015, he received the prestigious Abel Prize in mathematics (shared with Louis Nirenberg) for work on nonlinear partial differential equations. He is the only person to have received both the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences and the Abel Prize. Nash and his wife Alicia died in a taxi accident on the New Jersey Turnpike in May 2015, days after returning from the Abel Prize ceremony in Norway.

## From Zero-Sum Games to General Strategic Interaction

Before Nash's work, classical game theory was limited mainly to strictly competitive situations with zero sum between two players, where one party's gains exactly equal the other's losses. This model suits simple board games but is far too narrow to describe most real economic interactions, which usually allow partial cooperation, multiple players and interests that are not wholly opposed. Nash's contribution generalised the theoretical framework to cover this broader spectrum of situations, including incomplete competition among a few firms competing on the price or quantity of a product. That situation had been studied earlier by economists such as Augustin Cournot, but without the rigorous general framework that Nash provided.

## A Legacy That Extends Beyond Economics

The impact of the Nash equilibrium concept extended beyond economics. In evolutionary biology it was used to explain the stability of certain behavioural strategies among living organisms, through the concept of the "evolutionarily stable strategy" developed by John Maynard Smith. In political science it has been applied to the analysis of international negotiation and arms races, and in computer science to designing algorithms for allocating network resources. The equilibrium concept thus travelled well beyond economics.
