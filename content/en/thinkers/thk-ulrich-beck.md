# Ulrich Beck

Ulrich Beck (1944-2015) was a German sociologist best known for the concept of the "risk society", which holds that late modern societies are increasingly organized around managing hazards that modernization itself produces. He was also a leading theorist of individualization, reflexive modernization and cosmopolitan sociology.

## Life and Context

Beck was born in 1944 in Stolp, Pomerania, and studied sociology, philosophy, psychology and political science at the University of Munich, where he later became professor. He also held a position at the London School of Economics. *Risikogesellschaft* appeared in German in 1986, the year of the Chernobyl disaster, which many readers took as confirming its argument, and it became one of the most widely discussed sociological books of its time. He died in Munich in January 2015.

## Main Ideas

- Risk society: where industrial society produced and distributed wealth, the later phase produces risks, such as nuclear, chemical and ecological hazards, that are global, hard to perceive and often unattributable.
- Reflexive modernization: modernization confronts its own side effects, so that institutions and science are questioned from within.
- Individualization: traditional class, family and gender roles loosen, requiring people to construct their own biographies, often under conditions of insecurity.
- World risk society and cosmopolitanism: risks cross borders, creating forced interdependence, and he criticized "methodological nationalism" in social science.
- Second modernity as a transformation from the first, nation-state-based modernity.

## Major Works

- Risk Society: Towards a New Modernity (German 1986; English 1992)
- Reflexive Modernization (1994, with Anthony Giddens and Scott Lash)
- Ecological Politics in an Age of Risk (English 1995)
- The Normal Chaos of Love (English 1995, with Elisabeth Beck-Gernsheim)
- World Risk Society (1999)
- Cosmopolitan Vision (English 2006)

## Applications and Cross-References

Beck treated nuclear accidents, chemical contamination, climate change and financial crises as examples of risks produced by modernization itself, and in *World at Risk* (2009) he extended the argument to terrorism and the financial crisis. His individualization thesis, developed with Elisabeth Beck-Gernsheim (*Individualization*, 2002), has been used in studies of family, work and gender. With Anthony Giddens and Scott Lash he shaped the "reflexive modernization" debate; compare Giddens on late modernity and Mary Douglas on the cultural construction of risk. *Cosmopolitan Vision* applies his critique of methodological nationalism to research on migration, global inequality and politics.

## Method

Beck worked as a macro-sociological theorist, using diagnosis of epochal change and examples drawn from environmental, technological and economic crises rather than systematic empirical testing.

## Influence and Criticism

His concepts have been used in environmental sociology and in studies of disasters, work, family and globalization. Critics argue that he exaggerates the novelty of risk and the equalizing effect of hazards, since risks remain unequally distributed; cultural theorists such as Mary Douglas stressed the social construction of risk perception; and others see his claims as insufficiently grounded empirically.
