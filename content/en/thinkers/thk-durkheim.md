# Émile Durkheim

Émile Durkheim (1858-1917) was a French sociologist and one of the founders of the discipline. He argued that social facts are real, external to individuals, and explainable by other social facts, and he studied solidarity, suicide, and religion.

## Life and Context

Durkheim was born in Épinal, in the Lorraine region of France, in 1858, into a Jewish family. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure, taught philosophy in secondary schools and then pedagogy and social science at the University of Bordeaux, and from 1902 was at the Sorbonne in Paris. He founded the journal *L'Année sociologique* in 1898 and gathered a school of collaborators including Marcel Mauss. The French Third Republic's concerns about moral order and secular education shaped his work. His son André died in the First World War in 1915, and Durkheim died in 1917.

## Main Ideas

- **Social facts:** ways of acting, thinking, and feeling that are external to the individual and exert constraint.
- **Mechanical and organic solidarity:** in simple societies cohesion rests on similarity; in modern societies it rests on interdependence through the division of labor.
- **Anomie:** a condition of weak or unclear social regulation, linked to certain forms of suicide and to rapid social change.
- **Four types of suicide:** egoistic, altruistic, anomic, and fatalistic, related to levels of social integration and regulation.
- **Religion and collective effervescence:** religion expresses and reinforces the collective life of society, distinguishing the sacred from the profane.
- **Collective representations:** shared categories of thought originate in social life.

## Major Works

- *The Division of Labor in Society* (1893)
- *The Rules of Sociological Method* (1895)
- *Suicide* (1897)
- *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912)
- *Moral Education* (lectures, published posthumously in 1925)

## Method

Durkheim urged treating social facts "as things" and explaining them sociologically rather than psychologically. In *Suicide* he compared official suicide rates across groups and countries, a landmark of quantitative, comparative social research. In *Elementary Forms* he analyzed ethnographic material on Australian Aboriginal totemism.

## Influence and Criticism

Durkheim shaped functionalism, structural anthropology, and the sociology of religion and education. Critics have argued that his data on suicide were of uneven quality, that his account risks the ecological fallacy, that he understated conflict and power, and that his ethnographic sources were secondhand.

## Applications and Cross-References

Anomie was developed by Robert Merton in the theory of deviance, and Durkheimian ideas inform studies of civic ritual and social integration. Compare Marx on conflict and Weber on meaning and action.
