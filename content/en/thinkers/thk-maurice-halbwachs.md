# Maurice Halbwachs

## Definition

A French sociologist, a student of Émile Durkheim, professor at the Sorbonne and later at the Collège de France. He was arrested in 1944 because of his son's activity in the French Resistance, and died in a Nazi concentration camp (Buchenwald) in 1945.

## Historical Context

Halbwachs was intellectually formed in Durkheim's French sociological school, with its positivist orientation, which studies social phenomena as "things" external to the individual that impose themselves on him. Halbwachs applied this perspective to a matter that had traditionally been treated as purely individual, memory, to show that it too is a social phenomenon par excellence. He wrote his most important works in the interwar period, a time when France was undergoing intensive collective reshaping of its national memory after the shock of the First World War.

## Main Ideas and Method

- **The Social Frameworks of Memory (1925)**: the foundational work for the concept of "collective memory." He argues that individual memory is not itself an isolated psychological process, but is socially constructed through collective frameworks provided by the family, religion, class and nation. Even intimate personal memories are reformulated in a language and with concepts that society supplies.
- **Distinguishing collective memory from history**: he distinguishes between "collective memory," which is lived and constantly changing according to the needs of the present and carried by living individuals who belong to a particular group, and "history" in the academic sense, a critical record that seeks permanence after the living connection with the past has been broken. When the last individuals carrying a particular living memory die, that memory turns into "history" in the rigid academic sense.
- **The legendary topography of the Holy Land (1941)**: an application of his method to how Christianity built the geography of the sacred places in Palestine retrospectively over generations of pilgrims, not as a direct documentation of the events of the Gospels. It shows how each religious community projects its contemporary needs onto the terrain of the past.
- **The plurality of collective memories**: Halbwachs stresses that a single individual belongs to several simultaneous groups (family, profession, religion, nation) and therefore carries multiple collective memories, which may compete or complement one another within a single individual consciousness.

## Major Works and Representatives

Halbwachs is the theoretical founder on whose basis Pierre Nora later built his project *Les Lieux de mémoire*. His thesis on the social frameworks of memory is the cornerstone of the whole contemporary field of "memory studies," including research on intergenerational collective trauma and studies of Holocaust memory itself. This is a tragic paradox, since Halbwachs died as a victim of events that would later become the subject of the largest research field in collective memory studies.

## Impact and Criticism

Halbwachs's ideas decisively shaped the emergence of the field of "memory studies" as a whole from the 1980s onward, although his works had remained largely neglected for decades after his death. Some critics note that his strong emphasis on the wholly social character of memory may neglect the individual psychological dimension of remembering, a point that later scholars (such as Paul Ricoeur) have tried to balance against the individual dimension of experience and trauma.

## Major Works

- *The Social Frameworks of Memory* (*Les Cadres sociaux de la mémoire*, 1925).
- *The Collective Memory* (*La Mémoire collective*, unfinished, published posthumously in 1950).
- *The Legendary Topography of the Gospels in the Holy Land* (*La Topographie légendaire des évangiles en Terre sainte*, 1941).

## Supplementary Notes

A less well-known aspect of Halbwachs's career is his early interest in statistical sociology. Before his works on memory, he wrote studies on living standards and the working classes in France, which gave his later thinking about collective memory a solid empirical foundation rather than purely abstract philosophical speculation. It is also noted that the tragic circumstances of his death, arrested together with his son-in-law because of his son's activity in the Resistance, and dying in Buchenwald a few weeks before the camp was liberated, gave a symbolic tragic dimension to his intellectual legacy on the collective memory of historical traumas. His own story has become the subject of study within the field of Holocaust memory studies, a field whose first conceptual tools he helped establish without witnessing its full emergence.

Halbwachs's ideas also later interacted with the work of the French historian Philippe Ariès on the history of childhood and death, within a broader current of French social history concerned with collective mentalities (*mentalités*), which borrowed from Halbwachs the idea that collective representations are shaped socially as much as they are lived individually.

His surviving personal correspondence and diaries are studied today as an additional source for understanding French intellectual life between the wars, and for understanding how a French intellectual of partly Jewish origin (on his mother's side) lived the rising political tensions of 1930s France before the very war that he had studied intellectually, indirectly, through his concept of the collective memory of nations, claimed him.
