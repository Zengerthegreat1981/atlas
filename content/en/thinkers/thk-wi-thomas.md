# William Isaac Thomas

An American sociologist (1863–1947), a former professor at the University of Chicago, co-founder (with Florian Znaniecki) of the "personal document" method in sociology, and author of the influential "Thomas theorem."

## What He Contributed

- **The Polish Peasant**: together with Florian Znaniecki, he produced *The Polish Peasant in Europe and America* (1918–1920, five volumes). Relying on personal letters, diaries and autobiographical documents, it studied how Polish immigrants adapt to the social disorganization resulting from the move from traditional Polish village society to the industrial American city. In doing so he established the "Personal Document Method" as a legitimate sociological research tool.
- **The Thomas theorem**: the most famous formulation associated with him (with Dorothy Swaine Thomas, *The Child in America*, 1928) states that if men define situations as real, they are real in their consequences. Its central idea is that social action is built on the subjective interpretation that actors give to the situation, not on "objective facts" taken apart from that interpretation.

## Historical Context

William Isaac Thomas worked as a professor of sociology at the University of Chicago from the 1890s and became one of the most prominent names in the first founding department of American academic sociology, alongside Albion Small and Robert Park. He was dismissed from the university in 1918 following a personal moral scandal unrelated to his academic work. He then worked independently, with the support of private philanthropy, and completed his most important works right after this professional crisis, in collaboration with the Polish-born researcher Florian Znaniecki, who brought direct knowledge of the Polish language and society that was necessary for studying the immigrants' personal letters and documents.

## Method and Findings

*The Polish Peasant* drew on personal letters, diaries, newspaper material and the records of immigrant organizations and welfare agencies that the authors collected in Poland and in Chicago, including a long series of letters exchanged between immigrants and their families. It examined the breakdown of the traditional village community and the process of disorganization and reorganization that immigrants undergo in the American city. By treating personal documents as sociological evidence, it showed how to capture the subjective meanings that actors give to their experience, which statistics alone do not reach. The Thomas theorem was set out in full in *The Child in America* (1928), written with Dorothy Swaine Thomas (later his wife): social action rests on actors' definition of the situation, so that even false beliefs have real consequences when people act on them.

## Major Works and Representatives

The work of Thomas and Znaniecki contributed to the development of symbolic interactionism (Herbert Blumer was a student and later a member of the Chicago department) and to the qualitative case-study method that remained a distinguishing feature of the Chicago School throughout the twentieth century, from Nels Anderson's studies of hobos to Frederic Thrasher's studies of gangs.

## Impact and Critique

*The Polish Peasant* is regarded as an early and major American sociological work to adopt a systematic qualitative methodology in place of impressionistic description or abstract statistics alone, and it deeply influenced the establishment of the legitimacy of qualitative research within American academic sociology. The work was later criticized methodologically for its excessive reliance on a statistically unrepresentative sample of letters available by chance, and for the difficulty of verifying the authenticity of some of the personal documents used. These criticisms did not prevent the work from keeping its foundational standing in the history of American sociological methodology.

## Later Reception

The "Thomas theorem" is widely taught in introductory sociology, and Robert Merton developed his account of the self-fulfilling prophecy from it, with the example of a bank run.
