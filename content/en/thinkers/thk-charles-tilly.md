# Charles Tilly

Charles Tilly (1929-2008) was an American sociologist, historian and political scientist who studied state formation, collective action, revolutions and inequality through large-scale historical comparison.

## Life and Context

Tilly was born in Lombard, Illinois, and earned his doctorate at Harvard University. He held posts at the University of Michigan, the New School for Social Research and Columbia University. His early research on the Vendee counterrevolution in France led him to see political conflict as embedded in long-term structural change such as urbanization, state-building and capitalism.

## Main Ideas

Tilly argued that European states emerged largely from the demands of war, famously summarized as war making and state making being mutually reinforcing. In *Coercion, Capital, and European States*, he traced how different mixes of coercion and capital produced different state forms. On collective action, he developed the concept of repertoires of contention, the limited set of routines of protest available to people at a given time and place, and contributed to the political process approach to social movements. Later he focused on inequality, arguing that durable inequality rests on categorical pairs (such as male and female or citizen and noncitizen) sustained by exploitation and opportunity hoarding. He also wrote on democratization and on the role of trust networks.

## Method

Tilly championed relational and processual analysis over reified entities, and he used historical archives, event catalogs and systematic comparison. He distinguished several comparative strategies, from individualizing to encompassing comparisons.

## Influence and Criticism

His work shaped historical sociology, social movement studies and the study of the state. Critics have argued that the bellicist account of state formation fits Europe better than other regions, and that the political process framework gives limited attention to culture and emotion.
