# Bart D. Ehrman

American scholar of New Testament and early Christian studies, James A. Gray Distinguished Professor of Religious Studies at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and one of the leading authors in the academic field writing for a general readership over the past three decades. He was a student of Bruce Metzger, one of the foremost figures in New Testament textual criticism in the twentieth century, and later co-authored with him the fourth edition of *The Text of the New Testament* (2005), a standard reference work in the field.

## Definition

Ehrman is known as one of the most prominent figures to bring the results of academic historical-critical study of the Bible, long confined to specialists in universities and theological colleges, to a broad non-specialist readership through a series of bestselling books. This made him a publicly controversial figure in contemporary American religious debate.

## Historical Context

Ehrman began his academic career from a conservative evangelical background (he graduated from Moody Bible Institute). He then studied theology at Wheaton College and went on to a PhD at Princeton University under the direct supervision of Bruce Metzger, where he studied thousands of Greek manuscripts of the New Testament in depth. According to his own account in the prefaces of his books, this academic journey gradually led him to give up the doctrine of literal verbal inspiration of Scripture, and later to describe himself as an "agnostic."

## Method and Career

Ehrman combines technical expertise in textual criticism (comparing thousands of Greek manuscripts of the New Testament to trace the variations among them) with writing aimed at educated general readers. His bestselling work *Misquoting Jesus* (2005) became a common point of entry for a wide audience into the results of a field that had long been confined to specialists. In his writings he consistently distinguishes between what he regards as historical facts verifiable with the tools of the historian (such as the existence of numerous textual variants, and the historicity of Jesus' crucifixion) and theological questions of faith, which he considers outside his scope as a historian.

## Main Contributions

In *Misquoting Jesus* he presents to the general public the scale of textual variation among the Greek manuscripts of the New Testament and its possible causes (accidental copying errors and deliberate theological modifications). In *Jesus, Interrupted* (2009) he reviews the internal contradictions among the four Gospels from the perspective of historical criticism. In *Forged* (2011) he discusses the phenomenon of writing in the name of other authors (pseudepigrapha) within the New Testament texts themselves, a phenomenon documented in ancient Greco-Roman literature in general. In *Lost Christianities* (2003) he reviews the pluralism of competing early Christian movements (including the Gnostic currents whose texts were found at Nag Hammadi), before the fourth-century consolidation of church doctrine determined which current would be regarded as "orthodox" and which as "heresy."

## Impact and Criticism

Ehrman has been criticized from two directions: by evangelical conservatives, who argue that his conclusions undermine the reader's confidence in the reliability of the Bible more than the scientific evidence actually warrants; and by other academic scholars, who argue that his style, aimed at the general public, sometimes simplifies complex questions on which textual critics still disagree. Nevertheless, Ehrman remains one of the most influential academic voices in introducing the broad public to the tools of historical criticism applied to religious texts.

## Public Engagement

Ehrman continues his public activity through a personal blog, public lectures and frequent media interviews. He has stated that he donates the proceeds of his blog to charities addressing hunger and homelessness, reflecting a commitment to bringing the results of academic research to a broad public beyond the limits of traditional academic publishing confined to specialists.

## Synthetic Conclusion

Ehrman's own personal trajectory (from literal evangelical faith to agnosticism through the gateway of academic textual criticism) is itself an example of the tension that many American theology students face when their early religious formation comes into contact with the rigorous tools of critical historical research. It is a tension that recurs as a theme in the careers of a number of leading scholars in the history of the field.

## Specialized Academic Works

Alongside his books for the general public, Ehrman has also published more technical specialized works, most notably the Loeb Classical Library edition and translation of the Apostolic Fathers (2003) and his study of deliberate textual alteration for doctrinal reasons, *The Orthodox Corruption of Scripture* (1993). These works have a smaller popular readership but form the scholarly foundation on which his later popular books were built.

## Brief Summary

Ehrman remains a prominent contemporary example of an academic scholar who has managed to combine scientific rigor with wide popular reach without sacrificing one for the other.
