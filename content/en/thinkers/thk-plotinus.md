# Plotinus

A prominent Hellenistic philosopher of Egyptian origin (204–270 CE), born in 204 or 205 CE, in Lycopolis (Asyut) in Roman Egypt according to the biographer Eunapius (his birthplace is not given by Porphyry), founder of **Neoplatonism** and author of the **Enneads**.

## Plotinus in the Arabic Heritage

Plotinus held a special place in Arab-Islamic philosophy, but an indirect one: he reached the Arabs in the form of a text attributed to Aristotle. In the ninth century CE Ibn Na'ima al-Himsi translated into Arabic for al-Kindi the book "The Theology of Aristotle" (or "Rububiyyat Aristatalis"), a summary and commentary on parts of the fourth, fifth and sixth Enneads, with additions of unknown origin. From this the Arabs gave Plotinus the name "the Greek Sage" (al-Shaykh al-Yunani) and attributed his sayings to Aristotle, so that Aristotle's image among them changed through the introduction of the theory of emanation and the transcendent First Principle. Al-Farabi reconciled the views of the "two sages," Plato and Aristotle, in the light of this text; Ibn Sina read it and commented on it; Isma'ili, Sufi and Illuminationist philosophy were influenced by it, and Mulla Sadra al-Shirazi was among its readers. Abd al-Rahman Badawi collected the Arabic texts attributed to Plotinus in his book "Plotinus among the Arabs" and showed that they include a "Letter on Divine Science" and "Sayings of the Greek Sage." The Arabs did not know the Enneads in full, and did not know the name of Plotinus as the author of the text except in later reports.

## Life and Context

Plotinus studied in Alexandria for about eleven years under Ammonius Saccas, then accompanied the Emperor Gordian III on his campaign against Persia in 243 CE, hoping to learn from the wisdom of the Persians and the Indians. After the failure of the campaign he settled in Rome in 244 CE and opened a school that gathered around him friends and students, among them Porphyry and Amelius, and he enjoyed the patronage of the Emperor Gallienus and his wife. He wrote the Enneads late in life, between about 253 and 270 CE, and Porphyry arranged them after his death in six groups, each containing nine treatises, for a total of fifty-four treatises. He died in 270 CE in Campania. His context was that of the third century, which witnessed the crisis of the Roman Empire, the disorder of government, and the spread of Gnosticism and Eastern religions, so his philosophy came as an attempt to re-found Platonic rationality on spiritual foundations.

## What He Offered

He offered a deep phenomenological and inward analysis of the movement of the human soul in the world, of the experience of **alienation from the origin** (the One), and of spiritual ascent through purification and inward contemplation, culminating in **"the flight of the alone to the Alone"** (*phygē monou pros monon*).

His most important theses:
- **"The One"** (The One / To Hen): an ultimate metaphysical principle, surpassing all being and all thought.
- **"Intellect"** (Nous): the intermediary between the One and the world, containing the original "Forms."
- **"Soul"** (Psyche): the third of the principles, which turns toward the material world and then ascends to the origin.
- **"Nature"** (Physis): the lowest phase of Soul, which orders the material world; matter, at the very end of the procession, is the lowest degree.
- **"Ascent"** (Anagoge): the soul's journey from preoccupation with the world, to contemplation of Intellect, to union with the One.

## Emanation and Return

Plotinus describes the procession of beings from the One not as creation in time nor by will, but as a necessary emanation resembling the flow of light from the sun: the One does not change or diminish by what flows from it, so Intellect flows from it, which contemplates the One and becomes multiple through the Forms, and from Intellect flows Soul, which governs the sensible world. Then every rank turns toward its principle with longing and return (epistrophe), so every being is formed in proportion to its contemplation of what is above it. In this conception matter is the lowest degree, an absence of form and of the Good rather than an independent evil principle, and evil is a deficiency in the soul's aspiring toward its origin. His biography records that Porphyry testified that he attained union with the One four times in the years in which he knew him.

## His Place in the Current

He represents the **great ontological philosophical background** that nourished medieval Christian and Islamic spiritual thought. His thesis influenced:
- **Islamic philosophy**: al-Kindi, al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, the Isma'ilis and the Illuminationists, through "The Theology of Aristotle."
- **Christian theology**: Augustine, the Cappadocian Fathers, Dionysius the Areopagite.
- **Modern European philosophy**: through Ficino's translations in the Renaissance and their influence on the Platonists.

## Limits

His idealist and hierarchical metaphysical system makes him **different from contemporary materialist or secular existentialism**, though at the same time he enriches contemporary existentialism on questions of "the self," "meaning" and "death." Among the limits of knowledge about him is that the Enneads are not a book Plotinus composed in its present order, but treatises he wrote on various occasions and Porphyry arranged, and that what reached the Arabs of it was a distorted translation with additions, so Plotinus must not be held responsible for everything that appears in the "Theology." Later Christian and Islamic theologians criticized the idea of emanation because it makes creation necessary rather than free, and modern scholars criticized his conception of matter and of the soul.

## Most Important Works

- **The Enneads** (*Enneades*) — a collection of 6 "Enneads" containing 54 treatises, collected and arranged by his student Porphyry of Tyre.
