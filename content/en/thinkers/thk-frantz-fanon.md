# Frantz Fanon

Frantz Fanon (1925-1961) was a psychiatrist and political thinker from Martinique whose writings on racism, colonialism and decolonization became central to postcolonial thought and the sociology of race.

## Life and Context

Fanon was born in Fort-de-France in 1925 and attended the Lycée Schoelcher, where Aimé Césaire taught. He left Martinique to fight with the Free French forces in the Second World War. He then studied medicine and psychiatry in Lyon and trained with François Tosquelles at Saint-Alban, a center of institutional psychotherapy. In 1953 he took up a post at the psychiatric hospital of Blida-Joinville in Algeria. He resigned in 1956, joined the Algerian National Liberation Front, wrote for its newspaper *El Moudjahid*, and in 1960 represented the provisional Algerian government in Ghana. He died of leukemia in 1961 in Bethesda, Maryland, shortly after the appearance of *The Wretched of the Earth*.

## Main Ideas

- **Racialization and lived experience:** in *Black Skin, White Masks* he described how colonial racism shapes language, desire and self-perception, fixing the Black person under the white gaze.
- **Sociogeny:** psychological suffering among the colonized has social causes and cannot be explained by the individual alone.
- **The colonial world as divided:** colonizer and colonized inhabit separate and unequal spaces maintained by force.
- **Violence and decolonization:** he argued that colonial rule rests on violence and analyzed armed struggle as a means by which the colonized could overturn it and recover agency.
- **Pitfalls of national consciousness:** he warned that a national bourgeoisie might simply take the colonizer's place, and called for political education and social transformation.

## Major Works

- *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952)
- *A Dying Colonialism* (French 1959)
- *The Wretched of the Earth* (1961, with a preface by Jean-Paul Sartre)
- *Toward the African Revolution* (collected writings, 1964)

## Case Studies

*A Dying Colonialism* examines how the Algerian war changed the meaning of the veil, the radio, family relations and Western medicine. *The Wretched of the Earth* includes psychiatric case notes on mental disorders linked to the colonial war, among both Algerians and French.

## Method

Fanon combined clinical observation, phenomenological description, psychoanalytic ideas, Marxism and political analysis, often writing from direct experience.

## Influence and Criticism

His work influenced anticolonial movements, Black political thought in the United States and elsewhere, and later postcolonial theorists such as Homi Bhabha. His account of violence remains disputed: Hannah Arendt criticized the celebration of violence she associated especially with Sartre and with Fanon's admirers, while other readers stress that Fanon also documented its psychological damage. Feminist scholars have questioned his treatment of women, notably in his discussion of Mayotte Capécia, and his reading of the veil.

## Cross-References

Compare Du Bois on double consciousness, Said on colonial representation, and Spivak on the subaltern.
