# Thomas William Rhys Davids

A British orientalist (1843–1922) who founded the Pali Text Society in London in 1881. That institution made possible a systematic critical edition of the Pali Buddhist canon (the Tipiṭaka) and its translation into English, which had previously been available only in scattered manuscripts in Ceylon, Burma and Siam.

## Definition

Rhys Davids is among the founding figures of Western academic Buddhist studies, with a philological-textual method based on the careful editing of manuscripts and their translation. His work complemented that of the earlier French scholar Eugène Burnouf, who focused on Sanskrit sources.

## The Founding of the Pali Text Society

Rhys Davids organized the Pali Text Society in 1881 on a cooperative model. It gathered contributions from subscribers around the world to fund the editing of texts and their publication in successive critical volumes. The society still publishes today, well over a century after its founding.

## Historical Context

At the beginning of his career Rhys Davids worked as a British administrative official in Ceylon. There he learned Pali in the field from local Buddhist monks and studied rare manuscripts before turning fully to academic work in London. This path, from colonial administration to academic philology, illustrates clearly how the British colonial establishment was intertwined with the emergence of the Western field of Buddhist studies. It also marks a distinction from the path of the French scholar Burnouf, which was more purely philological from the start.

## Main Ideas and Method

Unlike Burnouf, who worked chiefly with Sanskrit sources from Nepal (though he also studied Pali), Rhys Davids focused on the Pali tradition, that is the Theravada tradition in Ceylon, Burma and Siam. He organized a long-term collaborative project to edit and translate the complete Pali text, which in updated forms remains the principal reference for academic researchers on the Pali Buddhist canon to this day. He adopted a rigorous philological method that compares the multiple manuscript copies from different countries in order to establish a single critical text, following the textual-editing methods then prevalent in European universities for classical Greek and Latin.

## His Educational Style

Alongside his specialized editorial work, Rhys Davids took care to write in a way that reached a broad audience of non-specialists. He gave public lectures in London and other British cities on Buddhism as a rational and ethical religion that could speak to the Victorian reader seeking an alternative to traditional Christian doctrine. This helped shape a popular image of Buddhism in British public awareness that extended beyond the university walls.

## Principal Works

Among his early authored works was *Buddhism: Being a Sketch of the Life and Teachings of Gautama, the Buddha* (SPCK, 1877), an introductory account aimed at the general British reader, to acquaint them with the life and teaching of the Buddha. He later translated *Buddhist Birth Stories* (1880), which contains the Jataka tales and its introductory translation *Nidānakathā*, an important text of popular Buddhist narrative about the Buddha's previous lives. He also supervised the lengthy series *Sacred Books of the Buddhists*, which he continued to publish over decades.

## Impact and Criticism

Rhys Davids complemented the projects of Burnouf and Müller by making the Pali tradition of Southeast Asia, rather than the northern Sanskrit tradition alone, the object of study. Alongside Müller, he belongs to the founding generations of the academic field of Buddhist studies, which later influenced the formation of what is known as Buddhist modernism as his translations interacted with contemporary Asian Buddhist reform movements. His work was later criticized by researchers, including Philip Almond, who argued that the exclusive philological focus on texts produced a "Protestant" image of Buddhism: a scriptural Buddhism that excluded living ritual and popular practice in favor of an idealized, abstract text resembling the European Protestant model of understanding religion.

## Connection to the Arab-Islamic Heritage

Rhys Davids's work does not deal directly with the Islamic heritage, as his effort was confined to the Pali Buddhist tradition. However, his method of philological editing of multiple manuscripts parallels, methodologically, the efforts of European orientalists contemporary with him in editing Arabic and Islamic manuscripts. Both projects, the Buddhist and the Islamic, arose in the same European colonial context, which gave access to Eastern manuscripts through colonial administration in India, Ceylon and elsewhere.

## His Partnership with Caroline Rhys Davids

Rhys Davids married Caroline Augusta Foley, who later became a prominent Pali scholar and translator of Abhidhamma texts under the name Caroline Rhys Davids. After his death in 1922 she succeeded him as president of the Pali Text Society and continued his editorial project for further decades, adding her own contributions to the analysis of Abhidhamma Buddhist psychology. This made the couple a rare scholarly and marital partnership in the history of British orientalism.
