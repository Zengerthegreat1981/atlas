# Maurice Dobb and Paul Sweezy

Two Marxist economists, the Briton Maurice Dobb (a professor at Cambridge) and the American Paul Sweezy (founder of *Monthly Review*), launched the most important Marxist debate of the twentieth century on the driving force of the historical transition from feudalism to capitalism.

## What They Gave

- **Maurice Dobb, *Studies in the Development of Capitalism* (1946)**: he argued that the collapse of European feudalism resulted primarily from **internal contradictions** within the feudal system itself: the economic inefficiency of forced labour (corvée), and the growing pressure of lords on peasants to extract more surplus. These internal crises pushed the system to disintegrate from within.
- **Paul Sweezy, the critical reply (1950)**: he put forward the opposing thesis. Feudalism, he argued, was an economic system that was relatively stable in itself, and it did not collapse from within but under the influence of an **external** factor: the growth of trade and towns (especially along long-distance trade routes), which gradually tore apart the closed local feudal relations from outside.
- **Framing the central question for later generations**: their debate defined the terms of the discussion that Robert Brenner reopened a quarter of a century later (1976, see `sch-feudalism-capitalism-transition-debate`). Brenner argued that both had overlooked local balances of class power between peasants and lords.

## Position in the Current

Dobb and Sweezy are considered the founders of the first systematic, empirically oriented Marxist debate about the mechanism of historical transition between modes of production. They also serve as a model of how historical materialism (`sch-historical-materialism-as-method`) can be tested empirically, not only theoretically.

## Major Works

- Dobb, Maurice. *Studies in the Development of Capitalism*. Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1946.
- Sweezy, Paul M. "The Transition from Feudalism to Capitalism." *Science & Society*, vol. 14, no. 2, 1950.
