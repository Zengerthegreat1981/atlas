# Stathis Gourgouris

Stathis Gourgouris (born 1958) is a Greek-American scholar, poet, essayist, and translator, and a professor in the Department of Classics and in the Institute for Comparative Literature and Society at Columbia University. He is best known for *Dream Nation: Enlightenment, Colonization, and the Institution of Modern Greece* (1996), a study of how modern Greece was imagined into existence, and for a body of work that brings together literature, psychoanalysis, political theory, and secular criticism. His work is relevant to anthropology through its treatment of nationalism, the imaginary institution of society, and the politics of religion.

## Life and Career

Gourgouris has taught at Princeton University and at Columbia University, where he has directed the Institute for Comparative Literature and Society, and he has been active in Modern Greek studies. He also writes poetry and translates.

## Key Works

- *Dream Nation: Enlightenment, Colonization, and the Institution of Modern Greece* (1996).
- *Does Literature Think? Literature as Theory for an Antimythical Era* (2003).
- *Freud and Fundamentalism: The Psychical Politics of Knowledge* (2010), editor.
- *Lessons in Secular Criticism* (2013).
- *The Perils of the One* (2019).

## Main Ideas and Contributions

*Dream Nation* is Gourgouris's founding work. Drawing on Cornelius Castoriadis's theory of the social imaginary, he argued that modern Greece was not simply the revival of an ancient nation but was shaped by the European Enlightenment's idealisation of ancient Greece and by the Greek national movement's engagement with that ideal. The Greek nation-state was thus formed in a colonial relation to philhellenic Europe, in which Greeks learned to see themselves partly through European eyes. The argument reframed the study of nationalism by showing that national identity is not only invented but imagined in dialogue with the desires of others, and the book became a central text in Modern Greek studies.

In *Does Literature Think?* (2003) Gourgouris argued that literature is not merely an object for theory but a mode of thinking in its own right. *Lessons in Secular Criticism* (2013) extended his argument to religion and politics, defending secular criticism, in the tradition of Edward Said, as a practice of questioning all orthodoxies, including secular ones.

*The Perils of the One* (2019) examines the figure of unity (the One) in philosophy, psychoanalysis, and political theory, and argues that the desire for oneness, whether national, religious, or political, is a source of dangerous political fantasies. Against it he defends multiplicity and the democratic imagination.

## Influence and Critique

Gourgouris's influence has been strongest in Modern Greek studies, comparative literature, and political theory, where *Dream Nation* is a standard reference. Critics have noted that his writing is demanding and that his argument about the "dream nation" emphasises the imaginary at the expense of the material and political forces behind Greek independence. Others have asked whether his critique of the One risks dissolving all forms of collective identity, including forms of solidarity that politics needs.
