# Karl Jaspers

Karl Jaspers (1883-1969) was a German psychiatrist and philosopher who made phenomenology a method in psychiatry before any therapeutic school of existential analysis existed, and who became a major philosopher of existence (*Existenz*), although he himself rejected the label "existentialism."

## Life

He was born in Oldenburg on 23 February 1883, trained in medicine, and worked in the Heidelberg psychiatric clinic from 1909. He became professor of psychology at Heidelberg in 1916 and of philosophy in 1921. His wife, Gertrud Mayer, was Jewish, and under the Nazi regime he was removed from his chair in 1937 and forbidden to publish; he was reinstated in 1945, and moved to Basel in 1948, where he died on 26 February 1969.

## His Methodological Contribution

In *General Psychopathology* (1913) he drew a distinction between **explaining** a phenomenon (tracing it to a cause) and **understanding** it (grasping its meaning from within the life of the person concerned), and insisted that psychiatry begin by carefully describing what patients actually experience. This distinction became a methodological foundation for later phenomenological psychiatry.

## Limit Situations

In *Psychology of Worldviews* (1919) and later in *Philosophy* he developed the idea of "limit situations" (*Grenzsituationen*): death, suffering, struggle and guilt, situations that cannot be solved or transcended, and confronting them is what opens authentic existence. The idea is a forerunner of later formulations of "ultimate concerns" in existential psychotherapy.

## Philosophy of Existenz

In *Philosophy* (1932, three volumes) he presented a philosophy built around the "Encompassing" (*das Umgreifende*), Existenz as the possibility of free selfhood, and transcendence, which can be approached only through "ciphers." He defended a "philosophical faith" without dogmatic revelation. After the Second World War he wrote on German guilt (*Die Schuldfrage*, 1946), on the "axial age" in *The Origin and Goal of History* (1949), and on the nuclear threat.

## His Forgotten Position

He is mentioned far less than he deserves in presentations of the existential school, even though he is the closest of its members to psychiatry in practice.

## His Most Important Works

*General Psychopathology* (*Allgemeine Psychopathologie*, 1913) · *Psychology of Worldviews* (*Psychologie der Weltanschauungen*, 1919) · *Philosophy* (*Philosophie*, three volumes, 1932) · *Reason and Existence* (*Vernunft und Existenz*, 1935) · *Introduction to Philosophy* (*Einführung in die Philosophie*, 1950)
