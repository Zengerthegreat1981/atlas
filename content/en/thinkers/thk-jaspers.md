# Karl Jaspers

The man who made phenomenology a method in psychiatry before a therapeutic school even existed.

## His Methodological Contribution

The distinction between <b>explaining</b> a phenomenon (tracing it back to a cause) and <b>understanding</b> it (grasping its meaning from within the life of the person concerned). This distinction is the methodological foundation of everything that comes after.

## Limit Situations

Death, suffering, struggle, and guilt: situations that cannot be solved or transcended, and confronting them is what opens authentic existence. The direct source of the idea of "ultimate concerns."

## His Forgotten Position

He is mentioned far less than he deserves in Arabic presentations of the school, even though he is the closest of its members to psychiatry in practice.

## His Most Important Works

Psychology of Worldviews (Psychologie der Weltanschauungen, 1919) · General Psychopathology (Allgemeine Psychopathologie, 1913) · Philosophy (three volumes) (Philosophie, 1932) · Reason and Existence (Vernunft und Existenz, 1935) · Introduction to Philosophy (Einführung in die Philosophie, 1950)

## Selected Quotations

"The ultimate limits (death, suffering, struggle, and guilt) are those in which the human being confronts his true existence." — Philosophy, 1932<br>"What cannot be explained causally may be understood from within." — General Psychopathology, 1913
