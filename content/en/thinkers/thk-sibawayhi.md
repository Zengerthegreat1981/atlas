# Sibawayhi

Abu Bishr Amr ibn Uthman ibn Qanbar, known as **Sibawayhi**, was a grammarian of Persian origin, from the district of Shiraz in Fars, born around 760 CE. The year is approximate. Later reports connect his death with a town in Fars, sometimes named al-Bayda'. He settled in **Basra** and studied under **al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi**, traditionally credited with establishing Arabic prosody (*'arud*) and with beginning the lexicon *al-'Ayn*, and also under Yunus ibn Habib and Isa ibn Umar. His one book, known simply as *al-Kitab* (The Book), is the earliest comprehensive Arabic grammar that survives. Earlier grammatical teaching, including al-Khalil's, is lost or survives only as it was taken into this book.

## Contributions

In Abd al-Salam Harun's edition *al-Kitab* fills five volumes. It treats syntax, morphology and, in the closing chapters, the points of articulation and the assimilation (*idgham*) of consonants. A large part of the book reports the teaching of al-Khalil and the opinions of Yunus. Sibawayhi also cites the Qur'an, poetry, and the speech of Arabs whose usage he or his teachers counted as eloquent. Heard usage is one of his methods. The book is not a private field survey standing apart from his teachers.

He fixed a technical vocabulary that Arabic grammar still uses: the governing element and what it governs (*al-'amil* and *al-ma'mul*), the agent and the direct object (*al-fa'il* and *al-maf'ul bihi*), and the division of words into noun, verb and particle. He also gives grammatical causes (*'ilal*), so the book argues as well as describes. Questions he raised were later filed as disputes between a Basran school and a Kufan school. Those school names were applied by grammarians writing after him. *Al-Kitab* became the chief authority of the Basran line.

He died young. The sources give 177 AH / 793 CE, 180 AH / 796 CE and other years. **al-Akhfash al-Awsat** (Sa'id ibn Mas'ada, d. c. 215 AH / 830 CE) is reported to have transmitted and explained the book. A famous story, of uncertain historicity, says that he lost a debate in Baghdad with the Kufan grammarian al-Kisa'i, the so-called question of the wasp (*al-mas'ala al-zunburiyya*), and left for Fars. The briefest accounts say only that he came to Baghdad, was humiliated by the grammarians there, returned to a town in Fars and died young.

## Influence

Later writers built the Basran school around *al-Kitab* and set it against the Kufans. Abu Uthman al-Mazini taught the book. Abu Sa'id al-Sirafi (d. 368 AH / 979 CE) wrote the major commentary. The text is a primary source for Basran grammatical theory of the later second century AH, and for the Qur'anic and poetic evidence those grammarians used. Its description of forms is detailed. It is also normative: it argues for why a form is as it is.

## Caveats

*al-Kitab* is highly dense and terse, to a degree that made it inaccessible to many readers without an intermediary explanation, which is why an entire industry of commentaries arose. Moreover, the biography of Sibawayhi relies on later sources (such as al-Sirafi and Yaqut al-Hamawi) that conflict in their details of his birth, his death and the reasons for his departure from Baghdad.

## Key Works

- **al-Kitab** (Sibawayhi did not give it this name; it became known by it later)
