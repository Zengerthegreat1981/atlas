# Christian M. I. M. Matthiessen

A Swedish-born linguist working in Australia and Hong Kong, a leading figure in Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL). He is the co-author, with Michael Halliday, of the third edition (2004) of *An Introduction to Functional Grammar* and the reviser of the fourth edition (2014), published as *Halliday's Introduction to Functional Grammar*, the standard reference text of the framework.

## Academic career

Matthiessen studied English and linguistics at Lund University and completed his doctorate at the University of California, Los Angeles. He worked with Halliday from the early 1980s. In Sydney he held a chair in linguistics at Macquarie University (2002–2008). From 2008 to 2021 he was Chair Professor in the Department of English at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. He has since held guest and distinguished professorships at universities in China. He has been a prominent teacher and supervisor in the SFL community in Australia and Asia.

## Computational linguistics

In the 1980s Matthiessen took part in the Penman text-generation project at the Information Sciences Institute of the University of Southern California. There the large systemic grammar of English known as Nigel was developed for natural language generation. Systemic grammar represents grammatical choices as explicit system networks, which made it well suited to computational implementation. At the same institute he worked with William C. Mann and Sandra A. Thompson on the description of rhetorical relations among spans of text, the line of research published as Rhetorical Structure Theory. His book *Lexicogrammatical Cartography: English Systems* (1995) is a large map of the systems of English grammar in the Hallidayan framework and grew out of the Nigel grammar.

In SFL, grammar is organized by rank (clause, group or phrase, word, morpheme) and by three metafunctions. The ideational metafunction construes experience, the interpersonal metafunction enacts social relations, and the textual metafunction organizes discourse. Matthiessen's cartography spells out the system networks through which English realizes these metafunctions, including the mood systems that distinguish declarative, interrogative and imperative clause types. The point of the description is not a list of structures but a network of choices, each choice associated with a meaning and a structural realization.

## Language typology and the architecture of language

Matthiessen has argued for a functional approach to comparing languages, asking how different languages realize the same metafunctions (ideational, interpersonal and textual) through different structural means. Together with Halliday he also developed the view of language as a system that construes human experience, set out in *Construing Experience through Meaning: A Language-Based Approach to Cognition* (1999), which relates the grammar to the nature of cognition and meaning. He was also a co-editor, with Alice Caffarel and J. R. Martin, of *Language Typology: A Functional Perspective* (2004), which presents systemic functional descriptions of a range of languages, including French, Japanese, Tagalog, Chinese and Vietnamese.

## Applied work

SFL is widely used in the study of educational and professional discourse and in translation studies, and Matthiessen has contributed to these applications, including work on how the metafunctions behave across a source text and its translation. The Sydney genre-based approach to teaching writing is chiefly associated with J. R. Martin and colleagues rather than with Matthiessen, although it belongs to the same wider SFL tradition.

## Impact

By maintaining and updating the core reference grammar, and by linking SFL to computation, typology and applied linguistics, Matthiessen has been one of the main figures carrying Halliday's framework into later generations of research.
