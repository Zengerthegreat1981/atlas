# Christian M. I. M. Matthiessen

A Swedish-born linguist working in Australia and Hong Kong, a leading figure in Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL). He is the co-author, with Michael Halliday, of the third edition (2004) of *An Introduction to Functional Grammar* and the reviser of the fourth edition (2014), published as *Halliday's Introduction to Functional Grammar*, the standard reference text of the framework.

## Academic career

Matthiessen studied linguistics in Sweden and completed his doctorate at the University of California, Los Angeles. He worked in the Sydney area, including at Macquarie University, and later moved to Hong Kong, where he has been a professor at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. He has been a prominent teacher and supervisor in the SFL community in Asia and Australia, and has worked closely with Halliday over several decades.

## Computational linguistics

Matthiessen took part in the 1980s in the Penman text-generation project at the Information Sciences Institute of the University of Southern California, where the large systemic grammar of English known as Nigel was developed for natural language generation. Systemic grammar represents grammatical choices as explicit system networks, which made it well suited to computational implementation. His book *Lexicogrammatical Cartography: English Systems* (1995) is a comprehensive map of the systems of English grammar and grew out of this work.

## Language typology and the architecture of language

Matthiessen has argued for a functional approach to comparing languages, asking how different languages realize the same metafunctions (ideational, interpersonal and textual) through different structural means. Together with Halliday he also developed the view of language as a system that construes human experience, set out in *Construing Experience through Meaning: A Language-Based Approach to Cognition* (1999), which relates the grammar to the nature of cognition and meaning. He was also a co-editor, with Alice Caffarel and J. R. Martin, of *Language Typology: A Functional Perspective* (2004), which presents systemic functional descriptions of a range of languages, including French, Japanese, Tagalog, Chinese and Vietnamese.

## Applied work

SFL is widely used in the study of educational and professional discourse and in translation studies, and Matthiessen has contributed to these applications, including work on how the metafunctions behave across a source text and its translation. The Sydney genre-based approach to teaching writing is chiefly associated with J. R. Martin and colleagues rather than with Matthiessen, although it belongs to the same wider SFL tradition.

## Impact

By maintaining and updating the core reference grammar, and by linking SFL to computation, typology and applied linguistics, Matthiessen has been one of the main figures carrying Halliday's framework into later generations of research.
