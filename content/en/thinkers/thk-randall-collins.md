# Randall Collins

Randall Collins (born 1941) is an American sociologist known for conflict theory, the sociology of interaction rituals, credentialism, violence, and the comparative sociology of intellectual networks and geopolitics.

## Life and Context

Collins was born in 1941. He studied at Harvard and Stanford and received his PhD at the University of California, Berkeley, in 1969. He taught at several universities, including the University of California, San Diego, and the University of California, Riverside, and spent much of his later career at the University of Pennsylvania. He has written on a wide range of topics and has drawn on Weber, Durkheim, Goffman, and Marx.

## Main Ideas

- **Conflict theory:** he built a general approach in which social life is a struggle over resources and status, with attention to micro-level interaction.
- **Credential society:** educational credentials function as status markers rather than only as evidence of skill, leading to credential inflation.
- **Interaction ritual chains:** successful rituals generate emotional energy, group solidarity, and symbols. Individuals seek situations that maximize emotional energy, which links micro situations to macro patterns.
- **Violence:** in *Violence: A Micro-sociological Theory* (2008) he argued that violence is difficult because of confrontational tension and fear, and that it succeeds in particular situational dynamics.
- **Geopolitics:** he analyzed state expansion and breakdown in terms of resources, positions, and the geopolitical environment, and argued, in work from the late 1970s onward, that geopolitical overextension and the weakness of its periphery would eventually lead to the fragmentation of the Soviet empire (see his 1995 article "Prediction in Macrosociology: The Case of the Soviet Collapse").
- **Intellectual networks:** philosophical creativity clusters in networks of rival schools.

## Method

Collins combines comparative-historical analysis with micro-sociology, using video and photographic evidence of confrontations in his violence research. He aims to explain macro patterns through situational mechanisms.

## Influence and Criticism

His work is widely cited in the sociology of emotions, education, and violence. Critics have questioned whether interaction ritual theory can account for enduring structures and for individual differences, and whether his explanations of intellectual history are overly deterministic.

## Applications and Cross-References

Interaction ritual theory has been applied to religion, sports, and protest. Compare Goffman on interaction, Durkheim on ritual, and Weber on status groups.
