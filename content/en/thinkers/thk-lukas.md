# Elisabeth Lukas

Elisabeth Lukas (born 1942) is an Austrian clinical psychologist, one of the best-known direct students of Viktor Frankl, and among those who developed logotherapy most in practical application. She has worked for many years from Germany, where she led the South German Institute for Logotherapy near Munich (Fürstenfeldbruck).

## What She Gave

She developed practical, applied formulations of logotherapy, drawing on Frankl's concepts of the will to meaning, the existential vacuum, dereflection and paradoxical intention, and made them accessible to clinicians, counsellors, teachers and the general public. Her writing is characterised by clear, example-rich presentation of how meaning orientation can be used in counselling, in education and in coping with suffering. She also wrote about preventing meaninglessness through meaning-oriented upbringing.

## Her Place in the School

Along with Alfried Längle, who is associated with a separate institutional line of existential analysis, she belongs to the generation that carried logotherapy forward after Frankl. Lukas remained closer to Frankl's own formulations, and is often regarded as one of the principal interpreters of his work for practitioners. The descriptions here are of an approach and not instructions for self-treatment.

## Selected Works

- *Meaning in Suffering: Comfort in Crisis through Logotherapy* (English edition, Berkeley: Institute of Logotherapy Press, 1986).
- *Lehrbuch der Logotherapie: Menschenbild und Methoden* (Munich: Profil, 1998).
