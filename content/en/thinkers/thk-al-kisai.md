# Al-Kisa'i

A Quran reciter and grammarian of Persian origin, born and raised in Kufa. He is regarded in the tradition as a founder of the Kufan grammatical school and the leading rival of Sibawayh in his era, and he was one of the Seven Readers whose recitations of the Holy Quran became canonical.

## Biography

He was Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Hamza ibn Abdullah al-Kisa'i, of Persian origin, born in Kufa or nearby around 119 AH (737 CE). He took up Quranic recitation and grammar from a group of Kufan scholars, most notably Abu Ja'far al-Ru'asi, who is regarded as the first to codify Kufan grammar. He then traveled to Basra and studied under Yunus ibn Habib al-Basri and under Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi himself, thereby combining the traditions of both schools. He joined the Abbasid court and became tutor to the sons of Harun al-Rashid, al-Amin and al-Ma'mun, which granted him considerable scholarly influence and made him a leading figure in the linguistic scene of his time. He died in 189 AH (805 CE) according to the most common report (some sources give other years, as early as 182 AH) in Rayy, on the same journey on which the jurist Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Shaybani died, so much so that it was said Harun al-Rashid buried a jurist and a grammarian on the same day.

## Contributions

### Founding the Kufa School as a Methodological Rival to Basra

Al-Kisa'i built on the foundation laid by al-Ru'asi and expanded it, formulating a Kufan grammatical method distinct from that of the Basrans. It rests on a broader acceptance of attested usage, including rare poetic evidence, and analogy based on it, in contrast to the Basrans' tendency to fix the rule and reduce exceptions. In this way al-Kisa'i became, in the later tradition, the foremost representative of the Kufa school in opposition to Sibawayh and the Basra school. Modern scholars such as Versteegh caution that the sharp Basra/Kufa dichotomy was in part a construction of later biographers and of Ibn al-Anbari-style treatments of disputed questions.

### The Zunburiyya Debate

The most famous episode associated with his name is his debate with Sibawayh over a well-known grammatical question known as the "Zunburiyya" question. It was held at the court of al-Rashid or in the majlis of Yahya ibn Khalid al-Barmaki, in the presence of scholars from both sides, and ended with a ruling in favor of al-Kisa'i. This ruling is reported by Kufan sources, while later writers have questioned its impartiality given al-Kisa'i's influence at court. The debate is recounted as the symbolic moment in which the rivalry between the two schools was embodied.

### Quranic Readings

Al-Kisa'i is one of the Seven Readers whose mutawatir (widely transmitted) recitations of the Holy Quran were established, and his reading is one of the seven recognized readings still in use in Quranic recitation today. He has well-known transmissions from his students, such as al-Duri and Abu al-Harith.

### Contribution to Language and Lexicography

Beyond grammar and recitation, al-Kisa'i was known for the breadth of his transmission of the Arabic language directly from the Bedouin, as he went out to the desert to hear the eloquent speakers and record rare evidence, following the habit of Kufan grammarians in general, who tended to widen the scope of attested usage more than their Basran counterparts. Sayings on the meanings of Quranic terms and the variants of readings are attributed to him and were collected in the works of his students, although no complete treatise in his own hand on this subject has survived.

## Impact

His students, most notably al-Farra', inherited his position and continued to develop the method of the Kufa school after him, so that al-Farra' became the most famous representative of the school in the following generation. His reading remains one of the seven readings with which the Quran is recited to this day across wide areas of the Islamic world, which is among the broadest practical effects of his work. In the history of grammar, his name became closely tied to the term "the Kufan school" in contrast to "the Basran school," a dichotomy that organizes the entire history of classical Arabic grammar for later scholars, most notably Ibn al-Anbari in *al-Insaf*.

## Limitations

The main sources on al-Kisa'i are mostly Kufan or late, and what has reached us of the details of the Zunburiyya debate and of his grammatical rulings comes largely through Kufan transmitters, or through his later Basran opponents in replying to him. No complete grammatical treatise in al-Kisa'i's own hand has reached us directly, as Sibawayh's *al-Kitab* has; his views must be extracted from later reports by writers such as al-Farra', Thalab, and Ibn al-Anbari. The account that Sibawayh left Baghdad psychologically defeated after the debate and died as a result is also doubted on historical grounds, and many modern scholars consider it a later Kufan exaggeration.

## Major Works

- **The Recitation of al-Kisa'i**: one of the seven canonical readings of the Holy Quran.
- Works attributed to him on **Ma'ani al-Qur'an** (the meanings of the Quran) and grammar, most of which did not reach us directly but through transmissions by his students.
- His transmitted contribution to establishing the grammatical method of the **Kufa school**, preserved mainly in the later works of al-Farra' and Ibn al-Anbari.
