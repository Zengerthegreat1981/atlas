# Vilfredo Pareto

An Italian economist and sociologist (1848–1923), first known for his contributions to mathematical economics (the Pareto distribution, Pareto optimality) before turning late in life to theoretical sociology, where he developed an influential theory of the inevitability of elite rule.

## Contributions

- **Circulation of elites (Circolazione delle élite)**: In the *Trattato di sociologia generale* (1916; English translation *The Mind and Society*, 1935), he argued that history is a series of replacements of one elite by another when the ruling elite loses its vitality and its ability to use force or cunning effectively, and not a class struggle in the Marxist sense between economic classes.
- **Logical and non-logical actions**: He distinguished between "logical" human actions (consciously directed toward a rationally calculated end) and "non-logical" ones (driven by sentiments and psychic residues, "Residues," rather than rational calculation), arguing that most actual social action is of the second kind even though actors later justify it with spurious rational discourses ("Derivations").
- **Residues and derivations**: in the *Trattato* he classified residues into six broad classes, of which the first two (the instinct for combinations and the persistence of aggregates) matter most for his theory of elites; he linked them to the "foxes" (rule by cunning and combination) and the "lions" (rule by force and conviction) he borrowed from Machiavelli.
- **Economics**: trained as an engineer, he succeeded Léon Walras at Lausanne (1893). In *Cours d'économie politique* (1896-97) he studied the distribution of incomes and found a recurring power-law pattern (the "Pareto distribution"); the popular "80/20 rule" is a later simplification of this idea, not Pareto's own formulation. "Pareto optimality", an allocation in which no one can be made better off without making someone worse off, is associated with his later economic writings, notably the *Manuale di economia politica* (1906).

## Place in the Current

- He is usually mentioned together with Gaetano Mosca and Robert Michels as one of the founders of "classical elite theory," although they developed their theses relatively independently and not through documented direct collaboration.
- His work is a direct challenge to the democratic and Marxist optimism that rule by a minority can be overcome; American elite theory, for example C. Wright Mills's *The Power Elite* (1956), engaged with the classical elite theorists, though Mills rejected their psychological explanations in favour of institutional ones.

## Limitations

- His binary classification (logical/non-logical) of human action has been accused of arbitrariness and circularity: it is sometimes hard to distinguish a spurious rational "derivation" from a valid rational justification without a clear independent criterion.
- His analysis of the circulation of elites was later exploited politically by Italian Fascism (Mussolini's government nominated Pareto a senator of the Kingdom in 1923, shortly before his death), a biographical context that requires careful presentation and not simplification.

## Major Works

- *Trattato di sociologia generale* (1916; English translation *The Mind and Society*, 1935).
- *Cours d'économie politique* (1896–1897).
