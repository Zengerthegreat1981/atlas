# Herbert Blumer

Herbert Blumer (1900-1987) was an American sociologist who coined the term "symbolic interactionism" and systematized the approach drawn from the social psychology of George Herbert Mead. He was also a notable critic of variable-centered survey research.

## Life and Context

Blumer was born in 1900 in St. Louis, Missouri, and studied at the University of Missouri before doctoral work at the University of Chicago, where he was influenced by Mead and the Chicago school of sociology. He taught at Chicago until 1952, then moved to the University of California, Berkeley, where he helped build the sociology department. He served as president of the American Sociological Association in 1956. He also played professional football in his youth. He died in 1987.

## Main Ideas

In *Symbolic Interactionism* (1969) Blumer set out three premises:

1. Human beings act toward things on the basis of the meanings those things have for them.
2. These meanings arise out of social interaction with others.
3. Meanings are handled and modified through an interpretive process used by the person in dealing with what they encounter.

From this he drew several implications. Society is understood as ongoing joint action rather than a fixed structure. Social life involves people continually interpreting and defining situations. He also studied collective behavior, public opinion, fashion, and social movements as processes of formation.

## Major Works

- *Movies and Conduct* (1933), a Payne Fund study
- "Social Psychology" (1937), a chapter in *Man and Society*, edited by Emerson Schmidt, where the term "symbolic interactionism" first appeared
- "Collective Behavior" (1939)
- "Sociological Analysis and the Variable" (1956)
- *Symbolic Interactionism: Perspective and Method* (1969)

## Method

Blumer championed what he called naturalistic inquiry: direct, close study of the empirical social world through observation, exploration, and inspection, using "sensitizing concepts" that suggest directions of looking rather than rigid definitions. He criticized the use of variables in mainstream sociology for ignoring interpretive processes.

## Influence and Criticism

Blumer shaped generations of qualitative researchers and interactionists, and students and colleagues of his era such as Howard Becker and Erving Goffman worked in the Chicago interactionist milieu. Critics, including Manford Kuhn of the Iowa school, argued that interactionism was too vague to test and that it underplayed social structure, power, and large-scale institutions. Others have noted that Blumer's presentation of Mead was selective.

## Applications and Cross-References

Interactionist work on deviance (labeling theory), occupations, and collective behavior builds on Blumer's premises. Compare Mead's account of the self, Goffman's dramaturgy, and Max Weber's emphasis on subjective meaning.
