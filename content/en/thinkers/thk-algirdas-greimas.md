# Algirdas Julien Greimas

A French linguist and semiotician of Lithuanian origin, who founded the Paris school of structural semiotics and developed formal tools for analyzing the structure of meaning in narrative and discourse.

## Structural Semantics

In *Sémantique structurale* (1966), Greimas sought to formulate a formal model of meaning that goes beyond the individual lexical item to the structure of semantic relations underlying any discourse, influenced by Saussurean structuralism and by Vladimir Propp's morphology of the Russian folk tale.

## The Semiotic Square

His best-known analytical tool is the **semiotic square** (Carré sémiotique), a four-term logical structure representing the possible relations between a semantic term, its contradictory, its contrary, and the contrary of its contradictory (for example: life/death versus non-life/non-death), as the deep mechanism on which systems of meaning in any text or culture are built.

## Narrative Grammar and the Actantial Model

Greimas also developed a general "narrative grammar" that describes the fixed structural roles in any story through the "actantial model" (Modèle actantiel): the subject who seeks to achieve an aim, the object desired, the sender who assigns the subject their task, the receiver who benefits from its accomplishment, the helper who assists the subject, and the opponent who obstructs it. This is a structure that recurs across all stories regardless of their specific surface content, a structural extension of Vladimir Propp's analysis of the structure of the Russian folk tale, but at a more abstract level applicable across a variety of narrative genres rather than the folk tale alone.

## Modal Semiotics and the Narrative Program

Later, Greimas and his Paris school (including Joseph Courtés and Jacques Fontanille) developed what he called "modal semiotics," which analyzes the "narrative program" of a subject through four basic modalities that govern its actions: will (the subject wanting to do something), ability (being able to do it), knowledge (knowing how to do it), and obligation (being obliged to do it). This analysis is accompanied by the concept of "isotopy," which describes the repetition of shared semantic features across an entire text, ensuring its overall semantic coherence and allowing the correct interpretive level of reading to be identified when possible ambiguity arises.

## The Paris School of Semiotics

Greimas founded what became known as the "Paris School" of structural semiotics, which developed an extensive methodological project aimed at building a general "grammar of meaning" extending beyond the application to literary narrative alone, to the analysis of legal, religious, advertising, and visual discourse alike. Together with Joseph Courtés, Greimas compiled a systematic encyclopedic glossary for this project titled *Semiotics: A Reasoned Dictionary of the Theory of Language* (Sémiotique: Dictionnaire raisonné de la théorie du langage, 1979), which documented the basic concepts of Greimassian semiotics with strict terminological precision.

## Personal Trajectory and Intellectual Movement

Greimas was born in 1917 in Tula, Russia, to Lithuanian parents and grew up in Lithuania. He studied in Kaunas and then in Grenoble, France, where he took up linguistics and philology, and he later taught in Alexandria, Ankara, Istanbul and Poitiers before settling in Paris in the 1960s. There he founded a research seminar at the École des hautes études en sciences sociales (EHESS) that attracted students from diverse backgrounds (linguistic, literary, and philosophical) and produced a whole generation of "Greimassian semioticians" who spread his method in France and particularly in Latin America.

## Impact and Critique

Greimas's analytical tools had a broad influence on French structuralist literary criticism and on narrative studies worldwide, and their application extended to the analysis of legal, advertising, and religious discourse. His project was criticized from post-structuralist directions, which regarded his ambition to build a rigorous, comprehensive "science" of meaning as exaggerated, and held that the formal character of the semiotic square, despite its logical elegance, may impose a predetermined binary structure on texts that are more complex and more open to interpretation than that abstract structure allows.

## Works in Lithuanian Anthropology

Alongside his French semiotic project, Greimas maintained an interest in his Lithuanian roots throughout his life and published research on Lithuanian mythology, notably *Des dieux et des hommes* (1985), applying the same semiotic tools (the semiotic square and the actantial model) to the analysis of pagan Baltic myths before their Christianization. This project ran parallel to his best-known French work but is less well known outside specialist circles of Baltic studies.

## Reception of His Work in English Translation

The dissemination of Greimas's work in the English-speaking world lagged behind France and Latin America, partly because of the difficulty of translating his precise technical terminology into English without losing its conceptual accuracy. English translations of his major works appeared only some years or decades after their French publication, which delayed his direct influence on English-speaking academic circles.
