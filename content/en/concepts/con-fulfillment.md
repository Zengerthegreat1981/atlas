# Fulfillment

Fulfillment, existentially, is not satisfaction of desires or achievement of goals, but alignment of lived life with acknowledged values. A wealthy, successful person can be unfulfilled; someone facing constraint may experience deep fulfillment.

## Fulfillment vs. Satisfaction

Satisfaction is gratification of desire; fulfillment is coherence between who one is, what one values, and how one lives. A person may be satisfied by accumulating possessions yet existentially unfulfilled because their life doesn't reflect actual values.

Frankl's observations clarify: fulfillment arises from sense that one's life has meaning, one lives in accord with values, one's choices are one's own. It is less common than satisfaction and more durable, independent of external circumstances.

## The Role of Constraints

Paradoxically, constraints can facilitate fulfillment. Someone living within limitations (poverty, illness, obligation) may be more fulfilled than someone with unlimited options if they have consciously chosen engagement within constraints and found meaning.

Van Deurzen notes fulfillment requires realistic appraisal of what's possible and commitment to making meaning within those possibilities. It is active practice, not destination.
