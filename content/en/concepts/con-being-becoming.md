# Being and Becoming

Sartre's dictum "existence precedes essence" means humans are not fixed essences but ongoing processes of self-creation. We are never merely a "self"; we are always becoming. Yet we also exist now, in a given state, with a past we cannot undo.

## The Paradox

The existential paradox is that we are both being (present facticity) and becoming (future possibility). In therapy, this tension manifests when someone says "I am not the type of person who..." (claiming fixed essence) or "I could never change" (denying possibility). The existential response is to notice both: your past is real (being) and your future is open (becoming).

This distinction matters clinically. Someone stuck in depression may need to acknowledge their depressed state (being) while also recognizing the possibility of different choices (becoming). Someone defensive may need to both accept their defensive patterns (being) while exploring possibilities beyond them (becoming).

## Becoming as Authentic Process

Authenticity is not achieving a final true self but an ongoing process of consciously engaging in self-creation. You are always in the process of becoming; the question is whether that becoming is conscious and chosen or unconscious and reactive.
