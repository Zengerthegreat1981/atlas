# The Monitor Model and Krashen's Five Hypotheses

A framework developed by Stephen Krashen from the mid-1970s and set out as five hypotheses in *Principles and Practice in Second Language Acquisition* (1982). It has had a large influence on communicative and "natural" language teaching, and it remains theoretically disputed.

## The distinction between acquisition and learning

The first and most influential hypothesis: Krashen sharply distinguishes between "acquisition," an unconscious process similar to a child's acquisition of a first language, which results from natural exposure to comprehensible input; and "learning," conscious explicit knowledge of language rules acquired through formal instruction, which (according to Krashen) does not turn into unconscious productive ability.

## The input hypothesis and the monitor hypothesis

The "Input Hypothesis" states that acquisition occurs when the learner is exposed to comprehensible input slightly beyond their current level (i+1). The "Monitor Hypothesis" describes the role of consciously learned knowledge as a "monitor" that corrects the output of the unconsciously acquired system, under limited conditions (sufficient time, focus on form, and knowledge of the rule).

## Practical impact and criticism

This model deeply influenced communicative language teaching approaches, which prefer natural exposure to comprehensible input over repeated explicit grammatical drilling. However, many critics (such as McLaughlin) questioned the testability of the hypotheses empirically, especially the difficulty of practically distinguishing between "acquisition" and "learning" in the actual behavior of learners.

## The other two hypotheses: natural order and the affective filter

The five hypotheses are completed by two further hypotheses. The "Natural Order Hypothesis" states that second language learners acquire grammatical structures in an order that is relatively predictable, regardless of the order in which they are explicitly taught. The "Affective Filter Hypothesis" assumes that psychological factors such as anxiety, low motivation and poor self-confidence create an affective "filter" that prevents comprehensible input from reaching the unconscious acquisition mechanisms, even when the appropriate input is objectively available.

## Practical application to language teaching

Taken together, these hypotheses had a major impact on the design of "Natural Approach" curricula, developed by Krashen with Tracy Terrell, which prefer exposing the learner to rich, comprehensible language input and reducing classroom anxiety over early focus on explicit correction of grammatical errors.
