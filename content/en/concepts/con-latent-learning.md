# Latent Learning

A concept associated with Edward Tolman in the 1930s (building on earlier maze work by Hugh Blodgett), which contradicts the classical behaviorist idea that learning requires reinforcement, and shows that learning can occur "latently" without appearing in performance.

## Definition

Latent learning is learning that occurs without direct reinforcement and remains "latent" (not apparent in performance) until motivation or a suitable context is available to bring it out. The central idea: learning is one thing, and performance is another. The organism may have learned, yet not show its learning in behavior.

## The classic experiment (Tolman & Honzik, 1930)

Three groups of rats in a maze:
- **Group A**: reinforcement on every trial.
- **Group B**: no reinforcement ever.
- **Group C**: no reinforcement until day 11, then reinforced.

The result: Group A improved quickly. Group B remained behind. Group C was like B until day 11, but it **jumped immediately** to the level of A after the first reinforcement. This is evidence that the rats in C had learned the maze during the first ten days, but did not show their learning until a motive (reinforcement) became available.

## Theoretical significance

Latent learning brought about a radical shift in psychology:
1. **Refuting S-R determinism**: Skinner and Hull held that learning is a lasting change in the probability of a response due to reinforcement, the assumption on which operant conditioning rests; the experiment shows that learning occurs without direct reinforcement, which posed an early challenge to the cognitive-behavioral school that later adopted the concept of internal representations.
2. **Laying a foundation for cognitive psychology**: it generated the concept of an "internal representation" of the environment, which Tolman developed in his concept of the cognitive map (1948). Much later, neuroscience on spatial representation in the hippocampus (for example the work recognised by the 2014 Nobel Prize to O'Keefe, May-Britt Moser and Edvard Moser) revived interest in cognitive-map ideas.
3. **Relation to implicit learning**: latent learning is sometimes discussed alongside implicit learning and memory, but the two are distinct: in latent learning the learning is unexpressed in performance until a motive appears, whereas implicit learning is learning without awareness of what was learned.

## Relevance beyond the laboratory

The distinction between learning and performance is used more generally: people may acquire knowledge, such as the layout of a neighbourhood or vocabulary in a language, before there is occasion to show it. Claims that latent learning explains the effects of particular therapies are speculative and are not established here.
