# The Collective Unconscious

A layer of the psyche posited by **Carl Gustav Jung**, deeper than the personal unconscious, formed not of what was repressed in the individual's life history but of what is **shared among human beings as a species**. For Jung it is not a store of inherited images but an **image-making predisposition** to produce images of a particular kind. This distinction is the hinge of the whole concept, and the aspect of it most often misunderstood.

## The Two Layers

Jung divided the unconscious into two parts:

- **The personal unconscious** (*das persönliche Unbewusste*): contents that were once conscious and then repressed or forgotten. This is in essence what **Freud** described. Its contents are **complexes** (*Komplexe*).
- **The collective unconscious** (*das kollektive Unbewusste*): it was never conscious and was not acquired individually. Its contents are **archetypes** (*Archetypen*).

Jung described it as a kind of deposit of humanity's collective experience, which belongs to its owner no more than to anyone else.

## "Predisposition", Not "Image"

The clearest reply Jung gave to the charge of inheriting contents is his repeated statement that what is inherited is the **possibility of representation**, not the representation itself; in his words, in paraphrase, archetypes are not inherited ideas but inherited possibilities of ideas.

For him the archetype is like the **axis of crystallization** in a mother liquor: an empty structure filled with the material of culture and personal biography. This is why the image of the "Great Mother" differs completely in material between Isis, Demeter and Mary, while resembling itself in function and structure.

## The Evidence on Which He Built

Jung relied on three sources:

1. **Mythological and religious parallels**: the recurrence of particular motifs (the flood, redemption, the twins, the hero) in cultures between which transmission of influence is hard to imagine.
2. **Patients' material**: images in the dreams and fantasies of his patients that parallel Gnostic and alchemical symbols with which the patient had no acquaintance. The most famous example is the case of the **"Sun-man with the tube"** at Burghölzli, which he claimed parallels a Mithraic conception not available to the patient.
3. **Alchemy**: his reading of alchemical texts as the projection of a psychic process rather than a description of material operations.

## Where It Stands in the Jungian Structure

The collective unconscious is the **field** in which the process of **individuation** takes place: the ego moves from its central position to a position subordinate to the **Self**, by passing through the **Shadow** and then the **anima/animus**. Without this layer, all of these categories lose their reference.

## Objections

This concept is among Jung's most objected-to categories, and the most important objections are three:

- **The genetic objection**: Jung's early formulation is read as **Lamarckian** (inheritance of the trace of acquired experience), which contemporary genetics rejects. Later thinkers, among them **Anthony Stevens**, have tried to recast it in the language of **evolutionary psychology** as selected behavioral patterns; this recasting is **proposed, not proven**, and to this day there is no direct genetic evidence for the inheritance of any particular archetypal structure.
- **The methodological objection**: the parallels between myths may be explained by cultural diffusion, by the limited number of solutions that shared conditions of life permit, or by the researcher's selectivity in collecting evidence.
- **The unfalsifiability objection**: what does **not** count as an archetype is not specified in advance, so the concept resists testing.

It should be noted that the use of the concept today in the therapeutic context rests mostly on its **interpretive** value in organizing the patient's material, not on a claim of biological proof.
