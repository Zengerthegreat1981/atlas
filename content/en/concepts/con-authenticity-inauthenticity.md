# Authenticity-Inauthenticity Dialectic

The relationship between authenticity and inauthenticity is not moral binary but dialectical tension central to existence. We necessarily inhabit both poles; work is not eliminating inauthenticity but being conscious of choice.

## Heidegger's Authenticity and Fallenness

Heidegger characterized most existence as "fallen"—living by "the They" (das Man), impersonal collective standards. Authenticity (Eigentlichkeit) is not natural state but choice, provoked by confrontation with mortality or anxiety.

Importantly, fallenness is not sin or mistake; it is structural feature. We cannot live entirely outside social structures. Question is not whether we are fallen but whether we are conscious of our falling.

## The Impossibility of Pure Authenticity

Sartre complicated this with "bad faith" (mauvaise foi): denial of freedom. But existentialists recognize some bad faith is structural. We adopt roles, conform, internalize others' views. Pure authenticity is impossible; work is maintaining consciousness of difference between actual choices and performed choices.

This suggests therapeutic stance less concerned with achieving authenticity than noticing inauthenticity: Where do I conform without awareness? Where do I perform rather than be? This noticing itself opens authentic choice.
