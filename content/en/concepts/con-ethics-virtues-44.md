# Respect Ethics

## Meaning and Definition

Respect ethics (Respect Ethics) is the system of values founded on recognizing the dignity and standing of the other, whether that other is an individual person, a group, a legitimate authority, or even the self. The other is treated as an end and not merely as a means used to achieve other aims.

## The Islamic Tradition

In the Islamic tradition, respect is expressed in the concept of *tawqir* (reverence), which includes reverence for God, for parents, for scholars, and for elders. A hadith states: "He is not one of us who does not revere our elders and show mercy to our young." Ibn Hazm, in *al-Akhlaq wa al-Siyar*, treats the avoidance of harming others and good conduct in companionship as part of moral discipline of the self. The Qur'an also affirms a dignity belonging to the children of Adam as such (17:70), alongside the added regard given to knowledge, piety and age.

## The Western Philosophical Context

Immanuel Kant gave an influential philosophical formulation of this concept through the second formulation of the Categorical Imperative: roughly: act so that you use humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always at the same time as an end and never merely as a means (*Groundwork* 4:429). Kant thereby articulated the concept of "dignity" (Würde) as a value that cannot be exchanged for a price. This principle was later employed in the philosophy of human rights, since the prohibition of slavery and torture rests on the idea that a human being deserves respect that is not conditional on their usefulness.

## Objections and an Example

The question arises of the limits of respect: is the sinner or the oppressor to be shown the same respect as the virtuous? Many moral philosophers answer with a distinction like Stephen Darwall's between "recognition respect," owed to persons as such, and "appraisal respect," which is earned through merit or conduct. An example is the treatment of prisoners of war in Islamic history, where, according to traditional reports about the captives of Badr, the Prophet enjoined kindness toward them despite their hostility, as an embodiment of respect for their humanity even in a state of enmity.

## Mutual Respect

Respect in its full form includes a reciprocal dimension. Just as the individual deserves the respect of others, they are also required to respect themselves first, since one who degrades themselves through submission to vice or through neglect of their dignity cannot demand respect from others. In the contemporary context, the concept of respect is invoked in discussions of cultural and religious diversity, where the question is how to respect the beliefs of others that differ from one's own without adopting or endorsing them. A distinction is usually drawn between "tolerance," which accepts coexistence despite difference, and "respect," which acknowledges the right of the other to their choice regardless of one's judgment of its content.
