# Form and Substance in Expression and Content

A precise formal extension, developed by Louis Hjelmslev, of Saussure's dichotomy of signifier and signified. It treats the two planes of the linguistic sign as expression and content, and divides each plane in turn into form and substance, both of which are distinguished from the unformed "purport" (matter) on which they are imposed.

## Four Levels Rather Than Two

Instead of Saussure's dichotomy (signifier/signified), Hjelmslev distinguishes four intersecting levels: **form of expression** (the abstract phonological or morphological structure that defines the phonemes and meaningful units as a system), **substance of expression** (the actual physical material of the sound or written letter in which this form is embodied), **form of content** (the abstract semantic structure that organizes meanings as a system of oppositions), and **substance of content** (the actual conceptual or real material to which this form refers).

## The Methodological Priority of Form

Hjelmslev insisted that the true scientific object of linguistics is **form**, not **substance**: the abstract system of relations between units is what determines the identity of a language as a structure, whereas substance (the actual physical sound, or the real material referred to) is a variable accident that may differ from one language to another without changing the formal structure itself.

## Impact

Glossematics represented a very high degree of formal abstraction in structural linguistics. Some later researchers saw in it a quasi-algebraic model of language, although it remained less widely applied in practice because of its extreme abstraction.

## A Cross-Linguistic Illustration

Hjelmslev illustrates how content is formed by comparing how different languages divide the same continuous color spectrum (the unformed purport or matter) into color categories that differ in number and boundaries (the form of content specific to each language); he draws on Welsh color terms for this purpose. Some languages distinguish blue and green with two separate words, while others unite them under a single word. This shows that the form of content is a conventional structure that each language imposes in its own way on a single shared continuum.

## Influence on French Semiotics

Greimas, Roland Barthes, and other pioneers of French structural semiotics adopted Hjelmslev's terminological apparatus (form/substance, expression/content) as a basis for analyzing literary and cultural texts that extend beyond natural language alone. They treated it as a neutral formal framework suitable for describing any signifying system, from myth, to the narrative of the novel, to diet as a cultural code.

## His Relationship to Saussure's Original Dichotomy

Hjelmslev replaced the Saussurean terms signifier/signified with expression/content, and introduced a finer internal distinction. Saussure had already insisted that language is "form, not substance," but he did not develop a systematic four-way distinction between the form and substance of expression and of content. Hjelmslev made this distinction central in his glossematic system.
