# Depression (Existential Perspective)

Depression, existentially considered, is sometimes a response to authentic conditions: awareness that one's life is not one's own, time is running out, meaning is unavailable. This does not deny clinical depression's neurobiological reality; it contextualizes it.

## Depression as Existential Symptom

Yalom distinguishes depression signaling existential avoidance from medical depression. The same person may experience both. Someone clinically depressed (neurochemistry altered) may also be depressed because avoiding freedom and responsibility.

A person living inauthentically—pursuing others' goals, maintaining constraining relationships—may develop depression as unconscious forces demand awareness. Depression is not merely malfunction but a message.

## The Risk of Medicalization

Treating all depression as primarily biochemical sidesteps existential questions. Someone medicated may have elevated mood but unchanged meaningless life. The existential therapist works alongside medications to ask: What is this revealing? What would need to change in how I live?
