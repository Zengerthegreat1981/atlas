# Arabic Semantics: al-Jurjani's Theory of Nazm

## Definition

This concept deals with the semantic theory developed by the rhetorician **Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani** (d. 471 AH / 1078 CE; some sources give 474 AH / 1081 CE), a scholar of Persian origin who wrote in Arabic, in his two books **Dala'il al-I'jaz** (*The Proofs of Inimitability*) and **Asrar al-Balagha** (*The Secrets of Rhetoric*). It is among the most profound contributions of Arabic linguistic thought to the theory of meaning, and it established Arabic rhetoric as a discipline independent of grammar and literary criticism.

## Main Features: The Theory of Nazm

The core of al-Jurjani's theory is the concept of **"nazm"** (composition, the ordering of words): al-Jurjani holds that the eloquence of speech lies not in individual words taken alone (as earlier critics thought when they searched for "clarity" in isolated vocabulary), nor in added decorative figures, but in **the grammatical-semantic relations** that link words to one another according to **the meanings of grammar** (*ma'ani al-nahw*). The order of words in a sentence is thus a direct reflection of the order of meanings in the mind. He described nazm as arranging words in the way the meanings and rules of grammar require. The choice of the position of each word (fronting, postponement, definiteness, indefiniteness, omission, mention) is what produces the rhetorical meaning, not the word in itself.

- **Rejecting the traditional split between "wording" and "meaning"**: al-Jurjani rejected the common dichotomy that separates a "wording" from a "meaning" as two layers, one clothing the other. He held that the grammatical formulation is itself an inseparable part of the meaning.
- **The rhetorical inimitability of the Qur'an**: al-Jurjani applied this theory in *Dala'il al-I'jaz* to explain the **inimitability of the Qur'an** linguistically, showing that the uniqueness of the Qur'anic text lies in the precision of its composition and its inner harmony, not in its individual words alone.
- **Meanings of grammar**: al-Jurjani tied his analysis to precise grammatical categories (such as fronting and postponement, joining and separation, and definiteness and indefiniteness), thus binding the science of grammar to the science of rhetoric in an organic functional relation.

## Context

Al-Jurjani's theory was a response to earlier conceptions in Arabic literary criticism (such as al-Jahiz and Qudama ibn Ja'far), which sought the standards of eloquence in separate categories (wording versus meaning, craft versus natural disposition). Al-Jurjani offered a more coherent theoretical synthesis that unified these dichotomies.

## Impact

Al-Jurjani's work laid the foundations for **'ilm al-ma'ani** (the science of meaning) and **'ilm al-bayan** (the science of eloquent expression) as two independent branches of the Arabic science of rhetoric. It became a basic reference for everyone who came after him in this field. Al-Sakkaki, in *Miftah al-Ulum*, organised al-Jurjani's ideas into a teaching system, and *Dala'il al-I'jaz* is still studied in Arabic rhetoric curricula today. Recent studies have also drawn attention to a striking convergence between the theory of nazm and some approaches in modern linguistics on the relation between syntax and semantics (the semantics-syntax interface).

## Limitations

Al-Jurjani's dense and far-ranging writing, and the absence of a clear systematic arrangement in his original texts, left his ideas in need of later organisation (by al-Sakkaki, for example). It also sometimes led to his theory being reduced, in later teaching manuals, to rigid rhetorical rules far from the vitality it has in his own texts.
