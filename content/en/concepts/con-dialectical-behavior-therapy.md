# Dialectical Behavior Therapy

A therapeutic approach developed by the American psychologist **Marsha Linehan** in the late 1980s. She published its full foundations in *Cognitive-Behavioral Treatment of Borderline Personality Disorder* (1993). It was originally designed to treat patients with **suicidal behavior and recurrent self-harm**, often associated with **borderline personality disorder**, after standard cognitive-behavioral therapy had shown limited success with this group, which is highly impulsive and emotionally intense.

## Foundation: Dialectics

The therapy takes its name from the principle of **dialectics**: the continuous synthesis of apparently opposing pairs without fully committing to either side. The central dialectic in this therapy is the balance between **acceptance**, meaning accepting the patient as they are now without judgment, and **change**, meaning active work to modify destructive behaviors. The model assumes that a therapist who demands change alone, without acceptance, makes the patient feel rejected, while acceptance alone, without pushing toward change, keeps the patient trapped in their suffering.

## The Biosocial Model

Linehan draws on a **biosocial** explanation for the emergence of emotional dysregulation. It arises from an interaction between a **biological emotional vulnerability** (heightened sensitivity to emotional stimuli, stronger and longer-lasting responses, and slow return to baseline) and an **invalidating environment** in childhood, in which the child's feelings are not recognized or responded to consistently and appropriately. This interaction produces a chronic inability to regulate emotion.

## Treatment Structure and the Four Modules

The standard program rests on four simultaneous components. The first is **weekly individual therapy** that addresses behaviors according to a strict priority order: behaviors that threaten life come first, then behaviors that interfere with treatment, then behaviors that impair quality of life. The second is a **weekly skills-training group** based on **four skill modules**: **mindfulness** (the foundation of the program and the first to be taught), **distress tolerance** (acceptance-oriented skills for getting through emotional crises without making the situation worse), **emotion regulation**, and **interpersonal effectiveness**. The last two are oriented toward change. The third is **telephone coaching** between sessions. The fourth is a **consultation team** for therapists, to support them in handling high-risk cases.

## Impact and Extension

Multiple randomized trials have reported that DBT reduces suicidal behavior and self-harm, with some evidence of fewer hospital admissions. This has made it among the best-supported treatments for borderline personality disorder. Its use was later extended to eating disorders, substance use, post-traumatic stress disorder, and adolescents with highly impulsive behavior, through adapted versions of the original program.

## Limitations

The full program is resource-intensive (individual session, group, telephone support, and a weekly consultation team). This makes faithful implementation difficult outside specialized centers. It has led to the spread of shortened versions, whose effectiveness has been less well established than that of the full program tested in clinical trials.
