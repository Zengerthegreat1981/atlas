# Self and Other

The fundamental existential reality is that I am a subject and the other is also a subject, not an object. Buber called this I-Thou relating as opposed to I-It. Yet in everyday life, we habitually treat others as objects (means to our ends) and defend against the vulnerability of genuine otherness.

## The Other as Radically Other

Levinas emphasized that the Other is not assimilable to my categories or control. No matter how well I know someone, there is an irreducible alterity—they are not fully mine to understand or possess. This is both a tragedy (we remain separate) and the basis of ethics (I cannot treat them as mine to use).

In therapy, understanding the self-other distinction is crucial. Many people suffer from enmeshment (losing the boundary between self and other) or from complete isolation (treating others as entirely separate). Authentic relating requires maintaining both connection and separateness.
