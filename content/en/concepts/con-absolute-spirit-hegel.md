# Absolute Spirit and Its Manifestations (Absolute Spirit)

The highest stage of spirit's self-consciousness in Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel, realized in art, religion, and philosophy as the highest rank, after spirit has passed beyond its subjective and objective stages. Spirit here is not a metaphysical being separate from human beings, but humanity's consciousness of itself as it realizes that what it counts as outside itself is its own work.

## Its Place in the Hegelian System

Hegel's notion of spirit has theological roots in his early writings of the Bern (1793-96) and Frankfurt (1797-1800) periods, where he reflected on the "spirit" of Christianity and of love as a unity overcoming division; these manuscripts were not published in his lifetime. The mature conception is developed in the *Phenomenology* and the *Encyclopedia*, and the Absolute Spirit section of the *Encyclopedia* appears in its definitive form in the third edition of 1830.

Hegel first set out the course of consciousness that culminates in Absolute Spirit in 1807 in the "Phenomenology of Spirit," and then reformulated it in a methodical, concentrated way in the third part of the "Encyclopedia of the Philosophical Sciences" (1817) under the title "Philosophy of Spirit." Absolute Spirit forms the conclusion of the movement of the Hegelian dialectic (often popularly summarised as thesis, antithesis and synthesis, a triad Hegel himself rarely used; his own key term is sublation, *Aufhebung*) on which the whole system is built, and it is the concept from which the Hegelian school, in its right and left branches, proceeds in reading the history of consciousness, the state, and religion. In the Encyclopedia, spirit progresses from subjective spirit, that is, soul, consciousness, and mind (treated as anthropology, phenomenology and psychology), to objective spirit, that is, right, morality, and ethical life, which includes the state, and then reaches Absolute Spirit, where it knows itself with pure knowledge.

## Art, Religion, and Philosophy

Absolute Spirit is manifested in three forms, each of which expresses the same truth in a different way. In art, truth appears in sensuous intuition, as in the Greek statue that embodies the divine in human form. In religion, truth is presented by representation, symbol, and rite, and Christianity for Hegel is the absolute religion because the idea of the incarnation of God in it expresses the unity of the divine and the human. Philosophy presents the same truth by the pure concept, so that spirit in it is conscious of itself without the mediation of image or symbol. This ordering means that art and religion are not false for philosophy, but two stages that are preserved in it.

## Its Relation to Hegel's Other Concepts

Absolute Spirit is linked to the concept of the "cunning of historical reason," which explains how the ends of spirit are realized through the passions of individuals and their historical conflicts, and to the "master–slave dialectic," which represents the first stage of the struggle for recognition between self-consciousness and another, before consciousness rises to its higher forms in art, religion, and philosophy. Karl Marx turned this construction upside down in his concept of "alienation": Marx considered that what Hegel calls the self-realization of Absolute Spirit in history is nothing but an idealistically inverted expression of the actual human being's alienation from his labour and his material product, and so he brought the concept back from the spiritual heaven to the material earth.

## Influence and Controversy

The Hegelian school split after Hegel's death in 1831 into a right that reads Absolute Spirit in a way consistent with Christianity and the existing state, and a left that reads it as a critique of religion and politics, among them Feuerbach and Marx. Hegelianism was received in modern Arab thought through translations and expositions; Abdurrahman Badawi wrote on it in the "Encyclopedia of Philosophy," and Imam Abdel Fattah Imam treated Hegel's thought in a series of works including "The Dialectical Method in Hegel" (1969) and "The Development of the Dialectic after Hegel."

## Limits and Criticism

Kierkegaard objected that the Hegelian system swallows the existing individual in the movement of the whole, and claims a complete knowledge that no finite being is capable of. Schopenhauer and the late Schelling objected that reason cannot deduce the whole of reality, so that actual existence remained an enigma that transcends the concept. In the twentieth century Adorno and the philosophers of the Frankfurt School accused the idea of justifying a closed totality and of turning historical suffering into a moment in the course of truth. Researchers still disagree over whether Hegel means by Absolute Spirit a metaphysical substance or whether it is in truth a historical social relation among human beings, as modern non-metaphysical interpretations read it.
