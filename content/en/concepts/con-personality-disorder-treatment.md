# Personality Disorder Treatment

## Definition

**Personality disorder treatments** refer to a group of specialized psychological interventions developed primarily to treat **borderline personality disorder** (BPD), the most researched pattern among the ten personality disorders classified in DSM-5. Until the 1990s, personality disorders were seen as inherently resistant to treatment, but the development of structured therapeutic protocols fundamentally changed this view.

## Main Therapeutic Approaches

- **Dialectical Behavior Therapy** (DBT), developed by **Marsha Linehan** in the late 1980s, combines mindfulness techniques, emotion regulation, distress tolerance, and interpersonal effectiveness skills. It is delivered through weekly individual and group sessions, with periodic consultation for the therapist team. It has the largest evidence base among these approaches.
- **Mentalization-Based Treatment** (MBT), developed by **Peter Fonagy** and **Anthony Bateman**, focuses on improving the patient's ability to understand their own mental states and those of others (emotions and intentions), through individual and group sessions.
- **Schema Therapy**, developed by **Jeffrey Young**, works with "schema modes," which in borderline personality disorder are commonly described as the abandoned/abused child, the angry/impulsive child, the detached protector, and the punitive parent, using experiential, cognitive, and relational methods.
- **Transference-Focused Psychotherapy** (TFP), developed by **Otto Kernberg**, relies on analysis of the patterns of the relationship with the therapist itself, as a reflection of deeper relational patterns.

## Effectiveness of Treatments

Randomized trials support several of these approaches. In a Dutch trial, Giesen-Bloo and colleagues (2006) compared schema therapy with transference-focused psychotherapy and reported greater improvement with schema therapy. Bateman and Fonagy (2009) reported that MBT was superior to structured clinical management over eight years of follow-up in an outpatient comparison. DBT has been tested in multiple trials since Linehan and colleagues' 1991 study. Direct head-to-head comparisons are few, and differences between specific psychotherapies are generally modest and uncertain, so choice often depends on availability, patient preference, and clinical characteristics.

## Impact

These protocols moved personality disorders, foremost borderline disorder, from the category of "untreatable" to conditions in which substantial improvement is possible; long-term follow-up studies describe symptomatic remission in many patients, though functional recovery is less consistent. They also contributed to developing combined therapeutic approaches (combining skills, analysis, and cognition) that influenced therapeutic psychiatry at large, beyond the boundaries of a single disorder.

## Limits

Most of these programs require intensive specialized training for therapists and institutional resources (consultation teams, regular group sessions) that are unavailable in many health care systems. Research on the effectiveness of these approaches in other, non-borderline personality patterns (such as narcissistic and schizoid) remains limited compared with the evidence on borderline disorder.
