# Aging

Aging, existentially, is not merely biological but continuous confrontation with limitation, loss, and finitude. It is lived awareness that future available is diminishing and certain possibilities are foreclosing.

## Aging and Mortality Salience

Existential significance of aging is making mortality concrete. An adolescent believes in death abstractly; an aging person confronts it daily in diminished capacity, lost peers, visible approach of end. This confrontation, Yalom argues, catalyzes authentic reassessment of priorities and values.

## Wisdom and Decline

Existential perspective resists both romanticization and despair. A person simultaneously experiences physical decline and psychological growth, loses relationships and deepens commitment to those remaining.

Therapeutic work navigates this paradox: help people grieve real losses while discovering what becomes possible in remaining time. Many who postponed authentic engagement discover aging forces the issue—no time for inauthenticity.
