# Presence and Absence

Presence—authentic engagement in the moment and in relationship—is central to existential therapy. Yet presence is always shadowed by absence: the awareness of what is not here (the dead, the lost, the unlived possibilities) and the awareness that this moment will pass.

## Presence in Therapy

The therapeutic relationship cultivates presence: the therapist's full attention to the client, the client's gradual capacity to be fully present rather than hidden or defended. This presence is distinct from mere attention; it is an I-Thou encounter in Buber's sense.

Many psychological difficulties involve inability to be present: dissociation (psychologically absent), rumination (mentally absent from the moment), anxious projection (fantasizing rather than engaging what is). Therapeutic work involves gradually recovering capacity for presence.

## Absence as Structural

Yet absence is not merely a failure or pathology. Absence shapes meaning. A person is defined partly by what is absent—the unlived life, the person no longer here, the possibility foreclosed. Acknowledging absence (rather than defending against it) is part of authenticity.
