# Individuation

Individuation is the process of becoming differentiated from the collective—family, culture, peer group—and discovering one's own authentic nature. Jung developed this concept as central to psychological maturation.

## Individuation and Authenticity

Existentially, individuation is inseparable from authenticity. To live one's own life rather than a borrowed one requires the capacity to distinguish between internalized others' voices and one's own authentic values. Many people never fully individuate; they remain psychologically fused with family systems or cultural expectations.

Individuation is not selfish isolation but the prerequisite for genuine relationship. Two individuals can meet; two non-differentiated people who are still fused with their families or cultures cannot truly encounter each other.

## The Anxiety of Individuation

Individuation is always anxiety-producing because it involves loss of merger and the burden of responsibility. A young person may defer individuation to avoid the guilt of leaving family expectations behind, or to avoid the freedom and responsibility that comes with claiming one's own life.
