# Practice Theory

A theoretical framework that emerged in anthropology and sociology from the late 1970s. It seeks to move beyond the classical dichotomy between **structure**, which determines the behaviour of individuals in advance, and **individual agency**, which assumes unconstrained freedom of choice. Practice theory views social life as a fabric of **repeated and embodied practices** through which actors simultaneously reproduce or transform social structure.

## Origins: Bourdieu and Giddens

The French thinker **Pierre Bourdieu**, the most prominent formulator of this framework, in his books *Outline of a Theory of Practice* (1972/1977) and *The Logic of Practice* (1980), introduced the concept of **habitus**: a set of durable practical dispositions, acquired through social upbringing and embodied in the body, modes of perception and taste, which generate "practical" behaviour that is entirely unconscious of its own rules, yet is ordered and consistent with the individual's class and social position. For Bourdieu, habitus is not a strict law but a "practical logic" that permits limited improvisation within socially imposed boundaries.

In parallel, the British sociologist **Anthony Giddens** developed the theory of **structuration** in *Central Problems in Social Theory* (1979) and *The Constitution of Society* (1984), emphasising the **duality of structure**: social structure is at once the medium of social action and its outcome, so that no structure exists independently of the practices that continually reproduce it. Giddens grants actors a greater degree of conscious **reflexivity** than Bourdieu does, since Bourdieu holds that habitus usually operates without conscious reflection.

## Ortner and Anthropology

The American anthropologist **Sherry Ortner** carried this framework into the heart of anthropological theory in her landmark article "Theory in Anthropology since the Sixties" (1984), proposing practice (or action) as the organising idea for a new anthropology of power and history, one that asks how actors deal with the constraints and contradictions of their societies by adapting, resisting or creatively reappropriating them. Ortner focused in particular on power and history, highlighting practice as a site of struggle rather than mere mechanical repetition.

## Impact and Applications

Practice theory has had a wide influence on cultural and social anthropology since the 1980s. It offered an alternative to both structuralism (which reduces the actor to a product of structure) and methodological individualism (which ignores social constraints). It has been used in studies of class and education (how the educational system reproduces class inequality through habitus), gender studies, and cultural political economy, and it has become one of the basic frameworks in the teaching of contemporary anthropological theory.

## Limitations

The concept of habitus is sometimes criticised for being empirically vague (how is it measured or directly observed?), and for the fact that, despite its claim to transcend the determinism of structure, it may fall into a hidden determinism that makes radical social change difficult to explain within its framework.
