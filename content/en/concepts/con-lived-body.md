# The Lived Body (Leib vs. Körper)

A founding concept in somatic phenomenology (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Henry), which distinguishes between:

- **Körper**: the body as an objective physical entity, an extended material thing studied by anatomy, biology, and the natural sciences.
- **Leib**: the lived body, the body experienced from within, the conscious subject and zero-point (*Nullpunkt*) of all our perceptual orientation.

## Historical Development

- **Husserl in *Ideas II* (written 1912–1928, published posthumously in 1952)**: Husserl established the foundational distinction between the "lived body" (*Leib*) and the "physical thing-body" (*Körper*). He argued that the body is the "center of orientation" through which consciousness experiences spatial reality. In the classic example of one hand touching the other (double sensation or *doppelte Empfindung*), the body is experienced simultaneously as a sensing subject and a sensed physical object.
- **Merleau-Ponty in *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945)**: He placed the lived body (*corps propre*) at the center of phenomenology, rejecting both Cartesian intellectualism (which reduces the subject to pure cogito) and physiological mechanism (which reduces the body to a mere object). For Merleau-Ponty, perception is inherently an embodied act, and the lived body is our primary vehicle for being-in-the-world (*être-au-monde*).
- **Michel Henry in *Philosophy and Phenomenology of the Body* (1965)**: Henry radicalized the phenomenology of the body by emphasizing radical immanence, distinguishing between the external transcendent body known through worldly representation and the lived body experienced in pure, immediate self-affection.
- **Helmuth Plessner in *The Levels of Organic Life and the Human* (1928)**: Formulated the essential anthropological duality: the human being both "has a body" (*Körper haben*) and "is a lived body" (*Leib sein*), a double relation he termed "eccentric positionality" (*exzentrische Positionalität*).

## Bodily Awareness and the Lived Body

The distinction between *Leib* and *Körper* is central to the analysis of bodily awareness in phenomenology and contemporary cognitive science:

- **Pre-reflective bodily awareness**: In everyday perception and action, the body functions as the tacit subject and medium of experience rather than its object. In kinaesthesis and proprioception, the agent does not observe limbs as external objects; bodily awareness structures intentional engagement with the world from within. Shaun Gallagher characterizes this non-thematic, automatic sensorimotor regulation as the **body schema** (*schéma corporel*), which governs posture and movement without requiring conscious monitoring.
- **Reflective bodily awareness**: When the body itself becomes an intentional object of attention—such as when viewing oneself in a mirror, experiencing localized pain, or undergoing medical examination—the lived body is thematic. Gallagher distinguishes this perceptual, emotional, and cognitive representation as the **body image**.
- **Bodily disappearance and dys-appearance**: As Drew Leder shows in *The Absent Body* (1990), the lived body typically recedes from focal attention during smooth, healthy functioning (focal disappearance). However, under conditions of illness, injury, or severe pain, it obtrudes into consciousness as an alien, resistant obstacle (*Körper*), a state Leder calls bodily "dys-appearance."

## The Body in the Islamic Philosophical Tradition

Classical Islamic philosophy did not deploy the German distinction between *Leib* and *Körper* in these terms, but Mulla Sadra Shirazi offered a metaphysical conception that parallels it in meaning. For Sadra, the body is not a foreign vessel accidentally conjoined to the soul, but an initial phase of its unified existence. Formulating his thesis that the soul is "corporeally originated and spiritually subsistent" (*jismāniyyat al-hudūth rūhāniyyat al-baqā'*), Sadra distinguished between the coarse physical body (*al-jism al-kathīf*) and the subtle, imaginal body (*al-jism al-laṭīf*) animated and governed directly by psychic presence. While Sadra's framework is metaphysical rather than phenomenological, it similarly overcomes mechanistic dualism.

## Influence

- **Feminist philosophy**: Iris Marion Young, in "Throwing Like a Girl" (1980), utilized the phenomenology of the lived body to analyze how patriarchal socialization restricts feminine bodily comportment, motility, and spatiality. Luce Irigaray similarly explored sexual difference in embodiment.
- **Phenomenological psychopathology and medicine**: Ludwig Binswanger and Medard Boss integrated embodiment into existential psychiatry. In clinical philosophy of medicine, S. Kay Toombs (*The Meaning of Illness*, 1992) described how illness transforms the lived body from a transparent medium of action into an explicit physical obstacle, producing an epistemological divergence between the physician's view of *Körper* and the patient's lived experience of *Leib*.
- **Postcolonial theory**: Frantz Fanon, in *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952), challenged classical European phenomenology for neglecting the social construction of embodiment, introducing the concept of a "historical-racial schema" that racialized subjects experience when objectified by the colonial gaze.
