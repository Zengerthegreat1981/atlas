# Documentary Evidence in Ancient History

Ancient historians increasingly relied on and valued documentary evidence—written records, inscriptions, official documents—alongside oral testimony.

## Definition and Overview

Documentary evidence in ancient historiography included written records, official inscriptions, letters, administrative documents, and other preserved texts. Ancient historians recognized that documents provided more reliable evidence than oral testimony (less subject to distortion through retelling). Polybius explicitly valued documents; Livy had access to official records. The challenge lay in finding, accessing, and correctly interpreting documents. Not all history left documentary traces, and historians had to work with what remained.

## Historical Context and Development

As historical writing became more sophisticated, historians increasingly sought to access documentary evidence. The development of writing and preservation of documents created possibilities for documentary investigation. Official record-keeping provided bases for historical writing. The loss of documents through time meant historians worked with fragmentary evidence.

## Key Contributions and Applications

Ancient historians contributed recognition that documents were valuable evidence; development of methods for evaluating document authenticity and reliability; integration of documentary and testimonial evidence; and systematic use of archives where accessible.

## Theoretical Framework

Documentary evidence was valued as more reliable than oral testimony; ancient historians recognized that not all events left documentary traces; and they understood that documents required interpretation.

## Examples and Case Studies

Polybius sought out official records when available. Livy had access to Roman official records and used them to verify accounts. Inscriptions provided fixed records of dates, officials, and events that could verify or challenge textual accounts.

## Contemporary Relevance

Ancient use of documentary evidence prefigures modern historical emphasis on documentary sources. Modern historians have expanded the types of documents considered (tax records, private letters, accounting documents) beyond what ancient historians typically used.

## Limitations and Gaps

Documentary evidence available to ancient historians was limited and fragmentary. Many documents were lost. Literacy restricted documentation to certain classes and institutions. Documents required interpretation and could be misleading or false.
