# Unequal Exchange

**Unequal exchange** is a Marxist thesis in the political economy of international trade. It holds that commercial exchange between advanced industrial countries (the centre) and developing countries that export raw materials and labour-intensive goods (the periphery) involves a systematic and hidden transfer of economic surplus from the periphery to the centre, even under trade that is apparently "free" and voluntary between the two parties.

## Emmanuel's Formulation

**Arghiri Emmanuel** provided the most detailed analytical formulation in his book *Unequal Exchange: A Study of the Imperialism of Trade* (1972), drawing on the Marxist labour theory of value. Emmanuel assumed that capital is mobile internationally so that profit rates tend to equalize, while labour is not, so that wage levels differ greatly and are treated as determined by historical and political factors rather than by productivity alone. Under these assumptions, goods embodying the same labour time exchange at prices that favour the high-wage country, so that one hour of labour in the periphery is exchanged for a smaller part of an hour of labour in the centre. In other words, workers in the periphery produce more value than they receive in return, and this difference (surplus value) passes to the centre through the normal mechanism of prices, without any visible fraud or violation of free-market rules.

## Samir Amin's Development of the Thesis

**Samir Amin** extended the thesis in *Accumulation on a World Scale* (French 1970; English 1974), within a broader account of peripheral accumulation. He argued that this pattern of exchange helps reproduce a dependent structure that keeps peripheries in lower-value activities while the centre retains capital- and technology-intensive sectors, and that free trade alone does not undo that pattern.

## Its Relationship to the Prebisch-Singer Hypothesis

This analysis intersects with the Prebisch-Singer hypothesis on the deterioration of the terms of trade, but goes further. Whereas the Prebisch-Singer hypothesis focuses on a price decline caused by differences in demand elasticity, Emmanuel grounds the inequality in the wage gap structurally, as the deeper root that explains the price deterioration itself.

## A Critique of Comparative Advantage

The framework constitutes a radical critique of the Ricardian theory of comparative advantage, which assumes that free trade always brings mutual benefit to both parties. Proponents of unequal exchange argue that this apparently mutual exchange conceals a substantively unequal redistribution of the value produced globally.

## Criticism

Emmanuel's argument rests on the labour theory of value and on the transformation of values into prices of production, both disputed even among Marxists. Charles Bettelheim, for example, criticised his treatment of wages as an independent variable, and many economists reject the notion of value transfer altogether.
