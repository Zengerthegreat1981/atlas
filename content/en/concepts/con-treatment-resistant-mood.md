# Treatment-Resistant Mood Disorders

## Definition

**Treatment-resistant mood disorders**, most prominently **treatment-resistant depression** (TRD), describe major depressive episodes or other mood disorders that do not respond sufficiently after at least two antidepressant trials from two different drug classes. The trials must be at an adequate dose and for an adequate duration (usually four to eight weeks), with actual adherence to treatment. Definitions vary, and estimates of the proportion of people with major depressive disorder who meet a treatment-resistance definition are commonly put at around a third, depending on the criteria used.

## Staging Models

Several models have been developed to classify the degree of treatment resistance. The most prominent is the **Maudsley Staging Model** (MSM), proposed by Fekadu and colleagues (2009), which calculates a composite score from the duration and severity of the episode, the number of failed antidepressant trials, and the use of augmentation strategies and ECT. A higher score indicates greater resistance and a poorer expected outcome. Other staging schemes include the Thase–Rush model.

## Treatment Options

- **Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)**: remains an established treatment for severe or treatment-resistant depression, with relatively high response rates even in severe cases or cases accompanied by psychotic symptoms.
- **Ketamine and esketamine**: Recent clinical trials, among them the ELEKT-D trial published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in 2023, showed that intravenous ketamine is no less effective than ECT in non-psychotic treatment-resistant major depression (response of about 55% versus about 41%). However, ketamine does not replace ECT in psychotic or medically critical cases. The US Food and Drug Administration approved **nasal esketamine** (Esketamine/Spravato) in 2019 for treatment-resistant depression, used together with an oral antidepressant.
- **Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS)**: a non-invasive brain stimulation option used in treatment-resistant depression.
- **Augmentation medications**: adding agents such as atypical antipsychotics or lithium to the primary antidepressant.

## Impact

The development of staging models and treatment-resistance criteria has helped standardize the language of clinical research and the design of comparative trials, such as the ketamine versus ECT trials. The approval of nasal esketamine opened a new chapter in psychiatric pharmacology, after decades of relative stagnation in discovering antidepressants with an entirely new mechanism of action (NMDA receptors instead of the traditional serotonin and norepinephrine pathways).

## Limits

There is no full consensus on the threshold for "treatment resistance." The Thase–Rush and Maudsley approaches, for example, differ. Most evidence for ketamine is relatively short-term, and its long-term effects and the risk of misuse remain the subject of ongoing research.
