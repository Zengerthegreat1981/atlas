# Thick Description

A central concept in the interpretive anthropology of Clifford Geertz. He borrowed it from the philosopher Gilbert Ryle and presented it in his essay "Thick Description: Toward an Interpretive Theory of Culture" (in *The Interpretation of Cultures*, 1973).

## Central Idea

Geertz distinguishes between "thin description," the neutral recording of the observable physical movement of an act (an eye blink, for example), and "thick description," which captures the layers of cultural meaning and social context that make the physical movement itself a signal with a specific significance: a mocking wink, a sign of agreement, or a parody of an earlier wink made to ridicule someone. Ryle's well-known example makes the point: the same physical movement of the eye may be an involuntary blink, a deliberate wink, or a mocking imitation of a wink, and these can only be distinguished by understanding the full cultural and social context.

## Method

Geertz held that the anthropologist's task is not to record observable behavior and classify it causally, as the natural sciences do. Rather, it is to "read" culture as a text carrying overlapping layers of meaning, and to write an ethnographic description that captures this semantic complexity instead of reducing it.

## Impact

The concept became one of the most frequently quoted in interpretive anthropology and in the social sciences generally. It influenced the "interpretive turn" in history, sociology, and literary studies. It was later criticized by the wave of the "crisis of representation," on the grounds that "thick description" is itself a single authorial voice of the researcher, one that conceals the multiple actual voices within the culture studied.

## Ethnographic Application: The Balinese Cockfight

Geertz applied this method in his famous essay "Deep Play: Notes on the Balinese Cockfight" (1972). He analyzed how a cockfight in Bali forms a dense symbolic text, in which relations of status, honor, kinship, and village rivalry are condensed into a single symbolic drama. The large sums wagered on the roosters cannot be understood in purely economic terms. They are better understood as a symbolic investment in the social standing of the bettors and their villages.

## Connection to the Arab Heritage

The method of thick description can be linked to an older Arab interpretive tradition that is concerned with context. From the early centuries, scholars of legal theory (such as al-Shafi'i in *al-Risala*) discussed how the apparent meaning of a text relates to its intended scope, and Quranic exegetes later attended to the occasions of revelation (*asbab al-nuzul*), treating historical and social context as necessary for grasping the meaning of a text or act. This offers a loose methodological parallel, in a completely different setting, to Geertz's distinction between the surface description of an apparent act and its full understanding within its cultural and historical fabric.
