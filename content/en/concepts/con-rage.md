# Rage

Rage, in existential terms, is the volcanic eruption of authentic affect—often suppressed authenticity finally breaching defensive structures. Unlike anger (which can be measured), rage is bodily rebellion against inauthenticity or violated boundaries.

## Rage as Existential Signal

May and Yalom observe rage surfaces when confronting one's own complicity in suffering. Someone who silently accepted humiliation may explode when triggered—not because of the trigger but because it touches accumulated self-betrayal.

This does not excuse destructive expression, but clarifies what rage communicates: a self fighting for recognition and boundaries. The therapeutic task is not to eliminate rage but to translate it into conscious choice: What is this revealing about what I have been tolerating?

## Rage and Responsibility

The existential paradox is that acknowledging rage requires acknowledging complicity. "I was forced" and "I was too afraid to refuse" are often both true. The confrontation with this paradox is existentially complex.

Schneider notes authentic rage (not reactive fury) leads to clarified boundaries and restored agency. Someone who consciously feels their rage and asks "what am I unwilling to accept?" may use that energy to restructure their life.
