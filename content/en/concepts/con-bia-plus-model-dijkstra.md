# Bilingual Interactive Activation Plus Model (BIA+)

## Definition

The BIA+ (Bilingual Interactive Activation Plus) model is a computational cognitive model that explains how bilinguals recognize written words. It was formulated by Ton Dijkstra and Walter van Heuven in 2002 as a successor to the earlier BIA model (Dijkstra and van Heuven, 1998; van Heuven, Dijkstra, and Grainger, 1998). The model assumes that the bilingual mental lexicon is a single integrated lexicon rather than two separate lexicons, and that recognizing any word automatically activates candidates from both languages before the word's membership in a particular language is determined. This is known as language non-selective lexical access.

## Structure and Levels

Like the BIA model, BIA+ represents word recognition through a network of interconnected nodes (letter features, letters, whole words, and language nodes that tag each activated word with its language); BIA+ additionally integrates orthographic, phonological, and semantic representations. Candidates from both languages compete inhibitorily until one wins, under the influence of contextual factors, word frequency, and formal similarity between the two languages (cognates).

## The Addition to the Original Model: The Decision System

The essential difference between BIA and BIA+ is the separation of the word-identification system from the task/decision system. In the 2002 model, language nodes no longer inhibit words of the non-target language from the top down, as they could in BIA. Non-linguistic context, such as the task instruction or the composition of the stimulus list, is assigned to the decision system. Linguistic context, such as a sentence, may affect identification through semantic and syntactic pre-activation, but it does not by itself switch off the non-target language. The model thereby expects non-selective activation even when the participant knows which language the target word belongs to.

## Experimental Evidence

The model is based on the results of lexical decision tasks and eye-movement tracking during reading, which showed that words that are formally similar across two languages (cognates) are processed faster than words familiar in only one language, and that interlingual homographs (words that look identical in both languages but have different meanings) can slow the response. Both patterns are hard to explain if lexical access were selective by language from the outset.

## Impact and Extensions

BIA+ has become a standard reference model in the psycholinguistics of bilingual visual word recognition. It later prompted more dynamic computational extensions, such as the Multilink model (Dijkstra et al., 2019), and it provided a theoretical background for research on code-switching and code-mixing, since it explains the continuous readiness of the lexical system for both languages during speaking and comprehension.
