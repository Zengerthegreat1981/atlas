# Anxiolytics & Benzodiazepines

## Definition

Anxiolytics are drugs that relieve tension and acute anxiety. The best known are the benzodiazepines, such as diazepam (Valium), alprazolam, lorazepam, and clonazepam. They are positive allosteric modulators of the GABA-A receptor, increasing the effectiveness of the inhibitory transmitter GABA, which produces calming, sedation, muscle relaxation, and prevention of seizures. Alongside them, alternatives have been used, such as buspirone, antihistamines such as hydroxyzine, and beta-blockers for physical symptoms.

## History of Discovery

Benzodiazepines superseded barbiturates, which are dangerous in overdose and quick to cause addiction. Leo Sternbach, a chemist at Hoffmann-La Roche, synthesized a series of compounds in 1955. In 1957 a compound from the series that had been neglected was re-tested and found to have a sedative property, becoming chlordiazepoxide (Librium), which was marketed in 1960, followed by diazepam in 1963. The drugs gained enormous popularity in the 1960s and 1970s, and diazepam became one of the most prescribed drugs in the world. In *The Age of Anxiety* (Basic Books, 2009), Andrea Tone recounts how these drugs created a therapeutic culture for the anxiety of modern life, and how they became associated with the image of the "little helper" for mothers. In 1977, Möhler and Okada reported high-affinity benzodiazepine binding sites in *Science*. In the same year Braestrup and Squires reported specific benzodiazepine binding in rat brain, an independent line of evidence for a recognition site on the GABA-A receptor complex.

## Effectiveness and Risks

Benzodiazepines are effective and fast-acting in acute anxiety, panic, short-term insomnia, acute seizures, and alcohol withdrawal. Prolonged use, however, produces tolerance, dependence, and withdrawal, which may include seizures. Lader, in *Addiction* in 2011, examined how repeated warnings since the 1970s had not changed the picture much. Ashton, in *Current Opinion in Psychiatry* in 2005, described the clinical picture of dependence and a widely cited approach to gradual withdrawal, which should be supervised by a clinician. Risks are heightened in older adults, for whom they are associated with falls, fractures, and cognitive impairment, and in combination with opioids and alcohol, where they suppress breathing. For this reason, SSRI antidepressants and cognitive behavioral therapy are preferred as first-line treatment for chronic anxiety (see the entries on Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors and Anxiety).

## Social Context

Use has been linked to social differences: benzodiazepines have been prescribed to women more than to men. Drug authorities impose limits on duration, and some national guidance (for example in the United Kingdom) recommends limiting use to about two to four weeks. In psychiatry they are also used for acute agitation and catatonia, where lorazepam is a first-line treatment.

## Limits of the Evidence

The drug does not treat the causes of anxiety, and its effect is temporary. The literature discusses that taking benzodiazepines during exposure therapy may weaken the learning of safety on which the treatment is based, which would reduce its long-term gains, although the evidence is not conclusive (see the entry on Exposure Therapy). This is an important consideration for people with phobias who take the medication before sessions.
