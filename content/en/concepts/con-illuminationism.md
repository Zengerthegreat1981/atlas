# Illuminationism (Hikmat al-Ishraq)

## Definition

**Illuminationism** (*Hikmat al-Ishraq*, "the Theosophy of Illumination") is an Islamic philosophical-mystical school founded by the Persian philosopher **Shihab al-Din Yahya al-Suhrawardi** (d. 1191 CE, known as "the Martyred Shaykh"). It reached its fullest form in his principal work, ***Hikmat al-Ishraq***, which he completed in 1186 CE. The school rests on the metaphor of **light** as the first ontological and epistemological category. For al-Suhrawardi, existence consists of degrees of light, descending from the "Light of Lights" (the absolute divine essence) to the lowest degrees of darkness (matter). Every existent is simply a degree of greater or lesser intensity of light.

## Origins and Context

Illuminationism arose from al-Suhrawardi's critique of the Peripatetic philosophy (the Aristotelian-Avicennan tradition) that had dominated Islamic philosophy before him, although he did not reject it entirely. He drew on the logic of **Ibn Sina** and on his final references to the "Eastern wisdom" (*al-hikma al-mashriqiyya*), which Ibn Sina left as an incomplete horizon. Al-Suhrawardi developed this into an integrated system that blends Greek philosophy (Plato and Neoplatonism), ancient Persian wisdom (Zoroastrian and earlier), and Islamic Sufism, and presented itself as an alternative to pure Peripateticism.

## Main Features

- **Metaphysics of light**: Existence is a chain of graded luminous emanations (*nur al-anwar*), instead of the abstract category of "being" in Ibn Sina.
- **Critique of the essence–existence distinction**: Al-Suhrawardi departed from the sharp Peripatetic distinction between essence (*mahiyya*) and existence (*wujud*). He treated "existence" as a mental consideration rather than an independent external reality.
- **Presential knowledge** (*al-'ilm al-huduri*): Alongside discursive knowledge, al-Suhrawardi emphasized a direct, non-mediated knowledge that the gnostic attains through unveiling (*kashf*) and inner witnessing, not through logical demonstration alone.
- **Symbolic language**: Al-Suhrawardi used symbolic tales (such as "The Red Intellect" and "The Tale of the Occidental Exile") to convey his wisdom, drawing on ancient Persian heritage.

## Impact

After al-Suhrawardi, the Illuminationist school spread through major commentators such as **Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi** and **Shams al-Din al-Shahrazuri**. It remained alive in Iranian philosophical circles through the Safavid period, when it merged with the Isfahan school of philosophy (Mulla Sadra), which built its "Transcendent Theosophy" (*al-hikma al-muta'aliya*) on a synthetic basis combining Illuminationism, Peripateticism and gnosis (*irfan*). Its ideas continue to be studied in Iranian philosophical and religious institutions today.

## Limitations

Al-Suhrawardi was executed in 1191 CE in Aleppo on charges of heresy (*zandaqa*), by order of Saladin. His school found its main continuation in Persian-speaking and Iranian philosophical circles, and its presence in the Arabic philosophical corpus of the Sunni world was limited compared with Peripateticism.
