# Responsibility

The troublesome twin of freedom, and the most dangerous concept in the school in terms of the potential for its misuse.

## Freeing the Concept

Responsibility here is neither guilt nor blame. It is for a person to be the one who responds: to move from "this is what happened to me" to "this is what I do with what happened to me." And there is disagreement over where its scope begins and where it ends.

## Core Disagreements within the Concept

The real disagreement is not philosophical but about the <b>condition</b>: when is it permissible to raise responsibility? May and Van Deurzen set conditions (awareness, capacity, and examining the dimensions). Sartre sets no condition. And the American formulation inherited Sartre without conditions, hence the criticism.

## How It Appears in the Session

It appears in language before anything else: the absence of an agent, generalized phrasings, and attributing everything to others or circumstances. The work is gradual: the agent is first restored in one small sentence, not in a big decision.

## The Most Common Misunderstanding

Confusing it with guilt and blame. Responsibility is directed forward (what am I doing now), while guilt is directed backward (what did I do). Confusing them turns a tool of liberation into a tool of infliction of pain.

## Example and Counter-Example

<b>Example:</b> A man discovers that his failed marriage was not fate but the result of accumulated choices, his repeated silence and his avoidance of confrontation, and begins to take responsibility for his actual role without self-flagellation. This is an authentic use: responsibility directed forward (what do I do now), not a judgment on the past.<br><b>Counter-example:</b> A therapist tells a client who was assaulted, "You are responsible for choosing how you respond to what happened," in a way that implicitly burdens her with the blame for what happened to her. This is a dangerous confusion between existential responsibility (for the situation and the response) and responsibility for the act itself (which falls on the perpetrator alone), and it is one of the most dangerous clinical misuses of this concept.

## What Should Not Be Inferred from This Concept

It <b>does not mean</b> that the victim is responsible for what happened to her, or that every bad outcome in a person's life is "their choice." This is a gross confusion between existential responsibility for the present situation and blaming someone who has suffered injustice or violence.<br>It <b>also does not mean</b> that structural factors (poverty, discrimination, illness) do not exist or have no effect: existential responsibility is exercised within these factors, not in isolation from them, and ignoring this turns a liberating concept into an outright tool of blame.

## The Arab and Cultural Context

The concept of absolute individual responsibility (no one decides on my behalf, and no excuse exempts me) collides in Arab contexts with real collective decision systems, where major decisions (marriage, work, even housing) are often taken through binding consultation with the family, not in isolation from it. Asking an Arab client to "take their individual responsibility" without acknowledging the collective decision structure within which they live may be unrealistic or even harmful.<br>There is no precise Arabic equivalent of the idea of "existential responsibility" in its exact Sartrean sense (which differs from moral or legal responsibility); the Arabic word "mas'uliyya" is closer to its traditional religious or social meaning. And the therapeutic literature that builds its techniques on this fine distinction was built entirely on Western individualist contexts, which calls for real adaptation, not literal translation, in Arab application.

## Comparative Table

| Thinker | His exact formulation | What follows in the room | His disagreement with others |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sartre | Full responsibility for the meaning of the situation, not for its occurrence. I do not choose the war, but I choose what it means to me and how I exist within it. | The decisive distinction in the session: responsible for the response, not for the event. | The most extreme formulation, and it needs Beauvoirian reservation to become usable. |
| Frankl | Responsibility is a response to a question that life poses. Life asks and the human answers with their life, not with their words. | Reversing the question in the session: instead of "what do I expect from life?" it becomes "what does life expect from me now?" | Assumes a system of meaning wider than the individual, and this is what the secular current rejects. |
| Beauvoir | Responsibility is ethical and political: my freedom binds me to the freedom of others. Whoever ignores the situation of others is not free but heedless. | Widening the circle of the question: your responsibility for yourself, and for those who share your situation. | A direct correction of Sartre's individualism. |
| Rollo May | Responsibility is a capacity to respond, and requires awareness and capacity. Whoever lacks either is not held to account. | A practical condition: before responsibility is raised, it is checked that the person sees their situation and has a margin to act. | The most mature clinical safeguard in the school for this concept. |
| Yalom | Avoiding responsibility is the most common defense: postponement, dependence, feigned helplessness, and attributing the decision to circumstance. And change does not begin before acknowledgment. | Spotting the forms of avoidance in the language itself, and confronting them gently. | Clinically useful, and prone to slipping into blame if the situation is neglected. |
| Van Deurzen | Responsibility is examined across the four dimensions: what one is responsible for physically, socially, personally and in terms of values, and these differ. | Prevents generalization: a person may be responsible in one dimension and powerless in another. | The most precise practical elaboration of the concept. |
| Heidegger | Not a moral responsibility but taking over existence: that my existence be my own existence, not the existence of "the They." | Not translated into a duty. The aim is for the person to see how much of their life they live on behalf of an anonymous someone. | The furthest of the formulations from direct therapeutic use. |
