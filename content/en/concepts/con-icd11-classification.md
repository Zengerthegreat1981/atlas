# ICD-11 Classification

The eleventh revision of the **International Classification of Diseases**, issued by the World Health Organization. The World Health Assembly adopted it in 2019. It came into effect for international mortality reporting on 1 January 2022. Countries introduce it for morbidity coding on their own timetables. It includes a chapter on mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders, the main international counterpart of the American DSM-5 and DSM-5-TR.

## General Structure

ICD-11 expands from the 22 chapters of ICD-10 to 26 chapters, plus a supplementary section for functioning assessment (V) and extension codes (X) used in postcoordination. New or relocated chapters include immune-system diseases, sleep–wake disorders, conditions related to sexual health, and a supplementary chapter on traditional medicine. Coding can add detail with stem codes and extension codes rather than by multiplying precomposed categories.

## Notable Changes in the Classification of Mental Disorders

- **Neurodevelopmental disorders**: the eight subcategories of "pervasive developmental disorders" in ICD-10 were replaced by a single unified category, **"autism spectrum disorder,"** matching the same shift seen in the American DSM-5.
- **Personality disorders**: ICD-11 adopted a new approach in which a single diagnosis of personality disorder is graded by **degree of severity** (mild, moderate, or severe) rather than assigned to traditional subtypes (such as narcissistic or antisocial), with trait domain specifiers (negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia) and an optional "borderline pattern" specifier.
- **Psychotic disorders**: the category was renamed from "schizophrenia and schizotypal and delusional disorders" to **"schizophrenia or other primary psychotic disorders."**
- **Mood disorders**: **symptom clusters** (affective, cognitive, and neurovegetative) were introduced for depressive episodes, and a distinction was made between partial and full remission.
- **New or reorganized categories**: complex post-traumatic stress disorder, prolonged grief disorder, hoarding disorder, binge-eating disorder, and gaming disorder were added as diagnoses, and body dysmorphic disorder was placed within the obsessive-compulsive and related disorders group. Gender incongruence was moved out of the mental disorders chapter into a chapter on conditions related to sexual health.

## The General Philosophy of the Classification

Despite substantial changes in specific categories, ICD-11, like its predecessor, remains essentially a **categorical** classification that relies on reported or clinically observed symptoms. It takes limited steps toward **dimensionality** in some categories (such as personality disorders), but it is not a full shift to the comprehensive dimensional model called for by some researchers. In 2024 WHO published the *Clinical Descriptions and Diagnostic Requirements* (CDDR) for the mental, behavioural and neurodevelopmental disorders chapter. That manual gives the clinical guidance used with the statistical codes and replaces the earlier draft clinical descriptions and diagnostic guidelines.

## Limits

- The difference between ICD-11 and the American DSM-5 remains in the details of many criteria despite their growing convergence, which creates challenges for comparative research across health systems worldwide.
- The partial shift toward dimensionality in the classification of personality disorders faces clinical application challenges that are still being evaluated in daily practice.
