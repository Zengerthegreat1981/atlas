# Community Mental Health

## Definition

Community mental health is an approach to organizing care in which treatment for people with mental disorders is moved from large isolated asylums to services close to populations and their environments. It includes clinics, day centers, mobile teams, supported housing, and integration with primary care, with a focus on prevention, community participation, and patients' rights.

## Roots in the Arab Maristan

Medieval Islamic societies preceded the West in establishing specialized institutions. The *maristans* in Baghdad, Damascus, and Cairo included wards for the insane and others for physical illnesses. According to Michael Dols's study in *Majnun: The Madman in Medieval Islamic Society* (Clarendon Press, Oxford, 1992), care for the insane was a moral and religious duty, and physicians treated them with measures such as baths, diet, music, and medication. The Maristan Qalawun in Cairo, founded in 683 AH (1284 CE), is cited as an example of a free institution open to all. A full identification with the modern concept is not valid, however: many patients were cared for in their homes and at shrines, and cases of restraint and cruelty were sometimes described.

## The Twentieth-Century Shift

Social studies criticized asylums as "total institutions," the best known being Goffman's *Asylums* (1961). In the United States, President Kennedy signed the Community Mental Health Centers Act on 31 October 1963, and Gerald Caplan wrote *Principles of Preventive Psychiatry* (Basic Books, 1964) on the levels of prevention. In Italy, Franco Basaglia led the "democratic psychiatry" movement in Trieste, and Law 180 (1978) halted new admissions to psychiatric hospitals and began their phased closure; he had published *L'istituzione negata* in 1968. Stein and Test showed in *Archives of General Psychiatry* in 1980 that an intensive community program, which later became the basis of the "assertive community treatment" model, reduced hospital admissions compared with hospital-based care. In several countries, notably the United States, community services were underfunded and did not absorb discharged patients, and critics linked this to homelessness and imprisonment. This is a real critique of the experience of "deinstitutionalization."

## Global Mental Health

The *Lancet* published a series on global mental health in 2007, including Patel and colleagues' article on treating disorders in low-income countries. The World Health Organization launched the mhGAP program in 2008 to train non-specialist workers. Thornicroft and Tansella presented a model of balance between community and hospital care in the *British Journal of Psychiatry* in 2004. In the Arab world, the Gaza Community Mental Health Programme was established in 1990 under the leadership of Eyad El Sarraj, integrating psychological treatment with the defense of rights in the context of occupation.

## Limits

The model requires sustained funding and a trained workforce. These conditions are absent in several countries, so that the transfer from the hospital becomes neglect. Community participation does not guarantee protection of the patient from stigma (see the entry on Mental Health Stigma) or from harmful practices carried out in the name of traditional healing (see the entry on Mental Health and Human Rights).
