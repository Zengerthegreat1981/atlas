# Psychotic Spectrum Disorders

## Definition

**Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders** is a diagnostic class in the DSM-5 that groups psychological disorders sharing one or more of five core domains: **delusions**, **hallucinations**, **disorganized thinking** (speech disturbance), **grossly disorganized or abnormal motor behavior** (including catatonia), and **negative symptoms** (such as flattened affect, poverty of speech, and social withdrawal). The disorders within this spectrum are arranged in ascending order according to the severity, number, and duration of symptoms.

## Structure of the Diagnostic Class

In order of increasing severity, the spectrum includes schizotypal personality disorder (which is also classified among the personality disorders), then **delusional disorder**, then **brief psychotic disorder** (at least one day but less than one month), then **schizophreniform disorder** (one to six months), then **schizophrenia** itself (continuous signs for six months or more, including at least one month of active-phase symptoms, with at least two of the first four symptom domains listed above, one of which must be delusions, hallucinations, or disorganized speech), and **schizoaffective disorder**, in addition to substance/medication-induced psychotic disorder, psychotic disorder due to another medical condition, and catatonia.

## Changes in DSM-5

The fifth edition (2013) abolished the traditional subtypes of schizophrenia (paranoid, disorganized, catatonic, and so on) because of their weak diagnostic stability and scientific validity, replacing them with a dimensional assessment of the severity of each symptom separately. It also reconceptualized **schizoaffective disorder** as a **longitudinal** diagnosis (covering the course of the illness over time) rather than a **cross-sectional** one (a single moment of assessment), as it had been in DSM-IV, a substantive change in diagnostic approach. DSM-5 also removed the special diagnostic weight previously given to Schneiderian "first-rank" symptoms.

## Neural Basis and Treatment

Psychotic disorders are associated with overactivity of dopamine in the mesolimbic pathway (the dopamine hypothesis, which is considered incomplete), and are treated primarily with **antipsychotics** (typical and atypical), alongside psychosocial interventions (cognitive rehabilitation, family therapy, and early intervention programs for first-episode psychosis) that are associated with better long-term outcomes and lower relapse rates. ## Impact

The concept of the "spectrum" helped shift clinical perspective from rigid, separate categories to a continuous dimensional classification. This was reflected in **early intervention** programs targeting individuals in the "at-risk mental state" before full psychosis emerges, with the aim of improving long-term prognosis and reducing the functional disability associated with schizophrenia.

## Limits

The line between schizoaffective disorder and schizophrenia accompanied by mood symptoms remains a continuing diagnostic debate because of overlapping criteria. Estimates of prevalence and prognosis also vary across cultures and social contexts, a gap that the manual itself acknowledges.
