# Essence vs Existence Debates

## Definition

**The debate over essence and existence** is one of the deepest metaphysical problems in medieval Islamic philosophy. It turns on the question of which is the basis and constituent of the other: **essence** (*mahiyya*, "what the thing is"), meaning the reality of a thing and its abstract definition, or **existence** (*wujud*), meaning the actual realisation of that thing outside the mind. **Ibn Sina** (370–428 AH / 980–1037 CE) formulated the basic terms of this debate, and Muslim philosophers later divided into those who held the primacy of essence (*asalat al-mahiyya*) and those who held the primacy of existence (*asalat al-wujud*).

## Historical Context

Ibn Sina clearly distinguished between essence (such as "horseness" as an abstract concept of the horse) and existence (the actual existence of a particular horse outside the mind). He held that existence is **additional to essence** in all contingent beings: the essence of a thing does not by itself entail its existence, so it requires an external cause to bring it into being. The exception is the **Necessary Existent** (God), whose essence is identical with its existence. This Avicennan distinction established the conceptual foundation on which later thinkers built their opposing positions over the centuries.

## Main Dimensions

- **Primacy of essence (in some readings of Ibn Sina and al-Suhrawardi)**: this tendency holds that **essence** is the authentic reality of things, and that "existence" is merely a mental, conventional concept that we add to explain that things are realised, not an independent external reality in itself. **Shihab al-Din al-Suhrawardi** (549–587 AH) in his Illuminationist philosophy adopted a position close to this tendency, regarding existence as a general mental concept rather than a concrete reality, in contrast to the concept of "light", which he made the authentic reality of his system. In *Hikmat al-ishraq* (The Philosophy of Illumination, 1186) he criticised the Peripatetic theory of definition and treated existence as a mental consideration (*i'tibari*), while light, graded in intensity from the Light of Lights downward, served as the basic reality; interpreters differ on how far this makes him an essentialist.
- **Primacy of existence (in Mulla Sadra al-Shirazi)**: the Safavid-era philosopher **Mulla Sadra al-Shirazi** (979–1050 AH / 1571–1640 CE) produced a radical reversal of this debate with his theory of **"the primacy of existence"** (*asalat al-wujud*). He inverted the traditional order, holding that **existence is the only authentic reality**, and that essences are merely "limits" or conventional mental images that we derive from different degrees of existence itself, not independent entities in their own right.
- **Gradation of existence (*tashkik al-wujud*)**: Mulla Sadra linked the primacy of existence to the principle of "gradation", meaning that existence is one reality that varies in **intensity and weakness** (like the varying intensity of light, not the difference of its types). The ranks of all beings, from God to the lowest creatures, are thus understood as degrees of one reality of existence, not as substantially distinct kinds.
- **Lasting philosophical impact**: this debate became one of the deepest points of dialogue between Islamic philosophy and later Western philosophy, especially in comparisons with Heidegger and his concept of "Being". The Sadrian primacy of existence remains a major reference framework in the philosophical teaching of Shi'i seminaries, especially in Iran.
