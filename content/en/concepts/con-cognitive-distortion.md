# Cognitive Distortions

## Definition

Cognitive distortions are systematic patterns of inaccurate or biased thinking that individuals with mood or anxiety disorders tend to use in interpreting themselves, the world, and the future, so that they sustain and feed negative feelings even when real evidence does not support them.

## Historical Context

Aaron Temkin Beck, the American psychiatrist, developed the concept of cognitive distortions within his theory of depression in the early 1960s, and documented his first clinical observations in his 1963 research article "Thinking and Depression," before elaborating the full framework in his landmark book *Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders* (1976). Beck noticed that his depressed patients showed, in psychoanalytic sessions, an automatic flow of distorted negative thoughts about the self, rather than merely repressed aggression turned inward, as Freudian theory then assumed.

## Principal Patterns of Cognitive Distortion

Among the best-known patterns identified by Beck and later developed by David Burns are: dichotomous thinking (everything is black or white with no middle shades), overgeneralization (drawing a general rule from a single incident), mental filtering (focusing exclusively on negative details and ignoring positive ones), jumping to conclusions (such as mind reading or catastrophic prediction), magnification and minimization, personalization (holding oneself responsible for events beyond one's control), "should" statements, and labeling (attaching a global negative description to oneself on the basis of a single mistake).

## Therapeutic Application: Cognitive Restructuring

Cognitive distortions form the main target of treatment in the technique of "Cognitive Restructuring" within cognitive behavioral therapy, in which the patient learns, with the therapist's help, to identify the distorted automatic thought, to examine the evidence for and against it, and to replace it with a more balanced and realistic alternative thought, without this necessarily meaning replacing it with artificial "positive" thinking.

## Impact and Criticism

The concept of cognitive distortions has become a cornerstone of modern cognitive behavioral therapy, which is supported by wide empirical evidence in treating depression and anxiety. Some researchers raise a controversy over the distinction between "cognitive distortion," a flaw in processing information that is in itself accurate, and "cognitive deficit" (Cognitive Deficit), which points to an actual shortage of processing skills, a controversy not fully settled in the clinical literature. The term "cognitive distortions" was also later extended beyond depression to other disorders such as anxiety, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and eating disorders, with the lists of specific patterns adapted to the particular thought content of each disorder separately.
