# Trauma (Existential Perspective)

Trauma, existentially, is rupture of assumptive world—coherent narrative by which we understood ourselves and world. Traumatic event reveals the world is not safe, control is limited, meaning cannot be guaranteed.

## Trauma and Loss of Groundedness

Where cognitive approaches treat trauma as memory to process and psychodynamic approaches as wound to heal, existential therapy asks: What existential realities has trauma exposed? What assumption has shattered?

This doesn't minimize trauma's neurobiological reality or necessity of trauma-focused treatments. Rather, acknowledges part of healing integrates existential knowledge: vulnerability is constitutive of human life, randomness and cruelty are real, safety is always provisional.

## Integration vs. Recovery

Existential framing suggests goal is not "recovery" (return to pre-trauma state) but integration: developing realistic, complex understanding of self, others, world. Someone traumatized has existential knowledge, and healing involves carrying that knowledge.
