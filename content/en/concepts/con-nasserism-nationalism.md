# Nasserism & Arab Nationalism

An Arab political ideology that emerged in Egypt with the July 1952 Revolution and was associated with the Egyptian president **Gamal Abdel Nasser** (1918–1970). It combined unitary Arab nationalism (pan-Arabism), Arab socialism, anti-colonialism and non-alignment, and formed the most influential political current in the Arab world during the 1950s and 1960s.

## Intellectual foundations

- **Arab unity**: a call to unite the Arab countries politically on the basis of the unity of language, religion, history and culture. Its most notable practical expression was the Egyptian-Syrian union (the United Arab Republic, 1958–1961), which ended with Syria's secession.
- **Arab socialism**: the nationalization of major economic sectors (such as the nationalization of the Suez Canal in 1956) and a broad agrarian reform, within an economic model that was statist rather than communist in the strict Soviet sense.
- **Anti-colonialism and non-alignment**: Nasser's founding stance at the Bandung Conference (1955) and in the Non-Aligned Movement, and confrontation with Western and Zionist influence in the region.
- **Populist revolutionary legitimacy**: a political discourse that addresses the "Arab masses" directly, going beyond traditional party and monarchical frameworks.

## Rise and decline

Nasserism reached the peak of its regional influence after the Suez crisis of 1956, which strengthened Nasser's standing as an Arab nationalist leader. It received a crushing blow with the defeat of June 1967 against Israel ("the Naksa"), which revealed the fragility of the military and political structure of the Nasserist project and sharply weakened its ideological appeal. Nasser died in 1970, and his successor Anwar al-Sadat gradually abandoned many of Nasserism's economic and political pillars (the economic opening and peace with Israel).

## Impact

Nasserism influenced many Arab nationalist parties and movements (such as the Arab Nationalist Movement), and remained a symbolic reference point for Arab nationalist and anti-imperialist discourse even after its direct political decline. Its organizational influence, however, receded before the rise of political Islam and of country-focused (*qutri*) currents after the 1970s.

## Caveats

- Scholars disagree over how coherent "Nasserism" was as a complete ideology, since in practice it was marked more by political pragmatism than by a strict codified doctrine.
- Assessments of its legacy are divided between seeing it as a liberating national modernization and seeing it as an authoritarian system that repressed internal political opposition.
